# Workgroup Server 9150 (`wgs9150`, `wgs9150_120`)

The Power Macintosh 8100 board in a server tower, as two profiles — one
per speed grade, because each grade has its own ROM image: `wgs9150`
(9150/80, 80 MHz 601 at 2:1, ROM `$9C7C98F7`) and `wgs9150_120`
(9150/120, 120 MHz at 3:1, ROM `$9B037F6F`).  Everything else is the
8100's model (pm8100.md, pdm.md) — literally: the board and profile
fields the three share are `PDM_8100_BOARD_COMMON` and
`PDM_8100_PROFILE_COMMON` in `pdm.h`, the SIMM totals the one exported
`pm8100_ram_options_kb`, so the two 9150 profiles spell out only their
name, id, clock, ROM and board.  The board descriptor carries three
deltas:

- **Machine ID `$3014`.**  The machine-ID register answers `$A55A3014`
  (the 9150's `%100` code).  Both 9150 ROMs carry a product record for
  `$3014`, and from it the 68k ROM sets BoxFlag `$21` (Gestalt `$27`)
  under the 80 MHz image and `$33` (Gestalt `$39`) under the 120 MHz one.
- **A fourth NuBus slot, `$A`** (`has_slot_a`).  BART decodes slot `$A`'s
  standard and super slot windows (two more entries in the window table,
  `PDM_BART_WINDOWS` = 10), and the FAT AMIC's pseudo-VIA2 slot bank
  carries its /NMRQ at bit 1 (`slot - 9`, the same law as `$B`..`$E`) —
  included in the slot aggregate only on this board.
- **The 3:1 bus on the 9150/120.**  HWInit measures the CPU:bus ratio
  through the HMC wait-state penalty; `wait_state_penalty = 3` lands the
  measurement on a 40 MHz bus (2, the 2:1 boards' value, measures 60 MHz).

Not modeled: BART 21's per-slot burst and expansion-chassis features (the
register file is BART 4's), the FAT AMIC-2 SCSI DMA timing bit, and the
512 KB / 1 MB L2 cache SIMMs.

Verification: `suite-wgs9150` boots System 7.5.3 (System 7.5 itself
refuses the machine) on the 9150/80 and 7.6 on the 9150/120 to the Finder
and opens About This Macintosh on each, boots the 7.6 "Install 1" floppy
on the 9150/80, and runs a 24AC's driver in slot `$A` under 7.6 on the
9150/80; it asserts BoxFlag, the machine-ID register
and, on the 120, the measured 120 / 40 MHz clocks.
