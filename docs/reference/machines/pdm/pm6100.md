# Power Macintosh 6100/60

The Power Macintosh 6100/60 is the smallest box of Apple's PDM platform and the machine the
platform is named after: the low-profile, under-monitor member of the first PowerPC
Macintosh generation of March 1994 — "even the low end of the Power Macintosh family, the
competitively priced Power Macintosh 6100/60, delivers more than twice the performance of
any previous Macintosh computer" [1] §"The First Generation of Power Macintosh" p. 2. A
60 MHz PowerPC 601 rides a 30 MHz CPU bus [1] Table 2-2 p. 19; the box has one
processor-direct slot (PDS) and no NuBus controller at all — NuBus arrives only through an
optional adapter card that carries the BART chip, and the 6100/60AV, whose PDS is occupied
by the AV card, has no NuBus capability whatsoever [1] §"Models and Configurations" p. 3,
§"NuBus Interface" p. 50. This page also covers the **Power Macintosh 6100/66**, the January
1995 refresh of the same box at 66/33 MHz [2] Table 1-2 p. 3 — which, uniquely among the
Enhanced models, keeps the original March 1994 boot ROM [2] Table 1-3 p. 5.

Everything board-level — the ASIC set, the address map, the interrupt model, the boot
contract — is the PDM platform and is documented once in the family doc
([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2–§5); this page carries only what is unique to the 6100, cites the
family doc and the device pages by section, and never restates them. The sibling machines
are the [Power Macintosh 7100](pm7100.md) and the [Power Macintosh 8100](pm8100.md).

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

The 6100 is one logic board shipped in three retail shapes: the **6100/60** (60 MHz, PDS
slot empty, no CD-ROM in the base configuration), the **6100/60AV** (the same board with
the AV card factory-installed in the PDS, on an angle adapter) and the **6100/66** (the
Enhanced refresh at 66 MHz) [1] Table 1-1 p. 4, §"Apple Expansion Cards" pp. 7–8; [2]
Table 1-1 p. 2. The board's project codename is **PDM** ("Piltdown Man") — the name the
platform carries in its own ROM tables, and the name of the whole first-generation
platform [3]; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §1.1. The AV variants are not different motherboards: the
AV card is a PDS card enumerated by its declaration ROM, and no identity register
distinguishes them ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6).

In physical form the machine "resemble[s] the Macintosh Quadra 610 and Macintosh Centris
660AV" — a low-profile enclosure designed to sit under the monitor [1] §"The First
Generation of Power Macintosh" p. 2. The block diagram the developer note draws for the
platform (Figure 2-1, drawn for the 8100/80) marks exactly two dotted-line omissions that
define this machine: on the 6100/60 "the BART NuBus controller and a single NuBus slot
connector are mounted on a plug-in NuBus adapter card", and the fast internal SCSI bus of
the 8100 "is omitted" [1] §"System Architecture" p. 13.

### 1.2 Identity values

The platform's identity machinery — the machine-ID register at the top of I/O space, read
by both halves of the shared ROM before anything else happens — is the family doc's
([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6), with the register itself in [hmc.md](hmc.md) §2.5. This
machine's values:

| Identity value | Value | Witness |
|---|---|---|
| Machine-ID register (`$5FFFFFFC`) | `$A55A3010` — every 6100, all speed grades, AV and non-AV alike; low 3 bits `%000`; readable as individual bytes | [1] Table 1-5 p. 10; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §2.5; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6; [3] |
| Software CPU ID | `$3011` — the 68k identity routine promotes the register's `$3010` to `$3011` after its AMIC revision probe; production 6100 hardware never answers `$3011` | [bart.md](bart.md) §4.2; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6; [3] (promotion at ROM file offset `$10070`; product record at file offset `$15064`) |
| Gestalt machine type | `$4B` (75) — synthesized from the machine-ID low bits plus the *measured* CPU clock; the /66 answers the same `$3010` and `$4B` and is distinguished only by its clock-derived speed grade | [1] Table 1-5 p. 10; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6, §6.4 |
| Board codename | PDM ("Piltdown Man") — the ROM's own product and decoder tables | [pdm.md](pdm.md) §1.1; [3] |
| Boot ROM | the March 1994 image — version `$077D`, header checksum `$9FEB69B3`, bootstrap string "Boot PDM 601 1.0" — on the /60 and the /66 alike (the /66 is the Enhanced model that does *not* take the January 1995 ROM) | [pdm.md](pdm.md) §1.3; [2] Table 1-3 p. 5; [3] |
| NuBus bridge identity | none on the board — BART exists in the system only when the adapter card is installed (slot `$E`); the 6100/66's optional adapter card carries the same BART 4 part | [bart.md](bart.md) §1.2; [2] §"NuBus Support" p. 4 |
| AV identity | none — the AV card is a PDS card at pseudo-slot `$E`, enumerated by its declaration ROM like any slot card | [pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6; [1] §"AV Card" p. 42 |

The `$3010`-to-`$3011` promotion is the machine's signature identity quirk and is
documented with its consequences in [bart.md](bart.md) §4.2: the boot ROM's NuBus reset
routine guards its slot-`$E` disable write on the *software* ID, so a 6100 with the
adapter card skips the write and keeps its only slot enabled, while reasoning from the raw
register value gives the wrong answer. The 68k identity flow reads the register, promotes
`$3010` to `$3011`, and matches the ROM's product record — the record the machine's
configuration tables are keyed by [3].

only way a /60 gains a slot card.

### 1.5 The low-profile enclosure

The case is the Quadra 610-class flat box: 3.2 inches high, designed to sit under the
monitor [1] Table 2-1 p. 12, §"The First Generation of Power Macintosh" p. 2. Its internal
drive complement is fixed by Table 3-4: one CD-ROM bay and one hard-disk bay accepting
drives up to 500 MB — no 1 GB drive fits [1] Table 3-4 p. 29, Table 1-2 p. 5 — and "The
Power Macintosh 6100/60 can contain a maximum of two internal SCSI devices" [1]
§"Automatic SCSI Termination" p. 33; the internal device locations are drawn in Figure 3-3
[1] p. 30. The back panel carries the HDI-45 monitor socket, the two serial ports, the ADB
socket, the sound jacks, the AAUI Ethernet socket, the DB-25 SCSI connector and the floppy
opening [1] Chapter 3; the enclosure's overall power-supply rating is not documented
anywhere in the evidence set (§6.11).

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The platform's clock architecture — Squidlet on the 1994 boards, fixed oscillators, the
7.8336 MHz 601 RTC input, clocks measured rather than tabulated — is [pdm.md](pdm.md)
§5.4's. This machine's column:

| Clock | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 601 core ("system clock") | 60.0000 MHz (/66: 66.0000 MHz) | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19; [2] Table 1-2 p. 3 |
| CPU bus clock | 30.0000 MHz (/66: 33.0000 MHz) | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19 |
| Core:bus ratio | 2:1 | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.5 |
| 601 RTC / decrementer | 7.8336 MHz — 31.3344 MHz ÷ 4; 1,002,700,800 RTCL-units per second | [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4–§4.5 |
| Clock generator | Squidlet, the 28-pin Apple part of the 1994 boards; the /66's vendor/part changes are "transparent to third-party software" | [1] §"Squidlet Chip" p. 17; [2] §"Clock Chips" p. 5 |
| HMC timing row | the ≥ 25 MHz table entry, refresh divider 8 (/66: the ≥ 33 MHz entry, divider 9) | [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4 |
| Dot clocks | 15.6672 / 25.175 / 31.3344 / 57.2832 MHz per video mode | [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4; [ariel.md](ariel.md) §1.3 |

Two consequences are worth stating at machine level. First, the 60 and 66 MHz figures are
not read from a strap: the boot ROM measures the CPU clock against the decrementer, snaps
the answer to its table of standard frequencies, and derives the bus clock from a second
measurement — the machine-ID register does not distinguish the /60 from the /66 at any
point ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4, §6.4). Second, the 30 MHz bus makes the 6100/60 the
platform's bandwidth-worst case: its bus serves a 60 MHz CPU and the built-in video's DRAM
fetches simultaneously, with no VRAM card to offload the raster — the family's arbitration
order gives video refresh the second-highest priority ([ariel.md](ariel.md) §1.2,
*inferred*; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2).

### 2.3 The single SIMM pair and the movable memory banks

The family's DRAM contract — pairs of 72-pin SIMMs, 80-ns parts, CAS-before-RAS refresh,
the discontiguous bank layout, the aliasing rules — is [pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.2 and
[hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.2's. The 6100's part of it is the one row of the family's memory
architecture that is *not* a fixed decode: **this machine's SIMM banks move**.

At power-on, with the HMC's SIMM_BANK_SIZE field at its reset value, the soldered 8 MB
decodes at `$00000000` and the single SIMM pair's two banks decode in 128 MB windows —
bank 1 at `$10000000`, bank 2 at `$08000000`, the actual SIMM contents aliased throughout
each window [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.2. The boot ROM, having taken the 6100 branch of the
machine-ID dispatch, probes exactly three windows — `$00000000–$007FFFFF`,
`$10000000–$17FFFFFF` and `$08000000–$0FFFFFFF` [3] ($FFF03060–$FFF030C0) — sizes the
banks, writes the measured second-bank size into SIMM_BANK_SIZE, and **re-bases the pair
contiguously after the soldered RAM**: bank 1 at 8 MB, bank 2 at 8 MB + bank 1's size
[3] ($FFF03CF0–$FFF03D54). With only bank 1 populated (a single-sided pair: the 10, 12
and 16 MB upgrades of the 8 MB machine) the code stays 0 and bank 1 is still recorded — and
decoded — at 8 MB [3] ($FFF03CF8–$FFF03D04). The packing, the size codes and the aliasing
contract are [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.2's, established there once for the family. No 7100 or 8100 bank ever
moves ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.2).

The capacity statements about this machine are:

| Statement | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Sockets | 2 (one pair) | [2] Table A-1 p. 9 |
| Maximum RAM | 72 MB (8 MB soldered + 64 MB of SIMMs) | [2] Table A-1 p. 9; [1] Table 1-2 p. 5 |
| "Highest accessible RAM address" | `$06FFFFFF` | [2] Table A-1 p. 9 |
| Per-pair expansion | "from 1 to 32 MB" | [2] §"RAM SIMMs" p. 9 |

The last two figures are in tension twice over. The `$06FFFFFF` address is consistent only
with the *fixed* discontinuous windows the appendix tabulates for the 8100 — after the
ROM's packing, a full 72 MB machine tops out at `$047FFFFF` — the discrepancy is recorded
as a known conflict in [hmc.md](hmc.md) §5, the shipped ROM's behavior being authoritative
for machines running it. And the "1 to 32 MB" per-pair sentence cannot produce the 72 MB
maximum of the same appendix's Table A-1, which requires a fully loaded pair of 32 MB
banks (the 16 MB bank organization of Table 4-6, two banks per SIMM [1]
§"Random-Access Memory" p. 14): the prose presumably means per SIMM (*inferred*; §6.4).

### 2.4 No NuBus controller on the board

The family's NuBus story is three on-board connectors and a BART on the logic board
([pdm.md](pdm.md) §4.3). This machine has neither: the block diagram's dotted line moves
BART and the single connector onto the adapter card, so the NuBus subsystem exists only
when the card is installed [1] §"System Architecture" p. 13. The software consequences
are [bart.md](bart.md)'s and are cited, not restated: the ROM's presence probe reads
`$F0000000` under a bus-error handler and a bare 6100 takes the clean recoverable fault
([bart.md](bart.md) §4.1, *observed*: the probe faults twice and the decoder-info flag
ends clear); the Slot Manager then finds zero NuBus slots and the machine boots normally.
The machine's single slot is slot `$E` — the slot whose window the 7100/8100 boot
sequence deliberately disables for their PDS video cards, and which the 6100's
`$3011`-promoted identity keeps enabled ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.4, §2.5, §4.2).

### 2.5 One SCSI channel, one video channel

The 8100's second SCSI controller (the discrete 53CF96 fast bus at `$50F11000`) does not
exist on this machine [1] §"System Architecture" p. 13; the I/O block's SCSI-B registers
are absent and the pseudo-VIA2 device bank carries no SCSI-B bits ([amic.md](amic.md)
§2.3). All SCSI traffic — internal and external, up to seven devices — runs over the one
Curio cell at 5 MB/s [1] §"SCSI Connection" p. 29.

Likewise the second video channel of the bigger boxes — the HPV VRAM card of the 7100/8100
or their AV cards — has no 6100 equivalent except on the /60AV: the 6100/60's video is
built-in video, full stop [1] Table 1-3 p. 6, Table 4-1 p. 50. The /60AV's AV card is the
same PDS card the 7100/66AV and 8100/80AV carry (§3.8), but its DAV connector is unusable
there — "Although it contains an AV card, the Power Macintosh 6100/60AV cannot exploit the
DAV interface because there are no other card slots available" [1] §"DAV Interface" p. 53.

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 The processor and the CPU bus

The 601 and its bus are the family's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.2, §5.2): the 64-bit
non-pipelined bus, single-beat and four-beat 32-byte cache-line transfers only, the HMC
arbitrating the address part with the processor holding default access. Nothing in the
processor wiring is 6100-specific except the rates (§2.2) and the identity of the
"expansion card" row of the arbitration table — on this machine that row is served by
whatever occupies the PDS: the NuBus adapter card mastering on behalf of a NuBus card, or
nothing at all ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.5). Apple's published rules for
a card taking that row — check ABB, accept the one-cycle latency, basic cycles and cache
line operations only, decode your own space, expect video refresh to absorb capacity —
are the family's and are not restated here ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2).

### 3.2 Memory

The board solders 8 MB of 80-ns DRAM and expands through **one pair** of 72-pin SIMM
sockets [1] §"Random-Access Memory" p. 14; [2] Table A-1 p. 9. The module rules are
Apple's published constraints [1] §"RAM Expansion SIMMs" p. 57:

- SIMMs are installed in **identical pairs** — the 64-bit PowerPC data bus against 32-bit
  SIMMs (IMPORTANT, [1] p. 57; [2] §"RAM SIMMs" p. 9).
- Devices must be 80 ns or faster, must accept CAS-before-RAS refresh, and a bank may
  carry **no more than eight chips** — "composite SIMMs cannot be used" [1] p. 57.
- "All Power Macintosh models ignore the parity connection on RAM SIMMs" [1] p. 57
  (Note).
- The legal bank organizations and their row/column address counts are Table 4-6's [1]
  p. 57: bank sizes 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 MB, the largest the 1-Mbit-by-16 organization with
  11 row and 9 column bits; a SIMM carries two banks of up to 16 MB each [1] p. 14.
- The connector pinout is Table 4-7 [1] p. 58 and the mechanical drawing Figure 4-2 [1]
  p. 59.

The bank *placement* behavior — the movable banks, the three probe windows, the packing
after the size-code write — is §2.3's, with the silicon contract in [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.2
and the probe algorithm in [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.2. One machine-level consequence of the
packing deserves emphasis: because the ROM re-bases the pair to sit contiguously after
the soldered RAM *and* records the result in the bank table it hands to the operating
system, the 68k environment sees contiguous logical RAM and never re-probes — the whole
operating system follows from the PPC-side sizing ([hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.3).

### 3.3 The L2 cache slot

The family's L2 contract — an optional 160-pin SIMM, 128 to 256 KB, its size sensed
through two connector pins at startup, pull-ups meaning "absent" — is [pdm.md](pdm.md)
§2.3 and [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.6's. On this machine the slot ships empty in every March
1994 configuration and full at 256 KB in every 6100/66 configuration [2] §"Configurations" p. 2 —
the one Enhanced-model cache change Apple documents ("All configurations are shipped with
256 KB of second-level cache memory"). The cache is not snooped and its coherency
consequences are the family's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2).

### 3.4 The processor-direct slot

The PDS is this machine's only connector, and the developer note tabulates it for exactly
this machine: "The pin assignments for the PDS connector in the Power Macintosh 6100/60
are given in Table 4-5" [1] §"PDS Expansion Cards for the Power Macintosh 6100/60" p. 54.
The 182-pin connector [1] Table 4-5 pp. 55–56, grouped:

| Group | Signals (pin) |

| Address | A0–A31 — the full 32-bit address bus |

| Handshake | /AddrAck (124), /XferAck (33), /XferErrAck (34), /ABB (125), /AddrRtry (18), /DataRtry (19) |
| Arbitration | /PDS REQ (178), /PdsGrant (179) |
| Interrupts | CPUint (4), PDS IRQ2 (180) |
| Clock, reset, power | PDS CLK (89), /Reset (87), /PFW (74), /KeyboardSW (5), STDBYPWR (73), +5 V / +12 V / −12 V / GND throughout |
| Sound frame bus | AwacClk (70), AwacSync (71), AwacDataOut (72), AwacDataIn (163) |
| Other serial buses | I²C clock (164), I²C data (165), ADB (96) |

Three observations a re-implementer needs. First, the connector exposes more transfer
protocol than the platform honors: TType0–4 and TSize0–2 are pinned out in full, yet the
HMC and AMIC reject the 601's extended transfer protocols outright ([pdm.md](pdm.md)
§5.3) — a card driving them gets a transfer error exception, not a transaction. Second,
the sound frame bus is brought to the slot: the four AWACS lines carry AMIC's 256-bit
time-division-multiplexed sound frame (256 × the sample rate on AwacClk, frame sync on
AwacSync, one subframe reserved for system sound) to any seated card [1] Table 3-16 p. 47;
[awacs.md](awacs.md) §3.1–§3.2 — this is the bus the AV card's DSP taps for its
subframes 2–4 ([awacs.md](awacs.md) §3.2). Third, the I²C and ADB pairs give a seated
card access to the two slow serial buses of the machine — the AV card's video decoder is
an I²C device [1] Table 3-14 p. 45 — although the note documents no protocol contract for
either pair on this connector (*inferred*; §6.9).

The developer note documents no semantics for the `CPUint` pin beyond its name; the slot
interrupt a driver actually uses is the AMIC slot-bank line behind `PDS IRQ2`
([amic.md](amic.md) §2.3). The slot's clock (`PDS CLK`, pin 89) is presumably the 30 MHz
bus clock by analogy with the 8100's synthesizer wiring [4] sheet 11 (*inferred —
unverified*; §6.8).

### 3.5 NuBus through the adapter card

Everything about the bridge — the register window at `$F0000000`, the reset pulse, the
slot-`$E` mask and its `$3011` guard, the burst enables, the fault contract, the
interrupt path — is [bart.md](bart.md) §2–§4. The machine-level facts are physical: the
adapter card "accepts a short NuBus expansion card, which lies parallel to the main
circuit board" [1] §"NuBus Cards for the Power Macintosh 6100/60" p. 52, Figure 4-1 p. 53;
the card accepted is the short (4-by-7-inch) form of the same physical configuration as
Quadra NuBus cards [1] §"NuBus Slot Connections" p. 51; and the adapter carries BART, so
"this chip is present in the Power Macintosh 6100/60 system only when the adapter card
is installed" [1] p. 52. The electrical budget for the seated card is Table 4-3's — 2.0 A
at +5 V, 0.175 A at +12 V, 0.15 A at −12 V [1] p. 52 — and the platform's silent-failure
locking rule applies to it ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.4). The 6100/66's optional adapter card
carries the same BART 4 silicon as the original [2] §"NuBus Support" p. 4.

### 3.6 Storage: one SCSI channel and the floppy

All SCSI is the Curio cell's: one 5 MB/s channel, internal 50-pin ribbon plus external
DB-25, electrically and mechanically compatible with the rest of the Macintosh family,
continuous across internal cable, board traces and external cable, with the automatic
termination the platform provides when the bus ends at the external connector [1]
§"SCSI Connection" p. 29, §"Automatic SCSI Termination" pp. 33–34. The device population
rules are the enclosure's: one CD-ROM and one hard disk of up to 500 MB internally, two
internal devices maximum, seven on the bus in total [1] Table 3-4 p. 29, Table 1-2 p. 5,
§"Automatic SCSI Termination" p. 33; the internal end of the bus is terminated in the
drive at the end of the cable, and the automatic termination removes the on-board
terminator when an external device supplies its own [1] pp. 33–34. The channel's DMA
path, DRQ pseudo-DMA and driver sequences are the family's ([amic.md](amic.md) §3.4;
[pdm.md](pdm.md) §4.5).

The floppy is the family's single internal SuperDrive on SWIM III [1]
§"Floppy Disk Drive Connection" p. 34, with the manual-inject drive and the 20-pin
connector of Table 3-7 [1] p. 34; controller detail is [swim3.md](swim3.md) §2–§4.

### 3.7 Sound, serial, ADB and Ethernet

None of these subsystems differs from the family baseline: AWACS on AMIC's serial frame
bus with the DMA engine behind it ([awacs.md](awacs.md) §2–§3; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §4.4), the
two GeoPort-capable ESCC ports of Curio, one ADB socket behind the Cuda, and MACE
Ethernet behind the AAUI socket ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §4.5). The 6100-specific wiring fact
is that the sound frame bus reaches the PDS connector (§3.4), which is what lets the AV
card's DSP join subframes 2–4 of the frame while system sound keeps subframe 1
([awacs.md](awacs.md) §3.2). The panel connectors' pinouts are the note's: ADB Table 3-1
[1] p. 27, Ethernet Table 3-2 [1] p. 27, serial Table 3-3 [1] p. 28, sound Table 3-15
[1] p. 46.

### 3.8 Video: the built-in path and the AV card

**The /60 has exactly one video system**, the built-in path: AMIC timing, DRAM frame
buffer at the bottom of physical memory, Ariel II CLUT/DAC, the HDI-45 AudioVision socket
— all [ariel.md](ariel.md) §2–§4 and [pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.7, §4.4. The supported monitors
are Table 3-8's five [1] p. 36 (12-inch RGB at 16 bits, 14-inch RGB at 16 bits, portrait
at 8, 16-inch RGB at 8, VGA at 8), with the timings of Table 3-10 [1] p. 39; the HDI-45
socket carries the monitor sense lines, video, sync — and the ADB and audio lines of the
AudioVision display [1] Table 3-9 p. 37. A user attaching a non-AudioVision monitor uses
the adapter cable with an AudioVision HDI-45 plug on one end and a DB-15 socket on the
other [1] §"I/O Accessories" p. 8. There is no VRAM card for this machine and the
pseudo-slot `$E` window stays with whatever PDS card is seated ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.5).

**The /60AV adds the AV card** — the same card the 7100/66AV and 8100/80AV carry, "an
Apple expansion card that plugs into the PDS slot of any Power Macintosh computer" [1]
§"AV Card" p. 41 — with one 6100-specific mounting difference: "On the Power Macintosh
6100/60AV, the AV card uses an angle adapter card that is similar to the one shown in
Figure 4-1 on page 53 but without a BART chip" [1] §"AV Card" p. 42. The card's silicon
is the Quadra AV video subsystem on a card: the **CIVIC** frame-buffer controller
managing 2 MB of 80-ns VRAM in four 512 KB banks, NTSC/PAL timing, VBL and video-in
interrupts; the **Sebastian** dual-CLUT/DAC mixing video and graphics with 1-to-8-bit
alpha; the **SAA7194** decoder turning the **TDA8708** ADC's digitized composite or
S-video (NTSC, PAL, SECAM) into 8-bit grayscale, 15-bit RGB or 16-bit YUV 4:2:2 with
window scaling; and the **Mickey** NTSC/PAL encoder feeding composite, S-video and RGB
outputs [1] §"Functional Units—AV Card" pp. 18–19, §"AV Card" pp. 41–45, Figure 3-10
p. 42. The block diagram also shows a **601-to-68040 bus adapter** between the PDS and the
card's 32-bit local bus [1] Figure 3-10 p. 42 — the AV chips speak the 68040 bus
protocol, and the adapter terminates the 601 transfers on the card's behalf. The card's
software address "in pseudo-slot space is $E" [1] §"AV Card" p. 42; its declaration ROM
is enumerated by the Slot Manager exactly as a slot card's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.5).

The card's capture rates bound what the machine can do with them: full-screen NTSC runs
at 18.43 MB/s and PAL at 22.12 MB/s, so "it is practical to record a video image up to
one-quarter screen in size on an output device such as an external hard disk drive in
real time, without data compression" — the note's contrast case, the 8100/80AV recording
larger windows over its fast SCSI bus, does not exist here [1] §"Video Inputs" p. 45. The
DAV connector is present on the card but unusable for want of a second slot (§2.5).

### 3.9 The boot ROM as this machine carries it

Both speed grades run the March 1994 image (§1.2) — the /66 is the Enhanced model Apple's
own comparison table marks "new ROM: no" [2] Table 1-3 p. 5. The consequences are all
NuBus-domain, because that is all the January 1995 ROM changed ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §1.3):
minor-slot addressing stays at slots `$9`–`$E` (no `$1`–`$8`), major slot 8 stays
unaddressable, and slot cacheability is **write-through only** — "The Power Macintosh
6100/66 and previous Power Macintosh models support only write-through mode" [2]
§"NuBus Support" p. 4. A NuBus card driver written against the 1995 ROM's
`_NuBusCacheCopyBack`/`_NuBusCacheInhibit` selectors must not expect them on this machine
([bart.md](bart.md) §4.6). The ROM's LocalTalk driver is likewise the original one — the
1995 update ships only with the new-ROM machines [2] §"LocalTalk" p. 5.

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The PDS card set

The PDS is the machine's entire expansion story, and its shipping state is unique in the
family: "Every Power Macintosh computer except the Power Macintosh 6100/60 is shipped
with a PDS expansion card installed. For this reason, Apple does not recommend that
third-party vendors offer PDS cards for these computers. The Power Macintosh 6100/60 is
shipped with its PDS slot empty and hence may accept a third-party PDS expansion card"
[1] §"PDS Expansion Cards for the Power Macintosh 6100/60" p. 54. The three occupants the
slot can hold, in Apple's own order of preference for the buyer [1] §"Apple Expansion
Cards" pp. 7–8:

| Occupant | Models | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| nothing | 6100/60 as shipped | the machine boots with the slot empty; the boot ROM's early probes of slot `$E` fault recoverably and move on ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.5, §4.2) |
| the Power Macintosh 6100 NuBus adapter card | optional, /60 and /66 | sold separately; carries BART; accepts one short NuBus card (§4.2) |
| the AV card | 6100/60AV (factory) | on an angle adapter without BART [1] p. 42; not sold separately [1] p. 7 |

A card in the slot masters the CPU bus under the published PDS rules — check ABB, one
CPU-cycle latency, basic cycles and cache-line operations only, decode its own space,
expect video refresh to preempt ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2) — and reaches the address space
directly: a PDS card sees every CPU-bus address, which is how the AV card claims its
fixed apertures (§3.8).

### 4.2 The NuBus adapter card

The adapter card is the only Apple accessory that adds a slot to the machine: it "plugs
into the PDS slot of the Power Macintosh 6100/60 and accepts one short (7-inch) NuBus
card, which lies parallel to the main logic board" [1] §"Apple Expansion Cards" p. 7,
Figure 4-1 p. 53. It carries the BART NuBus controller [1] p. 52, so installing it turns
the machine from a zero-slot box into a one-slot (slot `$E`) NuBus machine — everything
from the presence probe to the Slot Manager follows ([bart.md](bart.md) §4). The seated
NuBus card is limited to the short form factor [1] §"NuBus Slot Connections" p. 51 and to
the power budget of Table 4-3 [1] p. 52; its declaration ROM is searched and validated by
the Slot Manager exactly as on the three-slot machines ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.3). On the
6100/66 the option persists unchanged — "the optional NuBus adapter card for the Power
Macintosh 6100/66 continue[s] to use the BART 4 NuBus controller chip" [2]
§"NuBus Support" p. 4.

### 4.3 RAM expansion

One pair of sockets, populated with an identical pair of 72-pin SIMMs; the module rules
are §3.2's and the placement behavior is §2.3's. The reachable configurations are the
bank sizes of Table 4-6 [1] p. 57 — 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 MB per bank — with two banks per
SIMM pair up to the 72 MB ceiling [1] p. 14; [2] Table A-1 p. 9. The capacity-prose
conflict ("1 to 32 MB" per pair against the 72 MB maximum) is flagged in §2.3.

### 4.4 The cache SIMM

The 160-pin cache slot accepts the family's optional 128–256 KB second-level cache SIMM
[1] §"Cache Expansion SIMMs" p. 60; the HMC senses the size on two connector pins and
disables external cache operations when the slot is empty ([hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.6). On the
/60 it is a user installation; on the /66 it is factory-filled at 256 KB [2]
§"Configurations" p. 2. The cache SIMM's pin assignments are Table 4-8's [1] p. 60; Apple
notes that "although cache parity signals are shown, they are not used in Power Macintosh
systems" [1] p. 60.

### 4.5 Internal storage

Within the enclosure's two-device limit (§1.5): a CD-ROM can be added in the field to any
configuration shipped without one [2] §"Configurations" p. 2, and the hard-disk bay takes
drives up to 500 MB [1] Table 3-4 p. 29. Every internal device draws against the
machine's SCSI power budget — +5 V at 1.5 A, +12 V at 1.5 A per device [1] Table 3-5
p. 29 — and the bus-end device must be the terminated one [1] §"Installing Internal SCSI
Devices" p. 34.

### 4.6 What this machine does not expand by

- **No VRAM path.** The HPV VRAM expansion cards are 7100/8100 parts; the 6100's video is
  built-in video and its only second channel is the AV card [1] Table 1-3 p. 6,
  Table 4-1 p. 50.
- **No fast SCSI.** The 53CF96 internal bus is the 8100's alone [1] §"System
  Architecture" p. 13 — which caps the /60AV's video-capture practicality at the
  quarter-screen figure of §3.8.
- **No DAV use on the /60AV** — no second slot to run the ribbon cable to [1]
  §"DAV Interface" p. 53.
- **No NuBus at all on the /60AV** — the AV card occupies the PDS [1] §"NuBus Interface"
  p. 50.
- **No NuBus power-control service** — the 7100/8100 feature is not extended to this
  machine [1] §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9.
- **No 1 GB internal drive** — the bay takes up to 500 MB [1] Table 1-2 p. 5.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The boot contract is the family's four phases ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7); this section
walks it naming only this machine's branch points. Addresses are in the ROM's
`$FFFxxxxx`/`$403xxxxx` aliases of the 4 MB image [3].

1. **Power-on.** Cuda releases system reset, which AMIC fans out to every peripheral;
   the 601 fetches from `$FFF00100` and the hardware-init code (HWInit) runs
   ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.8, §5.5).
2. **HWInit, platform phase.** ROM checksum, re-base to `$40300000`, forced-I/O
   segments, AMIC quiesce, blank video; CPU-clock measurement against the decrementer —
   snapping to 60 MHz on a /60, 66 MHz on a /66 — and the wait-state bus-ratio
   measurement, landing on 2:1 and 30 (33) MHz ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4; [hmc.md](hmc.md)
   §4.4–§4.5). The HMC timing table's ≥ 25 MHz row is selected and the refresh divider
   computed as 8 — on a /66, the ≥ 33 MHz row and divider 9 ([hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4).
3. **HWInit, the 6100 branch.** The machine-ID dispatch reads bytes 2–3 of
   `$5FFFFFFC` and compares against `$3012`/`$3013`; this machine's `$3010` takes the
   else branch — **exactly three RAM probe windows** (`$00000000–$007FFFFF`,
   `$10000000–$17FFFFFF`, `$08000000–$0FFFFFFF`) [3] ($FFF0303C–$FFF030C0), the aliasing
   probe of [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.2, then the SIMM_BANK_SIZE write that re-bases the pair
   contiguously after the soldered 8 MB [3] ($FFF03CF0–$FFF03D54) and the bank table
   handed upward [3] ($FFF031B8–$FFF03270) ([hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.3). Flash probe, L2
   sizing (empty on a /60 as shipped), final HMC configuration, handoff to the
   nanokernel ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7).
4. **Nanokernel.** Unchanged from the family: bank-0 trim, page tables from ConfigInfo,
   the 68k emulator started with the ROM at `$40800000` ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7, §3.6).
5. **The 68k start, identity.** The Universal decode reads `$5FFFFFFC` and the
   machine-identity routine **promotes `$3010` to `$3011`** after its AMIC revision
   probe [3] (file offset `$10070`), matching the product record at file offset `$15064`
   — the flow whose downstream guard keeps BART's slot-`$E` path enabled
   ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.2). Gestalt synthesis derives the `$4B` machine type and the
   speed grade from the measured clock ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6).
6. **The 68k start, peripherals.** Interrupt sources disabled in turn, the Cuda
   handshake and PRAM read, the memory split — the 604 KB frame buffer at logical
   `$60B00000` allocated only if a monitor is sensed on the built-in port, the
   160 KB cache-inhibited DMA buffer ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.7; [ariel.md](ariel.md) §4.3;
   [amic.md](amic.md) §3.3) — the boot beep ([awacs.md](awacs.md) §4.2), built-in video
   bring-up ([ariel.md](ariel.md) §4.2).
7. **The 68k start, the slots.** The early "VidReset" probe reads declaration-ROM bytes
   at `$FEFFFF00` upward and must take a clean recoverable fault when the PDS is empty
   ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.2). The NuBus reset routine probes BART at `$F0000000`: on a
   bare 6100 the probe faults twice, the decoder-info flag stays clear and the routine
   performs **neither** of its two writes ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.1–§4.2, *observed*); on
   an adapter-equipped machine the `$3011` guard skips the slot-`$E` mask write, so the
   pulse at `+$00` is the routine's only write ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.2). The Slot
   Manager then enumerates: zero NuBus slots without the adapter, slot `$E` with it, and
   the AV card's declaration ROM on an AV machine ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.3).
8. **Handoff.** SCSI and floppy probe, boot-device selection from PRAM, and the System
   file's 'boot' resources load the machine-support components ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7).

A soft restart re-enters the same path warm — HWInit detects translation on, relocates
itself and re-runs everything including the RAM probe, and the machine is on its way
again ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.5; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.7).

## 6. Open questions

1. **The board and the adapter card are absent from the schematic evidence set.** The
   only board drawing available is the 8100/110's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 1); every
   pin-level claim about this machine — PDS wiring, SIMM wiring, the adapter card's
   internals — is inferred from the 8100 drawing and the developer note's tables.
2. **The SLT3 contradiction.** The developer note's interrupt figure italicizes SLT3
   (the slot-`$E`/PDS flag bit) as *absent on the 6100/60* [1] Figure 2-2 pp. 22–23, yet
   the machine's only expansion slot maps to exactly that bit when the adapter card is
   installed (open question 12 of [amic.md](amic.md) §6; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6). Whether a 6100's AMIC
   implements bit 5, and where the adapter card's `/NMRQ` actually lands, is unresolved.
3. **The Dev Note RAM-map conflict.** Table A-1's `$06FFFFFF` "highest accessible RAM
   address" against the ROM's demonstrably packed layout (§2.3) — recorded as a known
   conflict in [hmc.md](hmc.md) §5; whether later 6100 ROMs behave differently is
   unknown.
4. **The per-pair capacity prose.** The Enhanced note's "1 to 32 MB" per pair cannot
   produce its own 72 MB maximum (§2.3); the reading "per SIMM" is *inferred*, and no
   document lists the shipped 64 MB module set explicitly.
5. **The `$3011` identity.** Which hardware, if any, reports `$3011` from the register
   without software promotion, and what the variant recorded in the ROM's machine tables
   at `$A55A3011` physically is, remain unidentified (open question 10 of [bart.md](bart.md) §6).
6. **The adapter card's bus-mastering path.** The adapter is electrically a PDS card, so
   a mastering NuBus card is assumed to contend through the PDS request/grant pair into
   the HMC (open question 6 of [bart.md](bart.md) §6, *inferred — unverified*).
7. **The DOS Compatibility card of the 6100/66** (model M3633, [2] §"Configurations"
   p. 2): which connector it occupies, and its electrical contract, are documented
   nowhere in the evidence set.
8. **The PDS clock rate.** Pin 89's `PDS CLK` is presumably the 30 MHz bus clock, by
   analogy with the 8100's synthesizer wiring [4] sheet 11 — no document states it
   (§3.4).
9. **The I²C and ADB pairs on the PDS.** The connector carries both serial buses
   ([1] Table 4-5 pp. 55–56), and the AV card's decoder is an I²C device, but no
   document defines the protocol contract for either pair on this connector — who
   masters the I²C bus for a seated card is *inferred* from the AV card's needs.
10. **The angle adapter of the /60AV.** The note says it is "similar to" the NuBus
    adapter's mounting "but without a BART chip" [1] §"AV Card" p. 42; what else it
    carries or rewires is undocumented.
11. **The power supply rating** of the enclosure is not documented anywhere in the
    evidence set — only the per-device SCSI budget (Table 3-5) and the per-card NuBus
    budget (Table 4-3) are given.
12. **The 6100/66's silicon deltas.** Apple states that some ASICs "have been updated"
    for the Enhanced models without naming which ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 9); whether
    this machine's HMC and AMIC differ from the March 1994 parts is unverified, as is
    the Squidlet-generation clock wiring on either board ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 2).
13. **AV bring-up ordering.** Where in the boot the AV card's declaration ROM and video
    path are initialised is described only at the Slot-Manager level
    ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 13); the sequence is not pinned to ROM addresses.
