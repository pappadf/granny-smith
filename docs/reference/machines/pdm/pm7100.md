# Power Macintosh 7100/66

The Power Macintosh 7100 is the mid-range machine of Apple's **PDM** platform — the March 1994 first generation of Power Macintosh logic boards: a PowerPC 601 at 66 MHz on a 33 MHz CPU bus, board codename **Carl Sagan**, machine-ID register value `$A55A3012`, Gestalt machine type `$70` [1] §"Models and Configurations" p. 3, Table 1-5 p. 10; [3]. It is the smallest box of the family with the platform's full expansion set — three NuBus slots on the board plus the processor-direct video slot — and the machine every PDM box below it wants to be: the 6100's adapter-card NuBus ([Power Macintosh 6100](pm6100.md)) is standard equipment here, and the 8100's fast SCSI bus ([Power Macintosh 8100](pm8100.md)) is the one thing it lacks. One logic board serves two speed grades: the **7100/66** (March 1994, the subject of this page's title and of the original developer note) and the **7100/80** (the January 1995 refresh at 80 MHz) [2] Table 1-2 p. 3; the two are indistinguishable to the identity machinery and differ in software only through the measured CPU clock ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6, §6.4).

Everything board-level — the ASIC set, the shared address map, the interrupt model, the boot ROM contract — is the PDM platform and is documented once in the family doc; this page carries only what is unique to the 7100 and cites the family and device docs by section rather than restating them.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

The 7100 is one board in a desktop enclosure: "The Power Macintosh 7100/66 and 7100/66AV are housed in a full-sized desktop configuration similar to that of the Macintosh IIvx and Macintosh Quadra 650. They run at 66 MHz and have three NuBus slots for expansion cards plus a separate processor-direct slot (PDS) for extending their video capabilities. Besides built-in display support, the Power Macintosh 7100/66 is shipped with a VRAM expansion card installed and the Power Macintosh 7100/66AV is shipped with an AV card installed" [1] §"Models and Configurations" p. 3. The two shipped personalities — /66 and /66AV — are the same motherboard with a different card in the PDS slot; no register distinguishes them ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6), the AV card being a slot-$E PDS card enumerated like any other ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.4).

The platform membership rules — PDM ASIC set, one 4 MB ROM image in two releases, the `$A55A30xx` identity register — are [pdm.md](pdm.md) §1–§2; the 7100's row of that table is §6.2 of the family doc.

### 1.5 The desktop enclosure

The case is the wide desktop format of the IIvx/Quadra 650 class [1] p. 3, 13.0 inches wide by 16.6 deep by 6.0 high [1] Table 2-1 p. 12 — wide and low where the 8100 is a minitower, so its two internal drive bays plus the CD-ROM bay lie flat behind the front bezel (the CD-ROM position is Figure 3-4 [1] p. 30). The back panel carries the two serial ports, the ADB port, the Ethernet connector, the sound input and output mini phone jacks, the external DB-25 SCSI connector, the AudioVision HDI-45 video socket, and the three NuBus slot openings plus the PDS card's DB-15 or S-video connectors [1] Chapter 3 passim. No document rates the machine's power supply as a whole — only the per-device budgets of [1] Tables 3-5 and 4-3 (§6.10). The enclosure gives this machine the family's keyboard-and-software power contract: the ADB socket's pin 2 ties to pin 4 to turn the system on [1] Table 3-1 p. 27, and the NuBus slots carry the /PFW line that lets an expansion card power the machine on and off — a service the developer note grants to the 7100/66 and 8100/80 only [1] §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9.

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The clock architecture — straps and PLLs, no software-visible clock registers, the 7.8336 MHz 601 RTC input, the measured-not-tabulated speed reporting — is the family's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4). This machine's column of the family table:

| Clock | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 601 core (7100/66, /66AV) | 66.0000 MHz | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19 |
| CPU bus (7100/66, /66AV) | 33.0000 MHz — ratio 2:1 | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19; [3] (the boot ROM measures the ratio rather than reading it) |
| 601 core / bus (7100/80) | 80.0000 / 40.0000 MHz — ratio 2:1 | [2] Table 1-2 p. 3 |
| Clock generator | **Squidlet**, the 28-pin Apple clock chip of the 1994 boards [1] §"Squidlet Chip" p. 17; the 7100/80's vendor/part change is "transparent to third-party software and expansion cards" [2] §"Clock Chips" p. 5 | |
| 601 RTC / decrementer | 7.8336 MHz — 31.3344 MHz ÷ 4; the one clock the ROM trusts (1,002,700,800 RTCL-units per second hard-coded, CPU speed measured against the decrementer and snapped to a table whose entries include 66 and 80 MHz) | [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4; [3] |
| Fixed oscillators | the Table 2-3 set common to the family: I/O 31.3344, VGA 25.175, dot 57.2832, Ethernet/SCSI 40, sound 45.1584 MHz, Cuda 32.768 kHz | [1] Table 2-3 p. 20; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4 |

Two speed-grade consequences are worth stating exactly. First, the **66 MHz and 80 MHz figures are measured**, not read: a machine whose RTC rate is off wobbles the snap and picks the wrong HMC timing entry ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4) — the 7100 is identified as a /66 or an /80 by arithmetic on the decrementer, which is the only thing that distinguishes the two boards to software. Second, the HMC timing entry the ROM programs is selected from a bus-speed table keyed at the 33.3333/33 MHz thresholds for the /66 and the 40 MHz threshold for the /80 [3]; DRAM refresh is re-derived as bus-Hz/3,299,911 − 1 into the configuration register [3], [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4.

### 2.3 Memory: the two SIMM pairs

The discontiguous-bank geometry — a 64 MB bank window of which only the middle 32 MB answers, 32 MB gaps, the DMA-visible layout — is the family's and is not restated here ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.2; [2] Appendix pp. 9–10). The 7100's part of it:

- **Four sockets, two pairs.** Each 72-pin SIMM is 32 bits wide and carries two internal banks of up to 16 MB each [1] §"Random-Access Memory" p. 14; a pair makes one 64-bit unit and populates a pair of bank windows. Pair 1 fills banks 1–2 (`$01000000–$02FFFFFF`, `$05000000–$06FFFFFF`); pair 2 fills banks 3–4 (`$09000000–$0AFFFFFF`, `$0D000000–$0EFFFFFF`) [2] Table A-2 p. 10. A loaded 7100 therefore tops out at 8 MB soldered + 4 × 32 MB = **136 MB**, highest address `$0EFFFFFF` [2] Table A-1 p. 9 — banks 5–8 of the platform's eight windows are unreachable because there is nothing to plug into them.
- **The probe path.** HWInit reads the machine ID, sees `$3012`, and walks the **nine-window** probe: the soldered window `$00000000–$007FFFFF` followed by all eight Table A-2 windows, in 2 MB steps with alias detection [3]; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.2 for what a DMA card sees of the result. Undersized pairs alias inside their windows and are sized through the aliases; unlike the 6100, no bank is ever rebased — the 7100's banks live and die in their fixed windows [3] ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.2).
- **Module rules.** Pairs of identical SIMMs; 80 ns or faster DRAM; CAS-before-RAS refresh support; no more than eight chips per bank, so no composite SIMMs; the parity connection is ignored [1] §"RAM Expansion SIMMs" p. 57. The legal pair sizes and the organization/address-bit table are [1] Table 4-6 p. 57 (§4.6 below).
- **Bank-size validation.** The ROM requires SIMM-bank sizes from {0, 2, 8, 32, 128} MB, encodes the size into the HMC's configuration register, and validates that bank 1 is at least as large as bank 2 [3] — a 7100 with a larger pair in the second sockets than the first is rejected by the bring-up code (*observed* in the disassembled validation routine).

### 2.4 The shipped-with-a-card PDS contract

On every PDM machine except the 6100/60 the PDS slot is **occupied at the factory**, and Apple's expansion-capacity table records the consequence: the 7100's PDS row reads "None" — not because the slot is absent (§3.4) but because no shipped customer ever sees it empty [1] Table 4-1 p. 50. Two statements fix the contract: "Every Power Macintosh computer except the Power Macintosh 6100/60 is shipped with a PDS expansion card installed. For this reason, Apple does not recommend that third-party vendors offer PDS cards for these computers" [1] §"PDS Expansion Cards" p. 54; and, of the two cards, "at present they are not available separately, and the computers are not sold without the cards" [1] §"Apple Expansion Cards" p. 7. The 7100's expansion story is therefore: three NuBus slots for the world, one PDS slot for Apple's two video cards.

### 2.5 The cache as fitted

The L2 subsystem — 160-pin SIMM, 128–256 KB, size sensed by the HMC on two connector pins at startup, pull-ups forcing the cache off when no SIMM is present — is [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.6 and [pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.3. The 7100's row: the /66 and /66AV ship with the connector **empty** (the factory text names only the 8100/80 as cache-equipped [1] p. 3, and the cache expansion section lists the 7100 as a user-installable option [1] §"Cache Expansion SIMMs" p. 60), while every 7100/80 ships with the full 256 KB installed [2] §"Configurations" p. 2. The cache SIMM's pin assignments — including the parity signals the platform does not use — are [1] Table 4-8 pp. 60–61.

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 Processor, bus and clocks

The 601, its single external interrupt input, the 64-bit nonpipelined bus and the 2:1 CPU:bus ratio are the platform's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.2); the register-level bus protocol and arbitration are [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.1, §3.5. The 7100 adds nothing and removes nothing here — which is itself the delta worth writing down: the 7100's CPU bus carries the full master population of the family (601, HMC, AMIC's DMA engine, the data path chips, BART mastering for NuBus cards, the PDS card's request) because BART is on the board and the PDS is occupied [1] §"CPU Bus" p. 20; [bart.md](bart.md) §1.3. The arbitration order and the request/grant wiring are family facts ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.5) — but see §6.6: the request/grant detail this page inherits was read off the 8100 schematic, and a secondary Apple-adjacent description claims the 7100/8100 use an external arbiter chip; the 7100 board itself is in no schematic in the evidence set.

### 3.2 Memory subsystem

Section 2.3 gives the population and probe behavior; the register side is the family's: the HMC's 35-bit serial configuration register carries this machine's DRAM timing (selected at the 33 or 40 MHz threshold), the refresh divider derived from the measured bus clock, the bank-size codes from the probe, the L2 configuration, and the speed-grade strap [hmc.md](hmc.md) §2, §4; [3]. The discontiguous physical map, the aliases, and the 40 µs undecoded-address error contract are [pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.1–§3.2.

### 3.3 NuBus: BART on the board, three connectors

The bridge and the slot windows — BART 4's register file at `$F0000000`, minor/major slot space, the burst-enable latches, the fault contract that empty slots must satisfy — are [bart.md](bart.md) §1–§3. This machine's population:

| Slot | Presence | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `$0` | pseudo | built-in video's dispatch identity — its VBL arrives on pseudo-VIA2 slot bit 6 and dispatches as slot `$0` [1] Figure 2-2 p. 23; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6 |
| `$6` | disabled | carries a PRAM record only ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.4) |
| `$9` | disabled | PRAM and Ethernet interrupt duty ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.4) |
| `$B`, `$C`, `$D` | **physical** | the three NuBus connectors — NuBus slots 2/1/0 of the interrupt diagram, i.e. pseudo-VIA2 slot bits 2, 3, 4 [1] Figure 2-2 p. 23; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6 |
| `$E` | pseudo (PDS) | the PDS video card's software identity; BART's competing path is masked off every boot ([bart.md](bart.md) §2.5, §4.2) |

Mechanically the three connectors accept "both long (4 by 13 inches) and short (4 by 7 inches) expansion cards of the same physical configuration as those used with Macintosh Quadra computers" [1] §"NuBus Slot Connections" p. 51 — the full-size card population the 6100 cannot take. The 96-pin Euro-DIN pin assignments are [1] Table 4-2 pp. 51–52 (the /CLK2X and /CLK2XEN NuBus '90 double-speed pair included); per-card power is 2.0 A at +5 V, 0.175 A at +12 V, 0.15 A at −12 V, with capacitance limits of 1513/536/698 µF [1] Table 4-3 p. 52. Two of the connector's pins carry this machine's power-control service: /PFW (B23) is the line by which "expansion cards can turn the computer on and off" [1] p. 9, and STDBYPWR (B25) supplies standby power for that purpose [1] Table 4-2 p. 52.

### 3.4 The PDS video slot

One processor-direct slot sits on the 601 bus. A PDS card decodes its own address space on that bus ("A slave expansion card must decode its own memory space" [1] §"PDS Expansion Cards for the Power Macintosh 6100/60" p. 55), can master the bus through the request/grant pair subject to the published rules ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2) — though neither Apple card does — and concentrates its interrupt through AMIC's pseudo-VIA2 slot bank, bit 5, the interrupt diagram's "VDS" (video display slot) line [1] Figure 2-2 p. 23; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6. Its software identity is pseudo-slot `$E` [1] §"VRAM Expansion Cards" p. 40, and the two cards that occupy it are §4.2–§4.4's subject. The PDS connector's pin assignments are published only for the 6100 [1] Table 4-5 pp. 55–56; the sound-signal pins of that table (AwacClk/Sync/DataIn/DataOut at pins 70–72 and 163) are confirmed common to all models by the sound chapter's PDS table [1] Table 3-16 p. 47 — but no complete 7100-specific pin list exists (§6.11).

### 3.5 Storage: one SCSI channel, the bays, the floppy

The 7100 has the platform's standard SCSI configuration and nothing more: the fast internal bus is one of the two dotted-line omissions the developer note makes for this machine in Figure 2-1 [1] §"System Architecture" p. 13, so the single Curio 53C94-class channel ([ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §2) serves every drive at 5 MB/s [1] §"SCSI Connection" p. 29. The internal bays hold one CD-ROM and two half-height hard disks up to 500 MB each, within the seven-device SCSI total [1] Table 3-4 p. 29. The bus is continuous across internal cable, board traces and the external DB-25, with automatic termination at the external connector when nothing is plugged in there [1] §"Automatic SCSI Termination" pp. 33–34; internal termination lives in the last drive on the cable, and multiple terminators "will cause the computer to malfunction and the logic board may be damaged" [1] §"Installing Internal SCSI Devices" p. 34. The per-device power budget is 1.5 A at +5 V and 4.3 A at +12 V [1] Table 3-5 p. 29. The floppy is the family's manual-inject SuperDrive on SWIM III [1] §"Floppy Disk Drive Connection" p. 34; [swim3.md](swim3.md) §1.

### 3.6 Video: two display channels

Every 7100 has **two** video outputs, a property unique to it and the 8100 in this family:

1. **Built-in video** — AMIC timing, data path FIFO refresh from the RAM framebuffer at physical zero, Ariel II CLUT/DAC, HDI-45 AudioVision socket; five modes, monitors to 16 inches [1] pp. 35–36, Table 3-8 p. 36; the pipeline is [ariel.md](ariel.md) §3 and the framebuffer carve-out is [pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.7. The mode set: 512×384 and 640×480 at 16 bits, 640×870 and 832×624 at 8 bits, VGA 640×480 at 8 bits, with frame sizes from 307,200 to 614,400 bytes [1] Table 3-8 p. 36 — the source of the 604 KB carve-out.
2. **The PDS card** — the HPV VRAM card (§4.3) or the AV card (§4.4), a second framebuffer with its own VRAM, DAC and DB-15, enumerated at pseudo-slot `$E` by the Slot Manager exactly like a NuBus card [1] p. 40.

The two coexist as two displays; the boot screen's home when both have monitors is an open question (§6.4).

### 3.7 Sound, serial, ADB, Ethernet

The transport is the family's: AWACS on the AMIC frame bus ([awacs.md](awacs.md) §3), 16-bit stereo at up to 44.1 kHz through external mini phone jacks (Audio In 8 kΩ, Audio Line Out 37 Ω, 20 Hz–20 kHz ± 2 dB, < 0.05 % harmonic distortion, 82/85 dB SNR) [1] §"Sound I/O" p. 46. One 7100-specific fact belongs here: the AWACS frame bus is **brought out on the PDS connector** — AwacClk/AwacSync/AwacDataOut/AwacDataIn at PDS pins 70/71/72/163, 256× the sample rate, 10 ns setup / 8 ns hold, 20 pF maximum load [1] Table 3-16 p. 47 — because the AV card taps it there to feed the DAV interface's digital audio (§4.4). The serial ports are two 9-pin GeoPort-capable sockets: either port programmable for asynchronous or synchronous formats up to 9600 baud including AppleTalk, with the GeoPort pod clock on pin 2 (to 920 Kbit/s), pod wake-up/handshake on pin 7 and +5 V at 350 mA for the pod on pin 9 [1] §"Serial Ports" p. 28 Table 3-3, p. 9; the SCC cell itself is [scc.md](../../hardware/scc.md). Ethernet is the built-in MACE behind an attachment-unit-interface connector (DI±/DO±/CI± pairs) taking a drop-box cable [1] §"Ethernet Port" p. 27; [mace.md](../av/mace.md) §2 for the register block. ADB is the Cuda-driven single port whose pin 2, tied to pin 4, turns the system on [1] Table 3-1 p. 27; [cuda.md](../av/cuda.md) §2 for the transport.

### 3.8 Interrupt wiring as populated on this machine

The three-layer collector — pseudo-VIA1, pseudo-VIA2, the interrupt control register — and the level assignment are [amic.md](amic.md) §2.2–§2.4 and [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.1. This machine populates the slot bank completely: all four slot flag bits exist (SLT0–SLT3 = slots `$B`/`$C`/`$D` and the PDS `$E`), where the 6100's diagram italicizes SLT3 as absent [1] Figure 2-2 p. 23; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6. The lines are open-drain /NMRQ from each connector straight into AMIC pins with 4.7 kΩ pull-ups ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.6; the wiring is *observed* on the 8100 schematic [5] sheets 12, 22 and *inferred* identical here, §6.1). The built-in video's VBL rides bit 6 and dispatches as slot `$0`; the Ethernet chip interrupt is the level-3 source; every DMA completion flag lands at level 4 — all family facts ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.1). Nothing on this machine deviates from the family dispatch table except population: three live NuBus lines where the 6100 has its adapter's one, and a PDS line that on every shipped machine carries the HPV or AV card's vertical blanking (§4.3, §4.4).

### 3.9 Power and reset

The Cuda microcontroller owns power, reset, PRAM, the ADB and the RTC ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.8; [cuda.md](../av/cuda.md) §1). Machine-specific power facts: the replaceable plug-in battery [1] p. 9; the ADB keyboard power-on (§1.5); the NuBus /PFW card power-on/off service [1] p. 9; and GeoPort wake-up — Cuda "can power the machine up from a signal on either GeoPort" [1] §"Cuda Microcontroller Chip" p. 17. Reset distribution and the warm-restart contract (the 68k `RESET` path re-running HWInit from relocated RAM, the software NuBus reset pulse) are [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.5 and [bart.md](bart.md) §4.2; this machine's only branch in that flow is that the BART pulse and the slot-`$E` mask write both execute, since BART is present and the software CPU ID reads `$3012`, not the 6100's promoted `$3011` ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.2).

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The three NuBus slots

Three connectors, slots `$B`/`$C`/`$D`, on the board's BART ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.2, §1.4). What a card designer needs beyond the family's bus documentation:

| Property | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Card mechanics | long (4 × 13 in) and short (4 × 7 in), Quadra physical configuration; mechanics per *Designing Cards and Drivers*, third edition | [1] p. 51; [7] |
| Data and address width | 32 bits | [1] §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9 |
| Per-card power | +5 V 2.0 A; +12 V 0.175 A; −12 V 0.15 A | [1] Table 4-3 p. 52 |
| Per-card capacitance | 1513 / 536 / 698 µF at +5 / +12 / −12 V | [1] Table 4-3 p. 52 |
| Card power control | /PFW (B23): a card can turn the machine on and off; STDBYPWR (B25) | [1] pp. 9, 52 |
| Address windows | minor slot space `$Fs000000`, major slot space `$s0000000`, per slot `$s` = `$B`/`$C`/`$D`; old-ROM minor range `$9`–`$E` (so slots `$B`–`$D` fully addressable), new-ROM minor `$1`–`$E` plus major `$8` | [bart.md](bart.md) §3.1; [2] §"NuBus Support" p. 5 |
| Block transfers | per-slot burst-enable latches, armed only for cards whose `sBlockTransferInfo` declares slave sizes of 2 or 8 longwords | [bart.md](bart.md) §2.6, §4.4 |
| Cacheability | mapped write-through by default (no snooping); the /80's ROM adds the `_NuBusCacheWriteThru`/`CopyBack`/`Inhibit` `_HWPriv` selectors ($10/$0F/$0E) | [bart.md](bart.md) §4.6; [2] §"NuBus Support" p. 4 |
| Interrupts | open-drain /NMRQ per slot into AMIC slot bits 2–4, active-low flags, set/clear enables; service through the Slot Manager | [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6 |
| Slot priority order | the physical connector order on the 8100 board is B, D, C — the middle connector is slot `$D` (*observed* on the drawing [5] sheet 22; the 7100's silk-screening is undocumented, §6.7) | [bart.md](bart.md) §1.4 |

Declaration ROMs, sResources and the Slot Manager search are [bart.md](bart.md) §4.3 and [declaration-rom.md](../../hardware/nubus/declaration-rom.md); the NuBus '90 protocol itself is [nubus.md](../../hardware/nubus/nubus.md). The one behavior a 7100 card must not rely on is locking: locked NuBus transactions fail without a bus error, on the whole platform [1] p. 52; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.4.

### 4.2 The PDS video slot: two cards, one contract

The slot's electrical and software contract is §3.4's; its two occupants:

| | Power Macintosh 7100 VRAM expansion card (HPV) | Apple AV card |
|---|---|---|
| Ships in | every 7100/66 configuration except the 7100/66AV [1] p. 35 | the 7100/66AV [1] p. 35 |
| VRAM | 1 MB soldered, expandable to 2 MB by four SIMMs [1] p. 40 | 2 MB in four 512 KB banks [1] §"AV Card" p. 42 |
| Output | one DB-15 monitor connector [1] p. 40 | DB-15 plus S-video input and output connectors, NTSC/PAL/SECAM [1] pp. 41–43 |
| Max monitor | 16-inch RGB at 24 bits (2 MB), 21-inch at 16 bits [1] Table 3-11 p. 40 | 21-inch, 24-bit graphics / 16-bit video [1] p. 8, Table 3-13 pp. 43–44 |
| Video input | none | composite/S-video in NTSC/PAL/SECAM via TDA8708 ADC + SAA7194 decoder/scaler [1] §"Video Inputs" p. 45 |
| Software identity | pseudo-slot `$E` [1] p. 40 | pseudo-slot `$E` [1] §"AV Card" p. 42 |
| Extra interface | none | the 60-pin DAV connector for NuBus cards [1] §"DAV Interface" p. 53 |

Both cards are CPU-bus slaves with their own declaration ROMs at the top of slot-`$E` standard space, found by the Slot Manager like any NuBus card's ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.3), and both raise their vertical blanking through the PDS interrupt line (AMIC slot bit 5, the "VDS" bit) ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.6).

### 4.3 The HPV card

The **HPV** ("high-performance video") card is Apple's unaccelerated VRAM framebuffer: "Apple cards that plug into the PDS slots of Power Macintosh computers. They provide an additional DB-15 monitor output and an 80-ns VRAM frame buffer, to support larger monitors and greater color depths than are compatible with the built-in video support" [1] §"VRAM Expansion Cards" p. 39. The 7100 version carries 1 MB and reaches 2 MB with the four VRAM SIMMs of §4.5. Its monitor matrix, in addition to everything the built-in video drives [1] Table 3-11 p. 40:

| VRAM | Monitor | Image size | Maximum depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 MB | 12-inch | 512 × 384 | 16 bits |
| 1 MB | 13–14 inch | 640 × 480 | 16 bits |
| 1 MB | 15-inch portrait | 640 × 870 | 8 bits |
| 2 MB | 16-inch | 832 × 624 | 24 bits |
| 2 MB | 21-inch | 1152 × 870 | 16 bits |

Unlike the built-in path, the card supports the 1152×870 two-page timing and 24-bit direct color — its own timing generator and programmable dot clock, its own CLUT/DAC, its own declaration-ROM driver.

The card's register surface is known from one released source, the MkLinux DR3 driver for the "Video Direct Slot (aka HPV aka Radacal)" [6] — Apple-copyrighted code released under GPL, which maps the control registers at physical **`$FEC00000`**, a 4 KB window inside slot `$E`'s standard space (`$FE000000 + $C00000`; the PDS card decodes it on the CPU bus itself, as §3.4's rule requires). The driver's constants and init sequence [6]:

| Offset | Width | Register | Observed use in the driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| +$00 | 6+6 bits | frame buffer base address 1 | written 0 at init |
| +$08 | 6+6 bits | frame buffer base address 2 | written `$1000` at init |
| +$10 | 6+6 bits | row bytes | written as rowbytes/8, read back ×8 |
| +$F0 | byte | overflow register — the high 6 bits of each of the above | every register write puts bits 11–6 here and bits 5–0 at the register offset |
| +$108 | byte | interrupt mask | VBL enable; cleared after init |
| +$110 | byte | interrupt status | polled for the VBL bit |
| +$128 | byte | VBL clear | read to ack/clear |
| +$207 | — | RadACal (RAMDAC) base | CLUT address register here |
| +$237 | byte | CLUT data | written red, green, blue per entry |

Interrupt bits are VBL `$01`, animation `$02`, cursor `$04` [6]. All register traffic is byte-wide with `eieio` ordering [6]. What the driver proves is the shape — a 12-bit register file split across a low byte and an overflow byte, a CLUT at a byte-strided RadACal window, VBL mask/status/ack — but it is a console driver, not the card's own ROM driver: the full mode-set, dot-clock and sense-line programming the card's declaration ROM performs is not established from any document or dump (§6.2, §6.3), and no Apple publication names the card's controller chip.

### 4.4 The AV card

The AV card is the Quadra 840AV/660AV video subsystem moved onto the PDS card [1] §"AV Card" pp. 41–42: 2 MB of VRAM in four 512 KB banks usable as one graphics buffer or a graphics + video pair [1] p. 42; the CIVIC timing/frame-buffer controller, the Sebastian dual-CLUT DAC, and the Mickey NTSC/PAL encoder on the output side, with the TDA8708 ADC and SAA7194 decoder/scaler on the input side [1] pp. 42–45 — the chips whose register-level behaviour is documented with the AV family ([av.md](../av/av.md) §4, [civic.md](../av/civic.md) §2). Between the PDS connector and the card's 32-bit local bus sits a "601-040 Bus adapter" chip [1] Figure 3-10 p. 42, so the 68040-bus-native AV silicon hangs off the 601 bus unmodified. The card's monitor/depth matrix — 24-bit graphics on small monitors, 16/8-bit deeper into the 21-inch range, NTSC and PAL with convolved 8-bit modes — is [1] Table 3-13 pp. 43–44; full-screen video input runs at 18.43 MB/s (NTSC) or 22.12 MB/s (PAL), making quarter-screen capture practical in real time without compression [1] §"Video Inputs" p. 45.

On the 7100/66AV the card carries the **DAV interface**: a 60-pin connector (AMP 104549-8) exposing the unscaled 4:2:2 YUV input bus (Y7–0, UV7–0, vertical sync, HRef, CREFB, DIR, line-lock clock) and the AWACS serial sound frame (AwacSync/SerOut/SerIn/BitClk, the §3.7 PDS tap), for NuBus cards wired to it with a ribbon cable — typically 2.5 inches [1] §"DAV Interface" pp. 53–54, Table 3-4 p. 54 (the pin table is printed as Table 4-4). "One use for the DAV interface is to provide a hardware audio or video compression capability on an accessory card, which can write out compressed data to NuBus" [1] p. 53. The 7100/66AV is the smallest machine that can use it: the 6100/60AV has the card but no free slot [1] p. 53.

### 4.5 VRAM expansion SIMMs

The HPV card's four SIMM sockets take 68-pin SIMMs, 80-ns VRAM, loaded with two 128 Kbit × 8 chips each — a different population from the 8100 card's four 256 Kbit × 4 chips, though the pinout and mechanics are shared [1] §"VRAM Expansion SIMMs" p. 61. The pin assignments — DQ0–15 with serial SDQ0–15, split /CAS0//CAS1, /WE0/1, /SE0/1, /DT-OE0/1, the SC serial clock, DSF — are [1] Table 4-9 p. 62, the mechanical drawing Figure 4-3 p. 63. Four SIMMs take the card from 1 MB to its 2 MB ceiling [1] p. 40; the two cards' SIMMs are **not** interchangeable between the 7100 and 8100 despite the common footprint [1] p. 7.

### 4.6 RAM expansion rules

Restating §2.3's population rules as the installer's contract [1] §"RAM Expansion SIMMs" p. 57:

- SIMMs are installed **in pairs** — 32-bit modules on a 64-bit data bus; the pair must be identical.
- DRAM access time 80 ns or better; CAS-before-RAS refresh; at most eight chips per bank, no composite SIMMs ("Because of signal loading limits, there may be no more than eight chips per bank of RAM; composite SIMMs cannot be used" [1] p. 58); the parity pin is ignored [1] p. 57.
- Legal pair capacities and the chip organizations behind them [1] Table 4-6 p. 57:

| Bank size (MB) | Organization | Row address bits | Column address bits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 256 Kbit × 4 | 9 | 9 |
| 1 | 1 Mbit × 4 | 10 | 10 |
| 2 | 512 Kbit × 8 | 10 | 9 |
| 4 | 4 Mbit × 4 | 11 | 11 |
| 8 | 2 Mbit × 8 | 11 | 10 |
| 4 | 4 Mbit × 4 | 12 | 10 |
| 8 | 2 Mbit × 8 | 12 | 9 |
| 16 | 1 Mbit × 16 | 11 | 9 |

The 72-pin SIMM pin assignments are [1] Table 4-7 p. 58, the mechanical drawing Figure 4-2 p. 59. The address-relevant constraint is the HMC's own validation: SIMM-bank sizes from {0, 2, 8, 32, 128} MB, bank 1 ≥ bank 2 [3].

### 4.7 Cache expansion

One 160-pin SIMM socket, 128 to 256 KB, sized by the HMC at startup through two connector pins [1] §"Cache Memory" p. 15; the pin assignments are [1] Table 4-8 pp. 60–61 — including the CaParity signals the platform never checks [1] p. 60. The 7100/66 owner adds this after purchase; the 7100/80 ships with it full (§2.5).

### 4.8 Internal storage expansion

Beyond the factory drives: the second half-height bay, the field-installed CD-ROM of the /80 models [2] p. 2, and external SCSI devices within the seven-device total [1] Table 3-4 p. 29. The automatic termination at the external connector makes adding external devices termination-free [1] pp. 33–34; internal installs must leave exactly one terminator, on the last device [1] p. 34.

### 4.9 What this machine does not expand by

- **No fast SCSI** — the 8100's second bus does not exist here (§3.5).
- **No third-party PDS cards** — by Apple's own recommendation, because the slot ships occupied (§2.4); the DOS/Windows compatibility options of the refresh are the 6100/66's plug-in card and the 7100's SoftWindows software [2] p. 2, not a PDS card.
- **No processor upgrade path through the PDS** — the slot is the video slot; the 7100's CPU is soldered like the rest of the family's, and no 7100 processor upgrade is documented in the evidence set.
- **No more than two SIMM pairs** — banks 5–8 of the platform's window set are unreachable (§2.3).
- **No NuBus lock, no NuBus-to-peripheral reach** — platform-wide contracts ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.2, §3.4), restated here because the three-slot population makes this the machine where third-party card designers meet them.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The four-phase boot contract — HWInit, nanokernel, 68k emulator + Start Manager, system handoff — is the family's ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7), established from the ROM's own code and configuration record. This machine's branch points, step by step:

1. **Reset.** Cuda releases the system reset; the 601 fetches from `$FFF00100` and HWInit runs ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.8, §2.7).
2. **Checksum, re-base, quiesce.** ROM-image verification, the `$40300000` re-base, the forced-I/O segment registers, and the AMIC quiesce (all DMA channels soft-reset, sound stopped, both pseudo-VIA2 enable banks cleared) are common ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7); nothing here is 7100-specific.
3. **Clock measurement.** The CPU clock is measured against the 7.8336 MHz-fed decrementer and snapped to the standard table — 66 MHz for a 7100/66, 80 MHz for a 7100/80 — and the 2:1 bus ratio measured by HMC wait-state toggling [3]; [pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.4. The HMC timing entry is then selected at the 33.3333/33 or 40 MHz threshold and the refresh divider derived from the measured bus clock [3].
4. **Machine identification, first pass.** HWInit reads bytes 2 and 3 of `$5FFFFFFC`; the assembled `$3012` selects the **nine-window RAM probe** [3]. The soldered window and all eight bank windows are walked with alias detection; the 7100's banks never rebase ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.2; §2.3 above). SIMM sizes encode into the HMC configuration; the flash-ROM probe and L2 sizing follow, and find the 7100/80's 256 KB cache where the /66's socket is empty (§2.5).
5. **Nanokernel and emulator.** Page tables from ConfigInfo, the kernel/emulator data pages, the emulator entry at offset `$E0F8` (1994 ROM) or `$E104` (1995 ROM) ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7) — identical on this machine to the family contract.
6. **68k identification, second pass.** The Universal-table decode reads the same `$3012`, matches the `InfoPDMCarlSagan` product record, and the DynamicBoxFlag routine synthesizes Gestalt `$70` from the low bits `%010` and the measured clock [3]; the identity flow promotes only `$3010` to `$3011`, which this machine never reads ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.6).
7. **Cuda, PRAM, memory split.** The one-second/transport handshake over pseudo-VIA1, the PRAM read, then the framebuffer decision: 604 KB at logical `$60B00000` **forced to physical zero**, write-through, allocated only when a monitor is sensed on the built-in port (or the burn-in PRAM signature says so) — with two video outputs fitted, this is the built-in port's monitor that decides ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §3.7; [ariel.md](ariel.md) §4.3); the 160 KB DMA buffer at `$61000000` follows ([amic.md](amic.md) §3.3).
8. **NuBus and the PDS slot.** The BART presence probe at `$F0000000` succeeds — BART is on the board — so the decoder flag is set and every NuBus service runs ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.1). The boot reset sequence issues the /RESET pulse at `$F0000000` and then, because the software CPU ID is `$3012` and not `$3011`, writes the slot-`$E` disable mask at `$F0000011` — every boot, handing slot `$E` to the PDS card ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.2). The early "VidReset" probe of the PDS card's declaration ROM at `$FEFFFF00` finds the HPV or AV card every shipped machine carries (§2.4; [bart.md](bart.md) §4.2).
9. **Slot Manager.** The declaration-ROM search walks the three NuBus slots and pseudo-slot `$E`; empty NuBus slots must fault recoverably and be written off ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.3); slot `$0`'s VBL dispatches the built-in video's retrace ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.6). The video driver for the PDS card comes from the card's own declaration ROM — the system ROM carries no HPV driver (a string scan of the image finds none [3]) — so the Slot Manager's slot-`$E` pass is what brings the second display up.
10. **Boot device and handoff.** SCSI and floppy probe, PRAM-driven boot-device selection, the System file's 'boot' resources ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2.7). The shipped system software's API is the 7.1-based common set [1] Appendix B p. 75; the 7100/80 ships with system software 7.5 [2] p. 2.

The error paths are the family's: checksum failure is survivable and continues booting with a CLUT indication, diagnostics detour to an alert, and the warm-restart path re-runs HWInit through the live page tables ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.5). None branches differently on this machine.

## 6. Open questions

1. **No 7100 schematic exists in the evidence set.** The only board drawing is the 8100/110's (drawing 051-0333 [5]); every pin-level claim on this page about the 7100 — slot interrupt routing, PDS wiring, SIMM wiring, BART's clock feeds — is *inferred* from that drawing plus the developer note's block diagram. This is the family-level gap ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 1), scoped here to the machine.
2. **The HPV card's programming model beyond the MkLinux constants.** The released driver [6] proves the `$FEC00000` window, the 12-bit split register file, the RadACal CLUT and the VBL mask/status/ack — but not the mode set, the dot-clock programming, the monitor-sense handling or the init sequence the card's own declaration-ROM driver performs. No HPV declaration ROM has been dumped; the card's driver is the largest single unknown on this page.
3. **The HPV card's controller chip.** Board photographs identify a 343S1130 ASIC on both the 7100 and 8100 cards; no Apple document names it or any other part on the card. *Inferred — unverified* (photographic provenance only).
4. **Startup-display selection with monitors on both outputs.** The developer note says the two video channels coexist but not which one becomes the boot screen when both have monitors attached. The family observation for NuBus display cards — the built-in port wins when its monitor is present ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.7) — is *inferred* to carry over to the PDS card, and is unverified on real hardware.
5. **Boot with the PDS card removed.** Every shipped 7100 carries a card, the cards are not sold separately, and no Apple document describes a bare board. The recoverable-fault contract implies the "VidReset" probe and the Slot Manager tolerate an empty slot `$E` ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.5), but the behavior has not been observed.
6. **The 7100's bus arbiter.** The developer note attributes CPU-bus arbitration to the HMC [1] §"High-Speed Memory Controller" p. 15, and the 8100 schematic shows the request/grant pairs on the HMC [5] sheet 4 — but a contemporary Apple-authored magazine description states that on the 6100 the HMC handles arbitration "for the 7100 and 8100 there's an external chip" [8]. The claim is unresolved and the 7100 board is undocumented; treat the family arbitration table ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §5.2) as the working model.
7. **Physical slot order and lettering on the 7100 board.** The B/D/C connector order is *observed* on the 8100 drawing [5] sheet 22; the 7100's own silk-screening is unknown, and the tension between the connector labels and the interrupt-enable set the shipped OS raises (the connector-labels-versus-interrupt-enables question of [bart.md](bart.md) §6) applies to this machine identically.
8. **The 7100/80's silicon deltas.** Apple states some ASICs "have been updated" for the Enhanced models [2] §"Revised ROM and ASICs" p. 3, but beyond the clock-chip vendor change and the kept BART 4, no document says which; whether the 7100/80's AMIC and HMC differ from the 1994 parts is unverified (the family-level gap, [pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 9).
9. **The Squidlet output set.** Squidlet is named for the 1994 boards [1] p. 17 but no first-generation schematic exists; the assumption that the 7100's fixed-oscillator wiring matches the documented Table 2-3 set is *inferred* ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 2).
10. **The power supply's rating.** No document rates the 7100's supply; only the per-device SCSI budget [1] Table 3-5 p. 29 and the per-card NuBus budget [1] Table 4-3 p. 52 are published. The machine's total draw, and the supply's response to three fully loaded NuBus slots, is not established.
11. **A complete PDS pin list.** Apple publishes the PDS pin assignments only for the 6100 [1] Table 4-5 pp. 55–56; the sound-signal pins are confirmed shared by Table 3-16 [1] p. 47, but no document publishes the 7100 connector's full assignment, and the two tables swap the AwacDataIn/AwacDataOut pin numbers (72/163) between them — a naming-perspective discrepancy no erratum resolves.
12. **AV-model bring-up on this machine.** The 7100/66AV runs the same ROM as the /66; where in the boot sequence the AV card's video path is initialized (its slot-`$E` declaration ROM, its driver's PrimaryInit) is described only at the Slot Manager level and not pinned to ROM addresses (the family gap, [pdm.md](pdm.md) §7 item 13).
13. **The 7100/80's NuBus cacheability in practice.** The 1995 ROM grants write-through, copy-back and cache-inhibited page modes for NuBus superslot space [2] p. 4, but no shipped card or driver exercising the copy-back mode on a 7100/80 has been observed; the interaction between unsnooped L2 copy-back and a card's DMA under the new selectors is documented only at the selector level.
