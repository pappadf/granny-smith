# Power Macintosh 8100/80

The Power Macintosh 8100/80 is the flagship of Apple's PDM platform — the tower at the top of the first Power Macintosh generation, board codename **Cold Fusion**: a PowerPC 601 at 80 MHz on a 40 MHz bus, machine-ID register `$A55A3013`, three NuBus slots behind BART, the family's only fast internal SCSI bus, and the only board that can fill all eight DRAM bank windows (264 MB). It is also the machine the platform's documentation is drawn around: the developer note's block diagram is the 8100/80's circuitry, with the parts the smaller machines omit enclosed in dotted lines [1] §"System Architecture" p. 12, Figure 2-1 p. 13. Two later speed grades share the box and the board's identity — the 8100/100 and 8100/110 — and they carry more than a faster processor: a second NuBus-bridge silicon generation (BART 21) and, on the /110, a new clock synthesizer and cache SIMM [2] pp. 3–5, 8.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

The 8100 is one logic board shipped in three speed grades, each original with an AV variant: the **8100/80** and **8100/80AV**
(80 MHz, March 1994 [1] cover, §"The First Generation of Power Macintosh" p. 2), the **8100/100** (100 MHz, the October–January
1994–95 refresh [2] §"About This Developer Note") and the **8100/110** (110 MHz, "currently the fastest and most versatile
Macintosh computer" [2] §"Power Macintosh 8100/110" p. 7). All four boxes answer the same machine-ID register value, the same Gestalt
column and the same decoder tables; the speed grade is a strap-and-clock matter that software measures rather than reads (§1.2,
§2.2). The board is the platform's maximal member on every axis the family doc tabulates: most SIMM pairs, most DRAM windows, most
SCSI buses, the only shipped second-level cache of the 1994 lineup, and the enclosure the later PCI towers inherited — the 9500's
case is this minitower stretched by about three inches [1] Table 2-1 p. 12.

What the machine is *not* also matters. It is not a multiprocessor machine, not an Open Firmware machine, and — unlike every NuBus
Macintosh before it — its block diagram is the one the developer note draws in full, because the two dotted-line omissions of
Figure 2-1 (the adapter-card BART of the 6100, the fast SCSI bus absent from the 6100 and 7100) both resolve to hardware *present*
here [1] p. 13. The 8100 is consequently the machine on which the platform's shared architecture is most directly observed, and the
family doc leans on it: the schematic evidence for the whole family is this board's drawing [3], and the arbitration and interrupt
wiring quoted across the device pages is 8100 wiring.

Everything board-level — the ASIC set, the shared memory map, the interrupt, bus and clock architecture, the boot ROM image — is the
PDM platform and is documented once in the family doc ([pdm.md](pdm.md) §2–§5). This page carries only what is unique to the 8100,
citing the family doc and the device pages by section rather than restating them, per the three-level rule. The sibling machines are
[Power Macintosh 6100](pm6100.md) and [Power Macintosh 7100](pm7100.md).

### 1.2 Identity values

The identity machinery — the machine-ID register at `$5FFFFFFC`, its `$A55A` signature and byte-read requirement, the software-only
`$3011` value, and the Gestalt synthesis from machine-ID bits plus a *measured* CPU clock — is the family's (pdm.md §2.6), with the
register-level detail in [hmc.md](hmc.md) §2.5. This machine's values:

| Identity value | Value | Witness |
|---|---|---|
| Machine-ID register | `$A55A3013` — low word `$3013`, low three bits `%011` as Apple documents them | [1] Table 1-5 p. 10; pdm.md §1.2, §2.6 |
| Gestalt machine type (`gestaltMachineType`) | `$41` — documented for the 8100/80 and /80AV; the /100 and /110 reach the same value through the family's clock-derived synthesis path | [1] Table 1-5 p. 10; pdm.md §1.2, §2.6 (§6.4 for the unverified edge) |
| Board codename | **Cold Fusion** — the name the ROM's own product and decoder records carry | pdm.md §1.1, §6.3; [4] (product record `$3013`) |
| 68k product record | ProductInfo with cpuID `$3013`, at ROM file offset `$150C0` in the March 1994 image | [4] (*observed* in the disassembled product tables) |
| Platform decoder record | the "Cold Fusion" DecoderInfo at ROM file offset `$155CC`: the family's device bases plus the 53CF96 at `$50F11000` and Curio's SCSI cell at `$50F10000` | [4] (*observed*); pdm.md §3.4 |
| Gestalt speed grade | synthesized from the measured CPU clock against the snap table; the 8100's column holds one row per speed grade, the 80 MHz row answering for the /80 | pdm.md §2.6; [4] |
| Boot ROM | March 1994 image (`$077D`, checksum `$9FEB69B3`, "Boot PDM 601 1.0") on the /80 and /80AV; January 1995 image (`$9B7A3AAD`, "Boot PDM 601 1.1") on the /100 and /110 | pdm.md §1.3; [4], [5] |
| AV distinction | none — the /80AV is the same board with the AV card occupying the PDS slot; no register distinguishes them | pdm.md §2.6; [1] §"Models and Configurations" p. 3 |

Two branches of the identity machinery are 8100-specific and worth stating precisely, because both are load-bearing for a
re-implementation:

1. **The PowerPC half reads the register as two bytes and dispatches on `$3013` before RAM is sized.** HWInit loads bytes 2 and 3 of
   `$5FFFFFFC` individually, compares the assembled halfword against `$3012` and `$3013`, and treats anything else — including every
   6100's `$3010` — as the 6100 case; a `$3013` (or `$3012`) answer selects the nine-window RAM probe of §2.3 [4]
   (`$FFF0303C`–`$FFF0305C`; *observed* in the disassembly and in a full boot). The register must therefore support byte reads, and a
   machine that answers only in longwords mis-sizes its RAM.
2. **The 68k half can *derive* 8100 identity from the fast SCSI controller.** The classic environment's identity routine reads the
   register, probes the AMIC revision, and — on a `$3010` answer — probes for a 53CF96-class controller before committing to a
   product record; a hit promotes the software CPU ID to `$3013`, the Cold Fusion record [4] (identity flow at ROM file offset
   `$10070`; *observed*). The fast internal bus is thus an identity witness in its own right, and the 53CF96's register window at
   `$50F11000` answering (or faulting) is part of the boot's identity path.

### 1.5 The minitower enclosure

The box is the Quadra 800/840AV minitower, chosen because "the minitower design provides more room for internal disk drives and
expansion cards" [1] §"The First Generation of Power Macintosh" p. 2, at the dimensions of Table 2-1 [1] p. 12. The internal
storage complement it supports is one CD-ROM plus two ≤500 MB hard disks plus one ≤1 GB hard disk [1] Table 3-4 p. 29, with their
locations drawn in the note's Figure 3-5 [1] p. 31. Per-device power on the SCSI cables is bounded at 1.5 A at +5 V and 4.3 A at
+12 V [1] Table 3-5 p. 29 — the +12 V figure is the 8100's and the 7100's, roughly three times the 6100's. The battery is a
user-replaceable plug-in long-life cell backing the real-time clock and parameter RAM [1] §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9; the
fitted part is a 3.6 V lithium cell switched through an 8-pin battery/trickle switchover chip (*observed* on the schematic, sheet
16 — §3.8). The total power-supply rating is published nowhere (§6.10).

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The clock architecture — no programmable clock registers anywhere, straps and PLLs only, everything software-visible derived by
*measurement* against the 7.8336 MHz 601 RTC input — is the family's (pdm.md §5.4). This machine's column of it:

| Clock | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 601 core | 80.0000 MHz (/80, /80AV); 100.0000 MHz (/100); 110 MHz (/110) | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19; [2] Table 1-2 p. 3, §"Clock Speeds" p. 8 |
| CPU bus | 40.0000 MHz (/80, 2:1); 33.3333 MHz (/100, 3:1); 36.6667 MHz (/110, 3:1) | [1] Table 2-2 p. 19; [2] Table 1-2 p. 3, p. 8 |
| 601 RTC input | 7.8336 MHz = the 31.3344 MHz I/O oscillator ÷4 — the one trusted clock; the boot ROM measures the CPU and bus clocks against it | pdm.md §2.2, §5.4; [4] |
| I/O oscillator | 31.3344 MHz — AMIC; ÷2 the 15.6672 MHz Curio PCLK and 12-inch dot clock | [1] Table 2-3 p. 20; [3] sheet 11 |
| VGA / Dot oscillators | 25.175 MHz and 57.2832 MHz — AMIC dot clocks | [1] Table 2-3 p. 20; [3] sheet 11 |
| Ethernet/SCSI oscillator | 40 MHz — ÷2 = 20 MHz for Curio's SCSI cell and the MACE; 40 MHz direct to the 53CF96 **and to BART's processor-side logic** | [1] Table 2-3 p. 20; [3] sheets 11, 13, 14, 23 |
| Sound oscillator | 45.1584 MHz (the developer note's Table 2-3 prints 44.1584, a typo the schematic corrects) — ÷1024 = exactly 44.1 kHz | [1] Table 2-3 p. 20; [3] sheet 11; pdm.md §5.4 |
| Cuda crystal | 32.768 kHz | [1] Table 2-3 p. 20; [3] sheet 16 |
| DRAM refresh | CAS-before-RAS every 15.6 µs; divider computed from the measured bus clock — 11 at 40 MHz, 10 at 36.6667 MHz | [1] §"RAM Access" p. 22; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4 |

Two remarks. First, the bus-ratio row is the whole speed-grade story: the /80's 2:1 ratio is the family's original arrangement, and
the Enhanced 8100s are the only PDM machines shipped at 3:1 — which is why the /100's *bus* runs slower than the /80's even as its
processor runs faster, and why third-party DRAM and NuBus cards "may need to be retested for compatibility" where the bus rate is
higher [2] §"Clock Chips" p. 5. Second, the generator hardware split is per grade, not per family: the 1994 boards use Apple's
Squidlet [1] §"Squidlet Chip" p. 17; the /110 "uses a new clock chip" [2] p. 8, identified on its schematic as an ICS9178
synthesizer fed by a 36.6667 MHz reference oscillator (*observed*, [3] sheet 11), whose outputs drive the 601's phase clocks, the
HMC/AMIC bus clock, the SIMM clocks, the PDS, the data path chips and BART; the /100 is covered only by the vendor/part-change
statement [2] p. 5 (§6.2). The boot ROM's CPU-frequency snap table covers 80 MHz in the 1994 image and extends through 110 MHz in
the 1995 image, so both grades identify by measurement alone [4], [5] (§5).

### 2.3 Memory: four SIMM pairs and the full eight-bank map

The family's DRAM contract — pairs of 32-bit SIMMs on the 64-bit bus, each pair decoding into fixed windows whose usable middles
leave 32 MB gaps, sizing by a ROM probe that tolerates aliasing, the 7100/8100 banks never moving once encoded — is pdm.md §3.2 and
[hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.2, §4.2–§4.3. The 8100 is the maximal case: **four** pairs, eight sockets, a 264 MB ceiling (8 MB soldered +
4 × 64 MB usable per pair) reaching `$1EFFFFFF` [2] Table A-1 p. 9 — the only machine whose real RAM extends into the address space
Apple's physical-map table calls "RAM aliases" (pdm.md §3.1).

The machine's memory map with all four pairs loaded with 32 MB SIMMs, Apple's own table [2] Table A-2 p. 10:

| Source | Bank | Addressing range | Physical window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logic board RAM | — | 0–8 MB | `$00000000`–`$007FFFFF` |
| SIMM pair 1 | 1 | 16–48 MB | `$01000000`–`$02FFFFFF` |
| | 2 | 80–112 MB | `$05000000`–`$06FFFFFF` |
| SIMM pair 2 | 3 | 144–176 MB | `$09000000`–`$0AFFFFFF` |
| | 4 | 208–240 MB | `$0D000000`–`$0EFFFFFF` |
| SIMM pair 3 | 5 | 272–304 MB | `$11000000`–`$12FFFFFF` |
| | 6 | 336–368 MB | `$15000000`–`$16FFFFFF` |
| SIMM pair 4 | 7 | 400–432 MB | `$19000000`–`$1AFFFFFF` |
| | 8 | 464–496 MB | `$1D000000`–`$1EFFFFFF` |

Each SIMM carries two banks of DRAM of up to 16 MB per bank [1] §"Random-Access Memory" p. 14, so one pair fills the two windows of
its row; a pair provides from 1 to 32 MB of expansion depending on chip configuration [2] §"RAM SIMMs" p. 9. The soldered 8 MB
occupies the half of bank 1's 16 MB low end that no SIMM can reach [2] §"Discontinuous Physical Addressing" p. 10. Nothing in this
layout is 8100-specific silicon — the HMC decodes all eight windows on every board — but the 8100 is the only machine whose sockets
can answer them all, and the only one for which Apple published the fully populated map "If you are designing a NuBus expansion card
that uses direct memory access to or from RAM" [2] Appendix pp. 9–10, because card DMA sees these windows untranslated through BART
([bart.md](bart.md) §3.2).

Sizing on this machine runs the nine-window probe: HWInit's machine-ID dispatch compares the register's bytes 2–3 against
`$3012`/`$3013`, and a `$3013` answer sends the probe over the soldered window plus the eight bank windows above, in a 2 MB stepping
pattern that detects undersized SIMMs through their aliases [4] (*observed*: `$FFF0303C`–`$FFF0305C` and the probe that follows; the
windows probed match Table A-2 exactly). The 6100's three-window path never runs here; the bank-relocation code path that rebases the
6100's single pair does not apply, and the 8100's banks stay in their fixed windows from power-on onward (pdm.md §3.2).

### 2.4 The second-level cache as shipped

The family cache contract is an **optional** 160-pin SIMM, 128 to 256 KB, whose size the HMC senses through two connector pins at
startup, with pull-ups meaning "absent" and disabling external cache operations [1] §"Cache Memory" p. 15; the register-level and
bring-up detail is [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.6, §4.6. The 8100 inverts the family default in three steps:

1. **The /80 ships with the full 256 KB installed** — "this expansion is standard with the Power Macintosh 8100/80 and 8100/80AV"
   [1] §"Disk Storage and RAM" p. 4, §"Cache Memory" p. 15 — so the 8100/80's L2 bring-up path runs against responding silicon on
   every cold boot, where the other 1994 machines run it against pull-ups unless the user has fitted a SIMM.
2. **The /100 and /110 ship with 256 KB as well** [2] §"Configurations" p. 2, §"Configuration" p. 8 — so no 8100 exists without it,
   and the "no SIMM" path is unobservable on this machine.
3. **The /110's SIMM is a new design** — "Apple supplies a new design of 256 KB cache SIMM for the Power Macintosh 8100/110",
   addressed to cache-card developers retesting against the 36.6667 MHz bus [2] §"New Cache" p. 8. What differs beyond the speed
   grade is stated nowhere (§6.8).

One boot-time consequence is 8100-specific at the register level: the final HMC configuration write clears the cache-timing bit
(bit 24) only on machines whose measured bus clock is at least 40 MHz *and* whose cache probe found data — the 8100/80 is the one
1994 machine that meets both conditions, so its final configuration word differs from a cache-less 40 MHz machine's at the same
table row; the /100's 33.3333 MHz bus leaves the bit set [4] (*observed* in the HWInit cache-test tail; the row values are
[hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4's). The cache is not snooped — the HMC "does not support… cache snooping" [1] §"High-Speed Memory Controller"
p. 15 — so DMA coherency remains a driver obligation exactly as on the rest of the family (pdm.md §5.2).

### 2.5 The fast internal SCSI bus

The 8100's second SCSI subsystem is its most distinctive peripheral delta, so the summary defers to the device pages: the fast bus
is driven by a discrete **NCR 53CF96** (the "Fast SCSI" 53CF94/96-2 class part), a second controller in addition to Curio's
53C94-class cell, on its own internal 50-pin cable with an identical ribbon connector, transferring "up to 10 MB per second" where
the standard bus tops at 5 MB/s [1] §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9, §"SCSI Connection" p. 29. Which machines carry it and how
the two are enumerated — **SCSI Manager bus 0 is the fast internal bus, bus 1 the Curio's standard bus, on this machine only** — is
[ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §1.3; the register file at `$50F11000` and the pseudo-DMA aperture beside it are
§2.1's; the clocking (40 MHz direct from the board oscillator to the 53CF96, ÷2 to 20 MHz for Curio's cell) is §1.5's, *observed*
on this board's schematic [3] sheets 11, 13, 14. The wiring-level facts — the chip's position, termination and interrupt routing —
are §3.4's below.

### 3.2 Processor and CPU bus

The 601's platform bindings — the 7.8336 MHz RTC input, the single level-sensitive interrupt input, the ban on extended transfer
protocols, the `$FFF00100` reset fetch — are the family's (pdm.md §2.2), and the chip itself is the manufacturer's manual's business
as the family doc cites it. What is 8100-specific is the bus population: this is the only 1994 board on which every master position
of the CPU bus is occupied — the 601, the HMC, the AMIC as DMA master, BART as the NuBus cards' proxy, **and** a PDS card mastering
for its VRAM (the shipped HPV card's controller "supports data bursts from the main processor in all transfer modes" [1]
§"Cyclone Integrated Video Interfaces Controller" p. 18). The arbitration order itself is family level (pdm.md §5.2, [1] Table 2-4
p. 20); the wiring on this board is (*observed* [3]):

| Agent | Request / grant pair | Destination |
|---|---|---|
| AMIC (I/O DMA, all ten channels) | `IOReq*` / `IOGrant*` | HMC CBR_DMA / BG_DMA |
| BART (NuBus card mastering) | `NBCtlrReq*` / `NBGrant*` | HMC, sheet 23 |
| PDS card | `PdsReq*` / `PdsGrant*` | HMC CBR_PDS / BG_PDS, via J14 on sheet 10 |
| 601 | default ownership; `CPUGrant*` | HMC BG_601 |

The ownership nets `ABB*`/`DBB*` span the 601, the AMIC and the PDS connector; a mastering card must check that the bus is free on
ABB (pin 125 of the PDS connector), suffers a one-CPU-cycle latency on its original access, is recognized only for basic cycles and
cache-line operations, must decode its own space, and must expect video refresh to absorb much of the bus capacity at times [1]
§"CPU Bus Arbitration" pp. 20–21. The 601's interrupt line is AMIC's `CpuInt*` output, wired to the processor's INT pin and also
broken out to the PDS connector, with the programmer's-switch NMI entering through Cuda [3] sheets 2, 12, 16.

One published claim contradicts the drawing and is recorded as an open question: a contemporary Apple-authored magazine article
states that the HMC handles bus arbitration "on the 6100", with "an external chip" performing that role "for the 7100 and 8100"
[6] — while this board shows the request/grant pairs terminating on the HMC's own pins [3] sheet 4. The later, pin-level drawing is
the better authority; whether any unaccounted part sits in the request path is §6.6's.

### 3.3 Memory subsystem wiring

The soldered 8 MB occupies its own two sheets of the drawing [3] sheets 6–7, and the four SIMM pairs sit in eight 72-pin sockets on
sheets 8–9, with the memory decode owned by the HMC on sheet 4. The bank windows are fixed (§2.3); the SIMM rules — pairs, 80 ns,
CAS-before-RAS refresh acceptance, at most eight chips per bank, no composite SIMMs, parity ignored — are §4.2's (they are
connector rules, not board-specific ones [1] §"RAM Expansion SIMMs" p. 57). The cache SIMM connector, whose two size-sense pins the
HMC interrogates at startup [1] §"Cache Memory" p. 15, is on sheet 10 with the PDS and ROM connectors; its pin assignments are [1]
Table 4-8 pp. 60–61, with the parity signals present but unused in Power Macintosh systems. The ROM SIMM, its flash probe and its
HMC-controlled enables are family level (pdm.md §3.3, [hmc.md](hmc.md) §3.8), with the write strobe on the 8100's HMC sheet
(*observed* [3] sheet 4).

### 3.4 SCSI: the two buses

The machine's SCSI wiring, with the register files deferred to [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §2 and the DMA
channels to [amic.md](amic.md) §3.4:

- **Standard bus (Curio's 53C94-class cell).** One shared bus, continuous across the internal 50-pin header, the logic-board traces
  and the external DB-25 [1] §"Automatic SCSI Termination" p. 33; automatic termination at the external connector when no external
  device is attached, internal termination in the last internal drive [1] pp. 33–34; up to seven devices total across internal and
  external [1] §"SCSI Connection" p. 29. Pin assignments [1] Table 3-6 p. 32.
- **Fast bus (discrete 53CF96, U34, sheet 14).** Internal-only, single-ended — the 53CF96's differential transceiver-control strap
  is tied off (*observed* [3] sheet 14) — driven onto its own 50-pin header J23 by a ribbon cable "and connector identical to the
  standard SCSI internal cable"; internal devices may be connected to either cable, and in the shipping configurations the internal
  hard disk rides the fast cable while the CD-ROM rides the standard cable [1] §"SCSI Connection" p. 29. The bus is separately
  terminated [1] p. 33, with always-on board-end termination (*observed* [3] sheet 14;
  [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §1.4). The chip is clocked at 40 MHz directly from the board's Ethernet/SCSI
  oscillator; the same oscillator halved feeds Curio's SCSI cell [3] sheets 11, 13, 14;
  [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §1.5.
- **Interrupts and DMA.** The 53CF96's interrupt and DRQ land on AMIC's device-bank bits 6 and 2 — positions that exist in the
  register file on every board but are wired to nothing on the 6100 and 7100 [amic.md](amic.md) §2.3; its DMA is AMIC channel B,
  which unlike most channels keeps its own 32-bit physical buffer address register and terminates on the chip's transfer counter
  [amic.md](amic.md) §3.4. The ROM's decoder table gives the fast controller the internal-bus position, so SCSI Manager bus 0 is
  the fast bus here [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §1.3; [4].
- **Enclosure limits.** Three internal devices (one CD-ROM, two ≤500 MB drives, one ≤1 GB drive) [1] Table 3-4 p. 29, at the power
  budget of Table 3-5 [1] p. 29; the fast bus is also the enabler of the /80AV's video capture, since full-screen NTSC at 18.43
  MB/s and PAL at 22.12 MB/s exceed the standard bus — "larger video windows can be recorded on a Power Macintosh 8100/80AV
  internal hard disk, using the fast SCSI interface" [1] §"Video Inputs" p. 45.

### 3.5 NuBus: BART and the three slots

The bridge's full contract — the `$F0000000` register window, the slot address decode, the fault semantics, the burst enables, the
boot writes — is [bart.md](bart.md) §2–§4, and the slot-space and declaration-ROM machinery is
[nubus.md](../../hardware/nubus/nubus.md) and [declaration-rom.md](../../hardware/nubus/declaration-rom.md). The 8100-specific
facts:

1. **Three connectors, slots `$B`, `$C`, `$D`, in board order B, D, C.** The drawing labels them "NuBus Slot B" (J11), "NuBus Slot
   C" (J12) and "NuBus Slot D" (J13), each with its NuBusID straps, and the middle connector is slot `$D` (*observed* [3] sheet 22;
   [bart.md](bart.md) §1.4). Slot `$E` is **not** a NuBus connector on this machine — it belongs to the PDS video card (§3.6), and
   the boot ROM disables BART's slot-`$E` path on every start ([bart.md](bart.md) §2.5, §4.2). The slots accept both long (4 by 13
   inch) and short (4 by 7 inch) cards, of the same physical configuration as Quadra NuBus cards [1] §"NuBus Slot Connections"
   p. 51, on 96-pin Euro-DIN connectors whose pin assignments [1] Table 4-2 gives pp. 51–52.
2. **BART is on the board, not on a card** — the 6100's adapter-card arrangement does not exist here [1] §"NuBus Cards for the Power
   Macintosh 6100/60" p. 52; [bart.md](bart.md) §1.2 — and the /80 carries the BART 4 generation while the /100 and /110 carry
   **BART 21**, which "enables burst transactions for specific slots instead of for the bus as a whole" and "also handles burst
   read transactions by NuBus masters" [2] p. 4; with the new ROM this makes the Enhanced 8100s the only PDM machines with
   slot-specific burst transactions, minor-slot addressing down to $1, major slot 8 addressable, and per-slot NuBus cacheability in
   all three modes [2] Table 1-3 p. 5, pp. 4–5; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.7, §4.6. Existing cards "should work with the Power
   Macintosh 8100/100; however, compatibility cannot be guaranteed" [2] p. 3.
3. **The interrupt wiring bypasses BART.** Each connector's /NMRQ runs from the slot, open-drain with a 4.7 kΩ pull-up, straight to
   an AMIC pin — the three `NuBusIRQ<0..2>*` nets alongside `PdsIrq*` — so the slot-interrupt flags and enables live in AMIC's
   pseudo-VIA2 slot bank, not in the bridge (*observed* [3] sheets 12, 22; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6).
4. **Power and power control.** Per-card budget: 2.0 A at +5 V, 0.175 A at +12 V, 0.15 A at −12 V, with capacitance limits of
   1513/536/698 µF [1] Table 4-3 p. 52. The 8100 (with the 7100/66) additionally "provide[s] power control service to [its] NuBus
   expansion slots, so expansion cards can turn the computer on and off" [1] §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9 — the connector's
   /PFW (C23) and STDBYPWR (B25) pins of [1] Table 4-2 pp. 51–52 being the physical substrate; which connectors the service reaches
   is §6.7's question.
5. **The one hard bus contract** worth repeating at machine level because card buyers meet it here: locked NuBus transactions fail
   silently — "Resource locking will fail without a bus error being generated" [1] §"NuBus Slot Connections" p. 52;
   [bart.md](bart.md) §3.4.

### 3.6 The PDS video slot and the shipped cards

Every 8100 configuration ships with a card in the 160-pin PDS connector (J14, sheet 10): "Every Power Macintosh computer except the
Power Macintosh 6100/60 is shipped with a PDS expansion card installed. For this reason, Apple does not recommend that third-party
vendors offer PDS cards for these computers" [1] §"PDS Expansion Cards for the Power Macintosh 6100/60" p. 54. The slot's software
address in pseudo-slot space is `$E` [1] §"VRAM Expansion Cards" p. 40, its interrupt is `PdsIrq*` into the AMIC slot bank's bit 5
[3] sheets 10, 12; [bart.md](bart.md) §3.6, and its bus-mastering pair is §3.2's `PdsReq*`/`PdsGrant*`. Two cards ship with this
machine:

- **The 8100 VRAM expansion card (HPV).** "The Power Macintosh 8100 VRAM expansion card acts the same as the Power Macintosh 7100
  VRAM expansion card, described above, but it has 2 MB of VRAM and is expandable to 4 MB" [1] §"Apple Expansion Cards" p. 7 — an
  80-ns VRAM frame buffer with its own DB-15 monitor output, distinct from and in addition to built-in video [1] §"VRAM Expansion
  Cards" p. 39. Expansion is by four 68-pin SIMMs; the 8100 card's SIMMs carry four 256 Kbit × 4 chips each, where the 7100's carry
  two 128 Kbit × 8 [1] §"VRAM Expansion SIMMs" pp. 61–62, Table 4-9 p. 62. With its standard 2 MB the card drives a 16-inch 832×624
  display at 24 bits or a 21-inch 1152×870 at 16; expanded to 4 MB, 21-inch at 24 bits [1] Table 3-11 p. 40. The card's controller
  is a single ASIC marked 343S1130 — the same part as the 7100 card's (*observed*: the marking on production cards; the developer
  note names no chips for either card) — and the card's display driver lives wholly in its own declaration ROM: the boot ROM's 4 MB
  image contains no HPV-class display driver (*observed*: a scan of the shipped image's driver directory). The card's declaration
  ROM itself is otherwise undocumented (§6.12).
- **The AV card (on the /80AV).** 2 MB of VRAM in four 0.5 MB banks around the CIVIC controller, the Sebastian palette/DAC, the
  SAA7194 decoder and the Mickey encoder [1] §"Functional Units—AV Card" pp. 18–19, §"AV Card" p. 41 — the full video I/O set of
  the Quadra 840AV, including NTSC/PAL/SECAM capture and output [1] §"Apple Expansion Cards" p. 7. The /80AV "still accept[s]
  NuBus cards" — unlike the 6100/60AV, whose only slot is consumed — and its AV card carries the 60-pin DAV connector through which
  NuBus cards tap the unscaled YUV video input and the AWAC audio input by ribbon cable [1] §"Audio and Video Features" p. 5,
  §"DAV Interface" pp. 53–54.

### 3.7 Video, sound, storage and communications

Everything in this section is family silicon at family register addresses (pdm.md §3.4); only the fit is 8100-specific.

- **Built-in video.** The Ariel II RAMDAC and the AMIC video block drive a DRAM frame buffer for monitors up to 16-inch at 8 bits
  and 13-inch at 16 [1] Table 1-2 p. 5, §"Built-in Interface for Video Monitors" p. 35 — the supported set, timings and the
  framebuffer-at-physical-zero contract being [ariel.md](ariel.md) §4.5, §3.2 and pdm.md §3.7. The back-panel connector is the
  AudioVision HDI-45, whose pin assignment [1] Table 3-9 p. 37 carries video, sense lines, ADB and the telephony audio pairs; the
  second display (or on the AV machines the capture path) is the PDS card's.
- **Sound.** 16-bit stereo at up to 44.1 kHz through AWACS [1] §"Sound I/O" p. 46, with the input at 8 kΩ and 2 V rms maximum and
  22.5 dB of gain available, the output at 37 Ω and 0.9 V rms, attenuated −22.5 dB [1] Table 3-15 p. 46, and the frame-rate and DMA
  machinery at [awacs.md](awacs.md) §3.4, §2.9. The boot chime is [awacs.md](awacs.md) §4.2's.
- **Floppy.** One internal manual-inject Apple SuperDrive on SWIM III through the 20-pin connector [1] §"Floppy Disk Drive
  Connection" p. 34, Table 3-7 pp. 34–35; the controller, its DMA handshake and its drive interface are [swim3.md](swim3.md) §1,
  §3.1, §3.10.
- **Serial.** Two GeoPort-capable 9-pin mini-DIN ports, each independently programmable for asynchronous or synchronous formats up
  to 9600 baud including AppleTalk and the GeoPort protocol family [1] §"Serial Ports" p. 28; the SCC core, its FIFOs, clocks and
  AMIC DMA channels are family level (pdm.md §4.5), the connector's pin 9 supplying +5 V at up to 350 mA to a pod [1] Table 3-3
  p. 28. Either GeoPort can wake the machine [1] §"Cuda Microcontroller Chip" p. 17.
- **ADB.** One 4-pin mini-DIN port, +5 V at 500 mA behind a 1.25 A circuit breaker, its PSW pin doubling as the power key [1]
  §"Apple Desktop Bus" pp. 26–27, driven by Cuda with the transport and packet protocol at [cuda.md](../av/cuda.md) §3.8.
- **Ethernet.** The MACE inside Curio on the 14-pin Apple Ethernet connector [1] §"Ethernet Port" p. 27, Table 3-2 p. 27, with the
  station address in the 32×8 ID PROM and the register window, DMA rings and driver bring-up at [mace.md](../av/mace.md) §2.1, §4.
- **Cuda.** Power, reset, PRAM, ADB, the real-time clock and GeoPort wake-up [1] §"Cuda Microcontroller Chip" p. 17; the five-wire
  transport register-emulated by AMIC's pseudo-VIA1 (pdm.md §2.8; [amic.md](amic.md) §2.2).

### 3.8 Power, reset and battery

The power-on/reset contract is the family's (pdm.md §2.8, §5.5): Cuda owns the sequence, releases system reset through AMIC's
fan-out, and the 601 fetches from `$FFF00100`. The 8100's board-level implementation (*observed* [3] sheet 16):

- **Soft power is the battery-backed Cuda itself.** The power key arrives on `KeyboardSw*`; Cuda asserts `PFW*` (an open-drain
  output with a pull-up) to the power supply's connector J17 — there is no discrete soft-power latch on the board. The trickle
  rail that keeps the wake-source pull-ups alive when the machine is "off" comes from the PSU.
- **The battery chain.** A 3.6 V lithium cell feeds an 8-pin battery/trickle switchover chip (marked LS1176), which routes either
  the cell or the PSU's trickle rail onto Cuda's standby supply — the "long-life plug-in battery" of the features list [1]
  §"Features and Capabilities" p. 9, and the machine's only state that survives a mains outage.
- **The switches.** The chassis power switch enters on one Cuda pin; the reset button and the programmer's (NMI) switch enter
  separately, the reset button reaching AMIC's reset input through its own line so that a hardware reset does not depend on Cuda
  firmware; the NMI surfaces as classic level 7 (pdm.md §5.1).
- **Two connectors worth naming.** J17 carries the PSU interface including the trickle feed and the `PFW*` return; J27 is a power
  connector annotated on the drawing for "a Peltier-type active heatsink" — this being the family's machine whose processor ships
  with a heatsink provision. What Apple part, if any, used J27 is unrecorded (§6.13).

The power-key path, the ADB PSW pin and the one-second line are [cuda.md](../av/cuda.md) §1.4, §3.11's; the GeoPort wake-up inputs
are sheet 24's, with their pull-ups on the trickle rail (*observed* [3] sheet 24).

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The three NuBus slots

The slots' electrical and protocol contract is §3.5's and [bart.md](bart.md)'s; the expansion rules an 8100 owner lives by:

- **Card population:** up to three NuBus cards, long or short, of Quadra physical configuration [1] Table 4-1 p. 50, §"NuBus Slot
  Connections" p. 51. The slots exist "in addition to the PDS slot that supports a video expansion card" [1] §"Features and
  Capabilities" p. 9 — and unlike the 6100/60AV, the /80AV keeps all three: the AV machines' card trade-off does not arise here
  [1] §"Audio and Video Features" p. 5.
- **What the bus is for:** access between RAM or ROM and the cards — "It is not designed to let plug-in cards gain access to
  peripheral devices directly" [1] §"NuBus Interface" p. 50; the DMA-visible discontiguous RAM map a card designer must plan around
  is [2] Appendix pp. 9–10 (§2.3).
- **The AV machines' extra path:** on the /80AV a NuBus card can reach the AV audio and video streams through a flat ribbon cable
  onto the AV card's 60-pin DAV connector (AMP 104549-8), typically 2.5 inches long, whose pin assignment [1] Table 4-4 p. 54
  carries the Y/UV data, the AWAC serial taps and the line-lock clock [1] §"DAV Interface" pp. 53–54.
- **Block and burst transfers:** available per slot, enabled only for cards whose declaration ROMs declare the capability, through
  the selector at [bart.md](bart.md) §4.4 — and honored per slot with master-read coverage only on the /100 and /110 [2] p. 4.
- **Power:** the per-card budget of [1] Table 4-3 p. 52, plus the on/off power service of §3.5(4).

### 4.2 Memory expansion rules

RAM expands through 72-pin SIMMs in **pairs** — "RAM SIMMs must be installed in pairs" [1] §"RAM Expansion SIMMs" p. 57, because
the PowerPC data bus is 64 bits wide and the SIMMs store 32-bit words [2] §"RAM SIMMs" p. 9. The rules, all from [1] §"RAM
Expansion SIMMs" p. 57:

- DRAM access time of not more than 80 ns; the column address strobe must be accepted before the row address strobe for refresh.
- No more than eight chips per bank of RAM; composite SIMMs cannot be used (signal loading limits).
- The parity connection on the SIMMs is ignored by all Power Macintosh models.
- Reachable bank sizes and organizations per [1] Table 4-6 p. 57: 1 MB (256 Kbit × 4, or 1 Mbit × 4), 2 MB (512 Kbit × 8), 4 MB
  (4 Mbit × 4, in two address-multiplexing variants), 8 MB (2 Mbit × 8, in two variants) and 16 MB (1 Mbit × 16) — each pair
  providing from 1 to 32 MB [2] §"RAM SIMMs" p. 9. SIMM pin assignments [1] Table 4-7 p. 58.

Four pairs of 32 MB SIMMs make the 264 MB ceiling (§2.3); the machine tolerates mixed pair sizes, sized through the alias-tolerant
probe, with each pair landing in its fixed windows regardless of capacity (pdm.md §3.2).

### 4.3 Cache and VRAM expansion

- **Cache:** nothing to add on any shipped 8100 — the machine comes with the 256 KB family maximum installed (§2.4), and the
  family's SIMM range tops there [1] §"Cache Memory" p. 15. A user could in principle substitute a smaller SIMM; the /110's socket
  expects its new SIMM design [2] §"New Cache" p. 8. The connector and its two size-sense pins are family level; the pinout is [1]
  Table 4-8 pp. 60–61.
- **VRAM:** the shipped HPV card's four 68-pin sockets take it from 2 MB to 4 MB, with the 8100-specific SIMM loading (four
  256 Kbit × 4 chips; the 7100's SIMMs are not interchangeable with them despite sharing pin assignments and external dimensions)
  [1] §"VRAM Expansion SIMMs" pp. 61–62. The 4 MB configuration is what unlocks 21-inch 1152×870 at 24-bit color [1] Table 3-11
  p. 40. The AV card's 2 MB of VRAM is soldered in banks and not expandable [1] §"AV Card" p. 41.

### 4.4 Internal storage expansion

Within the seven-device SCSI total [1] §"SCSI Connection" p. 29, the enclosure's three internal bays populate across the two buses:
the hard disk on the fast cable, the CD-ROM on the standard cable as shipped [1] p. 29, and internal devices may be moved to either
cable — the connectors are identical [1] p. 29. Termination rules are strict and per-bus: the device at the end of each internal
cable carries the terminator and no other internal device may, too many terminators "may damage the logic board" [1] §"Installing
Internal SCSI Devices" p. 34, the standard bus additionally self-terminating at the external connector when nothing is attached
[1] §"Automatic SCSI Termination" p. 33, and the fast bus terminated on the board at its own end (§3.4). Per-device power is
bounded by Table 3-5 [1] p. 29.

### 4.5 What this machine does not expand by

- **No free PDS.** Every configuration ships the slot occupied (§3.6), and Apple recommends against third-party PDS cards for it
  [1] p. 54. The 6100's adapter-card route to NuBus does not exist here — the bridge is on the board.
- **No documented processor upgrade path.** Nothing in either developer note describes a 601 speed upgrade for this board; the /100
  and /110 are different builds, not upgrades of the /80 [2] §"About This Developer Note". The same ROM images' upgrade-card
  records belong to Quadra-class hosts, not to this machine (pdm.md §6.5).
- **No RAM above 264 MB.** The ceiling is the socket count times the 32 MB pair, not the controller: the HMC decodes a 512 MB
  architectural array of eight 64 MB banks [2] §"Discontinuous Physical Addressing" p. 10, of which this board's four pairs can
  answer half; no larger SIMM capacity exists in the supported organization table [1] Table 4-6 p. 57.
- **No clock or bus configuration.** Nothing is strappable by the user: the speed grade is fixed in the clock generation (§2.2), and
  the Enhanced note's retest warnings to card and DRAM vendors [2] pp. 5, 8 are the system's only acknowledgment that the rates
  matter.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The four-phase boot contract — HWInit from `$FFF03000`, nanokernel init, the 68k emulator and Start Manager, system handoff — is the
family's (pdm.md §2.7), established from the ROM's own code and ConfigInfo record. This machine's branch points, step by step:

1. **Reset.** Cuda releases the system reset; the 601, with `MSR[IP]` set, fetches from `$FFF00100` in the ROM's high alias and
   enters HWInit (pdm.md §2.8, §5.5; [4]).
2. **Identity, before anything else.** HWInit verifies the ROM checksum, re-bases to the `$40300000` alias, quiesces the platform,
   then reads bytes 2–3 of `$5FFFFFFC`: a `$3013` answer dispatches to the **nine-window RAM probe** — the soldered window plus the
   eight bank windows of §2.3, walked in 2 MB steps that see undersized SIMMs through their aliases [4] (*observed*). A machine
   answering anything else takes the three-window path and mis-sizes this board's RAM.
3. **Clock measurement and HMC configuration.** The CPU clock is measured against the decrementer and snapped to the standard
   frequencies — 80 MHz for the /80; the 1995 image's extended table carries the /110's 110 MHz [4], [5] — and the bus ratio is
   measured by HMC wait-state toggling: 2:1 on the /80, 3:1 on the Enhanced 8100s (pdm.md §2.7). The HMC timing table then selects
   by measured bus clock: the ≥40 MHz row (refresh divider 11) on the /80; the ≥33.33 MHz row on the /100; and on the /110 the
   **row the 1995 ROM added for it** — ≥36 MHz, low word `$0DCAF2B4`, refresh field 10, which the 1994 table would have mis-served
   with 33.33 MHz slow timing [4], [5]; [hmc.md](hmc.md) §4.4. The SIMM bank sizes probed in step 2 are encoded into the same
   serial configuration [4].
4. **The L2 cache probe finds silicon.** Unlike the smaller machines' pull-up answer, the probe on every 8100 finds the shipped
   256 KB SIMM; on the /80's ≥40 MHz bus the final configuration clears the cache-timing bit — the one 1994 machine-plus-cache
   combination that does [4] (§2.4).
5. **Nanokernel, then the 68k environment.** Page tables from ConfigInfo, the emulator started at its recorded entry offset — the
   1995 image's `$E104` versus the 1994 image's `$E0F8` [4], [5] — and the 68k ROM mapped at `$40800000` (pdm.md §2.7, §3.6).
6. **Machine identification, 68k side.** The identity routine reads the register and its product-record match lands on the `$3013`
   Cold Fusion record at file offset `$150C0`; the decoder record installed is the Cold Fusion table at file offset `$155CC` —
   the family's device bases **plus the 53CF96 at `$50F11000`** [4] (*observed*). The Gestalt answer is synthesized from the
   machine-ID bits and the measured clock, the 80 MHz row answering `$41` [4]; pdm.md §2.6. (The routine's `$3010`→`$3011`
   promotion and its fast-SCSI branch are §1.2's.)
7. **Cuda, PRAM, memory split, chime.** The Cuda handshake and PRAM read, the framebuffer carve-out at physical zero when a
   monitor is sensed on built-in video, the 160 KB cache-inhibited DMA buffer, and the startup chime are family level (pdm.md
   §2.7; [cuda.md](../av/cuda.md) §4.1–§4.3; [ariel.md](ariel.md) §4.3; [awacs.md](awacs.md) §4.2).
8. **The NuBus pass, with BART present.** The presence probe at `$F0000000` succeeds on this machine; the boot reset sequence then
   writes the /RESET pulse and — the guard's `$3011` test failing — **writes the slot-`$E` mask**, handing slot $E's window to the
   PDS video card on every start ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.1–§4.2; [4]). The early "VidReset" probe reads the PDS card's declaration
   space at `$FEFFFF00` before the Slot Manager runs, resetting the HPV or AV card ([bart.md](bart.md) §4.2), and the Slot Manager
   then walks the slots — the three connectors at `$B`/`$C`/`$D` answering, empty slots faulting recoverably ([bart.md](bart.md)
   §4.3; [4]). On the /100 and /110 the same pass runs with BART 21 fitted and the extended minor-slot range behind it [2] pp. 4–5.
9. **Storage bring-up, two buses.** SCSI enumeration presents the fast internal bus as bus 0 and the Curio bus as bus 1
   ([ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §1.3), so the shipping configuration's boot device — the hard disk on the
   fast cable [1] p. 29 — is found on the first bus; the floppy probe follows ([swim3.md](swim3.md) §4.1–§4.2).
10. **System handoff.** The System file loads the machine-support components and may replace the ROM's nanokernel with a newer
    image from disk (pdm.md §2.7) — the /100 and /110 shipping with 7.5 from the factory [2] p. 2.

A warm restart re-enters HWInit with translation on, re-verifies the checksum, re-programs the HMC and re-runs the RAM probe — and
the NuBus reset line, not asserted by hardware on a soft restart, is pulsed in software ([bart.md](bart.md) §2.2, §4.2; pdm.md
§5.5).

## 6. Open questions

1. **The /80 and /100 boards are not in the schematic evidence set.** Drawing 051-0333 is titled "Power Macintosh 8100/110"; every
   pin-level claim above about the /80 — its Squidlet wiring, its DRAM population, its cache SIMM — is *inferred* from the /110's
   drawing plus the block diagram, and the /100 has no drawing at all (the family-level instance of this gap is pdm.md §7 item 1).
   Whether the /80's board differs from the /110's in anything but the clock parts and the cache SIMM is unverified.
2. **Clock generation on the /100.** The "new clock chip" is documented only for the /110 [2] p. 8; the Enhanced note's
   vendor/part-change statement [2] §"Clock Chips" p. 5 is the /100's entire clock documentation. Whether the /100's 33.3333 MHz
   bus comes from an ICS9178-class synthesizer or another arrangement, and whether the /80's Squidlet's fixed oscillators match
   the /110's set exactly, are both open (the latter is also pdm.md §7 item 2).
3. **BART 4 vs BART 21 silicon.** Apple documents two behavioural differences ([bart.md](bart.md) §3.7) and nothing else: no part
   numbers, no package markings, no register deltas. Whether BART 21 carries registers beyond BART 4's file, and whether the
   "updated ASICs" statement [2] p. 3 touches the 8100/100's AMIC or HMC, are unestablished (pdm.md §7 item 9).
4. **The Gestalt machine type of the /100 and /110.** Apple documents `$41` for the 8100/80 and /80AV [1] Table 1-5 p. 10 only;
   that the Enhanced 8100s answer the same value follows from the family's synthesis path (pdm.md §2.6) but is stated by no Apple
   document, and the speed-grade rows of the box-flag synthesis are pinned only by the ROM's own tables [4], [5].
5. **The `$3014` product record** of the January 1995 ROM [5] — which machine it identifies is unknown; the 8100/110 class is the
   natural candidate but is not established (pdm.md §7 item 7).
6. **The external-arbiter claim.** An Apple-authored magazine article states bus arbitration uses "an external chip" on the 7100
   and 8100 [6]; this machine's own drawing shows the request/grant pairs on the HMC [3] sheet 4. Whether any unaccounted part
   sits in the arbitration path, or the article is simply wrong, is unresolved — the schematic is the later, pin-level authority,
   but nothing reconciles the two statements outright.
7. **The NuBus on/off power service.** The 8100/80 (with the 7100/66) "provide[s] power control service to [its] NuBus expansion
   slots" [1] p. 9, and the connector carries /PFW and STDBYPWR [1] Table 4-2 pp. 51–52 — but whether the service reaches all
   three connectors or a subset, how a card's power-on request is gated per slot, and whether the /80AV, /100 and /110 retain the
   service are documented nowhere.
8. **The /110's new cache SIMM.** "A new design of 256 KB cache SIMM" [2] §"New Cache" p. 8 is its entire description: what differs
   beyond the 36.6667 MHz speed grade, and whether a /80's SIMM functions in a /110, are unstated.
9. **BART 21 compatibility failures.** Existing NuBus cards "should work… however, compatibility cannot be guaranteed" [2] p. 3 —
   which cards fail, and on which transaction pattern (per-slot bursts, master burst reads), is recorded nowhere.
10. **The power supply.** No wattage, input range or rail budget for the machine as a whole appears in either note; only the
    per-device (Table 3-5) and per-card (Table 4-3) budgets and the battery/trickle chain of §3.8 are documented.
11. **The Workgroup Server 8150.** The server variant of this box is reported to share the platform; the evidence set contains no
    server documentation, so its membership, machine-ID value and deltas are unverified (pdm.md §6.5, §7 item 10).
12. **The HPV card's declaration ROM.** The card's display driver lives in it (the boot ROM carries none), but the card's ROM is
    not in the evidence set: its sResource set, its mode lists and its PrimaryInit are otherwise unknown, and the card's silicon
    beyond the observed 343S1130 marking is undocumented ([1] §"VRAM Expansion Cards" pp. 39–40 names no chips).
13. **The J27 heatsink connector.** The board provides a power connector annotated for "a Peltier-type active heatsink" (*observed*
    [3] sheet 16); what Apple part, if any, occupied it, and its power budget, are unrecorded.
14. **The slot-order tension.** The connectors are labeled B/D/C with the middle connector slot `$D` ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.4),
    while the slot interrupt enables the shipped OS raises cover a set that reads `$C`/`$D`/`$E` under the usual bit-to-slot
    mapping ([bart.md](bart.md) §1.4, its §6). The machine-level consequence — which physical connector answers which slot number in a
    running system — inherits that unresolved tension and is worth re-testing on this specific board.
