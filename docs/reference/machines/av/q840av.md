# Macintosh Quadra 840AV

The Macintosh Quadra 840AV is the shipping 40 MHz build of Apple's Cyclone board — the minitower flagship of the two-machine AV generation, a 40 MHz Motorola MC68040 on the YMCA/PSC chipset with the MUNI NuBus bridge, the DSP3210 and the CIVIC/Sebastian video chain on one motherboard, introduced in mid-1993.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

The Macintosh Quadra 840AV is the 40 MHz half of the Cyclone/Tempest pair: "housed in the same minitower configuration as the Macintosh Quadra 800", it "runs at 40 MHz and has three NuBus slots for expansion cards", and its base configuration "includes 8 MB of random-access memory (RAM) and a 230 MB internal hard disk" [1] p. 3. Apple's own preface ranks it without qualification: "The Macintosh Quadra 840AV is also the fastest Macintosh computer" [1] p. xxiii. It shipped in mid-1993; the shared boot ROM's universal declaration data carries the build date "Friday, June 25, 1993" [2] (*observed* in the ROM image).

Everything board-level — the ASIC set, the memory map, the interrupt, bus and clock architecture, the boot ROM image itself — is shared with the Macintosh Centris 660AV and documented once in the family doc ([av.md](av.md) §1.3, §3, §5, §2.9). This page carries only what is unique to the 840AV, and cites the family doc rather than restating it.

### 1.4 The shipped product record

The boot ROM carries one product record per board variant; on a strap match to $F the start-up code installs the 840AV's. What that record says, read from the ROM image [2] (*observed* in the per-variant product tables; the selection mechanism is av.md §2.4):

- **Identity.** Gestalt machine type 78, box flag 72 — the values of §1.2.
- **Expansion validity.** The 840AV's record asserts the second-level device bases as valid *without probing* — DSP, MACE, CIVIC, Sebastian and, unlike the 660AV's deliberately empty vector, **the MUNI** (av.md §3.3). The bridge is on this machine's main board [1] p. 14, so nothing needs probing: the ROM trusts the record, and the start-up code programs the MUNI's clock grade as part of the speed dispatch (§5).
- **Feature bits.** The record carries the full sound feature set (16-bit, stereo in and out, stereo mixing, play-and-record, line level) and the serial-DMA/serial-HAL features, but **not** the hard-power-off flag the 660AV's record adds [2] — the software-side reflection of this machine's soft-power design (§3.7).
- **Parameter sets.** RAM, video and slot information all come from the Cyclone records — the same parameter sets the unshipped 33 MHz Cyclone would use — so the 840AV shares its eight-bank 128 MB RAM ceiling and its three-connector slot map with that variant [2] (av.md §2.6, §6.3).
- **Ethernet configuration resource.** Id 78, named "Cyclone40" in the ROM's resource container, byte-identical to the other three variants' records [2] (*observed* at file offset $1AEEF0; av.md §6.4) — no device-visible difference exists between speed grades.
- **Slot PRAM defaults.** Id 78, declaring exactly three physical connectors: slots $C, $D and $E at board positions 1, 2 and 3 [2] (*observed* at file offset $124260).

### 1.5 Enclosure and factory configuration

The minitower enclosure is the Quadra 800's [1] p. 3, with "more room for internal disk drives and accessory cards" than the 660AV's low-profile box [1] p. xxiii. The base configuration is 8 MB of RAM and a 230 MB internal hard disk [1] p. 3 plus an Apple SuperDrive [1] p. 3. Internal storage options are a 500 MB hard disk, "a 1000 MB internal hard disk (in the Macintosh Quadra 840AV only)", and an internal CD-ROM drive [1] p. 3. The mounting facilities for internal devices and accessory cards are the developer note's mechanical appendix, with this machine's internal SCSI device space shown in its Figure 2-5 [1] pp. 25, Appendix D.

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The clock tree is the family's (av.md §5.5); this machine's column of it: **BClk 40.0000 MHz** — the platform's metronome, feeding the 68040, the YMCA, CIVIC, the PSC and the MUNI; **PClk 80.0000 MHz** to the processor, the YMCA and CIVIC; **CKI 66.6667 MHz** to the DSP and the YMCA's arbiter (the DSP counts its bus timing in CKI, av.md §5.2); PClk/4 at 20 MHz; and the I/O, NuBus, audio and video domains unchanged from the family table — C32M 31.3344 MHz into the PSC, 25 MHz to SCSI, 20 MHz to the MACE, 40 MHz to the MUNI with 20 MHz and 10 MHz out to the slots, the ATECS-generated 44.1/48 kHz families, and the fixed NTSC/PAL video oscillators [1] Table 2-2 pp. 17–18.

What the 40 MHz grade costs and buys, in the developer note's own numbers (the 840AV columns of Table 2-3 [1] p. 20):

| Access | Main processor | MUNI and PSC | DSP (CKI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single read, single / multiple | 6 / 7 BClk | 5 / 6 | 9 / 11 |
| Single write, single / multiple | 6 / 7 | 4 / 6 | 8 / 11 |
| Burst read, single / multiple | 6-3-3-3 / 7-3-3-3 | 5-3-3-3 / 6-3-3-3 | 9-4-4-4 / 11-4-4-4 |
| Burst write, single / multiple | 6-2-2-2 / 7-2-2-2 | 4-3-3-3 / 6-3-3-3 | 8-7-7-7 / 11-7-7-7 |

The DRAM refresh cycle costs eight processor clocks, "does not affect RAM access timing", and "does not occur when the DSP controls the CPU bus" [1] p. 20. ROM accesses cost seven BClk on this machine [1] p. 21 (§3.8).

### 2.3 DRAM population and sizing

- **Four SIMM slots, no soldered capacity**: "In the Macintosh Quadra 840AV, all RAM is on SIMMs, with space for four expansion SIMMs. Thus maximum possible RAM capacity (using 16 MB RAM chips) is 128 MB" [1] p. 12; the base configuration is 8 MB [1] p. 3.
- **This machine's own DRAM rule**: "The DRAM in the Macintosh Quadra 840AV must have an access time of not more than 60 ns" [1] p. 12, restated in the differences list as "requires 60 ns DRAM chips" [1] p. 7 — the flip side of running the family's memory controller at BClk 40.
- **Module rules are family-level** (av.md §2.8): 72-pin SIMMs, one or two banks per module at up to 16 MB per bank, CAS-before-RAS refresh, no more than eight chips per bank, no composite modules, the parity connection ignored. The 72-pin pinout is [1] Table 3-24 p. 52, the module mechanics Figure 2-20 p. 53.
- **Sizing** is the family algorithm (av.md §2.6): the start-up code drives the YMCA's eight per-bank blocks — boundary bits at 16 MB granularity plus three size bits, sized twice per bank in standard and wide mode, merged so contiguous banks concatenate — observed in the ROM at $408ADAFC [2] (§5). The controller presents eight banks whatever the four sockets hold; a bank is a SIMM bank. Minimum bank 1 MB, maximum 16 MB, ceiling 128 MB (av.md §2.6).
- **Interleaving**: the YMCA file has no interleave register and no shipped software writes one (av.md §2.6); if paired banks interleave on this machine it is automatic in hardware — *inferred — unverified* (§6.2).

### 2.4 VRAM as fitted and expanded

- **Standard capacity**: "Each computer is delivered with two banks of VRAM soldered in, each bank providing 0.5 MB of storage" [1] p. 33 — 1 MB, with the functional split (one bank for the graphics image, one for live video to be mixed by Sebastian) that the family doc carries (av.md §2.10).
- **Expansion is this machine's alone**: "The Macintosh Quadra 840AV computer's VRAM is expandable from 1 MB to 2 MB; the Macintosh Centris 660AV computer's is not" [1] p. 6. The user "expand[s] VRAM capacity by inserting 68-pin SIMM cards in its two VRAM expansion slots" [1] p. 54; the cards are 80 ns parts "using the same configuration of SIMM cards as VRAM expansion in other Macintosh Quadra computers" [1] p. 34, with the pinout at Table 3-25 and the mechanicals at Figure 2-21 [1] pp. 54–55.
- **What 2 MB buys**: "the resulting VRAM capacity can support mixed video and graphics in full 24-bit color on small and medium-sized monitors and in 16-bit or 8-bit color on larger monitors" [1] p. 34. The full depth/monitor table (Table 2-13, [1] p. 34) gives, at 1 MB: 16-bit graphics at 640×480, 8-bit at 640×870 portrait and 1024×768, 4-bit at 1152×870; at 2 MB: 32 bits at 640×480 and 832×624, 16 bits at portrait and 1024×768, 8 bits at 1152×870. The machine's headline video limits stand above both rows: graphics to 24 bits, the video window to 640×480 at 16 bits, 21-inch monitors supported [1] pp. 5–7.
- **How software discovers the capacity**: the boot initializer writes the four-byte constant 'Nano' at VRAM base + 2 MB − 8 and reads it back; a mismatch selects the 1 MB configuration ([CIVIC](civic.md) §2.5) — on this machine both answers are legal hardware states, unlike the 660AV where only 1 MB exists.

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 Processor and the CPU bus

The 68040 is the family's, with its FPU, dual 4 KB caches and on-chip paged MMUs, and without bus snooping [1] pp. 5, 12; av.md §1.4. The CPU bus carries the same four masters — 68040, PSC, MUNI, DSP — under the YMCA's arbitration (av.md §5.2); this machine's only contribution to that picture is the grade: the DRAM costs of §2.2, the always-present MUNI (§2.1), and the DSP running at 66.6667 MHz [1] p. 13, asynchronous to the CPU bus ([DSP board](dsp3210-board.md) §1.3). The CPU bus's single-address and four-address burst protocols, and the whole cache-coherency burden that falls on software because there is no snooping, are the family's (av.md §2.2, §5.2).

### 3.2 The YMCA as fitted

The same silicon as every AV board (343S1097; av.md §2.3, §4.1), and on this machine its strap registers read $F. The one machine-specific act toward the chip is the speed dispatch: immediately after identity, before any self-test, the start-up code writes the Cyclone-40 pattern [2] (*observed*; the code block at ROM offset $4080F3E6, dispatching on the product kind $48; av.md §2.5):

| Register | Value |
|---|---|
| DRAMspeed0 (+$00) | 0 |
| DRAMspeed1 (+$04) | all-ones (bit 31 set) |
| CPUspeed0 (+$08) | 0 |
| CPUspeed1 (+$0C) | all-ones |
| ROMspeed0 (+$10) | 0 |
| ROMspeed1 (+$14) | 0 |
| ROMspeed2 (+$18) | all-ones |

The write of every latch is a longword carrying the value in bit 31 — the file's one-bit-per-longword convention (av.md §2.3). The dispatch is guarded by the decoder kind: a machine identified by some other controller skips the writes entirely [2]. Nothing else is machine-specific: the bank and width registers are driven only during sizing (§2.3), DSPspeed/EPROMmode/040Mode are never written by any shipped software (av.md §2.3), and the two video routing bits — ClockSelect and Bypass — are written by the video driver exactly as on the 660AV (av.md §2.7).

### 3.3 PSC and interrupts

The PSC (343S1100) and its register file are the family's ([PSC](psc.md) §2); this machine rewires nothing. The seven-level interrupt model, the PSC-VIA2 window carrying slot, SCSI, floppy, sound-frame and MUNI interrupts at level 2, the device interrupt registers at levels 3–6, and the 60 Hz tick on VIA1 CA1 are all documented at family level (av.md §5.1; [PSC](psc.md) §3.1, §2.8). What is specific here is only the population: all three slot-interrupt lines exist and reach the level-2 window ([PSC](psc.md) §4.8), and the MUNI's interrupt line is present and — as on the whole platform — disabled by the boot ROM and never serviced (av.md §5.4). The platform's latency contract, "Do not disable interrupts for longer than 0.5 ms" [1] p. 7, binds this machine's software like any other's.

### 3.4 Storage and communications

- **SCSI.** The 53C96-class core in Curio at $50F18000 (av.md §3.3), served by PSC channel 0 and the SCSI Manager 4.3 library in ROM ([PSC](psc.md) §4.3; av.md §2.9). Machine-specific is the physical bus: up to three internal devices (§4.4), the terminator discipline and the automatic termination at the external connector [1] p. 27.
- **Ethernet.** The MACE core at $50F1C000 with its station-address PROM at $50F08000 ([MACE](mace.md) §4.3), PSC channels 1/2 (av.md §4.2); "The user can plug a drop-box cable … into a standard Ethernet connector on the back panel" [1] p. 22. The 840AV's Ethernet configuration resource (id 78) is byte-identical to the other variants' [2] (§1.4).
- **Serial.** The SCC at $50F04000, both channels, with the printer port carrying LocalTalk and the modem port carrying GeoPort [1] p. 6; the DMA serial path through PSC channels 4–6 is the family's ([PSC](psc.md) §4.5).
- **Floppy.** New Age at $50F2A000 ([New Age](new-age.md) §1), PSC channel 3, driving the Apple SuperDrive [1] p. 3.

### 3.5 Video

The video subsystem is the family chain — CIVIC at $50036000 (≡ $50F36000), Sebastian at $50F30800, VRAM at $50100000 (av.md §3.3; [CIVIC](civic.md) §2.1) — with this machine's three deltas:

- **The synthesizer fitted is Endeavor** [1] p. 17: the parallel-load form (an 8-bit numerator, an 8-bit denominator and a clock-select byte at $50F2E000), not the bit-serial Clifton/PUMA stream the 660AV's driver shifts into the same address ([CIVIC](civic.md) §1.4). Only two references to the synthesizer's base exist in the whole ROM, both inside video code [2].
- **The monitor reach is the full table**, 21-inch displays included [1] p. 7, with the depth/monitor pairs of §2.4's Table 2-13 [1] p. 34.
- **VRAM may be 2 MB**, and the 'Nano' probe at bring-up decides which ([CIVIC](civic.md) §2.5).

Television output (composite/S-video through Mickey, routed by the YMCA's Bypass bit) and the video-in chain (the Philips decoder/scaler pair reached over Cuda's I²C bus, the capture buffer in VRAM at $50200800) are wired exactly as the family doc and the device pages describe them (av.md §2.7, §2.10; [CIVIC](civic.md) §4.6, §2.5; [VDC](vdc.md) §2.1). The capture data rates are the platform's: 18.43 MB per second for full-screen NTSC, 22.12 MB for PAL, "practical to record a video image up to one-quarter screen in size … in real time, without data compression" [1] p. 33.

### 3.6 Sound

Singer, the PSC's sound DMA engine, the ATECS clock synchronizer and the DSP path are the family's (av.md §4.4; [Singer](singer.md) §1.3, §3.1); 16-bit stereo at up to 48 kHz [1] pp. 5–6, all of it through DSP tasks — there is no non-DSP sound path in the shipped software set (av.md §4.4). On this machine the DSP runs at 66.6667 MHz [1] p. 13. The machine's first audible act is family code with a Cyclone name: the boot chime player at $408C5BB0 ("CycloneBeep") plays the 2.30 s, 16-bit stereo, 24 kHz PCM stored in ROM at file offset $C5D24 by polling the sound engine's frame phase directly, DSP untouched ([Singer](singer.md) §4.2; [2]) — §5.

### 3.7 Cuda, power and reset

The system-management microcontroller is the family's Cuda (Apple part 341S0788, firmware self-identified "Cuda 2.37"; [Cuda](cuda.md) §1.1) owning ADB, PRAM, the real-time clock, the one-second tick and power and reset control through the VIA1 window at $50F00000 ([Cuda](cuda.md) §2.1). This machine's power story has three 840AV-specific facts:

- **It is a soft-power machine.** The product record's feature vector omits the hard-power-off flag the 660AV's carries [2] (§1.4) — on this machine Shut Down removes power under software control (av.md §6.2), and the Cuda command set performs it ([Cuda](cuda.md) §3.11).
- **Its slots can power the machine.** "The Macintosh Quadra 840AV provides power control service to its expansion slots, so plug-in cards can turn the computer on and off" [1] pp. 5–6; "The Macintosh Quadra 840AV lets a plug-in card turn the computer on and off. The Macintosh Centris 660AV does not" [1] p. 7. The only physical correlate anywhere in the documentation is the NuBus connector's standby-power pin (STDBYPWR, pin b25, a trickle +5 V supply) and the adjacent /PFW line [1] Table 2-17 pp. 41–42 — that these are the power-control path is *inferred — unverified* (§6.1).
- **Keyboard and external wake** reach the same controller as on the whole platform: the ADB connector's power-on sense pin and the GeoPort wake line ([Cuda](cuda.md) §3.11; [1] p. 16).

The real-time clock and PRAM run on the replaceable long-life plug-in battery [1] p. 6 ([Cuda](cuda.md) §2.5). Reset is Cuda-mediated ([Cuda](cuda.md) §3.11); this board's front-panel reset wiring and hard-switch arrangement are not documented anywhere (§6.4).

### 3.8 The boot ROM as this machine carries it

The 2 MB image is shared byte-for-byte with the 660AV (av.md §2.9): release $10F3, version word $077D, checksum $5BF10FD1, mapped at $40800000, its second megabyte also readable at $900000 in 24-bit mode (av.md §3.4), its per-variant product and parameter records selected by the straps. This machine's ROM-level specifics:

- **Timing.** 120 ns chips, seven BClk cycles per CPU-bus access [1] p. 21 — the ROMspeed (0, 0, set) pattern of §3.2.
- **The video records carry a Cyclone-era board identifier.** The machine's VideoInfo record (ROM offset $152AC) points at a built-in-video board sResource with spID $25 (37) rather than the product record's box flag 72, and the declaration block names that board "Macintosh 3A" with a board-ID immediate of $3D [2] (*observed*: the board sResource at $1FFDDA, name at $1FFDCC, within the video declaration data). The enabler's display-name resources repeat the "Macintosh 3A"/"Macintosh 3B" pair [3] (*observed*). Why the shipped declaration data carries the 37-class identifier is not documented (§6.3).
- **Everything else in the image is per-variant data, not per-machine code**: the byte-identical per-variant Ethernet resources [2], the slot-PRAM defaults declaring three connectors [2], and the shared drivers, managers, DSP segments and chimes that the family doc enumerates (av.md §2.9).

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The three NuBus slots

- **Geography.** "Each of the three Macintosh Quadra 840AV slots has a 4-bit geographic address. The addresses are $C, $D, and $E, corresponding to slots 4, 5, and 6 in the Macintosh II family of computers" [1] p. 39. The ROM's slot-PRAM default places the three connectors at board positions 1, 2 and 3 [2] (§1.4); slots $A and $B carry PRAM records but no connectors, and slot $0 is the built-in video/Ethernet pseudo-slot [2] (*observed* in the slot information records; av.md §5.4).
- **Cards.** "Macintosh Quadra 840AV NuBus slots accept both long (4 by 13 inches) and short (4 by 7 inches) accessory cards of the same physical configuration as those used with the Macintosh II and Macintosh Quadra families" [1] p. 40, through 96-pin Euro-DIN connectors [1] Table 2-17 pp. 41–42.
- **Power per slot.** +5 V at 2.0 A, +12 V at 0.175 A, −12 V at 0.15 A; maximum card capacitance 1513/536/698 µF respectively [1] Table 2-18 p. 42. The connector also carries the standby-power trickle (STDBYPWR, b25) behind the slot power-control service (§3.7).
- **The bridge is integral.** The MUNI sits on the main board [1] p. 14: no adapter card exists for this machine, the product record asserts the bridge valid [2], and start-up programs MUNI Control $1C as part of the speed dispatch (§3.2). The MUNI's own behaviour — register file, block-transfer enables, FIFOs, the 25.6 µs transaction timeout, the disabled interrupt line — is the family doc's (av.md §5.4), as are the YMCA's 32 µs NuBus-space cycle timeout and the requirement that empty slots fault cleanly (av.md §3.6).
- **Slot interrupts** arrive, active low, in the PSC-VIA2 window, level 2, and dispatch through the Slot Manager's per-slot service queues ([PSC](psc.md) §4.8) — the MUNI is not in that path (av.md §5.4).

### 4.2 The DAV connector

- **Placement.** "In the Macintosh Quadra 840AV, a digital audio/video (DAV) expansion connector is mounted on the main circuit board in line with NuBus slot address $C (the slot nearest the center of the computer)" [1] p. 42. "Both models can accept a short NuBus accessory card that accesses the DAV connector; the Macintosh Quadra 840AV can also accept a long card" [1] p. 42 — the card is simultaneously a NuBus card in slot $C and a tap on the audio/video streams.
- **The connector.** A 40-pin KEL 8801-40-170L, pinout at Table 2-19 [1] pp. 42–43.
- **What it carries** is the family's digital audio/video expansion bus (av.md §2.11): the 4:2:2 unscaled YUV video input on the CCIR 601 format the era's compression chips consume [1] pp. 42, 45, and the Singer serial bus — subframe 1 of each 256-bit frame reserved for system sound, the other subframes free for the card [1] p. 44 ([Singer](singer.md) §3.1) — with singerMClk at 24.576 MHz and the bit clock at 256× the sample rate [1] Table 2-20 p. 44. The connector also passes the I²C clock and data lines (pins 26 and 22) [1] Table 2-19 p. 43 — the same bus, otherwise mastered by Cuda ([VDC](vdc.md) §2.1), on which the video-in decoder and scaler live; a DAV card can therefore see or contend for that bus.
- **Signal discipline.** 10 ns minimum setup, 8 ns minimum hold, 20 pF maximum load on the sound signals [1] p. 44; the video timing (HRef-defined lines, LLClk, VS) at [1] p. 45.
- **Sourcing.** A DAV card may disable the video-in decoder "and feed its own YUV video to the VDC" [1] p. 32 (av.md §2.11; [VDC](vdc.md) §1.1) — the connector is an input path as well as a tap.

### 4.3 What this machine does not expand by

- **No processor-direct slot.** "The Macintosh Centris 660AV can accept an accessory card that connects directly to the main processor bus; the Macintosh Quadra 840AV cannot" [1] p. 7. NuBus (three slots) plus the DAV connector is the entire expansion story.
- **No 040INPROGRESS consumer.** The alternate burst-write protocol form selected by that line is documented only for the 660AV's processor-direct connector [1] p. 51 (av.md §2.2); no connector on this machine drives it.
- **Ceilings.** RAM tops out at 128 MB (§2.3), VRAM at 2 MB (§2.4); the internal SCSI bus at three devices (§4.4). No comm slot or secondary storage bus exists.

### 4.4 Internal storage

- **Device count.** "The Macintosh Quadra 840AV accepts up to three removable SCSI devices inside its case" [1] p. 3, within the platform's seven-device, six-external ceiling [1] p. 6; the internal device space is drawn in the developer note's Figure 2-5 [1] p. 25, with mounting drawings in its mechanical appendix.
- **Terminator discipline.** "The device at the end of the internal SCSI cable includes terminators; all other internal devices do not. When installing internal SCSI devices, the installer must make sure that the device at the end of the cable has terminators and must remove terminators from any other internal SCSI devices" — none terminated malfunctions, more than one "will malfunction and the logic board may be damaged" [1] p. 28.
- **Automatic termination.** When no external device is connected, "special circuitry terminates the bus on the logic board near the external connector", and it disconnects itself when external termination is detected at reset [1] p. 27.
- **Options.** A 500 MB or 1000 MB internal hard disk (the 1000 MB "in the Macintosh Quadra 840AV only") or an internal CD-ROM drive [1] p. 3, plus the SuperDrive floppy [1] p. 3.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The reset picture — ROM overlaid at address zero, the initial stack pointer from the ROM's checksum longword, the initial PC from the longword at offset $04 — is the family's (av.md §2.9, §3.5). The sequence below is what the shipped ROM actually does on a strap-$F machine, all *observed* in the disassembly of the 2 MB image [2] unless marked otherwise:

1. **Reset.** The 68040 fetches SSP from the overlaid checksum longword and PC $0000002A, which is `JMP $40800074` — the start-up entry (av.md §2.9).
2. **StartBoot ($40800074).** SR set to $2700; a CACR probe distinguishes the 68040; caches and MMU turned off; the reset-time transparent translations installed — **DTT0 = $807FC040** (NuBus space) and **DTT1 = $500FC040** (the whole $5xxxxxxx I/O island) — so POST can reach the CPU-ID register and the I/O island with the MMU off; a `RESET` instruction; then the ROM's return-in-A6 convention branches into:
3. **Hardware init ($4080F1D0) — before any self-test.** Stack at $2600; a temporary bus-error handler installed; **GetCPUIDReg ($4080FDA0)** reads $5FFFFFFC and validates the $A55A signature and that the location is not writable; **GetHardwareInfo ($4080F69A)** reads the four strap longwords ($4080F6EC–$4080F70E), assembles CPUID3…CPUID0 = $F, and matches the 840AV product record (Gestalt 78, box flag 72) — a mismatch parks at the infinite loop $4080F762 (§1.2); the ROM existence check and relocation to $40800000; a second identification pass; VIA initialization; the Cuda sync cycle ([Cuda](cuda.md) §4.1, §4.2); then the speed dispatch: the **Cyclone-40 block ($4080F3E6)** writes the seven YMCA speed latches of §3.2 and, gated on the expansion probe, **MUNI Control $1C at $4080F41A** — this machine's unique write pattern. On a real 840AV the gate passes by construction: the MUNI is on the board and its validity bit is asserted in the product record [2] (§1.4).
4. **PortableCheck** — compiled into the universal image but a no-op here: the machine's configuration record has no Power Manager bit, and the code returns immediately [2].
5. **Power-on self-test ($408A51F6).** The exception table at TMVectors ($408A4F70) installed as VBR; a third identification pass; the diagnostic-ROM check; a loop-back check on the VIA1 port lines that also touches the SCSI controller's status register; PRAM signature reads over the VIA1 shift register to Cuda ([Cuda](cuda.md) §4.3); cache invalidation; the **ROM checksum test**; the external-hardware probe (on this machine the MUNI read completes rather than faulting); then the **boot chime**: the chime player at $408C5BB0 plays the stereo PCM at file offset $C5D24 by polling the sound engine's frame phase at interrupt level 7 with the caches off, the DSP still untouched ([Singer](singer.md) §4.2). Then **SizeMemory**: the width registers zeroed, boundary and size bits shifted into all eight banks, every bank sized twice (standard then wide mode, the width registers programmed per bank pair from the offsets table at ROM offset $AE070), contiguous banks merged (§2.3; av.md §2.6); a memory pattern test; and the POST subtests — **generic only**: the subtest table has no AV-specific entry for either AV box flag, so the machine runs the serial-and-VIA tests and nothing else — no sound, no DSP, no video self-test [2] (*observed* in the subtest dispatch).
6. **OS initialization.** Low-memory globals filled from the product record; the VIA, SCC and SCSI bases installed from the decoder record; the SCC hardware-reset (WR9 = $C0) at $50F04000; the 68040 floating-point support package installed; caches enabled — but the **data cache stays off until after slot initialization**, because the MUNI terminates some cycles in a way the '040 must observe (av.md §5.2); interrupt sources masked; the PSC's post-processing — the MUNI's interrupt disabled outright (no handler exists; av.md §5.4) and the level-4 enables set; the Cuda manager and ADB bring-up ([Cuda](cuda.md) §4.4); **slot initialization** — the built-in video's primary init runs here: the CIVIC reset sequence, monitor sensing, the 'Nano' VRAM probe that decides 1 MB versus 2 MB (§2.4; [CIVIC](civic.md) §4.2), the Endeavor frequency words, the Sebastian CLUT load; the startup screen; then the drivers (`.NewAge` among them), the SCSI Manager, ADB and the desktop, and the machine boots from its SCSI disk.
7. **Error paths.** A strap mismatch hangs at $4080F762 before anything else. POST failures sound the numbered error chimes through the same chime player — the error chime PCM sits in ROM at file offset $FBA74 [2] — and a dead or wedged Cuda aborts the early polled transaction with error code $30 ([Cuda](cuda.md) §3.12).

## 6. Open questions

1. **Slot power control's mechanism.** The feature — "power control service to its expansion slots, so plug-in cards can turn the computer on and off" [1] pp. 5–6 — is documented only as a capability. Which connector line implements it (the STDBYPWR trickle pin is the *inferred — unverified* candidate), what current it budgets, and what protocol a card follows to request power on or off are all unstated; the developer note's connector tables name the pin and nothing more.
2. **DRAM interleaving.** The YMCA has no interleave register and no shipped software writes one (av.md §2.6, §7.5); whether this machine's paired SIMM banks interleave automatically in hardware is unverified, as is the effect on the §2.2 access costs.
3. **The declaration data's board identifier.** The shipped declaration block names this machine's built-in video board "Macintosh 3A" under spID $25 (37) — a Cyclone-33-era identifier — while the product record's box flag is 72 [2] (§3.8). Whether later production ROMs corrected the identifier, and what (if anything) software made of the mismatch, is unknown.
4. **Hard power switching.** How the physical AC switch and the soft-power path interact on this board, and how the front-panel reset (if any) is wired, are documented nowhere — the developer note describes only the soft path, and the Cuda page carries the same hole from the controller side ([Cuda](cuda.md) §3.11, §6.11).
5. **Power supply ratings.** The developer note gives no wattage, input range or rail budget for the whole machine (only the per-slot budget of Table 2-18); the supply is not otherwise documented in the evidence set.
6. **ROM revisions.** Only the $10F3/$077D/$5BF10FD1 image (declaration data dated June 25, 1993) is in evidence; whether production 840AVs ever shipped a different revision of the 2 MB image is unknown.
7. **The diagnostic-ROM slot.** The machine's decoder record carries a diagnostic-ROM base ($58000000) with its validity bit set, yet no shipped code ever touches it [2] (§1.4's record; the family map, av.md §3.1, has no entry for it). Whether shipping boards carry the socket at all is unknown.
8. **Factory memory population.** The 8 MB base configuration across four slots is presumably two 4 MB SIMMs — *inferred — unverified*; the developer note documents capacities, not factory load-outs.
9. **The burn-in jumper.** The POST loop-back check reads VIA1 port lines and the SCSI status register [2]; whether production boards fit the jumper that selects the extended burn-in path is not documented.
10. **DAV reach beyond slot $C.** The connector is in line with slot $C [1] p. 42 and the card must be a NuBus card in that slot; whether a card in slot $D or $E can physically reach the DAV ribbon is not stated — *inferred — unverified* that it cannot.
11. **The 33 MHz Cyclone.** The boot ROM carries a complete product record for a 33 MHz Cyclone (av.md §6.3, §7.11); whether any such board existed in hardware, and whether the two Cyclone grades differ in anything but straps and crystals, remains unknown.
12. **The video window limit's provenance.** The 640×480-at-16-bits video-window ceiling [1] p. 6 is documented as a fact without a reason; whether it is a VRAM-bandwidth, CIVIC-timing or capture-path constraint is not stated anywhere in the evidence set.
