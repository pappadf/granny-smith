# The Power Macintosh G3 family — the Gossamer platform (1997–98)

The **Gossamer** platform is Apple's third-generation Power Macintosh logic board: the one mini-ATX board that shipped, from November 1997, in the beige Power Macintosh G3 desktop and mini tower enclosures, in the education-only All-in-One, and — with two PCI cards added — as the Macintosh Server G3. The ROM names the platform itself: its configuration record carries `BootstrapVersion = "Boot Gossamer 0."` and the Open Firmware of the first ROM revision publishes `compatible = "AAPL,Gossamer"` [9], [10]. The generation it defines is the PowerPC 750 ("G3") on a 66 MHz processor bus, Motorola's MPC106 **Grackle** as a combined memory controller and single PCI host bridge, Apple's **Heathrow** I/O controller sitting *on* that PCI bus as a real PCI function, ATA replacing SCSI for the boot device, SDRAM DIMMs in place of the TNT generation's fast-page-mode array, the second-level cache moved off the memory controller onto the processor module's back side, and the entire audio/AV subsystem split out onto a plug-in **personality card** in the PERCH slot.

It is a new platform, not a delta of the TNT generation ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §1.1): every one of the TNT board's structural pillars is replaced — Hammerhead and Bandit by Grackle ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §4.1–§4.2), Grand Central by Heathrow, the dedicated video bus by an ordinary PCI graphics device ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.1, §2.4), the Hammerhead-owned front-side L2 by the 750's own back-side cache ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.3), the TNT identity registers by a Gossamer board register ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.6). What is inherited is the *firmware architecture*: an OldWorld Macintosh whose 4 MB ROM carries the 68k Toolbox, the NanoKernel, the 68k emulator and Open Firmware in one image, with Open Firmware performing the hardware bring-up and building the device tree every operating system reads — the contract of [tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.7, with a two-generation-newer firmware (2.0f1 and 2.4 against TNT's 1.0.5). The machines this platform replaced were the 1997 TNT follow-ons, which still reported the 1995 identities in Open Firmware ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §6.5); the G3 family goes further and has no identity register that distinguishes its own members at all — see §2.6.

This page is the family doc for a family whose machine docs are not yet written: the four machines each get a short subsection in the per-machine index (§6) naming the future machine doc, and this page plus the cited TNT device pages are their current specification.

## 1. Overview & membership

### 1.1 What the platform is

Apple's developer note states the shape in one sentence: "The architecture of the main logic board is based on the PowerPC G3 microprocessor and two buses: the processor bus and the PCI bus. The processor bus connects the microprocessor, video, cache, and memory; the PCI bus connect the expansion slots and the I/O devices. The PCI interface on the Power Macintosh G3 computers conforms to PCI specification revision 2.1" [1] §"Block Diagrams and Main ICs" p. 38. The board itself: "The main logic board in all three enclosures has a mini-ATX form factor that supports the PowerPC G3 microprocessor module with a back-side second-level (L2) cache, three PCI card expansion slots, three SDRAM DIMM slots, one SO-DIMM connector for video RAM expansion, and a PERCH slot. The PERCH slot accepts three configurations of Apple Macintosh input/output cards that provide either standard Macintosh sound I/O or enhanced AV features" [1] §"Introduction" p. 13.

The note counts five custom ICs, one of which is not on the logic board at all: "the Grackle memory controller and PCI bridge; the Heathrow I/O subsystem and DMA controller; the Screamer sound processor (on Audio/Video and Audio input/output cards, not on the main logic board); the Cuda soft-power controller; the ATI 3D RAGE video subsystem" [1] §"Custom ICs" p. 44 — the family's architecture in miniature: two chips carry the machine, the sound silicon moved onto the personality card, and the video silicon is merchant PCI.

Family-level specification, in one table (the details are §2's and §4's; per-machine deltas in §6):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Processor | PowerPC 750 at 233/266/300/333 MHz, on a ZIF module with the L2 on it; 32 KB each I and D cache [1] pp. 14, 42 |
| Processor bus | 64-bit, 66 MHz class, split address and data tenures [1] p. 16; publishes 66,820,000 Hz [10] (*observed*) |
| L2 cache | 512 KB or 1 MB back-side SRAM at half core speed [1] pp. 14, 43 |
| RAM | 0 MB soldered; three 168-pin 3.3 V SDRAM DIMM slots, 8–256 MB each, "expandable to 768 MB" [1] pp. 14, 43, 64 |
| ROM | 4 MB on a 160-pin DIMM, 64-bit data bus [1] pp. 14, 43 |
| Video | ATI RAGE II+DVD or RAGE PRO, 2 MB SGRAM soldered, to 4 or 6 MB by SO-DIMM [1] pp. 14–15, 48 |
| Storage | one ATA-2 hard disk, 24X ATAPI CD-ROM, SCSI (MESH), SWIM3 SuperDrive [1] pp. 15, 50–52 |
| Ports | two serial, 10Base-T RJ-45, 25-pin SCSI, ADB [1] p. 16 |
| Sound | on the personality card — Screamer or Burgundy, 16-bit stereo [1] pp. 45–46, 52 |
| Expansion | three PCI slots (33 MHz, 15 W, 5 V), the PERCH slot, the cards' 112-pin modem slot [1] pp. 33, 69 |
| Enclosure | one logic board; the Server G3 is the mini tower plus two PCI cards [1] pp. 20–28, [2] |

Membership is identified by what the ROM and the firmware publish, not by name plates — and on this platform those values are *the same for every member* (§2.6). |---|---|---|---|---|---|

Two facts define the membership mechanically. First, "the Desktop and Minitower computers use the same logic board, but there are jumper settings that differ between them" [3] §"Basics — Shared Logic Board" p. 4 — the enclosure is a jumper (J28) plus sheet metal. Second, all four machines report the same gestalt value: "All of the Power Macintosh G3 computers have a machine ID value of 510 (hexadecimal 1FE)" [1] §"Machine Identification" p. 33, and the Server G3 note repeats it with the reason: the value "is the same machine type value returned for the Power Macintosh G3 computer, because the system ROM is not modified for its Macintosh Server G3 counterpart" [2] §"Compatibility Issues". The `compatible` string the firmware publishes is likewise shared by the whole family — `AAPL,Gossamer` with `MacRISC` on the 2.0f1 firmware, `AAPL,PowerMac G3` with `MacRISC` on 2.4, and `model "Power Macintosh"` in both [10] (*observed* on real machines of both firmware generations).

### 1.3 The one ROM

Every machine in §1.2 runs the same 4 MB ROM image in four field revisions, identified by the two header words the machines publish: `077D` at ROM offset `+$08` and the subversion at `+$12` — the two words Apple's own sample code reads (`*(UInt16*)(LMGetROMBase() + 0x12)`) to tell the boards apart [1] §"Machine Identification" p. 33.

| Revision | Subversion | First shipped | Open Firmware | Graphics controller | Header checksum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rev A | `$77D.40F2` | November 1997 | 2.0f1 | ATI 3D RAGE II+DVD | `$79D68D63` [9] (*observed*) |
| Rev B | `$77D.45F1` | May 1998 roll-out [1] §"Introduction" p. 13 | 2.0f1 | ATI 3D RAGE PRO | `$78FDB784` (secondary-attested, image not in evidence; §7 item 5) |
| Rev C ("Silk") | `$77D.45F2` | "Models began shipping with the 0x45F2 ROM in August 1998" [1] p. 13 | 2.4 | ATI 3D RAGE PRO / PRO Turbo | `$78F57389` [9] (*observed*) |
| Rev D | `$77D.45F3` | not established as a shipping configuration | 2.4 | — | `$78E842A8` (secondary-attested, image not in evidence; §7 item 5) |

The image decomposes into six components [9], at physical `$FFC00000 + offset` (the layout is §3.5's subject):

| Offset in image | Size | Component |
|---|---|---|
| `+$000000` | 3 MB | `Mac68KROM` — the 68k Toolbox ROM, 180 resources |
| `+$300000` | 48 KB | PowerPC exception vectors **and the Gossamer hardware-init/boot program** — the reset entry lives at `+$300100`, the boot program at `+$303000` |
| `+$30D000` | 1 page | `ConfigInfo` — the record the boot program and NanoKernel read (§2.7, §3.6) |
| `+$310000` | 64 KB | the NanoKernel (v01.01, byte-identical in Rev A and Rev C) |
| `+$320000` | 192 KB | the Open Firmware XCOFF bundle (2.0f1 or 2.4); `HWInitCodeSize = 0` — there is **no separate HWInit/POST blob** on this platform [9] |
| `+$360000` | 128 KB | the 68k emulator code, with its opcode table at `+$380000` |

Three structural facts against the TNT image ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §1.3). First, the hardware initialization and the Serial Test Manager live *inside* the exception-table component — the diagnostics menu strings (`T0 ROM Checksum … T9 NTAWord Test`, `X) Exit STM (Continue booting)`) sit in the boot program, not in a diagnostics object [9] (*observed*). Second, the Open Firmware bundle is larger (192 KB against TNT's 128 KB) and two generations newer. Third, the configuration record's `BootstrapVersion` is `Boot Gossamer 0.` — the 16-byte field is full, there is no minor digit [9] (*observed*).

One firmware build serves this board and the PowerBook G3 Series: the candidate `compatible` template inside both ROM revisions contains `AAPL,Gossamer` and `AAPL,WallStreet` (2.0f1) or `AAPL,PowerMac G3` and `AAPL,PowerBook1998` (2.4), and the firmware picks the board string at run time [9] (*observed*). The 2.4 root builder's literal pool shows how: it maps the board-register page and reads `$FF000004` [9] (*observed*).

## 2. Board architecture common to the family

### 2.1 The two buses

The **processor bus** is the PowerPC 60x bus with exactly two agents: the 750 and Grackle. Apple states its width and its tenure discipline — "Processor bus: 64-bit wide, 66 MHz, supporting split address and data tenures" [1] p. 16 — and the firmware publishes the exact rate, 66,820,000 Hz, as the root node's `clock-frequency` [10] (*observed*). This is the structural difference from TNT's "AR bus", the multiple-master 60x bus with arbiter classes, snooping masters and a 32-byte coherency protocol ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §5.2; [hammerhead.md](../tnt/hammerhead.md) §3.5–§3.6): Gossamer has no second processor socket, no cache snooping to perform (the L2 is private to the 750, §2.2), and no coherency handshake across the PCI bridge.

The **PCI bus** is one bus, 32-bit multiplexed, at "a 33.33 MHz clock frequency" [1] §"PCI Expansion Slots" p. 69, conforming to PCI revision 2.1 [1] p. 38. Grackle is its only host bridge, and the bridge "runs synchronously. The processor bus operates at a clock rate of 67 MHz, and the PCI bus operates at 30 or 33 MHz" [1] §"Grackle IC" p. 44. Everything that is not RAM, ROM or the 750 hangs off this one bus: Heathrow with its thirteen DBDMA channels, the on-board ATI, the PERCH slot and the three expansion slots all share it (§5.3). The bridge "generates PCI parity as required by the PCI bus specification, but it does not check parity or respond to the parity error signal" [1] p. 45.

There is **no third bus**. Where TNT carries a dedicated video bus with its own bridge, framebuffer and RAMDAC ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.1, §2.4), Gossamer's display controller is an ordinary PCI device at an Open-Firmware-assigned BAR (§3.3, §4.6) — the design decision that most simplifies the family's memory map.

### 2.2 The processor module and the back-side L2 cache

"The PowerPC G3 is located on a plug-in microprocessor card module which includes the L2 cache" [1] §"PowerPC Microprocessor" p. 42. The module sits in a ZIF socket and is a service part, not a customer upgrade: "previous Power Macintosh computers featured a user-installable processor card, this logic board uses a processor module that must not be removed by the customer" [3] §"Basics — Processor Module Vs. Card" p. 4. Each module ships with its jumper block: "The jumper block you place at location J16 configures the board to work with different processor modules", color-coded 233 MHz red, 266 MHz white, 300 MHz black [3] §"Troubleshooting — Jumper Location J16" p. 216 — "Failure to install the correct jumper may result in a computer that won't boot up" [3] p. 4.

The cache is the 750's own: 512 KB or 1 MB of SRAM on the module, clocked at "one half the speed of the microprocessor, a ratio of 2:1" [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 43 — 166.5 MHz on a 333 MHz module [1] p. 14 — driven by the 750's integrated L2 cache controller and tag RAMs [1] p. 42, programmed through the 750's L2CR register ([8] §9.1.3). This is the inversion of the TNT contract, where the memory controller owns an *optional, front-side* L2 whose size is sensed on connector pins ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.3): here the cache is never absent, never sensed, and the memory controller has nothing to do with it.

The board learns the module's cache configuration from the board register (§2.6): bits 7–5 (the processor-module ID) index an eight-entry L2CR table inside the ROM's boot program, and on a 750 the selected entry is written straight into L2CR at reset — code 1 a 512 KB configuration (`$A9100000`), code 2 a 1 MB configuration (`$B9100000`), codes 5 and 6 re-assigned to later module types between Rev A and Rev C, code 7 "no L2 set-up" [9] (*observed*; the L2CR field decoding is [8] §9.1.3's). The core clock is a multiple of the bus clock selected on the module — the 750's PLL, configured by `PLL_CFG[0–3]` and readable back as `HID1` ([8] §7.2.11.3, §2.1.2) — and the boot program derives the three numbers the firmware publishes (`clock-frequency`, `bus-frequency`, `timebase-frequency`) from the board register's bus code and `HID1` [9] (*observed*; §5.4).

### 2.3 The memory subsystem

Grackle owns the memory subsystem: it "provides the control and timing signals for ROM and RAM. The memory control logic supports byte, word, longword, and burst accesses to the system memory. If an access is not aligned to the appropriate address boundary, Grackle generates multiple data transfers on the bus" [1] §"Grackle IC" p. 44. The controller's bank and timing registers are §3.1's and §4.1's subject.

Main memory is SDRAM — the family's second structural break with TNT's fast-page-mode DIMM pairs ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.3):

- **Module**: 3.3 V unbuffered 8-byte (x64, non-parity) SDRAM DIMMs, 168-pin, one bank of 64 bits; JEDEC MO-161 mechanics, JEDEC 21-C electricals, one factory DIMM installed [1] §"RAM DIMMs" p. 64. Sizes 8–256 MB, one-, two- and four-bank, at most 16 devices per DIMM [1] p. 43; controller limits: bank 2–64 MB, largest DIMM a 4-bank 256 MB of 128 Mbit devices [1] p. 64.
- **Population is flexible and linear**: "The SDRAM DIMMs can be installed one or more at a time. The Power Macintosh G3 logic board supports only linear memory organization; therefore no performance gains are seen when two DIMMs of the same size are installed", and the installed DIMMs "will be configured as a contiguous array of memory" [1] p. 65 — no pairing rule, no 128-bit interleave, in sharp contrast to TNT ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.3). DIMM height is the only constraint: 1.15 inches in the desktop, 1.5 in the tower and All-in-One [1] §"RAM Expansion" p. 34.
- **Sizing is by presence detect**: the JEDEC presence-detect EEPROM "is required and must be set properly" [1] §"Presence Detect EEPROM" p. 66; the boot program reads the SPD EEPROMs through the Cuda's I2C pass-through and programs Grackle's bank and mode registers from what it finds [9] (*observed*: the I2C transactions are read from the boot program; the SPD-byte-to-register mapping is not decoded — §7 item 3).
- **Device speed**: 100 MHz/10 ns or faster, CAS latency 3, 7 ns access from clock, burst length at least 4 [1] §"SDRAM Devices" p. 67 — on a 66 MHz-class bus, well below rating.
- **Refresh**: CAS-before-RAS, "every 15.6 μs" [1] §"RAM Refresh" p. 67 — the same architectural constant as every earlier Macintosh generation, now a Grackle-programmed behaviour.

Two capacity statements coexist in Apple's own documents and a third in observation: the 1998 developer note says "expandable to 768 MB" [1] p. 14; the Server G3 note says "System memory can be expanded to 384 MB" [2] §"Configurations"; and real machines with three 256 MB DIMMs run 768 MB, published as `/memory reg 00000000 30000000` [10] (*observed*). Grackle's controller ceiling is 1 GB [6] §3.2.6. The 384 MB figure is the smaller of Apple's own numbers and does not bind the hardware (§7 item 4).

The **ROM** completes the memory subsystem: "The ROM consists of 4 MB of ROM installed in a DIMM socket" [1] §"ROM" p. 43, with a 64-bit data bus width [1] p. 14, on a 160-pin DIMM. The board register's bit 14 tells the boot program whether the fitted ROM is burst-capable, and it selects the ROM timing field in the memory-controller mode register accordingly [9] (*observed*).

### 2.4 The personality-card split

The family's most visible design decision is that **the audio/AV subsystem is not on the logic board**. "The sound system for the Power Macintosh G3 computers is implemented entirely on the Apple DVD-Video and Audio/Video Card, Apple Audio Input/Output Card, or Apple Audio/Video Input/Output Card" [1] §"Sound" p. 52, and the same is true of video input and video output [1] §"Video Input", §"Video Output" pp. 29–30. All of it hangs off the **PERCH slot**: "The PERCH slot connector is a 182-pin microchannel connector. The PERCH slot is a superset of the PCI specification, and it does not accept standard PCI cards" [1] §"Video Input" p. 29, on the desktop and tower enclosures it supports the three Apple cards, "the All-in-one enclosure does not support the DVD-Video and Audio/Video Card", and "This note does not provide the electrical specification for the PERCH slot" [1] §"Introduction" p. 15 — with the explicit warning that "Apple does not support development of third-party cards for the PERCH slot" [1] §"Expansion Features" p. 63.

The three cards [1] §"Architecture" pp. 39–42:

| Card | Sound IC | Carries | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple Audio Input/Output Card | Screamer | line-level stereo in and out, the 112-pin modem slot, front-panel volume buttons (All-in-One) | the `Whisper` codename is what its identity EEPROM reports [10] (*observed*) |
| Apple Audio/Video Input/Output Card | Screamer | the above plus composite and S-video in and out, RCA audio, the 60-pin DAV connector, the 320×240-capture video input module | community-named "Wings"; not attested in the evidence set (*unverified*) |
| Apple DVD-Video and Audio/Video Card | Burgundy | the AV card's features with a VPX3224 capture IC (640×480 NTSC / 768×576 PAL capture, no scaling), DVD playback support, **no DAV connector** | community-named "Bordeaux"; not attested in the evidence set (*unverified*) |

The card identifies itself to the firmware through a small I2C EEPROM read via the Cuda: the device tree carries a `/perch` node with `compatible "Whisper"` and `version 2` on real machines [10] (*observed*). The All-in-One's card additionally wires the built-in display: "Addition of RGB signals on the Audio and Audio/Video input/output cards to support the built-in internal monitor. A ribbon cable is connected between the RGB output connector on the card and the internal monitor" [1] §"All-in-one Features" p. 18 — and it is the presence of that card, not the enclosure, that the board register's bit 4 reports (§2.6). The modem slot also lives on the cards, not the board: "The modem slot is located on the Apple DVD-Video, Audio/Video, and Audio input/output cards rather than on the main logic board", and it is "strictly for modem cards that do not require the use of the PCI signals" — not the comm slot II of the Power Macintosh 5500/6500 [1] §"Modem Slot" p. 33, §"The Modem Slot" p. 70.

The one logic-board-side interface the cards need is the **DAVbus**: Heathrow contains "sound control logic and buffers" and "a serial interface and sound control logic for the Screamer sound IC" [1] §"Heathrow IC" p. 45, reached at window offset `+$14000` [10] (*observed*). The AV card carries the DAV connector out to PCI: "a bi-directional digital audio video (DAV) connector for adding a video processor on a PCI expansion card" [1] §"Video Input" p. 29 — 60 pins giving access to "the Audio/Video card's 4:2:2 unscaled YUV video input data bus" over a 7-inch ribbon [1] §"The DAV Connector" p. 70.

### 2.5 Endianness — the one structural rule

The platform is big-endian at the top and little-endian at the bottom, and the seam runs through Grackle instead of through a bridge slice. Byte order on the processor bus and in RAM/ROM is big-endian; the PCI bus is little-endian, and so are Grackle's two configuration ports and every 32-bit register inside the Heathrow window (the interrupt banks, the feature control register, the DBDMA channel registers), while the byte-wide cells — the SCC, the VIA, SWIM3, MESH and the NVRAM aperture — have no byte order [10] (*observed*; the cell convention is the Grand Central lineage's, [grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §3.1). PowerPC code reaches all of it with byte-reversing loads and stores, exactly as on TNT ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.5) — but the swap lives elsewhere: TNT's Bandit translated for a fixed window behind the bridge ([bandit.md](../tnt/bandit.md) §3.5); Gossamer's Grackle forwards PCI memory space one-to-one, and the little-endian discipline belongs to the devices and the config ports themselves ([6] §7.4.5.2).

### 2.6 Machine identity

TNT derives the machine model from a Hammerhead identifier plus a Grand Central strap register, in both halves of the ROM ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.6). Gossamer replaces that machinery with one register and — critically — **stops distinguishing its own members**.

The register is a read-only halfword at physical `$FF000004`, inside the `$FF000000` board-register page decoded in Grackle's ROM bank 1 space — not a PCI device, not inside the Heathrow window (§3.4). Five consumers read it: the ROM's reset/boot code, the Open Firmware 2.4 root builder, the 68k Toolbox ROM, the firmware's All-in-One helper words, and a factory-written NVRAM script that sets the All-in-One display's default brightness and contrast [9], [10] (*observed*). Its bits, with the consumers established from the ROM [9] (*observed*):

| Bit | Meaning | Reader |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | 1 = Macintosh-style SWIM3 floppy controller fitted; 0 = PC-style MFDC — the boot program switches Heathrow's floppy cell accordingly | boot program [9] (*observed*) |
| 14 | 0 = burst-capable ROM present — selects the ROM timing field in the memory mode register | boot program [9] (*observed*) |
| 13–8 | PCI slot PRSNT# pairs for slots C1, B1, A1 | no reader found in the ROM (*unverified*) |
| 7–5 | processor/cache module ID — indexes the boot program's L2CR table (§2.2) | boot program [9] (*observed*) |
| 4 | 0 = All-in-One personality card present, 1 = Whisper/Wings-style card | boot program, 68k ROM at `$FFC0133A`, OF 2.4 root builder, the firmware's `is-all-in-one-gossamer` word [9], [10] (*observed*) |
| 3–1 | bus-clock code — indexes the boot program's clock table (§5.4); every shipping board carries code 6 | boot program [9] (*observed*) |
| 0 | 0 = Bose audio module detected | no reader found in the ROM (*unverified*) |

The gestalt value comes from the 68k half's single product record: the ROM carries **one** `ProductInfo` record (`ProductKind $7F`, machine-type base `$1F8` = 504) where the TNT ROM carries four [9] (*observed* in the disassembled product directory and identification routine), and the published machine type is 504 + 6 = 510 — the same `+6` relation the TNT ROM's product records use ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.6). The one branch the identification code takes after matching the record is the All-in-One test: bit 4 of `$FF000004`, setting a flag in low memory [9] (*observed*).

The Open Firmware half publishes `model "Power Macintosh"` and a root `compatible` that depends on the **firmware**, not the box: `AAPL,Gossamer` + `MacRISC` on Open Firmware 2.0f1, `AAPL,PowerMac G3` + `MacRISC` on 2.4 [10] (*observed* on real machines of both). The 2.4 builder reads the board register to pick even that choice [9] (*observed*).

The consequence for software is the family's defining quirk, and the G3's own echo of the 1997 TNT situation ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §6.5): **no identity register distinguishes a Desktop from a Mini Tower from a Server G3, and none distinguishes the ROM revisions.** Software separates the members only by what hangs off the board — the PERCH card's EEPROM, the PCI cards, the built-in display's presence — and separates board generations only by the ROM subversion at `+$12`, the ATI device ID at PCI device `$12` (§4.6) and the Open Firmware version. Everything else every OS knows comes from the device tree the firmware builds.

One legacy footnote belongs here because it bites re-implementations: the 68k ROM still carries the inherited model-identification routine that probes earlier platforms' registers (`$5FFFFFFC`, `$F8000000`, `$F301A000` and others). On this ROM it is dead code for product selection — the single product record makes its result irrelevant — and it never reads `$FF000004`; but its reads must still complete harmlessly, and none may accidentally satisfy a TNT or Catalyst test [9] (*observed*; §7 item 7).

### 2.7 The boot contract

The cold-boot path is established from the ROM's own boot program [9] (*observed*; decoded from the image, cited here at the family level because every machine in §1.2 runs it identically):

1. **Reset.** The 750 fetches `$FFF00100`, inside the ROM decode. The first instructions test SRR1/HID0 for a hard reset and branch to the **boot program** at ROM offset `+$303000` — the code Apple's configuration record names with `BootstrapVersion "Boot Gossamer 0."` [9].
2. **Real mode and a first map.** Real mode, BATs zeroed, a 256 MB identity BAT0 map, per-processor HID0 setup (caches off) [9].
3. **Grackle bring-up** through the little-endian configuration ports (§3.2): probe host device 0, set `PICR1`/`PICR2` for a 750, program the `0x70` group, then — the two writes everything else depends on — Heathrow's BAR0 `$F3000000` and command register `$0016` [9].
4. **Heathrow bring-up**: `Mask1 = $80000000`, `Clear1 = $FFFFFFFF`, then the feature-control sequence `$033EFF3A` → `$01BEFFFA` asserting and releasing the cell resets, with the floppy-cell selection from board-register bit 15 [9] (§4.2, §5.1).
5. **The Cuda and the DIMMs**: VIA port setup, Cuda handshake, one undocumented Cuda pseudo-command, the SPD reads over the Cuda's I2C bus, the memory-controller programming (§2.3) [9].
6. **Clocks and cache**: bus/CPU/timebase frequencies from the board register and `HID1` (§5.4); L2CR from the module ID (§2.2) [9].
7. **Open Firmware, called as a subroutine.** The boot program calls the OF bundle's entry with the info records and `PA_OpenFirmware = $00400000`; Open Firmware runs — probing PCI, instantiating the mac-io children from its own FCode images, building the tree, `probe-all → install-console → banner → boot`, with the built-in `boot /AAPL,ROM` handing control back to the ROM's 68k half — and returns [9], [10] (*observed*). The startup conformance is the same IEEE 1275 sequence the TNT firmware claims ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.7; [11]).
8. **NanoKernel, then 68k.** On OF's return the boot program drops translation and enters the NanoKernel with the four info records — the entry contract of the TNT NanoKernel [9] (*observed*). The 68k emulator then runs the Toolbox half: the single product record, the All-in-One flag read, the Name Registry built from the OF tree, the native libraries (`heathrow_ata`, the ATI ndrv, `InterruptTreeHeathrow`, `MotherBoardHAL`, ATA Manager, SCSI Manager 4.3), the boot beep, and the classic Start Manager path. The Start Manager behaviours recorded for TNT — the PRAM-default-first boot-device search and its roughly thirty-second spin-up wait ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.7) — are 68k-side and carry over unchanged (*inferred - unverified*).

The order in step 7–8 — hardware init → **Open Firmware** → NanoKernel → 68k — is read directly from the Gossamer boot program's call sequence, and it revises the TNT family doc's stated order: the identical call construction exists in the TNT image, so the Gossamer decode is the better reading of both ROMs [9] (*observed* here; the TNT half remains to be re-decoded — §7 item 11).

Boot-time firmware behaviours guests depend on, all *observed* on real machines [10]: `use-nvramrc?` defaults to **false**, so with factory NVRAM no user script runs; `load-base` defaults to `$4000`; holding Cmd-Opt-O-F at the keyboard stops the startup at the Open Firmware prompt; `diag-switch?` selects a floppy-based diagnostics loader (`fd:diags`); and the `/options` variable `pci-probe-list` (default `-1`) masks which PCI device numbers the firmware probes at all — clearing bit 18 makes the firmware skip the on-board ATI entirely.

## 3. Memory map & address decode shared by the family

### 3.1 The physical map: Grackle address map B

The map is not Apple's invention: Grackle is a merchant part implementing the CHRP "address map B", selected by the `DBG0` configuration pin at reset and read-back writable as `PICR1[ADDRESS_MAP]` [6] §3.1.2. Map B divides the 4 GB processor space into system memory, PCI memory, PCI I/O and ROM [6] Table 3-4. The table below merges the manual's map with what the Gossamer firmware actually places in each range — the `ranges` and `translations` properties are from a real machine's Open Firmware [10] (*observed*), the semantics from [6]:

| Processor range | Size | Grackle meaning | What the board puts there |
|---|---|---|---|
| `$00000000` – RAM top | ≤ 1 GB | system memory, eight banks programmed by start/end/enable registers [6] §3.2.6 | SDRAM, sized from SPD and made contiguous by the boot program |
| `$40000000` – `$7FFFFFFF` | 1 GB | reserved: a memory-select error if enabled, else reads as all-ones and writes dropped [6] Table 3-4 note | nothing |
| `$80000000` – `$FCFFFFFF` | 2000 MB | PCI memory, forwarded 1:1 | every BAR the firmware assigns: Heathrow at `$F3000000`, the on-board ATI, slot cards |
| `$FD000000` – `$FDFFFFFF` | 16 MB | PCI/ISA memory: forwarded with the top 8 bits cleared, to PCI memory `0x00000000`–`0x00FFFFFF` [6] Table 3-4 note | published in the host node's `ranges` [10] (*observed*); unused by the on-board devices |
| `$FE000000` – `$FE7FFFFF` | 8 MB | PCI/ISA I/O: forwarded to PCI I/O `0x000000`–`0x7FFFFF` [6] | the ATI's I/O BAR lands here [10] (*observed*) |
| `$FEC00000` – `$FEDFFFFF` | 2 MB | `CONFIG_ADDR`, every word aliased [6] §7.4.5.2 | the PCI configuration address port (§3.2) |
| `$FEE00000` – `$FEEFFFFF` | 1 MB | `CONFIG_DATA`, every word aliased [6] | the PCI configuration data port (§3.2) |
| `$FEF00000` – `$FEFFFFFF` | 1 MB | PCI interrupt acknowledge: reads generate an INTA cycle [6] | published by the firmware but nothing on the board answers it [10] (*observed*) |
| `$FF000000` – `$FF7FFFFF` | 8 MB | ROM bank 1 (`RCS1`), always 64-bit wide [6] | the **board-register page** at its base (§3.4) |
| `$FF800000` – `$FFFFFFFF` | 8 MB | ROM bank 0 (`RCS0`) | the 4 MB ROM DIMM at `$FFC00000` (§3.5); the lower half is used only as a logical alias for Open Firmware |

The whole arrangement is published by the firmware in one property: the host bridge's `ranges` carries exactly the three forwarding windows (PCI memory 1:1 from `$80000000`, the ISA hole from `$FD000000`, PCI I/O from `$FE000000`), and a boot log on a real machine reports the same three [10] (*observed*). The contrast with TNT's fixed bridge slices ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §3.1–§3.2) is total: there are no `$F2000000`/`$F4000000` bridge windows, because there is no second bridge and no third.

### 3.2 PCI configuration mechanism and the fixed device numbers

Configuration cycles use the CHRP indirect scheme [6] §7.4.5.2: write a 32-bit configuration word anywhere in `$FEC00000`–`$FEDFFFFF`, then read or write the datum at `$FEE00000`–`$FEEFFFFF`, byte, halfword or word. Both ports are little-endian; the word is the PCI 2.1 format, identical for bus 0 and remote buses:

```
$80000000 | bus << 16 | device << 11 | function << 8 | (register & 0xFC)
```

For type-0 cycles "the MPC106 translates the device number field of CONFIG_ADDR into a unique IDSEL signal for up to 21 different devices. Each device connects its IDSEL input to one of the AD[31-11] signals" [6] §7.4.5 — device *n* asserts AD[*n*], which is why no useful device exists below device 11 on this bus. The host bridge itself is device 0.

The devices the firmware knows about, with the numbers and interrupt lines it assigns (*observed* in a real machine's tree and in the ROM's literal pools [9], [10]):

| Device | IDSEL | What it is | IDs | Interrupt (Heathrow source) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `$00` | — | Grackle, the host bridge | `$1057:0002`, revision `$40`; `compatible "grackle"` | — |
| `$0C` | AD12 | the PERCH slot, as a PCI device | the personality card's own ID, if any | source `$1C` |
| `$0D` | AD13 | PCI slot **A1** | card | source `$17` |
| `$0E` | AD14 | PCI slot **B1** | card | source `$18` |
| `$0F` | AD15 | PCI slot **C1** | card | source `$19` |
| `$10` | AD16 | **Heathrow** — `mac-io@10` | `$106B:0010`, revision 1, class `$FF0000` | source `$15` |
| `$12` | AD18 | the on-board ATI graphics controller — "slot F1" | `$1002:4754` (Rage II+, Rev A) or `$1002:4750` (Rage Pro, Rev C) | source `$16` |

The firmware probes exactly these device numbers, in a fixed order encoded in its literals [9] (*observed*), and the `/options` variable `pci-probe-list` masks them bit by bit (§2.7).

### 3.3 BAR placement

Nothing below the PCI-bridge boundary has a fixed address except one: **Heathrow's BAR0 is `$F3000000`, 512 KB, non-prefetchable**, written there by the ROM's boot program before Open Firmware runs and republished identically by both firmware generations [9], [10] (*observed*). The address is a convention the firmware itself maintains — the same number Grand Central's window occupies on TNT ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §3.1) — but unlike TNT, where the bridge slice fixed it, here it is only the value the boot program writes into a perfectly ordinary PCI BAR, and it would move if the firmware moved it.

Everything else is assigned by Open Firmware at boot, and the assignments differ by firmware generation (*observed* on real machines [10]):

| Device | Open Firmware 2.0f1 (Rev A/B) | Open Firmware 2.4 (Rev C) |
|---|---|---|
| On-board ATI, framebuffer aperture | `$82000000` | `$A1000000` |
| On-board ATI, registers | `$80020000` (reassigned by the OS) | `$A0001000` |
| On-board ATI, I/O | PCI I/O `$0400` | not assigned |
| First slot card memory | — | allocated upward from `$80800000` |
| First slot card I/O | — | allocated upward from `$0800` |

The lesson is the family-level contract: a re-implementation must let the firmware assign addresses and must not hard-code the TNT framebuffer convention ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §3.3). The standard assignment flow — the device's FCode or the firmware sizing the BARs, the firmware writing `assigned-addresses` — is [pci.md](../../hardware/pci/pci.md) §4's subject.

### 3.4 The board-register page at $FF000000

A 4 KB page of Gossamer-specific registers decoded by the board's ROM-select logic in Grackle's ROM bank 1 space (§3.1) — the one address region on the machine that is neither RAM, ROM nor a PCI device. The firmware maps it permanently cache-inhibited and guarded (`translations` carries `ff000000 00001000 ff000000 00000028`) [10] (*observed*), and the NanoKernel's page-map template extends the 1:1 I/O segment sixteen pages beyond TNT's, to `$FF00FFFF`, precisely so this page is reachable from the 68k half [9] (*observed*).

Only one register is established: the halfword at `+$4` (§2.6). Whether anything decodes elsewhere in the page — and what the upper halfword at `+$6` reads — is not attested (§7 item 6).

### 3.5 The ROM decode

The ROM DIMM is decoded at `$FFC00000`, 4 MB, 64-bit wide, inside Grackle's ROM bank 0 [6] §3.1.2; the processor's hardware-reset vector `$FFF00100` lies inside the decode, so the first fetch of the cold boot is a ROM access served by Grackle (§2.7 step 1). The bank is 8 MB wide in the map while the module is 4 MB; whether `$FF800000`–`$FFBFFFFF` aliases the ROM or floats is open (§7 item 6). Open Firmware's *running* image is not here: the firmware copies itself to RAM at physical `$00400000` and executes from logical `$FF800000` — that megabyte is withheld from `/memory available` [10] (*observed*).

The image layout is §1.3's table; the component entry the boot program uses is `ConfigInfo`, the one-page record at `+$30D000`:

| ConfigInfo field | Gossamer value | TNT value ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §3.5) |
|---|---|---|
| `BootstrapVersion` | `Boot Gossamer 0.` | `Boot TNT 0.1p` |
| `HWInitCodeOffset`/`Size` | `+$320000` / **0** | `+$330000` / `$10000` |
| `OpenFWBundleOffset`/`Size` | `+$320000` / `$30000` (192 KB) | `+$330000` / `$20000` (128 KB) |
| `LA_InterruptCtl` | **`$F3000028`** — Heathrow's Clear1, not the window base | `$F3000000` |
| `InterruptHandlerKind` | **7** — a Heathrow handler this NanoKernel build adds | 2 |
| `LA_OpenFirmware` / `PA_OpenFirmware` | `$FF800000` / `$00400000` | same |
| `LA_KernelData` / `LA_EmulatorData` / `LA_DispatchTable` / `LA_EmulatorCode` / `LA_InfoRecord` / `LA_HardwarePriv` | `$68FFE000` / `$68FFF000` / `$68080000` / `$68060000` / `$5FFFE000` / `$FFF0C000` | same |
| Low-memory seeds | `$01D4 = $F3016000` — VIA1 at Heathrow `+$16000` | — |

### 3.6 The logical (MMU) map

The NanoKernel builds the initial translation from ConfigInfo's BAT and page-map templates [9] (*observed*). The kernel-owned logical constants are the table above's; the two template differences that matter against TNT ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §3.5) are the attribute bits:

- **every memory-coherency (M) bit TNT set is cleared** — RAM, ROM, the kernel and emulator pages, the BAT write-through bits — and **every I/O mapping gains the guarded (G) bit** alongside cache-inhibited (I). That is exactly what a uniprocessor 750 wants: no snooping traffic for M, and G to stop the 750's speculative loads from touching device registers [9] (*observed*; the rationale is *inferred - unverified* — no Apple document states it).
- The 1:1 I/O segment is `$F0000000`–`$FF00FFFF`, sixteen pages larger than TNT's, to cover the board-register page (§3.4).

Open Firmware's own view, published as the `/cpus` node's `translations` and inherited by client programs [10] (*observed*): the mac-io window at `$F3000000` (16 MB, 1:1, inhibited+guarded), the PCI I/O base `$FE000000`, both config ports, the board-register page, one megabyte of RAM at `$00400000` seen at logical `$FF800000`, and the ROM at `$FFC00000` cacheable. The `/memory` map withholds the exception-vector pages `$0000`–`$3FFF`, `$8000`–`$9FFF` and OF's own megabyte — the low pages hold the exception vectors and the info records the boot program built (§2.7) [9], [10] (*observed*).

### 4.1 The north: Grackle

Grackle is the one chip that does what Hammerhead and Bandit did on TNT ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §4.1–§4.2): "The Grackle IC functions as the bridge between the PowerPC microprocessor bus and the I/O and graphics devices on the PCI bus. It provides buffering and address translation from one bus to the other" [1] §"Grackle IC" p. 44, and it is the memory and ROM controller (§2.3). Unlike Hammerhead and Bandit — Apple customs whose register files exist only in ROM code and driver headers ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §7 item 2) — Grackle is a merchant part with a published user's manual [6], which makes it the best-documented part of any Power Macintosh platform to date.

At family level four things matter. **Its configuration registers are PCI configuration registers** at device 0, reached only through the two ports of §3.2 — `PICR1` (`$A8`) and `PICR2` (`$AC`) [6] Table 3-35, the memory bank start/end/enable registers at `$80`–`$A0` [6] §3.2.6, and `MCCR1`–`MCCR4` at `$F0`–`$FC` [6] §3.2.6.4. **The boot program writes them before anything else**: the 750's processor type into `PICR1`, the ROM timing into `MCCR1`, and the SDRAM mode fields from the SPD-derived timing tables [9] (*observed*). **Its error behaviour defines what a stray access does**: the reserved 1 GB raises a memory-select error if enabled, else reads return all ones and writes are dropped [6] Table 3-4 note 2 — the behaviour the 68k legacy identification routine's stray probes rely on (§2.6). And **its configuration pin selects the whole map**: `DBG0` sampled at reset chooses map B, readable back as `PICR1[ADDRESS_MAP]` [6] §3.1.2 — the one pin a re-implementation must model as strapped.

### 4.2 The I/O controller: Heathrow

"The Heathrow IC is an I/O controller and DMA engine for Power Macintosh computers using the PCI bus architecture. Heathrow also provides power-management control functions for energy saving features" [1] §"Heathrow IC" p. 45. It is the Grand Central architecture's successor — the same cell set, the same DBDMA engines, the same interrupt-controller design — with four family-defining changes:

1. **It is a real PCI function** (`$106B:0010`, revision 1, class `$FF0000`), with a header, a BAR and a command register, occupying device `$10` [10] (*observed*) — where Grand Central sits behind a Bandit bridge with no PCI identity of its own ([grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §1.3).
2. **Its window is 512 KB** (BAR0), four times Grand Central's, with the ATA channels and the NVRAM aperture living in the space Grand Central did not have [10] (*observed*).
3. **Its interrupt controller has two banks**: the Grand Central register set at `+$20`–`+$2C` serves sources 0–31, and a second bank *below* it at `+$10`–`+$1C` serves sources 32–63 (§5.1).
4. **It is gated by a Feature Control Register** (`+$38`), a clock/cell-enable register with no Grand Central equivalent, which the boot program, Mac OS and every other OS program at boot and at sleep/wake [9] (*observed*). The bit names are the firmware's own, published in the device tree's method list: `reset_scc`, `slow_scc_pclk`, the IDE and media-bay resets, the cell enables for SCC, sound, SWIM3/clone-floppy/MFDC, SCSI and IOBus, `floppy_en`, `ide_int_en`, `-mb_pwr`, `in_use_led` [10] (*observed*). The boot program's reset sequence writes `$033EFF3A` then `$01BEFFFA`, and the floppy-cell selection adds a third write on MFDC boards [9] (*observed*).

Its DMA engine is unchanged DBDMA — the channel model, command set and state machine of [dbdma.md](../tnt/dbdma.md) §1–§4 — with thirteen channel blocks at `+$8000 + n·$100`: MESH 0, SWIM3 1, BMAC transmit/receive 2/3, ESCC A tx/rx 4/5, ESCC B tx/rx 6/7, audio out/in 8/9, ATA 0/1 at 11/12 [10] (*observed*). One inherited rule breaks here: on TNT, "a DBDMA channel's number is its interrupt number" ([dbdma.md](../tnt/dbdma.md) §1.4; [tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §5.1) — on Heathrow the channel numbering and the interrupt sources decouple (§5.1), and the ATA channels' block index (11/12) is not their interrupt number.

The Heathrow control block (`+$00`–`+$3F`) also carries the media-bay control register at `+$34` and, at `+$32`/`+$33`, two 8-bit registers the All-in-One's built-in display uses for brightness and contrast — the firmware's helper words and the factory NVRAM script write them on All-in-One machines only [10] (*observed*). Both facts matter to a re-implementation: the control block must accept byte and halfword writes, not just 32-bit ones.

### 4.3 The cells inherited from Grand Central

Four Heathrow cells are Grand Central's silicon moved, and their register files live in the TNT page:

- **MESH** — the SCSI cell, now at `+$10000` (Grand Central's `+$18000`), publishing `clock-frequency 50000000` as before [10] (*observed*). One behavioural delta: the note states "The SCSI controller cell in the Heathrow IC is an 8-bit MESH controller. The MESH cell supports data transfer rates of 5 MB per second" [1] §"Heathrow IC" p. 45 — half of TNT's documented 10 MB/s internal rate ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §4.3). The internal and external connectors "share the same SCSI bus", the external connector (25-pin) "supports a maximum data transfer rate of 5 MB per second", and "the internal end of the SCSI bus is terminated by an active terminator ... located on the main logic board" [1] §"SCSI Bus" p. 52.
- **The ESCC** — the 85C30 SCC cell, "an 8-bit device. The SCC circuitry supports serial and LocalTalk protocols" [1] p. 45, with the two apertures TNT documents: the 68k-style compatibility aperture at `+$12000` (the decoder table's address) and the MacRISC aperture at `+$13000`, channel A at `+$13020` [10] (*observed*).
- **SWIM3** — the floppy cell at `+$15000`, "a SWIM3 floppy drive controller" [1] p. 45, driving one internal high-density SuperDrive on a 20-pin cable [1] §"Floppy Disk Drive" p. 51. The decoder table publishes the same base the 68k Toolbox side uses [9] (*observed*).
- **The VIA/Cuda transport** — the VIA cell at `+$16000`, 8 KB, registers at a `+$200` stride, fronting the Cuda microcontroller [10] (*observed*); the Cuda holds "management of soft system resets; warm reset is controlled by the ADB cell in the Heathrow IC; management of the real-time clock; software control of the power supply" [1] §"Cuda IC" p. 46. The ADB itself is "functionally the same as on other Macintosh computers" [1] §"ADB Port" p. 50.

### 4.4 The new cells: ATA, BMAC and the NVRAM aperture

Three Heathrow cells have no Grand Central ancestor:

- **The ATA channels** — "IDE hard disk interface controller" [1] p. 45, two of them at `+$20000` and `+$21000`, published as `ide@20000`/`ide@21000` with `AAPL,bus-id` 0 and 1 [10] (*observed*). The implementation is a subset of "the ATA interface specification, ANSI proposal X3T9.2/90-143, Revision 3.1" [1] §"ATA Hard Disk" p. 51 — ATA-2, with "PIO, singleword DMA, and multiword DMA data transfers" [1] §"CD-ROM Drive" p. 34. The task-file registers sit at a 16-byte stride (`+$00` data through `+$70` status/command), the alternate status/device control at `+$160`, an Apple timing-configuration register at `+$200` [10] (*observed*); each channel has its own DBDMA block (§4.2) and interrupt source (§5.1). The original ROM supports one device per channel; the ATA Device 0/1 master/slave specification arrives with the enhanced boards' ROM [1] §"Design Enhancements" p. 19 — a ROM software limit, not a cell limit (§6.5).
- **BMAC** — the 10Base-T Ethernet cell at `+$11000` [10] (*observed*), replacing Grand Central's MACE ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §4.3). The port is an RJ-45 10Base-T [1] p. 16 — the AAUI connector of the TNT generation is gone — with DBDMA transmit and receive channels 2 and 3 [10] (*observed*).
- **The NVRAM aperture** — "8 KB nonvolatile RAM control" [1] p. 45, at `+$60000`: byte *n* of the store at `+$60000 + 16·n`, a sparse 128 KB region for 8 KB of content [10] (*observed*). The store carries the PRAM, the Open Firmware environment and the diagnostics state; on TNT this lives behind Grand Central's bank-select apertures ([grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §2.6) — on Gossamer it is one flat byte-wide window, and the firmware reads and writes it directly.

### 4.5 The personality-card parts: Screamer and Burgundy

"The Screamer custom IC combines a waveform amplifier with a 16-bit digital sound encoder and decoder (codec). The Screamer IC supports all of the audio input and output features on the Audio/Video and Audio input/output cards. The Screamer IC provides improved audio performance over the AWACS IC used in previous Macintosh computers" [1] §"Screamer Sound IC" p. 46 — the codec-law descendant of the TNT machines' AWACS ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §4.3; [awacs.md](../pdm/awacs.md) §1). "The Burgundy custom IC combines a waveform amplifier with an internal 18-bit digital sound codec (only 16 bits supported for analog to digital/digital to analog sound capture and playback)" and serves the DVD-Video and Audio/Video card [1] §"Burgundy Sound IC" p. 46. Both are reached through the Heathrow DAVbus at `+$14000`, whose device-tree node publishes `compatible "awacs", "screamer"` [10] (*observed*) — the same register geometry, a superset codec, and a device-tree identity that deliberately claims AWACS compatibility.

The audio plumbing's contract is Apple's own: "The Screamer IC ... provides separate sound buffers for input and for stereo output so that the computer can record and send digitized sound to the sound outputs simultaneously. The Burgundy IC provides the sound buffers on Power Macintosh G3 configurations with the Apple DVD-Video and Audio/Video Card" [1] §"Digitizing Sound" p. 54, with the input and output sample rates "physically locked together" [1] p. 54. The sound ports' electricals are the cards': line input 10 kΩ, 2 Vpp maximum [1] §"Sound Input Specifications" p. 54; 16-bit stereo in and out at 11.025, 22.05 and 44.1 kHz on the Screamer cards, 44.1 kHz only on the Burgundy card [1] §"Audio Input and Output" p. 31.

### 4.6 The on-board graphics

The display controller is merchant silicon soldered to the logic board, occupying PCI device `$12`: "an ATI 3D RAGE II+DVD graphics controller ... used on systems with the V4.0F2 ROM, an ATI 3D RAGE PRO graphics controller is used on systems with the V4.5F1 and V4.5F2 ROM" [1] §"Custom ICs" p. 44. It is a complete 2D/3D accelerator — "display memory controller, built-in drawing coprocessor, video scaler, color space converter, clock generator, and true color palette video DAC", DDC1 and DDC2B+ monitor sensing, a 64×64 hardware cursor [1] §"Graphics Controller" p. 47 — and the note names its generational improvement precisely: the enhanced boards carry the RAGE PRO "and 100 MHz graphics bus" [1] §"All-in-one Features" p. 18, and support the 1920×1080 display mode the RAGE II boards do not [1] §"Built-in Display Video" p. 56.

The framebuffer is its own memory, not main RAM: "The display memory on the main logic board is separate from the main memory. The display memory consists of 2 MB of 100 MHz/10ns SGRAM devices configured to make a 64-bit data bus. The display memory can be expanded to 4 MB or 6 MB with a 2 MB or 4 MB SGRAM SO-DIMM card" [1] §"Display RAM (Video RAM)" p. 48 — the JEDEC MO-190 144-pin graphics SO-DIMM [1] §"Video RAM SO-DIMMs" p. 68. The only display mode 6 MB adds over 4 MB is 32-bit colour at 1280×1024 at 60 Hz [1] §"Expanding Video RAM" p. 68; the rest of the expansion's value is 3D texture storage [1] p. 35.

Family-level wiring facts a re-implementation needs (*observed* on real machines [10]): the device carries `AAPL,slot-name "F1"` — a "slot" that is soldered down; its interrupt is Heathrow source `$16`; its BARs land as §3.3's table; and the firmware's helper words (`is-all-in-one-gossamer`, `enable-gossamer-monitor-sense`, `enable-gossamer-termination`) poke the board-level monitor-sense and termination lines around it [9]. The All-in-One's internal monitor is this controller's RGB routed through the personality card (§2.4), with mirror output to the external DB-15 up to 1024×768 at 16 bits [1] §"All-in-one Features" p. 18; the external connector's pin assignments are the Power Macintosh 5500/6500's [1] §"External Video Connector" p. 56.

## 5. Interrupt, bus, and clock architecture

### 5.1 Interrupts: two banks, sixty-four sources

The interrupt controller is inside Heathrow, and it is Grand Central's design plus a second bank — sixty-four sources, one output to the 750's external-interrupt pin. Eight little-endian registers head the window [10] (*observed*; the register set is the Grand Central lineage's, [grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §2.2):

| Offset | Register | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| `+$20` / `+$24` / `+$28` / `+$2C` | Events1 / Mask1 / Clear1 / Levels1 | 0–31 |
| `+$10` / `+$14` / `+$18` / `+$1C` | Events2 / Mask2 / Clear2 / Levels2 | 32–63 — the second bank sits *below* the first |

Source *N* is bit *N & 31* of bank *N >> 5*. The board's wiring, every value *observed* from real machines' `AAPL,interrupts` properties and the ROM's device-to-line literal pairs [9], [10]:

| Source | Device | DBDMA completion bits |
|---|---|---|
| `$0C` | MESH SCSI | bit `$00` |
| `$0D` / `$0E` | ATA channel 0 / channel 1 | **bits `$02` / `$03`** — not Ethernet as on Grand Central |
| `$0F` / `$10` | ESCC channel A / channel B | bits `$04`,`$05` / `$06`,`$07` |
| `$11` | DAVbus (Screamer) | bits `$08` / `$09` (out/in) |
| `$12` | VIA / Cuda | — |
| `$13` | SWIM3 floppy | bit `$01` |
| `$14` | NMI — the programmer's switch | — |
| `$15` / `$1A` | the PERCH card's two lines | — |
| `$16` | the on-board ATI (device `$12`) | — |
| `$17` / `$18` / `$19` | PCI slots A1 / B1 / C1 (devices `$0D`/`$0E`/`$0F`) | — |
| `$1C` | the PERCH slot as PCI device `$0C` | — |
| **`$2A`** | **BMAC Ethernet — the one bank-2 source** | **bits `$20` / `$21`, in bank 2** |

Two family-level rules come with that table. First, the TNT identity "a DBDMA channel's number is its interrupt number" ([dbdma.md](../tnt/dbdma.md) §1.4; [tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §5.1) is **broken twice over**: the ATA channels' DBDMA blocks are 11 and 12 while their sources are `$0D`/`$0E`, and BMAC's completions fold into bank 2. Second, the controller has two modes: the boot program writes `Mask1 = $80000000` before anything else, selecting the 68k-compatible mode the NanoKernel's kind-7 handler expects [9] (*observed*); native operating systems run the other mode, where events latch on the rising edge and acknowledging a still-asserted DBDMA level must drop the held level so the channel can interrupt again [10] (*observed* at the driver level; which bit selects the mode is the working hypothesis — §7 item 2 — and the mode concept is Grand Central's, [grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §3.3). The kernel-side facts are ConfigInfo's: `LA_InterruptCtl = $F3000028` — the Clear1 register — and `InterruptHandlerKind = 7` [9] (*observed*).

### 5.2 The processor bus

The 60x bus at 66.82 MHz with split address and data tenures (§2.1) carries two agents: the 750 masters, Grackle answers. There is no arbiter to document beyond Grackle's internal logic, no snooping (the L2 is private to the 750), no multiprocessor doorbell ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §5.2 contrast), and no parity checking beyond Grackle's own internal paths [6]. The bus clock comes from the board, not the module — the inversion of TNT's card-supplied bus clock ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.2): the board register's bus-speed code (§2.6) fixes 66.82 MHz for every module, and the module only selects the core multiplier through `HID1` [9] (*observed*).

### 5.3 PCI arbitration

Arbitration for the one PCI bus is inside Heathrow: the first entry of its function list is a "gated clocks PCI bus arbiter" [1] §"Heathrow IC" p. 45 — where TNT needed "a separate logic device (gate array)" for slot priorities ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §5.3). The practical consequence is structural: one 33 MHz bus carries Heathrow's thirteen DBDMA channels (each mastering the bus on its device's behalf, [grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §1.3), the on-board ATI's framebuffer traffic, the PERCH card and the three slots — the bandwidth budget every machine in §6 shares, and the reason the Server G3's disk and network cards (§6.4) are on the same bus as everything else.

### 5.4 Clocks

One board clock, one code, and everything else is a ratio:

| Clock | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|

| Core clock | bus × 3.5 / 4 / 4.5 / 5 = 233.87 / 267.28 / 300.69 / 334.10 MHz | module PLL, `HID1[PLL_CFG]` ([8] §7.2.11.3); 267,280,000 Hz measured exactly on a real 266 MHz machine [10] (*observed*) |
| Back-side L2 | core ÷ 2 | [1] pp. 14, 43 |
| Time base and decrementer | bus ÷ 4 = **16,705,000 Hz** (`$00FEE5E8`) | [10] (*observed*); "the decrementer register is decremented and the time base is incremented at a speed that is one-fourth the speed of the bus clock" [8] §2.1 implementation note |
| PCI bus | 33 MHz — published as `$01FCA055` = 33,333,333 Hz | [1] pp. 44, 69; [10] (*observed*; the same literal the TNT bridge nodes publish, [pm9500.md](../tnt/pm9500.md) §3.4) |

### 5.5 Reset and power

Reset authority is the Cuda's: "management of soft system resets; warm reset is controlled by the ADB cell in the Heathrow IC; management of the real-time clock; software control of the power supply" [1] §"Cuda IC" p. 46, reached only through the Heathrow VIA transport ([grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §3.5). Soft power is from the keyboard [1] p. 16. The supply delivers 161 W continuous (210 W peak for 12 seconds at startup) at +5 V/18.3 A, +3.3 V/12.5 A, +12 V/6.2 A, −12 V/0.4 A, no more than 104 W combined on the 5 V and 3.3 V rails [1] §"DC Power" p. 61, from a manually selected 100–120 or 200–240 V AC input [1] §"AC Line Input" p. 60; the monitor socket is unswitched [1] §"Power Supply" p. 34. The fan is thermally controlled [1] p. 16, with the tower's baffle for one or two auxiliary 60 mm fans at up to 0.2 A each [1] §"Auxiliary Cooling Fans" p. 71. ADB devices draw at most 500 mA total, three devices maximum [1] p. 61.

## 7. Open questions

1. **The board register's unconsumed bits.** Bits 13–8 (the PCI slot PRSNT# pairs) and bit 0 (a "Bose audio module" detect) have no reader in the ROM's boot program, and the firmware's `prsntbits` word has not been decompiled; only their positions are reconstructed, from secondary sources (*unverified*).
2. **The interrupt-controller mode bit.** Mask1 bit 31 as the mode select is the working hypothesis — the boot program writes `Mask1 = $80000000` before anything else, and native operating systems run the other mode with edge-latched events — but the TNT family documentation records a different selection mechanism for the same controller design ([grand-central.md](../tnt/grand-central.md) §3.3), and the unpublished Apple ERS that would settle it is not in evidence.
3. **The SPD-to-register mapping.** The boot program's memory bring-up reads the DIMMs' SPD EEPROMs over the Cuda's I2C bus and programs Grackle's bank and mode registers, but the byte-to-field mapping — which SPD bytes select which `MCCR` fields for which bus-speed code — is not decoded from the boot program.
4. **The memory ceiling.** Apple's 1998 developer note says 768 MB [1] p. 14; the Server G3 note [2] and the All-in-One service source [5] say 384 MB, the latter limiting DIMMs to 64 MB; real machines run 768 MB [10]; Grackle's ceiling is 1 GB [6]. Which document mis-states which, and whether the 384 MB figures are early-module-availability limits, is unresolved.
5. **Rev B and Rev D.** Neither ROM image is in evidence; their header checksums and the Rev B/Rev D properties in §6.5 are secondary-attested. Whether Rev D ever shipped in a product is unknown.
6. **The board-register page's extent.** Only the halfword at `+$4` is attested; what the rest of the 4 KB page decodes, and what `$FF800000`–`$FFBFFFFF` (the lower half of Grackle's ROM bank 0) returns on a 4 MB DIMM, are unprobed.
7. **The legacy 68k identification routine.** It probes `$5FFFFFFC`, `$F8000000`, `$F301A000` and other earlier-platform registers; whether it executes on a real G3, and what those addresses return on real hardware, is unlogged. The Open Firmware `translations` do map 4 KB at `$F8000000` [10] (*observed*), suggesting something is expected there.
8. **The Cuda.** The part number and firmware version quoted in secondary sources are not established from primary evidence in this corpus; and the boot program issues one undocumented Cuda pseudo-command (`$26`, three unit parameters) whose function is unknown [9] (*observed* that it is sent).
9. **The PERCH electrical specification.** Apple states it is not published [1] p. 15 and that third-party cards are unsupported [1] p. 63; the personality cards' identity EEPROM contents beyond the `Whisper` sample, and the Wings/Bordeaux codenames themselves, are unverified.
10. **The Heathrow control block's unknowns.** Register `+$30` has no documented reader; the media-bay control register's bit fields beyond the ID lanes, the aux control register's bits, and the polarity of two feature-control bits the name-derived reading contradicts, are all unestablished.
11. **The TNT boot order.** The Gossamer boot program calls Open Firmware before entering the NanoKernel, and the same call construction appears to exist in the TNT image; the TNT family doc's stated order ([tnt.md](../tnt/tnt.md) §2.7) has not been re-verified against this finding.
12. **Two clock details.** The ESCC PCLK (3.6864 MHz assumed, 3.672 MHz claimed in secondary sources, no ROM value published), and the published-versus-real PCI rate: Grackle's 2:1 PLL mode on a 66.82 MHz bus implies 33.41 MHz real against the published 33,333,333 Hz — a 0.24 percent difference no software measures.
13. **Which SCSI controller the Server G3's Ultra Wide card carries.** Apple names the card's capability (40 MB/s, 16-bit, three internal devices [2]) but not its silicon; no claim is made here.
