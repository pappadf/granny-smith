# Power Macintosh 9500/132

The Power Macintosh 9500 is the maximum configuration of Apple's TNT platform — the tower at the top of the 1995 PCI Power Macintosh line: a PowerPC 604 at 120 or 132 MHz on the replaceable processor card, twelve DIMM slots, a soldered 512 KB second-level cache, and the 1995 generation's only six-slot PCI backplane, served by two Bandit bridges. It is also the only TNT machine with no video hardware of its own: a display card in a PCI slot is a requirement, not an option [1] §"Video Display Card" p. 13.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

The 9500 is one logic board shipped in two speed grades, distinguished only by the processor card and the factory load-out: the **9500/120** (PowerPC 604 at 120 MHz, 16 MB of RAM) and the **9500/132** (604 at 132 MHz, 32 MB), the subject of this page's title [1] §"Appearance and Features" pp. 2–3, Table 1-2 p. 5. Apple's developer note covers both in one document and treats them as one computer [1] §"About This Developer Note" p. ix; the same board later carried faster cards in the 1996 speed-bump builds, which no document in evidence describes (§6.3).

Everything board-level — the ASIC set, the shared memory map, the interrupt, bus and clock architecture, the boot ROM image — is the TNT platform and is documented once in the family doc ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2–§5). This page carries only what is unique to the 9500, and cites the family doc rather than restating it. The one-sentence shape is Apple's own: "The architecture of the Power Macintosh 9500 computers is based on three buses: the processor bus and two PCI buses. The processor bus connects the microprocessor and the memory; the PCI buses connect the expansion slots and the I/O devices" [1] §"Main ICs and Subsystems" p. 8 — and the 9500 is the machine where all three are fully populated.

### 1.4 Factory configurations

The standard configurations at the time of the developer note [1] Table 1-2 p. 5:

| Model | DRAM | Hard disk | L2 cache | Apple video card | Internal CD-ROM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power Macintosh 9500/120 | 16 MB | 1 GB | 512 KB | Yes | Yes |
| Power Macintosh 9500/132 | 32 MB | 2 GB | 512 KB | No | Yes |

The 16/32 MB factory load-outs arrive as two or four DIMMs respectively [1] §"Random-Access Memory" p. 9. The /132 ships without a video card by design: "The configuration that does not include the Apple video card is intended for users who wish to install a third-party video card", and any such card must comply with the PCI Local Bus Specification rev 2.0 and "include startup ROM that conforms to the IEEE Standard 1275" [1] §"Configurations and Options" p. 5.

One ceiling is stated twice and differs between the sentences: the maximum installable RAM is 1.5 GB, "the maximum memory size supported by the current Mac OS is 1 GB" [1] note to Table 1-1 p. 5 — the 1995 system software's limit, not the hardware's.

### 1.5 The tower enclosure

The enclosure is the 8100's tower stretched by about three inches, giving the two extra internal storage bays and the six-card backplane [1] §"Appearance and Features" p. 2. The internal drive complement the case supports, beyond the factory floppy and hard disk, is "one 5.25-inch, one or two 3.5-inch" devices [1] Table 1-1 p. 4. Back-panel furniture: the two serial ports, the ADB port, the sound input and output jacks, the 25-pin external SCSI connector, and the Ethernet connectors [1] §"I/O Ports" p. 18, §"Ethernet Port" p. 20. The machine's power supply as a whole is not rated anywhere in the developer note — only the 90 W slot budget of §4.1 is given (§6.13).

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The platform's clock architecture is tnt.md §5.4's; the card-supplied bus clock is tnt.md §2.2's. This machine's column of the table:

Two remarks. First, the 604 manual pins the time-base law — "the time base frequency is 1/4th of the bus clock frequency (as is the decrementer frequency)" [5] §1 p. 1-24 — which makes the /132's time base 11 MHz; the family doc's open question about what the ROM publishes as `timebase-frequency` (tnt.md §7 item 5) is therefore answerable from the hardware side even though the firmware literal remains unisolated (§6.12). Second, a live 9500's root node publishes `clock-frequency 02FAF080` — 50 MHz — which the technote's author identifies as "the frequency of the AR bus" [4] §"Properties and Methods of the Node /" p. 4. Neither 120 nor 132 MHz is one-third of 50, so the sample machine is a later speed grade on the same board, not a /120 or /132 (*inferred — unverified*; §6.3).

### 2.3 The twelve-slot memory array

The family memory contract — 8-byte DIMMs, contiguity manufactured by Hammerhead's bank base registers, the 128-bit interleave, the 2 GB address allocation — is tnt.md §2.3's and [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §3.2's. The 9500's part of it, in the developer note's own numbers:

- **Twelve slots, no soldered capacity**: "The computers have twelve DIMM slots that can provide up to 1.5 GB of main memory" [1] §"Random-Access Memory" p. 9, and "The Power Macintosh 9500 computers do not have any main memory soldered to the logic board. At least one RAM DIMM must be present for the computers to operate" [1] §"RAM DIMMs" p. 26. The module is the 8-byte DIMM — 64-bit data, 168 pins — mechanically per JEDEC MO-161 and electrically per JEDEC Standard 21-C, in a Burndy ELF168E5GC-3Z50 (or equivalent) connector [1] p. 26.
- **Population is flexible**: "The 8-byte DIMMs can be installed one or more at a time" [1] p. 26 — unlike the paired-only rules of later Apple boards. Interleaving is opportunistic: same-size DIMMs "installed in corresponding slots" become a single 128-bit bank, and both DIMMs of a pair must also share an address-multiplexing mode [1] p. 26.
- **Bank geometry**: "The minimum bank size supported by the Hammerhead IC is 4 MB and the largest is 64 MB; the largest DIMM supported is a two-bank DIMM holding 128 MB" [1] p. 26. A DIMM is therefore one or two banks, and twelve slots is the array's outer bound; the note's interleave description names one pair ("adjacent banks (Bank 0–Bank 1)") as its example [1] §"Data Interleaving" p. 10.
- **Reachable sizes** [1] Table 4-1 p. 26: from 4 MB DIMMs (48 MB with all twelve populated) through 16 MB and 32 MB DIMMs to the 128 MB two-bank DIMM, twelve of which make the 1.5 GB ceiling.

The register-level detail — how the firmware sizes the slots, what it writes into the bank base file, the address-multiplexing modes — is §3.2's.

### 2.4 The soldered L2 cache

The family L2 contract is an **optional** SIMM whose size is sensed through two connector pins at startup, with pull-ups meaning "absent" (tnt.md §2.3). The 9500 is the exception the family doc names: its 512 KB second-level cache is built in — "a built-in second level (L2) cache comprising 512 KB of high-speed memory", a write-back, direct-mapped (single-set) cache with allocate on read or write, its tag store in standard SRAM and its data store "with synchronous burst SRAM devices that have an access time of 11 ns" [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 10.

Three consequences. The cache never needs sensing: on this machine the size is a board constant, and the firmware's L2 registers are programmed for one answer every boot. The cache is present in the firmware's own tree as a root-level node — `/l2-cache@0,0` appears in a real machine's Open Firmware listing [4] §"Viewing the Device Tree" p. 2 (*observed*). And the POST diagnostics object's `.L2DataCacheTest` runs against real silicon on every cold boot rather than against the "no SIMM" pull-up state — its register choreography (Hammerhead `+$E0` size decode, `+$F0` strobe) is [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8–§2.9, §4.4's.

### 2.5 No built-in video

The TNT video subsystem — Chaos, Control, RaDACal, the VRAM slots, Plan B capture (tnt.md §2.4) — is absent from this machine entire. The comparison table says so in the negative: VRAM lives "on PCI display card", 21-inch support "depends on PCI video card", and the DAV connector is "No" [1] Table 1-1 p. 4; the architecture chapter names no video ICs at all, and instead: "The Power Macintosh 9500 computers require an external monitor and a video display card installed in a PCI expansion slot" [1] §"Video Display Card" p. 13.

The firmware agrees with the board. The Open Firmware model decode is what it is on every TNT machine (tnt.md §2.6), but the 9500's identity answer produces a tree with **no display nodes at all** — no `chaos`, no `control` — which is the correct description of the hardware (*observed*; tnt.md §6.3). The one vestige is the alias table: a real 9500's `devalias` listing still carries `vci0 → /chaos@F0000000` even though no chaos node exists to resolve it against — the alias is dangling, and the firmware is content [4] §"Other Properties and Words" p. 5, §"Viewing the Device Tree" p. 2 (*observed*). What, if anything, decodes the Chaos bridge slice at `$F0000000`–`$F1FFFFFF` on a machine with no Chaos fitted is not established (§6.7; tnt.md §7 item 11).

Everything a display needs therefore comes over PCI: the card contract is §3.8's, and the boot-time consequences (no ROM mode-set of a built-in display; the startup screen drawn by the card's FCode boot driver) are §5's.

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 The processor card and the AR bus

The processor subsystem card is the family's — "a plug-in card that contains the PowerPC 604 microprocessor and its associated clock circuits", the upgrade path to a more advanced microprocessor [1] §"Processor Subsystem Card" p. 8 — and on this machine it supplies 40 or 44 MHz to the board (§2.2). The card carries the 604 whose unit inventory is §1.3's; the 68k half of the ROM identifies it through the PVR, and Open Firmware publishes the not-a-601 flag into `AAPL,cpu-id` [2]; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.4.

The AR bus is the family's multiple-master 60x bus (tnt.md §5.2; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §3.5–§3.6), and on this machine its agent set is: the 604, Hammerhead as the slave for RAM, ROM and the L2, and **two** Bandit bridges. The family roster's fourth agent — Chaos — does not exist here (§2.5); the 9500 is the one TNT machine on which the display bus has no bridge at all. The bus's coherency granularity (32 bytes, L2-forced L1 inclusion), parity-checked data path and exception behavior are [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §3.5–§3.6's, restated nowhere here.

### 3.2 Memory: the array and its configuration

The shared memory-subsystem behavior — bank base registers making addresses contiguous from `$00000000`, the 128-bit interleaved read into a 128-bit buffer transferred 64 bits at a time to the 64-bit system data bus, critical-word-first ordering (critical quadword for a 601, critical doubleword for the 603/604), CAS-before-RAS refresh — is tnt.md §2.3 and [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §3.2's, established largely from this machine's own developer note. The 9500-specific hardware contract, all from Chapter 4:

| Rule | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Module | 8-byte DIMM, 64-bit data, 168-pin; MO-161 mechanics, JEDEC 21-C electricals; Burndy ELF168E5GC-3Z50 connector | [1] §"RAM DIMMs" p. 26 |
| Minimum configuration | one DIMM — nothing is soldered | [1] p. 26 (IMPORTANT) |
| Pairing | DIMMs installed one or more at a time; same-size pair in corresponding slots → one 128-bit bank, provided both share the address mode | [1] p. 26 |
| Bank size | 4 MB minimum, 64 MB maximum; largest DIMM a two-bank 128 MB module | [1] p. 26 |
| DRAM devices | 1, 4, 16 or 64 Mbit, T_RAS ≤ 70 ns; no device needing more than 12 address bits | [1] §"RAM Devices" p. 31, §"RAM Address Multiplexing" p. 30 |
| Load limits | at most two devices per data line and eight per /RAS line on each DIMM | [1] §"RAM Devices" p. 31 (IMPORTANT) |
| Presence detect | PD(8:1), /PDE and ID(1:0) are **not used** — sizing cannot come from the connector's identity pins | [1] note to Table 4-3 p. 29; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §3.2 |
| Power on the connector | +5 V only — "No +3-V power is provided on the DIMM connector" | [1] note p. 29 |
| B(0) | the alternate-of-A(0) pin that would split the 64-bit bus into two 32-bit halves is tied to A(0) on the main logic board | [1] §"RAM DIMM Connectors" p. 29 |
| Address multiplexing | two modes, selected per bank by software writing the address-mode bits in the bank base registers during sizing; the row/column maps are the note's Tables 4-4 and 4-5 | [1] §"RAM Address Multiplexing" p. 30 |
| Module height | only the shorter two of MO-161's three heights; maximum 1.255 in | [1] §"RAM DIMM Dimensions" pp. 31–32 |
| Refresh | CAS-before-RAS every 15.6 µs; refreshes 2K-refresh parts within 32 ms | [1] §"RAM Refresh" p. 31 |

Sizing is Hammerhead's and the startup software's: the bank base register for each bank carries a base address — "the sum of the sizes of all the lower numbered banks" — plus control bits for the multiplexing mode and the interleave enable [1] §"Bank Base Registers" p. 10. The register file behind that prose — its observed `+$1C0`–`+$4F0` extent, and the unknown stride and packing inside it — is [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.10's; how twelve slots, some of them two-bank DIMMs, map onto that file is open (§6.2).

Two capacity statements coexist in the note: the address map allocates 2 GB for main memory and the controller "supports up to 1.5 GB" [1] §"Random-Access Memory" p. 9, while the Hammerhead section says the IC "supports main memory sizes up to 2 GB" [1] §"Hammerhead Memory Controller IC" p. 10. Twelve 128 MB DIMMs reach 1.5 GB and no further, so the slots bound the machine below both figures; the discrepancy is §6.1's.

### 3.3 The L2 cache as fitted

§2.4 carries the configuration. The wiring-level facts are Hammerhead's: the cache's L2 controller, its size/mode register at `+$E0`, its flush/fill strobe at `+$F0`, and the firmware's bring-up order, are [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8–§2.9, §3.4, §4.4. The one machine-level statement worth adding is the family's inversion: on every other TNT board the L2 SIMM's size is probed through two pins and absent SIMMs disable the external cache (tnt.md §2.3) — here the answer is fixed, so the bring-up path runs unconditionally.

### 3.4 The two Bandits and the PCI topology

The 9500 is the platform's canonical two-bridge machine: "two Bandit custom ICs, one for PCI slots 1–3 and the other for PCI slots 4–6" [1] §"PCI Expansion Slots" p. 33. Each Bandit is the family's bridge — register file, configuration ports, byte-order translation, burst and posting behavior are [bandit.md](bandit.md) §2–§3 — and each owns its fixed 32 MB physical slice, Bandit 1 at `$F2000000` and Bandit 2 at `$F4000000` (tnt.md §3.1–§3.2; [bandit.md](bandit.md) §1.4).

A real machine's firmware publishes the whole arrangement verbatim [4] §"The PCI Bus" pp. 6–7 (*observed*):

| Property | Bandit 1 (`pci1`) | Bandit 2 (`pci2`) |
|---|---|---|
| `reg` | `F2000000 02000000` | `F4000000 02000000` |
| `bus-range` | 0–0 | 1–1 |
| `clock-frequency` | `01FCA055` (33 MHz) | `01FCA055` |
| `AAPL,interrupts` | `$16` (source 22) | `$1A` (source 26) |
| `slot-names` | mask `$E000`, labels `A1`, `B1`, `C1` | mask `$E000`, labels `D2`, `E2`, `F2` |
| `ranges` — I/O | PCI I/O at `F3000000`, 16 MB | PCI I/O at `F5000000`, 16 MB |
| `ranges` — config | at the bridge base, 8 MB | at the bridge base, 8 MB |
| `ranges` — memory | 1:1 at `80000000`, 256 MB | 1:1 at `90000000`, 256 MB |

The slot-name masks decode as device numbers 13, 14 and 15 on each bus — three slots per bridge at IDSEL `$D`, `$E`, `$F` — with the bridge's own configuration header at IDSEL device 11 and Grand Central at device 16 (`/gc@10`) on **Bandit 1's bus only** [4] §"Viewing the Device Tree" p. 2, §"The PCI Bus" pp. 6–7 (*observed*); the IDSEL map and its software rules are [bandit.md](bandit.md) §3.2's. The bridges also appear as their own PCI children — `/bandit@F2000000/pci106b,1@B` and a second `pci106b,1@B` under Bandit 2 [4] p. 2 — the self-node at device 11 that [bandit.md](bandit.md) §2.4 documents.

Two structural consequences of the two-bridge shape. Each bridge forwards an independent 256 MB PCI memory space (`$80000000` and `$90000000`), so three slots' worth of card frame buffers never contend with the other three', and the I/O stack hangs behind Bandit 1 alone — Grand Central, and therefore every DBDMA channel, is a device on bus 0 (tnt.md §4.3; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §1.3). And the per-bus arbitration is the family's external gate array — Grand Central first, then the slots and the Bandit master round-robin [1] §"Bandit Bus Bridge ICs" p. 11 — replicated for both buses ([bandit.md](bandit.md) §3.7).

### 3.5 Grand Central as fitted: the external interrupt wiring

Grand Central itself is unmodified family silicon: the register window at `$F3000000`, the 32 collected sources, the mode-1 change latch, the DBDMA channel file — all [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2–§3. What is specific to this machine is the **external** interrupt wiring, the board-level lines on sources 20–30. Apple's Network Server documentation — written against a machine whose I/O core is this board's — tabulates the 9500's column explicitly [3] §4.2 p. 16:

| GC source | 9500 line | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 20 (EXT0) | `Cuda_NMI` | the kernel's only level-7 source |
| 21 (EXT1) | Reserved | — |
| 22 (EXT2) | `Ban1_Int` | Bandit 1 — "Bandit only interrupts on bus timeouts" [3] §4.2 p. 16 |
| 23–25 (EXT3–5) | `SlotA_Int`, `SlotB_Int`, `SlotC_Int` | Bandit 1's three slots |
| 26 (EXT6) | `Ban2_Int` | Bandit 2's timeout line |
| 27–29 (EXT7–9) | `SlotD_Int`, `SlotE_Int`, `SlotF_Int` | Bandit 2's three slots |
| 30 (EXT10) | `SecToPri_Int` | the second processor's interprocessor interrupt |

The table agrees with the firmware's own `AAPL,interrupts` properties for the bridges — sources 22 and 26 (§3.4) — and with the source-number assignments of [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §3.2. The machine-level reading of it: the source-26 bit, which on the 7500/8500 carries the built-in Control video's vertical blank, carries Bandit 2 here — the 9500's expression of having no built-in video to interrupt; and source 30, the Control VBL slot on the 7500/8500's sibling machines, is the multiprocessor doorbell (§4.3). The slot lines are collected per slot with the INTA–INTD pins OR-combined ([bandit.md](bandit.md) §3.9; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §3.2).

### 3.6 Storage: two SCSI buses and the floppy

The SCSI story is the family's two-controller design (tnt.md §4.3) with this machine's wiring:

- **Internal (MESH)**: the fast bus — "Because that bus is internal only, its loading characteristics can be more closely controlled than those of an external bus, permitting higher transfer speeds" [1] §"MESH SCSI Controller IC" p. 13 — up to 10 MB/s [1] p. 3. It carries the internal hard disk [1] §"Internal Hard Disk Drive" p. 24 and the internal CD-ROM drive, which connects by a 50-pin header [1] §"SCSI Port" p. 21. Termination is on the board: "The internal end of the SCSI bus is terminated by a 110 Ω passive terminator. The terminator is located on the main logic board near the portion of the internal chassis connector that contains the signals for the internal CD-ROM drive. The internal CD-ROM drive does not include a terminator" [1] p. 21, repeated at p. 24.
- **External (53C94 in Curio)**: up to 5 MB/s [1] p. 3, on a 25-pin D-type connector, "with automatic termination like that on the earlier Power Macintosh computers" — active when nothing is attached, removed when an external device supplies termination [1] §"SCSI Port" p. 21. Both cells are driven through Grand Central's DBDMA channels ([dbdma.md](dbdma.md) §1.4, §4.6).
- **Floppy**: one internal high-density Apple SuperDrive on SWIM III through a 20-pin connector, whose pinout the note tabulates [1] §"Floppy Disk Drive" pp. 23–24; SWIM III "supports DMA data transfers and does not require disabling of interrupts during floppy disk accesses" [1] §"Grand Central I/O System IC" p. 11.

### 3.7 Sound, serial, ADB and Ethernet

- **Sound.** AWACS — the waveform amplifier plus 16-bit codec conforming to the IT&T ASCO 2300 specification [1] §"AWAC Sound IC" p. 13 — with "the sound system in the Power Macintosh 9500 computers... similar to that in the earlier Power Macintosh computers" [1] p. 13. The input jack is a stereo mini-phone jack with an extra contact supplying power to an Apple PlainTalk microphone; input impedance 8 kΩ, maximum level 2 V rms, maximum gain 22.5 dB, signal-to-noise 82 dB [1] §"Sound Input Jack" p. 22. The output jack is a standard stereo mini-phone jack — 37 Ω output impedance, 0.9 V rms maximum, 20 Hz–20 kHz ±2 dB, less than 0.05 percent harmonic distortion plus noise at 1 V rms, 85 dB signal-to-noise — and inserting a plug disconnects the internal speaker; crosstalk degrades from −80 dB to −32 dB with 32 Ω headphones attached [1] §"Sound Output Jack" p. 22. The sample clocks and channel wiring are [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.5, §3.7's.
- **Serial.** Two back-panel ports in 9-pin circular mini-DIN sockets accepting 8- or 9-pin plugs, each independently programmable for asynchronous or synchronous formats up to 9600 baud, AppleTalk and the full GeoPort protocol family included; the handshake input takes an external clock up to 920 Kbit/s [1] §"Serial Ports" p. 18. The GPi pin of each port wires to the corresponding data-carrier-detect input of the SCC section in Curio [1] p. 19. Pin 9 supplies +5 V at up to 100 mA, and the total for ADB plus both serial ports is 500 mA behind a self-resetting circuit breaker [1] pp. 19–20.
- **ADB.** One 4-pin mini-DIN port; the bus is the family's single-master, multiple-slave serial bus driven by the Cuda microcontroller, with the connector's PSW pin generating the reset and interrupt key combinations [1] §"Apple Desktop Bus (ADB)" p. 19.
- **Ethernet.** Built-in, with two connectors — 10BASE-T and the Apple AAUI adapter connector — "Both connectors can be occupied, but only one at a time can be used"; the electrical and mechanical characteristics are the same as on other Macintosh computers [1] §"Ethernet Port" p. 20. The MACE core, its ID PROM and its DBDMA channels are family wiring (tnt.md §4.3; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.5).
- **Cuda.** The custom 68HC05 holding soft power, reset management, parameter RAM, ADB control and the real-time clock [1] §"Cuda Microcontroller IC" p. 12 — behind the VIA1 transport as on every machine of the platform (tnt.md §4.3; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §3.5).

### 3.8 Video: the PCI card contract

With no silicon of its own (§2.5), the machine's display path is a contract a PCI card must satisfy, and Apple states it twice. The bundled card of the /120 is the Apple Accelerated PCI Graphics Card: "a graphics accelerator with a 64-bit data bus to either 2 MB or 4 MB of fast VRAM", accelerated graphics on monitors up to 1280 by 1024, 2 MB of VRAM soldered down plus an optional expansion card to 4 MB, a DB-15 connector carrying RGB with separate sync, and a boot ROM whose "firmware on the card contains a video device driver that supports the initialization required during system startup... the video driver provides the same function calls and has the same capabilities as the NuBus video driver" [1] §"Video Display Card" pp. 13–14. The card's boot ROM "conforms to the IEEE Standard 1275" and follows PCI rev 2.0 [1] pp. 5, 13. Monitor identity comes over the connector's three sense lines, with the extended sense codes the note tabulates supporting multisync 15/17/20-inch and VGA and 19-inch displays, and the Monitors control panel can switch timings live — "the user does not need to restart the computer" [1] §"Detecting the Monitor Type" p. 15.

A real machine's tree shows what the contract produces: the example ATI card sits at device `$D` (slot A1) with `AAPL,slot-name "A1"`, its name and `width`/`height` properties (`$280`/`$1E0` — a 13-inch, 640 by 480 startup mode) set by its own FCode boot driver, and `assigned-addresses` granting its registers at `$81000000` inside Bandit 1's forwarded memory space [4] §"A Look at the ATI Card" p. 8 (*observed*). The card-identification and BAR-assignment machinery behind that is [bandit.md](bandit.md) §4.2's.

### 3.9 The boot ROM as this machine carries it

The image is the family's single 4 MB ROM — two August 1995 releases, the six components of tnt.md §1.3, the decode machinery of §2.6 — with nothing 9500-specific in the bytes. The per-machine content is all identity-selected at boot: the product record, the TNT decoder table (tnt.md §3.6), and the tree this machine's registers produce. What the identity selects on the 9500 is the two-Bandit, no-display tree of §3.4 and §2.5 — the second `bandit` node at `$F4000000` exists on this machine and the 7500's tree lacks it [2], [4] p. 2.

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The six PCI slots

- **The buses.** Six slots, three per Bandit, both buses at 33 MHz [1] §"PCI Expansion Slots" p. 33; slot positions, IDSEL numbers, labels and interrupt lines are §3.4's table.
- **The card contract.** "The Power Macintosh 9500 computers accept standard PCI cards as defined by the PCI Local Bus Specification, Revision 2.0", and "The computer requires all PCI cards to use the 5-V signaling standard described in the specification", in the standard ISA fence [1] p. 33. Startup support comes in three developer-selectable grades — no driver (a tree node only), a run-time driver, or a boot driver embedded in the card's FCode — per the Open Firmware startup description [1] §"Open Firmware Startup" p. 40.
- **Signals.** The slots support the required PCI signals plus the interrupt pins — AD, C/BE, PAR, FRAME#, TRDY#, IRDY#, STOP#, DEVSEL#, IDSEL#, REQ#, GNT#, CLK, RST#, and INTA#–INTD#, "wired together on each slot" [1] Table 4-6 p. 33. They do **not** support the 64-bit bus extension, the cache support signals, JTAG boundary scan, LOCK#, or the PERR#/SERR# error-reporting pair [1] p. 33.
- **Power.** "The computer provides a total of 90 W of power for the six expansion slots. Both 5 V and 3.3 V are supplied; the total power at both voltages must not exceed the 90-W maximum" [1] p. 33. No per-slot or per-rail breakdown is stated (§6.11).

### 4.2 Memory expansion rules

§2.3 and §3.2 carry the full rule set. The operator-level summary: populate the twelve slots with 8-byte DIMMs in any count from one up; match sizes (and address modes) in corresponding slots to earn the 128-bit bank; stay inside 4-to-64 MB banks and the 128 MB two-bank DIMM ceiling; demand 70 ns-or-faster devices within the two-devices-per-data-line, eight-per-/RAS load limits; and keep module height at or under 1.255 in [1] pp. 26–32. The 1.5 GB hardware ceiling is twelve 128 MB DIMMs; the system software of the day stops at 1 GB [1] note to Table 1-1 p. 5.

### 4.3 Processor upgrades and the 9500/MP

The processor card is the machine's stated upgrade path — "an upgrade path to a more advanced microprocessor or coprocessor" [1] §"Processor Subsystem Card" p. 8 — and the family doc records what the ecosystem did with it: the same board family spanned 100-to-350 MHz cards across the generation, with the bus clock a property of the installed card (tnt.md §2.2, §6.5), and the 1997 machines answering the 9500's identity outright (tnt.md §1.2).

The **9500/MP** puts two 604s on the one card. The multiprocessing surface is Hammerhead's three registers — the arbiter configuration, the who-am-I latch and the interprocessor interrupt ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.5–§2.7, §4.5) — plus this machine's external line: the second processor interrupts the first through `SecToPri_Int`, Grand Central source 30 [3] §4.2 p. 16 (§3.5). The reset-time protocol is described against the derived server design and stated to be shared: the first code after the `$FFF00100` fetch determines whether the processor is the primary, which continues with initialization, POST and Open Firmware, "or if the processor is the secondary it will enter a spin-wait for an interprocessor interrupt", and "the way software accesses the dual processor hardware implementation is the same as in the PowerMac 9500 family, although the physical connector and card form factor are specific to the Network Server" [3] §3 p. 10. Enabling the second processor is an operating-system concern [3] §3 p. 10. The 9500/MP's own card — its clocks, its population, its shipping configurations — is described by no document in evidence (§6.8); how its surface behaves in use is recorded on [pm9500mp.md](pm9500mp.md).

### 4.4 Internal storage

Beyond the factory floppy, hard disk and optional CD-ROM, the case supports one 5.25-inch and one or two 3.5-inch additional internal devices [1] Table 1-1 p. 4, §"Appearance and Features" p. 2. The hard disk options run 1 or 2 GB [1] pp. 3, 24; the CD-ROM is the AppleCD 600i, tray-loading, reading CD-ROM, CD-ROM XA, CD-I and PhotoCD plus audio, with a data buffer, sustained transfers of 600 KB per second [1] §"CD-ROM Drive" p. 24 — though the same note calls the mechanism double-speed while its comparison table says "4X speed" [1] Table 1-1 p. 4, an inconsistency left open (§6.10). Internal devices land on the MESH bus with the board-mounted terminator discipline of §3.6. The shipped system software (7.5.2) extended the largest disk partition from 4 GB to 2 TB with 64-bit volume APIs [1] §"New Features" p. 36 — disk-side headroom this machine's drives never approached.

### 4.5 What this machine does not expand by

- **No NuBus.** The 9500 is the first top-of-line Macintosh with no NuBus slots at all; its comparison table against the first generation lists "6 PCI slots" where the older machines list NuBus [1] Table 1-1 p. 4.
- **No DAV connector.** The audio/video expansion bus of the other TNT machines is absent [1] Table 1-1 p. 4 — a consequence of having no AV subsystem for a DAV card to tap.
- **No video expansion on the board.** There are no VRAM slots; the framebuffer grows only by replacing the PCI card (§3.8).
- **No communication-slot or processor-direct expansion** of any kind appears in the note; six PCI slots, twelve DIMM slots, the processor card and the internal drive bays are the machine's entire expansion surface.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The cold-boot contract — reset vector, NanoKernel, Open Firmware as the bring-up, POST as diagnostics, `boot /AAPL,ROM` into the 68k emulator — is the family's (tnt.md §2.7), established from the ROM's own code and configuration record. This machine's branch points, step by step:

1. **Reset.** The 604 fetches the hardware-reset vector at `$FFF00100`, inside the ROM decode, at image offset +$300100 (tnt.md §2.7, §3.4). On a multiprocessor card the first code determines primary from secondary; the secondary spin-waits for an interprocessor interrupt [3] §3 p. 10 (§4.3).
2. **NanoKernel.** The configuration record's translation templates and constants — `InterruptHandlerKind = 2`, the interrupt-controller address, the kernel and emulator pages — are the family's and are unchanged here (tnt.md §3.5).
3. **Open Firmware.** Reads the identity registers; the `$39`/bit-30 answer selects `compatible = "AAPL,9500"` and builds the **no-display, two-Bandit** tree — no `chaos`/`control` nodes, the second `bandit` node present, the dangling `vci0` alias left in the table (§2.5, §3.4; tnt.md §2.6). It sizes the twelve DIMM slots through Hammerhead's bank base registers (§3.2; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.10), probes both Bandits and Grand Central, assigns the cards' BARs — a real machine's video card ends up with registers at `$81000000` [4] §"A Look at the ATI Card" p. 8 — and manages NVRAM, reformatting a blank store before the OS reads it (tnt.md §2.7).
4. **POST.** The diagnostics object logs through Grand Central's NVRAM apertures and runs `.L2DataCacheTest` against the soldered 512 KB cache (§2.4; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8–§2.9, §4.4; tnt.md §2.7).
5. **`boot /AAPL,ROM`** hands the PowerPC to the 68k emulator; the identification routine lands on the `$3D` product record, the TNT decoder table installs the legacy device bases, and the classic Macintosh start-up proceeds (tnt.md §2.7, §3.6; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3).
6. **The startup screen.** The family's native video driver, which mode-sets the built-in display through the Control registers on the 7500/8500, has nothing to drive here; the first image on the monitor is drawn by the display card's own FCode boot driver, whose initialization the card contract requires (§3.8; tnt.md §2.7).
7. **System software.** The machine ships with system software 7.5.2 — not a reference release for earlier models — with the large-partition, Drive Setup and Open Transport features of [1] §"Software Features" pp. 35–39.
8. **Boot-device behavior.** The Start Manager honors the PRAM default boot device first and walks the whole drive queue only after a spin-up timeout of roughly thirty guest-seconds — a machine with no disk at the recorded ID looks stalled for that long before proceeding (tnt.md §2.7, *observed*).
9. **Error paths.** An identity miss yields `"AAPL,????"` and a tree with no display nodes (tnt.md §2.6) — on this machine, a miss and a match look alike in the display department, which is why the identity registers must still answer correctly for the two-Bandit tree to exist. The POST factory-test strap (BoxID bit 8) detours the boot into the ROM's serial test monitor (tnt.md §2.6, §7 item 4, *observed*).

## 6. Open questions

1. **The controller's ceiling.** The note's RAM section says Hammerhead "supports up to 1.5 GB of main memory" [1] p. 9; its Hammerhead section says the IC "supports main memory sizes up to 2 GB" [1] p. 10. Whether 2 GB is a silicon capability the twelve slots simply cannot reach, or one of the two figures is wrong, is not resolvable from the note; no other Apple document states either number for this board.
2. **The twelve-slot-to-bank-file mapping.** How the twelve slots — some holding two-bank DIMMs — map onto the bank base register file is open: the file's observed extent (`+$1C0`–`+$4F0`) and the unknown stride and packing inside it are [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.10's open question, and this machine is the one that most stresses it. Whether interleaving pairs beyond the note's "Bank 0–Bank 1" example (banks 2–3, 4–5, …) follow the identical rule is implied by the "corresponding slots" wording but stated nowhere.
3. **The later speed grades.** A real 9500's root node publishes a 50 MHz AR-bus `clock-frequency` [4] §"Properties and Methods of the Node /" p. 4 — a rate the /120 and /132's one-third rule cannot produce. The 1996 speed-bump 9500s (the 150 and 200 MHz builds) are the presumed explanation, but no developer note in evidence covers them; their bus ratios, cards and identity behaviour are undocumented (*inferred — unverified*).
4. **The Apple Accelerated PCI Graphics Card's silicon.** The accelerator's chip set, its 2-to-4 MB VRAM expansion card's mechanics, and its driver-level behavior beyond "same function calls as the NuBus video driver" [1] p. 13 are documented nowhere in the evidence set.
5. **The 9500's data-path chip.** The server documentation proves a "PowerMac 9500 data path chip" exists — parity memory is implemented "by a modification of" it [3] §2.4.1 p. 7 — but names nothing else about the part: its identity, its position in the DRAM path, and whether the unmodified 9500 chip carries any parity of its own are all unattested. Whether the 9500's firmware performs the same detected-timing DRAM configuration (60 ns with parity, 70 ns without) the server notes describe is likewise not stated for this machine ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §3.2).
6. **The BoxID strap value.** No Apple document states what a 9500's BoxID register actually reads. The 68k model dispatch never consults it on this machine (§1.2), but Open Firmware folds BoxID bits 11–15 into `AAPL,cpu-id` — so the `3900A69D` value published by a real machine [4] p. 4 constrains those bits without stating them, and the register's remaining bits are attested only by their consumption (tnt.md §7 item 4).
7. **The unpopulated bridge slices.** With no Chaos fitted, whether anything on this board decodes the `$F0000000`–`$F1FFFFFF` display-bridge slice — and what the allocation scheme's reserved fourth-bridge range means for any machine of the family — is not established (tnt.md §3.1, §7 item 8, §7 item 11).
8. **The 9500/MP card.** The dual-604 card's clocks, bus loading, population and shipping configurations are described by no document in evidence; the developer note covers only the single-processor /120 and /132, and the multiprocessing description that exists is written from the derived server design and asserts only that the software mechanism is shared [3] §3 p. 10.
9. **Slot power distribution.** The 90 W total across six slots at two voltages [1] p. 33 has no published per-slot or per-rail breakdown; the one-Bandit machines' notes give their own smaller budgets, so the 9500's per-slot figure cannot be derived by division with any confidence.
10. **The CD-ROM speed grade.** The note's prose calls the AppleCD 600i "double-speed" while quoting 600 KB per second sustained and its comparison table says "4X speed" [1] pp. 4, 24; which figure describes the shipping drive is not decidable from the note.
11. **The power supply.** No wattage, input range or rail budget for the machine as a whole appears in the developer note; only the slot budget is stated (§1.5).
12. **The published time base.** The 604 fixes the hardware rate at one-fourth of the bus clock — 11 MHz on the /132 [5] §1 p. 1-24 — but the `timebase-frequency` value this machine's firmware publishes in its `cpu` node was never isolated from the ROM image (tnt.md §7 item 5), so the published figure cannot be confirmed against the computed one.
13. **Primary-versus-secondary determination.** The first post-reset code decides which 604 is primary [3] §3 p. 10, but the register basis of that decision on the 9500 — presumably the Hammerhead who-am-I latch ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.6) — is not stated by any document.
