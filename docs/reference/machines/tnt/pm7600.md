# Power Macintosh 7600

The Power Macintosh 7600 is the Power Macintosh 7500 reissued around a faster processor card: the 7500's compact-desktop chassis, logic board, one-Bandit PCI topology, Control/Chaos video subsystem with video input, and the whole Grand Central I/O stack, driven by a 604e-class card in the family's replaceable-card socket ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §1.1). The model line spans three speed grades — the 7600/120 and 7600/132, which Apple's 1997 developer note names as the *previous* models of the 7600/200, and the 7600/200 itself, the note's documented configuration and — per that same note — a Japan-only one [1] Table 1-1 p. 2 and note. Because the machine's ROM carries a byte-identical Open Firmware image, it answers `"AAPL,7500"` in every way software can ask, and "software can only tell them apart by the CPU's PVR, the L2 configuration or the AV hardware, never by the identity registers" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5); this page carries what the identity does not say — the grades and their clocks, the enclosure, the factory population, and the one 1997 ROM release the line carries.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

Apple's developer note for this generation is a delta guide — "This developer note describes only the changes that make the new models different from their earlier counterparts" [1] §"About This Developer Note" p. vii — and its comparison table defines the 7600 by a line the family's other pages do not have: the 7600's previous models are *more 7600s*. Where the table maps the 8600/200 back to the 8500/150 and /180, and the 9600/200 back to the 9500/200, the 7600/200's row names "Power Macintosh 7600/120" and "Power Macintosh 7600/132" as its earlier counterparts, with the major changes "Clock speed 200 MHz, 2 GB hard drive, 12X-speed CD-ROM drive" [1] Table 1-1 p. 2. The model line therefore predates the note's own wave: the /120 and /132 shipped first, and the /200 is the late grade the note documents.

Two facts about that late grade constrain everything else on this page. Its availability: "The Power Macintosh 7600/200 is available only in Japan" [1] §"New Models" p. 2 note — so the 7600 the rest of the world bought is one of the two grades the note names but does not otherwise describe. And its coverage: of the note's tables, the 7600 appears in the clock table and the configurations table only as the /200 [1] Table 1-2 p. 4, Table 1-4 p. 7; the /120 and /132 have no documented clock, memory or disk rows anywhere in the corpus (§6.1, §6.3).

Everything the note does not change is inherited by its compatibility clause — "Except for the changes described in this developer note, the features of the faster Power Macintosh 7300, 7600, 8600, and 9600 computers are the same as those of their earlier counterparts" [1] §"Compatibility Issues" p. 17 — and for this machine the chain of "earlier counterparts" runs 7600/200 → 7600/120 and /132 → the Power Macintosh 7500, whose board and chassis the line reissues ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §1.1). The inherited platform is documented once in the family doc and cited here by section, never restated; the inherited 7500-specific wiring is [pm7500.md](pm7500.md)'s, likewise cited. The identity consequence is the family doc's headline for the 1997 wave — the byte-identical firmware makes the 7600 report the 7500's identity outright ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5) — and §1.2 states which of the three discriminators that leaves actually work for this pair.

The discriminating question — what actually separates a 7600 from a 7500 — deserves the same precise treatment the sibling pages give it, because of the family doc's three discriminators ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5), a different set works for this pair than for the 8600 and 9600 (*inferred — unverified* as a conclusion; each input fact is cited):

- **The AV hardware does not separate them.** Both machines carry the input-only AV configuration: the 8758/7196/Plan B capture chain and an A/V panel with composite and S-video inputs, no television-output encoder ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.5; [1] §"Video Input" p. 5 for this machine's panel).
- **The stock L2 separates them.** The 7500 leaves the factory with its cache slot empty; the 7600 line carries "a 256 KB DIMM installed in a slot" in every documented configuration [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 4, Table 1-4 p. 7. This is the discriminator that breaks the 7600's tie — though it discriminates the *configurations*, not the boards: an owner-installed 256 KB module makes a 7500 read the same as a stock 7600 on this axis ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.3).
- **The PVR always separates them.** The 7500's 601 reports processor version 1; the 7600's 604e reports version number 9, with the revision level starting at `$0100` [3] §"Processor Version Register" pp. 2-6, C-3. An `mfpvr` read is the one instruction that answers "which machine is this?" with the machines' own silicon ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3; [pm9600.md](pm9600.md) §1.2).
- **The ROM release separates the generations, not the models.** This machine's image reports ROM version `077D.34F2` ([4]; [pm9600.md](pm9600.md) §1.2) — but the 1995 releases serve the 7500 identically in every component the identity decodes touch, and the enabler generation spans both [1] p. 18, so a ROM-version read distinguishes the *ROM*, not the box (§6.10).

The device tree this machine's firmware builds is therefore the 7500's tree, byte for byte — the one `bandit` node, Grand Central with all cells including `mesh`, the `chaos`/`control` display bus, the same `reg` properties, Apple part numbers and `AAPL,interrupts` values ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]) — and shipping software folds the pair together: the machine table that names "PowerMac 8500/8600" and "PowerMac 9500/9600" as single entries has an `AAPL,7500` entry named simply "PowerMac 7500", with no 7600 string to match because no firmware emits one [5]; [tnt.md](tnt.md) §1.2.

### 1.4 Factory configurations

The note's configurations table carries one 7600 row [1] Table 1-4 p. 7:

| Model | DRAM | Hard disk | L2 cache | Special features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power Macintosh 7600/200 | 32 MB | 2 GB | 256 KB | Video input |

The generation-level statements that bind the row: "The new models of the Power Macintosh 7300, 7600, 8600, and 9600 computers come with 16 or 32 MB of main RAM installed in RAM DIMM slots. All models have a built-in second-level (L2) cache of either 256 or 512 KB. The size of the internal hard disk is either 2 or 4 GB" [1] §"Configurations" p. 7, and "All the new models include a built-in 12X-speed CD-ROM drive" [1] p. 7 note. The 7600 sits mid-range on all three axes — and at the *bottom* of the L2 split, because the 512 KB figure is the 9600 pair's soldered cache; "the L2 cache in the other models consists of a 256 KB DIMM installed in a slot" [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 4.

What the table does not carry is any row for the /120 and /132 — the grades the rest of the world could buy. Their DRAM, disk and cache populations are not documented by Apple anywhere in the corpus; that they match the /200's is *inferred — unverified* from the note's generation-level ranges alone (§6.3). The 32 MB load-out is four 8 MB DIMMs or two 16 MB DIMMs; that the factory ships a same-size population in corresponding slots — so the machine leaves the factory with its 128-bit interleave enabled ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3) — is likewise *inferred — unverified*: the note documents the rule, not the shipped population.

### 1.5 The enclosure and the security bar

The new tower enclosure of this generation "is used for the Power Macintosh 8600 and 9600 computers" [1] §"Appearance of the New Tower Enclosure" p. 8 — the 8600's and 9600's pages carry it ([pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §1.5; [pm9600.md](pm9600.md) §1.5). The 7600 keeps the 7500's compact desktop with its hinged top chassis that folds out for access to the DIMM slots, cache socket and PCI sockets ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §1.1, §4.3). The one mechanical feature the note adds to this machine: "The Power Macintosh 7300 and 7600 models have a security bar that conceals the latches for the top cover. The security bar snaps into place on the front of the case and is held in place by a screw" [1] §"Security Bar" p. 6 — a physical anti-theft and tamper measure over exactly the access the hinged chassis gives, listed among the generation's new features [1] §"New Features" p. 3. Whether the bar is new with the 7600 line or a running change to the 7500's own case is not stated by any document in evidence (§6.9); the 1995 notes describe no such part ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §1.3).

The back-panel furniture is the 7500's, unchanged by the compatibility clause [1] p. 17 and drawn for this machine in the note's Figure 1-1 [1] §"Video Input" p. 5: the A/V panel with "RCA jacks for stereo pairs of audio inputs and outputs, an RCA jack for composite video input, and a multipin connector for S-video input" [1] p. 5 — input only, the 7500's panel exactly ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.5). The serial, ADB, Ethernet and external-SCSI connectors are inherited with it ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6).

### 1.6 The 7300, the model the note groups with this one

The 7300 shares this page's ROM release, this page's developer note, this page's desktop enclosure and security bar [1] §"Security Bar" p. 6 — and is *not* this page's sibling platform. What the note documents for it: three grades, the 7300/166, /180 and /200, whose processor clocks run at 168, 180 and 200 MHz on 48.0, 45.0 and 50.0 MHz buses [1] Table 1-2 p. 4 (with the note's own correction that "the processor clock speed of the Power Macintosh 7300/166 is actually 168 MHz" [1] p. 4 note — the 168/48 pair being the 7:2 ratio, a 604e-only setting [3] §1.2 p. 1-2); a 256 KB L2 module in a slot [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 4; 16 MB or 32 MB of DRAM and a 2 GB disk [1] Table 1-4 p. 7; and, uniquely in this generation, a *new* identity: "The Power Macintosh 7300 has a new machine ID value: 109 (hexadecimal 6D)" [1] §"Machine Identification" p. 17 — the 1997 machine the firmware can actually name, its `AAPL,7300` string being one of the five the Open Firmware image carries ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.6). Its platform, however, is not the one this page describes: the evidence places the 7300 on the 7200's Catalyst platform — the memory-and-video controller that is not Hammerhead, no processor card, no MESH, no Control/Chaos display bus ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.4 describes the architecture; the attribution to the 7300 is community-corroborated, and the machine table that groups "PowerMac 7200/7300" as one entry is [5]) — so nothing in this page's §3 applies to it, and its per-machine deltas belong to a Catalyst page set of its own ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.4). The note itself documents none of the 7300's internals beyond the clock table, and this page claims nothing further about it (§6.12).

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The clock architecture is the family's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §5.4): the card supplies the system bus clock, PCI runs at 33 MHz asynchronously, the video bus is synchronous with the main system bus, DRAM refresh is a CAS-before-RAS cycle every 15.6 µs, and the two SCSI cells run at their own rates. This machine's column, with the /200's documented rates and the earlier grades' gap:

| Clock | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Processor core, /200 | 200 MHz | [1] Table 1-2 p. 4 |
| Processor (AR) bus, /200 | 50.0 MHz | [1] Table 1-2 p. 4 |
| Core-to-bus ratio, /200 | 4:1 — `PLL_CFG[0–3] = 1010` [3] Table 7-6 pp. 7-37–7-38 | [3] §1.2 p. 1-2 |

The earlier grades' bus rates are the page's one genuinely open clock question, and the ratio arithmetic is worth stating because it rules an answer out. The 604e "supports processor-to-bus frequency ratios of 1:1, 3:2, 2:1, 5:2, 3:1, 4:1, and 7:2" [3] §8.1 p. 8-2 — and 120 MHz is not reachable from a 50 MHz bus by any of them. So if the /120 and /132 carry 604e parts on this platform, they run a slower bus: 40 MHz at 3:1 or 48 MHz at 5:2 for the /120, 44 MHz at 3:1 for the /132 — the rates the family's other 120/132 MHz grades are inferred to use ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.2 states the same law for the 8500/120 and /132). The family doc's statement that the 1997 machines "run the system bus at 50 MHz" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5) is documented fact for the /200 and open for the rest (§6.1). Because the bus clock is a property of the installed card ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2), every rate downstream of the card — the AR bus, the 12.5 MHz time base and decrementer, the synchronous video bus — moves with a processor upgrade; the 33 MHz PCI clock and the two SCSI cell clocks do not.

### 2.3 Memory population: the 1997 DIMM rules

The banking law — eight slots paired A1–A4 with B1–B4, the 128-bit interleave of matched pairs, contiguity manufactured by Hammerhead's bank base registers, the 4 MB-to-64 MB bank and 128 MB two-bank-DIMM ceilings, the 1 GB total — is the 7500's and is not restated here ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.2; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.10, §3.2). The 1997 note restates the interface and adds rules of its own:

- **The DIMM itself is unchanged.** "The method of RAM expansion in the new models is essentially the same as in their earlier counterparts: 168-pin, 8-byte DIMMs as defined in the JEDEC MO-161 specification" [1] §"RAM DIMM Modifications" p. 18.
- **EDO devices are accepted — and neutralized.** "The RAM expansion DIMMs in the new computers can use either extended data out (EDO) or fast page mode (FPM) DRAM devices. The computer always operates the devices in fast page mode" [1] p. 18. An EDO DIMM is electrically tolerated but buys no speed: the controller's access discipline stays the 7500's FPM timing, with its 70 ns-or-faster device law and load limits ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.2).
- **Power on the slots is 5 V only.** "As in the previous Power Macintosh models, only 5-V power is available on the RAM DIMM slots. Devices that require 3.3-V power cannot be used" [1] p. 18 note.
- **The 1.1-inch height limit applies.** The taller-DIMM allowance of this generation is an enclosure feature, not a platform one: "The new tower enclosure for the Power Macintosh 8600 and 9600 accommodates RAM DIMMs of 1.1 and 1.255 inches in height. Other Power Macintosh computers accommodate only DIMMs with the 1.1-inch height" [1] p. 18. The 7600 is one of the "other Power Macintosh computers" — its desktop chassis takes the 1.1-inch module only, which also resolves the 1995-note contradiction the 8600's page carries ([pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §6 item 5) for this machine: nothing on this page depends on the 1.255-inch figure.

### 2.4 The second-level cache as shipped

The L2 architecture is Hammerhead's — write-back, direct-mapped, allocate on read or write, synchronous-burst-SRAM data store, size sensed electrically through the module connector's `CachePrsnt` and `CacheSize(1:0)` pins rather than configured ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3; [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.3; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8–§2.9). The generation-level facts the 1997 note adds: "All the new models include a second-level (L2) cache", with a claimed "performance improvement of up to 40% over machines without such a cache" [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 4 — where the 7500 left the factory with the module absent and the 8500 with 256 KB installed, every 1997 configuration ships one ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3). On this machine, "the L2 cache in the other models consists of a 256 KB DIMM installed in a slot" [1] p. 4 — the "other models" being every 1997 machine but the 9600 pair, whose cache is soldered down.

The size the sensing path reports is code `b01` — 256 KB — the code the POST diagnostics object's `.L2DataCacheTest` decodes on this personality's cache path at every boot ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8, §4.4); the module grows to 512 KB, 1 MB or 4 MB within the existing code field ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.3). The terminology delta the 8600's page pins applies here too: the 1995 note calls the 7500's module a "cache SIMM", the 1997 note calls this machine's a "DIMM installed in a slot" — whether the module, its connector or only the word changed is not stated by any document in evidence ([pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §6 item 4; §6.6 here). The timing law is unchanged by the processor swap: the 604e "has the same signal configuration as the 604", the differences being internal [3] §1.3.8 pp. 1-27–1-28, with the precharge-timing rules for `ABB`, `DBB`, `ARTRY` and `SHD` that a 604e-era bus model must honour [3] §1.1 p. 1-8.

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 The processor card and the PowerPC 604e

The card is the family's — the main processor and its associated clock circuits on a plug-in card that "provides an upgrade path to a faster or more powerful microprocessor" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2) — and it supplies the board's bus clock (§2.2). The note's processor claim covers the generation's new models: "All the new models use the PowerPC 604e microprocessor. The PowerPC 604e is a version of the PowerPC 604 with the following enhancements: on-chip data and instruction caches of 32 KB each; processor clock speed up to 5 times the bus clock speed" [1] §"PowerPC 604e Microprocessor" p. 3 — the /200's 200 MHz on 50 MHz being exactly that 4:1 multiple, one the plain 604 cannot be configured for [3] §8.1 p. 8-2. For the /120 and /132 the note makes no claim at all; that they carry 604-family parts is fixed by the family doc's class statement ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5), whether specifically 604e or plain 604 is not established by any document in the corpus (§6.2).

The manual's inventory of the part, for the properties that matter to this machine [3] §1.2 p. 1-2:

- **Caches**: separate 32 KB instruction and data caches, four-way set associative, 32-byte blocks, physically indexed, with line-fill buffer forwarding and three copy-back write buffers — double the 7500's 601's unified 32 KB cache, the geometry change a guest's cache-flushing code can feel ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.1).
- **The PVR**: version number 9, revision level from `$0100` [3] §"Processor Version Register" p. 2-6 — against the 601's version 1 and the 604's 4 [3] Appendix C p. C-3 — the one register that separates this machine from a 7500 outright (§1.2).
- **`HID1`** (SPR 1009): bits 0–3 read back the current `PLL_CFG` value [3] §2.1.2.4 p. 2-12 — software can read the card's clock configuration out of the CPU, which is how a guest can learn the /200's 4:1 multiple without touching the device tree.
- **Split voltage planes**: 2.5 V core and 3.3 V I/O, with the design rule "for replacement compatibility, 604/604e designs should provide both 2.5-V and 3.3-V planes and the ability to connect those two planes together and disable the 2.5-V plane for operation with a 604" [3] §1.1 p. 1-8 — the electrical shape of the card's upgrade obligation; whether this card implements the dual-plane rule is not documented, that it must accept the platform's cards is the contract ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2).

Firmware-side, the CPU identity flows the family's two ways: `AAPL,cpu-id` with the not-a-601 flag set ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3), and the 68k half's CPU-identification dispatch at every boot ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2). The 1997 kernel this machine carries adds processor-table entries for parts the line never shipped — cache geometries matching the PowerPC 750 class, 32-byte line size (*observed* in the `$960E4BE9` NanoKernel component; [4]) — G3 upgrade-card support in a TNT chassis, the software half of the platform's upgrade promise (§4.3). The kernel–emulator contract change of the 1997 kernels — the IPL halfword handed to the 68k emulator getting bit 15 set — is present in this machine's image too (*observed*; [4]); it is not a hardware change, and the interrupt classification is untouched ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5). The late-revision `$960FC647` kernel's split of the SDA and MMCR0 capability flags is *not* in this machine's release ([4]; [pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §3.1) — on the 7600's image the pairing holds as in 1995, the delta the 8600's page records being the 8600/9600 refresh's alone.

### 3.2 The inherited board: one Bandit, Grand Central, the whole I/O stack

The chip set is the 7500's, and the 1997 ROM comparison is what turns the compatibility clause into an established fact: Open Firmware is byte-identical between the 1995 and 1997 images, and the diagnostics object is rebuilt with every hardware probe instruction-identical ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]). So the device tree this machine's firmware builds is the 7500's tree, byte for byte: the one `bandit` node (alias `pci1` at `$F2000000`), Grand Central behind it at `$F3000000` with its eleven DBDMA channels, the `chaos`/`control` display bus, the `mesh`, `curio`-served `mace`/`53c94`/`escc` cells, `swim3`, `awacs` and `via-cuda`, and every `reg` property, Apple part number and `AAPL,interrupts` value of the 7500's set ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.1–§3.2, §4; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.5, §4.1; [bandit.md](bandit.md) §1.4; [dbdma.md](dbdma.md) §1.4; [4]).

The wiring consequences are inherited with it and are documented once on the 7500's page: the three PCI sockets share the one Bandit's single bus with the DAV connector and the whole I/O stack — the structural limit of the 7500-class board ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §2.1, §3.7); the socket IDSELs are inferred (13, 14, 15) and the label strings unpublished ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.7, §6 item 5); the per-socket interrupt assignment is unattested ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §6 item 4); and whether the firmware emits a `planb` node for the fitted capture hardware was never established ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §6 item 3). None of these open questions moved between the 7500 and the 7600, because the bytes that would answer them did not move either ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).

The cells' interrupt, DMA and clock behaviour — the 32-source interrupt file, the VIA1 cascade behind the Cuda, the channel-number-equals-interrupt-number identity — is the family's and each device page's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §4.3, §5.1; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.2, §3.2–§3.5).

### 3.3 Video: the display path

The 7600 keeps the 7500's entire video subsystem, by the compatibility clause [1] p. 17 and the byte-identical firmware ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5). The monitor path is the 7500's page's, cited not restated: Chaos bridging the video bus to the processor bus, Control providing addressing and control with its 64 MB VRAM aperture and 4 KB register block, RaDACal converting and generating timing and the hardware cursor, four VRAM slots at 1 MB per SIMM — 2 MB as shipped, 4 MB maximum, 24 bpp to 17-inch monitors at 2 MB and to 21-inch at 4 MB — the DB-15 connector with its sense-code system, the pixel clock programmed through the Cuda's I²C passthrough, and the boot mode-set the ROM's native Control driver performs ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.4; [bandit.md](bandit.md) §3.10). The vertical-blanking interrupt is collected by Grand Central on external source 26, while the tree still publishes the display node's `AAPL,interrupts` as 30 — the Platinum wiring's number — a discrepancy a guest-visible model reproduces rather than reconciles ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.4, §6 item 4; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §3.2).

The one 1997 change inside the subsystem is in software, and it is the family doc's: of the 180 Toolbox resources, exactly six changed between the 1995 and 1997 ROMs, three of them the video drivers — Control, Platinum and Sixty6 — "consistent with new display timings rather than new silicon" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5). Whether this machine's monitor-mode or gamma tables differ from the 7500's is not established: neither driver has been disassembled in either generation (§6.4).

### 3.4 Video input and the A/V panel

The 1997 note's only subsystem statement about the 7600 is its video-input section, and it is a full description of the panel: "The new Power Macintosh 7600 has the same video input capabilities as the Power Macintosh 8500 and 8600 models. The back of the computer has a small panel with RCA jacks for stereo pairs of audio inputs and outputs, an RCA jack for composite video input, and a multipin connector for S-video input" [1] §"Video Input" p. 5, with the connector arrangement drawn in Figure 1-1 [1] p. 5. Two things follow. The capability is the 8500's — but so is the 7500's, whose input chain is the same 8758 ADC, 7196 decoder/scaler and Plan B two-channel DBDMA capture path, up to 24 bpp and 640×480 at 30 frames per second for display, frame grab or QuickTime capture ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.5; [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.4; [tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.4; [dbdma.md](dbdma.md) §1.2). And the panel is the 7500's exactly — input connectors and stereo audio in and out, no video output ports ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.5): the note's phrase "the same video input capabilities" is precise, because the *output* stream the 8500 and 8600 add (the Sixty6 convolver and 7187 DENC, [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.5) is not fitted on this machine, as on the 7500. The 7-pin S-video input connector's I²C pair (pins 5 and 7) serves an undocumented device — the 7500's page's open question, inherited ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §6 item 8).

The DAV connector carries the same interface to PCI cards as on the 7500 — the 4:2:2 unscaled digital video input signal into the 7196, and digital audio into the codec ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §4.2; [tnt.md](tnt.md) §4.5).

### 3.5 Storage: the 2 GB disk, the 12X CD-ROM, and the drives this machine does not have

The two-bus SCSI architecture is the 7500's ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6): MESH as the internal controller at up to 10 MB/s, Curio's 53C94 behind the external connector at up to 5 MB/s, both decoded through Grand Central, both in the TNT decoder record ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.6). The drive complement is the generation's:

- **The hard disk is 2 GB** [1] Table 1-4 p. 7, against the family's "either 2 or 4 GB" range [1] §"Configurations" p. 7 — the 4 GB figure being the 9600 pair's [1] Table 1-4 p. 7. Which of the two SCSI buses it occupies is not restated by the note; on the 7500 the hard disk "is connected to the internal SCSI bus" ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6), and the same placement here is *inferred — unverified* (§6.7).
- **The CD-ROM is the AppleCD 1600i**, a 12× mechanism "support[ing] sustained data transfer rates of 1600 KB per second and a data buffer", sliding tray, reading CD-ROM, CD-ROM XA, CD-I and PhotoCD discs plus audio [1] §"12X-Speed CD-ROM Drive" p. 5 — built into every model of the generation [1] p. 7 note, where the 7500's AppleCD 600i was optional ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §1.4). Its bus is likewise unstated; the 7500's CD-ROM sat on the external bus's internal end ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6), and the same placement is *inferred — unverified*.
- **No Zip drive.** "One of the new models, the Power Macintosh 8600, has a built-in Iomega Zip removable cartridge drive" [1] §"Built-in Removable Cartridge Drive" p. 5 — the 8600's alone ([pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §3.4), not this machine's, in any configuration the corpus documents.

The floppy is the 7500's internal 1.4 MB Apple SuperDrive on SWIM III ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6). Two ROM-side storage behaviours changed for the generation [1] §"Changes in the ROM" p. 16: "The SCSI Manager has been revised to correct a problem with the asynchronous receive-acknowledge signal in the SCSI controller", and "The default value of the hard disk time-out has been increased from 25 to 31 seconds" — the timeout an SCSI Manager waiting on a spun-down drive now takes five seconds longer to declare.

### 3.6 Sound: the codec the machine reports

The sound subsystem is the 7500's electrically and in its jack set ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6), and the 1997 firmware publishes the same codec the 1995 firmware did: the only codec string anywhere in the byte-identical Open Firmware image is AWACS, so on a machine booted from this ROM "no driver takes a Screamer path … even if the board carries the register-compatible superset part" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5) — the firmware-visible fact, with the silicon question left open there and here (§6.8). The one 1997-updated audio component is `.DAVAudio`, the AV audio component of the six changed Toolbox resources ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]) — a driver delta on the DAV-facing audio path (§3.4), not a codec identification. The sample-rate family, the DBDMA channels and the codec register surface are the family's ([grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.5, §3.7; [dbdma.md](dbdma.md) §1.4).

### 3.7 The boot ROM as this machine carries it

The image is the family's 4 MB ROM in its first February 1997 release, header checksum `$960E4BE9`, ROM version `077D.34F2` — the release covering the 7300, 7600, 8600 and 9600 ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §1.3; [4]; [pm9600.md](pm9600.md) §1.2). The second 1997 release, `$960FC647`, is named for the 8600 and 9600 ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §1.3); whether any 7600 shipped it is not attested (§6.10). The component-level comparison against the 1995 pair is the family doc's record, cited not restated: Open Firmware byte-identical across all four images; the configuration record changed only in the I/O pages' Guarded attribute; the NanoKernel and exception table rebuilt (kernel–emulator contract details, not hardware); POST rebuilt with every hardware probe instruction-identical — Hammerhead was **not** revised; and six of the 180 Toolbox resources changed ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).

What is this machine's own is the ROM's Apple-documented change list, four behaviours [1] §"Changes in the ROM" p. 16:

- the SCSI Manager revision for the asynchronous receive-acknowledge problem (§3.5);
- the hard-disk timeout raised from 25 to 31 seconds (§3.5);
- "The software now sets the guarded bit in the MMU for cache-inhibited address space" — the Apple-documented counterpart of the configuration-record change the ROM comparison observes ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.5, §6.5), and the one a guest can see: a page-table walk on this machine finds every I/O mapping cache-inhibited **and** Guarded, where a 1995 machine's are cache-inhibited only;
- "The ROM has been modified to fix bugs that affected multiprocessor operation" — a fix in the MP-capable kernel that this uniprocessor machine carries in its ROM without exercising.

The system software that ships with it: "The Mac OS for the new models is a modified version of System 7.5.5 with a new enabler" [1] §"System Software" p. 16 — and "the new enabler also supports the earlier counterparts of these computers, including the Power Macintosh 7500" [1] p. 18, the one software statement that spans the 7500/7600 pair in both directions.

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The three PCI sockets

The slot complement is the 7500's three, inherited through the compatibility clause [1] p. 17, and the full card contract — 33 MHz, PCI Specification Revision 2.0 cards in the 5 V signaling standard with the standard ISA fence, the supported and absent signal sets, the 50 W combined slot budget, the FCode boot-driver grades, the Slot Manager and external NuBus chassis answers — is documented once on the 7500's page: [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §4.1, §3.7. The sockets did not change; the 1997 note's power accounting is the tower enclosure's ([1] §"Larger Power Supply" p. 12; [pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §4.1), and no supply rating for the desktop enclosure is stated in either generation's notes (§6.11). The machine's three sockets, the DAV connector and the whole Grand Central I/O stack contend for the one Bandit's single bus exactly as on the 7500 ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §2.1) — the structural limit that distinguishes the 7500-class board from the two-bridge 8500/9500/8600/9600 machines ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.1, §6.5).

### 4.2 The DAV connector

The connector is present with the AV subsystem whose signals it taps, and the interface itself — the 60-pin connector, the 4:2:2 unscaled digital video input signal, the digital audio input to the codec, the raw-data-over-DAV / compressed-data-over-PCI compression-card architecture, and the pin set — is [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §4.2's, cited not restated. The generation keeps the on-board video-input subsystem this machine has, so the DAV path here is the 7500's in both directions; the `.DAVAudio` driver rebuild (§3.6) is the one 1997 software delta on the audio half of it ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).

### 4.3 Memory, VRAM, cache and processor expansion

- **Memory**: eight slots, any population from one DIMM up, same-size pairs in corresponding slots for the interleave, inside the 4-to-64 MB bank and 128 MB two-bank-DIMM ceilings, EDO or FPM devices operated in fast page mode, 5 V only, 1.1-inch height only (§2.3) — ceiling 1 GB ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.2).
- **VRAM**: four slots at 1 MB per SIMM, 2 MB as shipped to 4 MB maximum (inherited — [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.4).
- **Cache**: the module socket grows the L2 within the four size codes, 512 KB / 1 MB / 4 MB ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.3; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8).
- **Processor**: the card is the stated upgrade path ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2, §6.5), and the generation's documented ceiling is the 604e's 5× bus multiple [1] §"PowerPC 604e Microprocessor" p. 3 — 250 MHz at this machine's 50 MHz bus, with the later speed grades of the family documented nowhere in the evidence corpus beyond the family index's class statement ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; §6.1 here). A card swap moves the bus clock and everything downstream of it (§2.2). The kernel this machine's ROM carries already tables the cache geometries of a later generation's upgrade parts (§3.1) — the 7600 line is the platform's upgrade-hosting configuration, and the ROM treats it as such.

### 4.4 Internal drive bays

The enclosure is the 7500's, so its drive law is: "The computer has space for two additional internal drives: one 5.25-inch drive (optional CD-ROM occupies this space) and one 3.5-inch drive" [2] p. 8 — one bay fewer in the 3.5-inch class than the 8500's two ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §4.4). The 7600's shipped complement fits it: hard disk in the 3.5-inch internal position, AppleCD 1600i in the 5.25-inch bay, floppy in its own — leaving the spare bay count the 7500's, unlike the tower machines' seven spaces ([pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §4.3). Which SCSI bus serves the spare bay's connector is not stated, as it was not stated for the 7500 ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §6 item 9). The generation's bay-replacement rules are the tower's [1] §"Accessible Devices" p. 11; no 1997-era replacement rules for the desktop enclosure are documented (§6.7).

### 4.5 What this machine does not expand by

- **No NuBus**, and no Slot Manager without an external NuBus chassis — the 7500's contract, inherited ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §4.5).
- **No video output path.** The 8500/8600's television encoder chain is not fitted and there is no connector for it; composite/S-video output can only come from a PCI card (§3.4).
- **No second PCI bus.** The `$F4000000` slice of the family map ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.1) is unpopulated; a bridge cannot be added by the owner.
- **No multiprocessing.** The generation's MP card is the 9600/200MP's [1] §"Dual Processor Configuration" p. 3; no MP 7600 is documented, and the platform's MP surface — Hammerhead's three registers — is the 9500/9600 pair's to exercise ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.3, §6.5; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.5–§2.7). A uniprocessor model answers with `TwoCPU` clear and `WhoAmI` = primary.
- **No taller DIMMs, no 390 W supply, no seven bays, no Zip.** The tower enclosure's allowances are the 8600's and 9600's ([pm8600.md](pm8600.md) §1.5; [pm9600.md](pm9600.md) §1.5); this machine's desktop chassis takes none of them (§2.3, §4.4, §3.5).
- **Ceilings**: 1 GB RAM, 4 MB VRAM, 4 MB L2, three PCI sockets, one DAV card.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The boot contract is the family's — Open Firmware performs the hardware bring-up, `HWInit` is only the POST diagnostics object, and the default `boot-command` loads the Mac OS boot path out of the ROM itself ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.2). The sequence below walks that contract naming this machine's branch points; everything unmarked is the family doc's.

1. **Reset.** The 604e fetches the hardware-reset vector at `$FFF00100`, inside the ROM decode, and enters the NanoKernel's reset entry ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7 step 1, §3.4).
2. **NanoKernel.** The kernel builds the BAT and page-table mappings from the 1997 configuration record — the same templates and `LA_*` constants as 1995, with every I/O mapping now Guarded as well as cache-inhibited ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.5, §6.5; [4]). The 1997 kernel hands the 68k emulator an IPL halfword with bit 15 set — a kernel–emulator contract change, not a hardware one (*observed*; [4]) — and tables the upgrade-part cache geometries of §3.1 against the CPU it finds.
3. **Open Firmware.** The byte-identical image runs the 7500's decode — Hammerhead `$39`, machine-class bit 31, BoxID bit 13 set and bit 11 clear — and selects `"AAPL,7500"` ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.6, §6.5; the 7500's outcome *observed*, this machine's *inferred* from the unchanged registers and firmware, [4]). It builds the 7500's tree in full — the one `bandit` node, Grand Central with `mesh`, the `chaos`/`control` display bus with Control's BARs assigned ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §5; [bandit.md](bandit.md) §3.10) — with the CPU node carrying `bus-frequency` and `clock-frequency` for the installed card, sizes the eight DIMM slots through Hammerhead, manages NVRAM (reformatting a blank store before the OS reads it), and sets up `aliases`, `options` and `chosen` ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7 step 3).
4. **POST.** The diagnostics object runs; its `.L2DataCacheTest` reads the presence pin — set, the module being factory-installed — decodes size code `b01` (256 KB), and writes the enable word ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.4; §2.4 here). Results log into NVRAM through Grand Central's bank-select and data apertures ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7 step 4; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.6). The probes are instruction-identical to the 1995 POST's — this step is where the 7600 most differs from a factory 7500, whose empty cache slot skips the test entirely ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §5 step 4) — while the bring-up Hammerhead sees is unchanged ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).
5. **`boot /AAPL,ROM`.** The 68k emulator starts the Toolbox half. The identification routine synthesizes the same product ID the 7500's registers produce and lands on the 7500's product record — `BoxFlag` `$3E`, gestalt machine type 68 ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [1] §"Machine Identification" p. 17; [pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §5 step 5, *observed* on the 7500) — installing the TNT decoder record, byte-identical to 1995's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.6; [4]). The 68k half's CPU-identification dispatch reads the 604e's PVR — version 9 — and takes the not-a-601 path the whole 604 family shares ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3).
6. **Mac OS start-up.** The machine ships Mac OS 7.5.5 with the new enabler [1] §"System Software" p. 16. Low-memory setup, driver loading, then the native video driver's Control-register mode-set of the built-in display, pixel clock through the Cuda's I²C path ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.4, §5) — now through the 1997-rebuilt Control driver ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; §3.3), and with the SCSI Manager revision and 31-second disk timeout of §3.5 in force beneath it.
7. **Boot-device search.** The Start Manager honours the PRAM default boot device first and walks the drive queue only after the spin-up timeout ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7, *observed*). This machine's drive queue is two deep at the factory — the 2 GB disk and the 1600i — with no removable-media boot device of the 8600's kind [1] §"Built-in Removable Cartridge Drive" p. 5.

## 6. Open questions

1. **The earlier grades' bus rates.** The /120 and /132's processor speeds are named [1] Table 1-1 p. 2, their bus rates are not; 120 MHz is unreachable from a 50 MHz bus at any 604e ratio [3] §8.1 p. 8-2, so the candidates are 40 MHz (3:1) or 48 MHz (5:2) for the /120 and 44 MHz (3:1) for the /132 — the rates the 8500's own 120/132 MHz grades are inferred to use ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.2) — and the family doc's 50 MHz statement ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5) is documented only for the /200. No Apple document in the corpus settles it.
2. **Which processor the /120 and /132 carry.** The note's 604e claim covers the new models [1] §"PowerPC 604e Microprocessor" p. 3; the /120 and /132 are previous models, and no document in evidence names their part — 604e or plain 604. The distinction is guest-visible through the PVR (version 9 versus 4, [3] pp. 2-6, C-3).
3. **The earlier grades' configurations.** The /120 and /132 have no rows in the configurations table [1] Table 1-4 p. 7; that they match the /200's 32 MB / 2 GB / 256 KB population is *inferred — unverified* from the generation-level ranges [1] §"Configurations" p. 7.
4. **The 1997 video drivers.** The Control, Platinum and Sixty6 drivers are three of the six changed Toolbox resources ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5), extracted but undisassembled in both generations [4]. Whether this machine's monitor-mode or gamma tables differ from the 7500's — the "new display timings" hypothesis — is unverified.
5. **The factory memory population.** The 32 MB configuration [1] Table 1-4 p. 7 is not broken down by module count or placement; that it leaves the factory interleaved (same-size modules in corresponding slots, [tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3) is *inferred — unverified*.
6. **The L2 module's form factor.** The 1995 note's "cache SIMM" and the 1997 note's "DIMM installed in a slot" ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.3; [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 4) name the same socket's module differently; whether the module, its connector or only the word changed is not stated anywhere in evidence.
7. **The drive-to-bus assignment.** The 7500 note puts the hard disk on the internal (MESH) bus and the CD-ROM on the external bus's internal end ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §3.6); the 1997 note names no bus for the 7600's 2 GB disk or its 1600i, and no 1997-era bay rule for the desktop enclosure is stated [1] §"Accessible Devices" p. 11 being the tower's. Both placements here are *inferred — unverified*.
8. **The codec silicon.** The firmware publishes AWACS on every 1997 machine ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5), and no driver takes any other path; whether this board carries the AWACS part itself or the register-compatible superset is not determinable from the ROM and is not stated by the note (§3.6).
9. **The security bar's vintage.** The 1997 note describes the bar on the 7300 and 7600 [1] §"Security Bar" p. 6; the 1995 notes describe no such part for the 7500 ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §1.3), and whether the bar is new with the 7600 line, or a running change to the 7500's case, is not stated.
10. **Which ROM release a given 7600 shipped.** The two 1997 images are identified by checksum, the first covering the 7300/7600/8600/9600 and the second named for the 8600/9600 ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §1.3; [4]); no Apple document ties a checksum to a serial range or a speed grade, and whether any 7600 carries the second image is unattested.
11. **The desktop enclosure's power budget.** The 7500's 50 W combined slot budget ([pm7500.md](pm7500.md) §4.1) is not restated for the 7600, and the 1997 note's supply figures are the tower's [1] §"Larger Power Supply" p. 12; no document states the desktop machine's supply rating or whether its slot budget moved.
12. **The 7300's platform.** The 7300's Catalyst-platform attribution rests on the community evidence and the machine-table grouping ([5]; [tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.4 describes the architecture); no Apple document in the corpus states the 7300's logic-board composition, its processor-card presence, or its video-output capability, and its identity outcome (`AAPL,7300`, gestalt 109, [1] §"Machine Identification" p. 17) has not been observed on a 7300 boot.
13. **The `/200`'s market beyond the note.** "The Power Macintosh 7600/200 is available only in Japan" [1] §"New Models" p. 2 note is the only availability statement in evidence; what the note's frame implies about the /120 and /132's own introduction dates — and therefore about which grades the February 1997 ROM release actually first shipped in — is not established by any dated document.
