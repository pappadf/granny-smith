# Power Macintosh 8600/200

The Power Macintosh 8600/200 is the 1997 reissue of the Power Macintosh 8500: the same TNT logic board with the family's full audio-video capability — video input *and* the second, television-bound video output stream — behind a 200 MHz PowerPC 604e on the removable processor card, running the system bus at 50 MHz, in a new tower enclosure shared with no machine but the 9600. Because its ROM carries a byte-identical Open Firmware image, the machine answers `"AAPL,8500"` in every way software can ask ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5); this page carries everything the identity does not say — the enclosure, the card and its clocks, the memory and drive population, and the two 1997 ROM releases the machine can carry.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

Apple's developer note for the 1997 generation is a delta guide: "This developer note describes only the changes that make the new models different from their earlier counterparts" [1] §"About This Developer Note" p. vii, and its comparison table names the 8600/200's earlier counterparts as the Power Macintosh 8500/150 and 8500/180 [1] Table 1-1 p. 2. The changes the table does list are the machine's definition: "Clock speed 200 MHz, new tower enclosure, 2 GB AV-capable hard drive, 12X-speed CD-ROM drive, internal Zip cartridge drive" [1] Table 1-1 p. 2 — with "video input and output" called out in the configurations table [1] Table 1-4 p. 7 and stated plainly in the video section: "The Power Macintosh 8600, like the earlier Power Macintosh 8500, has both video inputs and outputs" [1] §"Video Input" p. 5 note. Of the four machines the note covers, the 8600 is the only one that keeps both AV directions (the 7300 drops video input; the 9600 has no on-board video at all — [tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).

Everything the note does not change is the 8500's by construction: "Except for the changes described in this developer note, the features of the faster Power Macintosh 7300, 7600, 8600, and 9600 computers are the same as those of their earlier counterparts" [1] §"Compatibility Issues" p. 17. That makes the logic board the 8500's in full — the family's AV configuration ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.2; [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §1.1; §3.2 below) — with the shared platform documented once in the family doc ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2–§5).

The identity consequence is the family doc's headline for the 1997 wave and this machine's defining quirk: because the 1997 ROMs carry a byte-identical Open Firmware image, the 8600 reports the 8500's identity outright, and "software can only tell them apart by the CPU's PVR, the L2 configuration or the AV hardware, never by the identity registers" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5). For the 8500/8600 pair specifically, two of those three discriminators fail (§1.2), which makes the machine the purest case of the 1997 identity reuse in the family.

### 1.2 Identity values — and what separates this machine from an 8500

The identity machinery — the two independent decodes reading Hammerhead and Grand Central's BoxID register — is the family doc's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.6; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3). This machine's values are exactly the 8500's:

What actually separates a stock 8600/200 from a stock 8500 is worth stating precisely, because two of the family doc's three discriminators do not work for this pair (*inferred — unverified* as a conclusion; each input fact is cited):

- **The AV hardware does not separate them.** Both machines carry the full AV set — Plan B input, Sixty6/7187 second output ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.4; [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.4, §3.5).
- **The stock L2 does not separate them.** Both leave the factory with a 256 KB second-level module installed ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4; [1] Table 1-4 p. 7).
- **The PVR always separates them.** The 8500's 604 reports processor version number 4; the 8600's 604e reports version number 9, with the revision level starting at `$0100` [3] §"Processor Version Register" pp. 2-6, C-3. An `mfpvr` read is the one instruction that answers the question "which machine is this?" with the machines' own silicon.
- **The bus clock separates the /200 from an 8500/120.** The 8600's 50 MHz processor bus is documented ([1] Table 1-2 p. 4); the 8500/120's is an inference from the one-third ratio law ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.2), and the /150 and /180's bus rates are stated by no document in evidence (§6.12).

The device tree the 8600's firmware publishes is therefore the 8500's tree — both `bandit` nodes, the `chaos`/`control` display bus, the `planb` and `sixty6` AV nodes, the same `reg` properties, part numbers and interrupt numbers ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]); a second entry in a machine table that folds the two machines into one name is [5]'s `AAPL,8500` / "PowerMac 8500/8600".

### 1.4 Factory configurations

The documented configuration is one row of the note's table [1] Table 1-4 p. 7: 32 MB of DRAM, a 2 GB hard disk, a 256 KB L2 cache, and the special features "Built-in Zip drive, AV-capable hard disk, video input and output". The generation-level statements around the table also bind this machine: "The new models … come with 16 or 32 MB of main RAM installed in RAM DIMM slots. All models have a built-in second-level (L2) cache of either 256 or 512 KB" [1] §"Configurations" p. 7, and "All the new models include a built-in 12X-speed CD-ROM drive" [1] p. 7 note.

The 32 MB load-out is four 8 MB DIMMs or two 16 MB DIMMs; that the factory ships a same-size population in corresponding slots — so the machine leaves the factory with its 128-bit interleave enabled ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3) — is *inferred — unverified*: the note documents the rule, not the shipped population (§6.6). The same inference was recorded for the 8500's 16 MB pair ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §1.4).

### 1.5 The new tower enclosure

The enclosure is the 8600's largest physical delta and its only mechanical one: "The new tower enclosure is used for the Power Macintosh 8600 and 9600 computers" [1] §"Appearance of the New Tower Enclosure" p. 8 — the 8500's tower [2] §"Features of the Power Macintosh 8500" p. 9 is gone, and with it the 8500's drive-space limit of one additional internal storage device [2] Table 1-3 p. 5. The note's own summary of what the new enclosure buys [1] §"New Tower Enclosure" p. 8:

- more space for internal storage devices — seven device spaces (§4.3)
- a larger power supply, 390 W total [1] §"Larger Power Supply" p. 12
- easier access: the whole left side panel unlatches and lifts off [1] §"Removable Side Panel" p. 13, and the power supply and disk drives — the components that obstructed the DIMM slots on the 8500 and 9500 — tilt out on a hinged subchassis [1] §"Hinged Subchassis" p. 14
- a locking feature: a rear tab engages the side panel's latch, held locked by a security cable or padlock through its hole [1] §"Locking Feature" p. 15
- space for larger-size DIMMs — the 1.255-inch MO-161 height, this machine and the 9600 only (§2.3)

The dimensions [1] Table 1-5 p. 10:

| | Height | Width | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| millimeters | 429 | 224 | 440 |
| inches | 17.3 | 8.8 | 17.3 |

The back-panel furniture is the 8500's, unchanged by the compatibility clause and drawn for this machine in the note's back view [1] Figure 1-4 p. 8: the two serial ports, the ADB port, the sound jacks, the external SCSI connector, the Ethernet connectors and the A/V panel of composite and S-video inputs and outputs ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.7; [2] pp. 33–34).

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The clock architecture is the family's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §5.4): the card supplies the system bus clock, PCI runs at 33 MHz asynchronously, the video bus is synchronous with the main system bus, DRAM refresh is a CAS-before-RAS cycle every 15.6 µs, and the two SCSI cells run at their own rates. This machine's column of it:

| Clock | Value | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Processor core | 200 MHz | [1] Table 1-2 p. 4 |
| Processor (AR) bus | 50 MHz | [1] Table 1-2 p. 4 |
| Core-to-bus ratio | 4:1 — a ratio the plain 604 does not support (§3.1) | [3] p. 8-2, Table 7-6 pp. 7-37–7-38 |
| Time base and decrementer | one quarter of the bus clock — 12.5 MHz; the time base counter is enabled by the TBE input | [3] pp. 2-7, 2-55 |
| PCI bus | 33 MHz, published by both bridge nodes as `clock-frequency` | [tnt.md](tnt.md) §5.4; [bandit.md](bandit.md) §1.5 |
| Video bus | synchronous with the main system bus | [tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.1 |
| SCSI cells | 53C94 at 25 MHz, MESH at 50 MHz | [tnt.md](tnt.md) §5.4 |
| AWACS sample clocks | the 44.1 kHz family by rate code | [tnt.md](tnt.md) §5.4; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §3.7 |

Because the bus clock is a property of the installed card ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2), every rate downstream of the card — the AR bus, the 12.5 MHz time base and decrementer, the synchronous video bus — moves with a processor upgrade; the 33 MHz PCI clock and the two SCSI cell clocks do not. The generation's card ceiling is 5× the bus clock [1] p. 3; the later 8600 speed grades are outside the note's coverage ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; §6.1 here).

### 2.3 Memory population: the 1997 DIMM rules

The banking law — eight slots in four corresponding pairs, the 128-bit interleave, contiguity through the bank base registers, the 4 MB-to-64 MB bank and 128 MB two-bank-DIMM ceilings, 1 GB total — is the 8500's and is not restated here ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.3; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.10, §3.2). The 1997 note restates the interface and adds three rules of its own:

- **The DIMM itself is unchanged.** "The method of RAM expansion in the new models is essentially the same as in their earlier counterparts: 168-pin, 8-byte DIMMs as defined in the JEDEC MO-161 specification" [1] §"RAM DIMM Modifications" p. 18.
- **EDO devices are accepted — and neutralized.** "The RAM expansion DIMMs in the new computers can use either extended data out (EDO) or fast page mode (FPM) DRAM devices. The computer always operates the devices in fast page mode" [1] p. 18. An EDO DIMM is electrically tolerated but buys no speed: the controller's access discipline stays the 8500's FPM timing, with its 70 ns-or-faster device law and load limits ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.3).
- **Power on the slots is 5 V only.** "As in the previous Power Macintosh models, only 5-V power is available on the RAM DIMM slots. Devices that require 3.3-V power cannot be used" [1] p. 18 note.

The mechanical rule has two statements in the note, both about the enclosure's clearance. The `Taller RAM DIMM` section: "The JEDEC MO-161 specification shows three possible heights for the 8-byte DIMM. All Power Macintosh computers can accommodate DIMMs with the shortest of the three specified heights: 1.100 inches. The new Power Macintosh 8600 and 9600 can also accommodate DIMMs with a height of 1.255 inches" [1] §"Taller RAM DIMM" p. 16 — and the note's second statement says the same of "the new tower enclosure" [1] p. 18. The 1995 note's DIMM-dimensions section, by contrast, accepts "the shorter two of three heights, maximum 1.255 inches" for the 8500 ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.3) — a contradiction between the two notes this page does not resolve (§6.5).

### 2.4 The second-level cache as shipped

The L2 architecture is Hammerhead's — write-back, direct-mapped, allocate on read or write, synchronous-burst-SRAM data store, size sensed electrically through the module connector's `CachePrsnt` and `CacheSize(1:0)` pins rather than configured ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3; [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8–§2.9). The generation-level fact the 1997 note adds: "All the new models include a second-level (L2) cache" — with a claimed "performance improvement of up to 40% over machines without such a cache" [1] §"Second-Level Cache" p. 4 — where the 7500 and 8500 left the factory with the module optional and the 9500 soldered its 512 KB down ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3, §6.1). On this machine, "The L2 cache in the other models consists of a 256 KB DIMM installed in a slot" [1] p. 4 — the `other models` being every 1997 machine but the 9600 pair, whose cache stays on the logic board [1] p. 4.

The size the sensing path reports here is code `b01` — 256 KB — which POST's `.L2DataCacheTest` decodes on this personality's cache path at every boot ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.4; [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4); the module grows to 512 KB, 1 MB or 4 MB within the existing code field ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4). One terminology delta is worth pinning because it is easy to read as a hardware change and the note does not explain it: the 1995 note calls the 8500's module a "cache SIMM" on a connector carrying `CachePrsnt`/`CacheSize` [2] §"Second-Level Cache SIMM" p. 45, the 1997 note calls this machine's a "DIMM" [1] p. 4 — whether the module, its connector or only the word changed is not stated by any document in evidence (§6.4).

The timing law is unchanged by the processor swap: the 8500 note's L2 operation section describes the 604-era handshake ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4), and the 604e "has the same signal configuration as the 604", the differences being internal (new precharge timings for `ABB`, `DBB`, `ARTRY` and `SHD`, with `ARTRY`/`SHD` precharge disable in `HID0[7]`) [3] p. 1-8.

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 The processor card and the PowerPC 604e

The card is the family's — the main processor and its associated clock circuits on a plug-in card that "provides an upgrade path to a faster or more powerful microprocessor" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2) — and it supplies the board's 50 MHz bus clock (§2.2). This machine's card carries the generation's standard part: "All the new models use the PowerPC 604e microprocessor" [1] §"PowerPC 604e Microprocessor" p. 3; the multiprocessor card is the 9600/200MP's alone [1] §"Dual Processor Configuration" p. 3, and no multiprocessor 8600 appears in any document in evidence (§6.9).

The 604e is the 604's die revision, and the two published enhancements the note names are the ones a user can feel: "on-chip data and instruction caches of 32 KB each" — double the 604's 16 KB — and "processor clock speed up to 5 times the bus clock speed" [1] p. 3. The manual's fuller inventory, for the parts that matter to this machine [3] §"Overview" pp. 1-7–1-8:

- **Caches**: separate 32 KB instruction and data caches, four-way set associative, 32-byte blocks, physically indexed — "each cache has twice as many sets [as the 604's], logically separated into 128 sets of odd lines and 128 sets of even lines" — with line-fill buffer forwarding (data made available as it arrives, not only the critical double word) and three copy-back write buffers to the 604's one.
- **A new SPR surface**: `HID1` (SPR 1009), a read-only register whose bits 0–3 report the current `PLL_CFG` value [3] p. 2-12 — software can read the card's clock configuration back out of the CPU — plus `MMCR1` and counters `PMC3`/`PMC4` for the performance monitor.
- **A wider PLL**: processor-to-bus ratios of 1:1, 3:2, 2:1, 5:2, 3:1, 4:1 and 7:2 — of which 5:2, 7:2 and 4:1 "are not supported in the 604" [3] p. 8-2. The 8600's 200 MHz core on its 50 MHz bus is the 4:1 ratio — encoding `PLL_CFG[0–3] = 1010` in Table 7-6 [3] pp. 7-37–7-38 — which is why the /200's clock pair is a 604e-only combination: an 8500's 604 cannot be configured for it (*observed* as an arithmetic consequence of the two documents' figures; the ratio table is the manual's).
- **Split voltage planes**: 2.5 V core and 3.3 V I/O, with the design rule that "for replacement compatibility, 604/604e designs should provide both 2.5-V and 3.3-V planes and the ability to connect those two planes together and disable the 2.5-V plane for operation with a 604" [3] p. 1-8 — the electrical shape of the card's upgrade obligation; whether this card implements the dual-plane rule is not documented, that it must accept a 604-era card is the platform's contract ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2).

Firmware-side, the CPU identity flows the family's two ways: `AAPL,cpu-id` with the not-a-601 flag ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3), and the 68k half's CPU-identification dispatch at every boot ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2). The 1997 kernels add one 604e-era behaviour around the new SPRs: in the late-revision ROM image the kernel probes SDA (SPR 959) independently of MMCR0, and `mfspr`/`mtspr` against SDA raise a privileged-instruction fault when the flag is clear, where the 1995 kernels assumed the pair came together (*observed* in the ROM's NanoKernel component; [4]). That is a kernel–emulator contract, not a hardware fact — but a CPU model for the 8600 must present SDA and MMCR0 independently or the shipped kernel mis-classifies it.

### 3.2 The inherited board: two Bandits, Grand Central, the whole I/O stack

The chip set is the 8500's, and the 1997 ROM comparison is what turns that from a compatibility claim into an established fact: Open Firmware is byte-identical between the 1995 and 1997 images, and the diagnostics object is rebuilt with every hardware probe instruction-identical ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]). So the device tree this machine's firmware builds is the 8500's tree, byte for byte: both `bandit` nodes (aliases `pci1` at `$F2000000`, `pci2` at `$F4000000`), Grand Central behind Bandit 1 at `$F3000000` with its eleven DBDMA channels, the `chaos`/`control` display bus, the `planb` capture engine, the `sixty6` convolver, and every `reg` property, Apple part number and `AAPL,interrupts` value of the 8500's set ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.1–§3.2, §4; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §4.1; [bandit.md](bandit.md) §1.4; [4]).

The wiring consequences are inherited with it and are documented once on the 8500's page: the three PCI sockets' bridge assignment is unresolved in the sources, Bandit 2's declared interrupt line collides on paper with the built-in video's vertical-blanking line, the per-socket interrupt lines are unpublished, and the Sixty6/Plan B register surfaces remain the platform's least-documented ground ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.2, §6 items 3–6). None of these open questions moved between the 8500 and the 8600, because the bytes that would answer them did not move either.

The cells' interrupt, DMA and clock behaviour — the 32-source interrupt file, the VIA1 cascade behind the Cuda, the DBDMA channel-number-equals-interrupt-number identity, the cell apertures — is the family's and each device page's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §4.3, §5.1; [grand-central.md](grand-central.md) §2.5, §3.2; [dbdma.md](dbdma.md) §1.4).

### 3.3 Video: the display path and both AV streams

The 8600 keeps the 8500's entire video subsystem, which the 1997 note attests in one line — "The Power Macintosh 8600, like the earlier Power Macintosh 8500, has both video inputs and outputs" [1] §"Video Input" p. 5 note — and the compatibility clause extends to the rest ([1] p. 17). The three streams and their wiring are the 8500's pages':

- **The monitor path** — Chaos bridging the video bus to the processor bus, Control providing addressing and control with its 64 MB VRAM aperture and 4 KB register block, RaDACal converting and generating timing and the hardware cursor, four VRAM slots at 1 MB per SIMM, the DB-15 monitor connector with its sense-code system, the pixel clock programmed through the Cuda's I²C passthrough: [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.3, with the VRAM budget table (24 bpp to 17 inch at 2 MB, to 21 inch at 4 MB) and the mode-set path.
- **The input path** — the 8758 ADC and 7196 DESC behind Plan B's two DBDMA channels (write channel for pixels out of the DESC's FIFO, read channel for the 1-bit-per-pixel clip mask), play-through clipping, capture to display, frame grab or QuickTime at up to 24 bpp and 640×480 at 30 frames per second: [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.4.
- **The second output stream** — the Sixty6 RGB-to-YUV convolver and 7187 DENC, NTSC or PAL, mirror or independent image, dual-stream operation with 4 MB of VRAM: [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.5.

The one 1997 change inside the subsystem is in software, and it is the family doc's, not this machine's: of the 180 Toolbox resources, exactly six changed between the 1995 and 1997 ROMs, three of them the video drivers — Control, Platinum and Sixty6 — "consistent with new display timings rather than new silicon" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5). What changed inside the Control and Sixty6 drivers, and whether the 8600's monitor-mode table differs from the 8500's, is not established: neither driver has been disassembled in either generation ([4]; §6.3).

The generation's genlock and dual-stream capability claims carry over with the subsystem ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.5).

### 3.4 Storage: the AV-capable hard disk, the 12X CD-ROM and the Zip drive

The two-bus SCSI architecture is the 8500's ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.6): MESH as the internal controller at up to 10 MB/s, Curio's 53C94 behind the external connector at up to 5 MB/s, both decoded through Grand Central, both in the TNT decoder record ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.6). The 1997 machine's drive complement is new:

- **The hard disk is 2 GB and AV-capable** — "The 2 GB hard disk in the Power Macintosh 8600 can support AV applications. It can transfer video data at 30 frames per second with no dropped frames" [1] §"AV-Capable Hard Disk" p. 4, a rating unique to this machine in the note's coverage. Which of the two SCSI buses it occupies is not restated; on the 8500 the hard disk "is connected to the internal SCSI bus" ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.6), and the same placement here is *inferred — unverified* (§6.7).
- **The CD-ROM is the AppleCD 1600i**, a 12× mechanism "with sustained data transfer rates of 1600 KB per second and a data buffer", sliding tray, reading CD-ROM, CD-ROM XA, CD-I and PhotoCD plus audio [1] §"12X-Speed CD-ROM Drive" p. 5 — in every model of the generation [1] p. 7 note.
- **The Zip drive is this machine's alone**: "One of the new models, the Power Macintosh 8600, has a built-in Iomega Zip removable cartridge drive. The built-in Zip drive is similar to its external counterpart. The user has the option of placing a system folder on a Zip cartridge and starting up the computer from the Zip drive" [1] §"Built-in Removable Cartridge Drive" p. 5 — the third front-accessible bay occupant (§4.3) and a boot device the Start Manager can be pointed at (§5).

The bay-replacement rules confirm the drive class for all three front bays — "The floppy disk drive can be replaced with a 3.5-inch SCSI hard disk drive up to 1 inch high. The CD-ROM drive and the Zip drive can be replaced with 5.25-inch or smaller SCSI devices up to 1.625 inches high" [1] §"Accessible Devices" p. 11 — but name no bus: the note does not say which SCSI bus the Zip drive sits on, or how it is terminated (§6.7).

The floppy is the 8500's internal 1.4 MB Apple SuperDrive on SWIM III ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.6). Two ROM-side storage behaviours changed for the generation [1] §"Changes in the ROM" p. 16: "The SCSI Manager has been revised to correct a problem with the asynchronous receive-acknowledge signal in the SCSI controller", and "The default value of the hard disk time-out has been increased from 25 to 31 seconds" — the timeout an SCSI Manager that must wait on a spun-down AV drive now waits five seconds longer to declare.

### 3.5 Sound: the codec the machine reports

The sound subsystem is the 8500's electrically and in its jack set ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.7), and the 1997 firmware publishes the same codec the 1995 firmware did: the only codec string anywhere in the byte-identical Open Firmware image is AWACS, so on a machine booted from these ROMs "no driver takes a Screamer path … even if the board carries the register-compatible superset part" ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5) — the firmware-visible fact, with the silicon question left open there and here (§6.8). The one 1997-updated audio component is `.DAVAudio`, the AV audio component of the six changed Toolbox resources ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]) — a driver delta on the DAV-facing audio path (§4.2), not a codec identification.

### 3.6 The boot ROM as this machine carries it

The image is the family's 4 MB ROM in its two February 1997 releases, header checksums `$960E4BE9` — covering the 7300, 7600, 8600 and 9600 — and `$960FC647`, the late-revision 8600/9600 refresh ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §1.3; [4]). The component-level comparison against the 1995 pair is the family doc's record, cited not restated: Open Firmware byte-identical across all four images; the configuration record changed only in the I/O pages' Guarded attribute; the NanoKernel and exception table rebuilt (kernel–emulator contract details, not hardware); POST rebuilt with every hardware probe instruction-identical; six of the 180 Toolbox resources changed ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).

What is this machine's own is the ROM's Apple-documented change list, which names four behaviours [1] §"Changes in the ROM" p. 16:

- the SCSI Manager revision for the asynchronous receive-acknowledge problem (§3.4);
- the hard-disk timeout raised from 25 to 31 seconds (§3.4);
- "The software now sets the guarded bit in the MMU for cache-inhibited address space" — the Apple-documented counterpart of the configuration-record change the ROM comparison observes ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.5, §6.5), and the one a guest can see: a page-table walk on this machine finds every I/O mapping cache-inhibited **and** Guarded, where a 1995 machine's are cache-inhibited only;
- "The ROM has been modified to fix bugs that affected multiprocessor operation" — a fix in the MP-capable kernel that this uniprocessor machine carries in its ROM without exercising.

One machine-specific question the second image raises: the `$960FC647` NanoKernel contains a special case the 1995 kernels and the first 1997 image lack — a `mfpvr`-and-compare against PVR upper halfword `$0060` that skips the entire performance-monitor SPR probing sequence for a CPU the kernel refuses to probe (*observed* at NanoKernel blob offset `$3134`, in neither earlier image; [4]). The corpus cannot identify that CPU (§6.2) — but the image is named for the 8600 and 9600, so whatever silicon trips the path is late-revision 8600 hardware.

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The three PCI slots

The slot complement is the 8500's three, inherited through the compatibility clause [1] p. 17, and the full card contract — 33 MHz, PCI Specification Revision 2.0 cards in the 5 V signaling standard and the standard ISA fence, the supported and absent signal sets, the 50 W combined slot budget, the FCode boot-driver grades — is documented once on the 8500's page: [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §4.1. Nothing changes the sockets; what the 1997 note adds is the enclosure-level power accounting: the supply provides "a total of 390 watts" and "can support as many as six 15-watt cards or four 25-watt cards", with the per-device rail budget of Table 1-6 [1] §"Larger Power Supply" p. 12:

| Device | Voltage | Current | Power |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expansion card (15-watt grade) | +5 V | 3 A | 15 W |
| | +12 V | 0.5 A | 6 W |
| | −12 V | 0.1 A | 1.2 W |
| | +3.3 V | 2 A | 6.6 W |
| Expansion card (25-watt grade) | +5 V | 5 A | 25 W |
| | +12 V | 0.5 A | 6 W |
| | −12 V | 0.1 A | 1.2 W |
| | +3.3 V | 2 A | 6.6 W |
| Storage devices | +5 V | 9 A | 45 W |
| | +12 V | 3 A | 36 W |
| | +12 V | 7.5 A peak | — |

(peak power is for startup only [1] p. 12 note.) The six-card and four-card capability figures are written for the enclosure the 9600 shares — the 8600's three sockets cannot host six cards, and whether the 8600's own combined slot budget remains the 8500's 50 W or tracks the new supply is not stated (§6.10). The slot-to-bridge and per-slot interrupt assignments stay where the 8500's sources left them ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.2, §6 items 3 and 5).

### 4.2 The DAV connector

The connector is present and located as on the 8500 — the removable side panel exposes "PCI expansion slots, the DAV connector, and the space for internal RAID storage devices" [1] §"Removable Side Panel" p. 13 — and the interface itself (the 60-pin connector, the 4:2:2 unscaled digital video input signal, the digital audio input to the codec, the raw-data-over-DAV / compressed-data-over-PCI compression-card architecture) is [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §4.2's, cited not restated. The 1997 generation keeps the on-board A/V subsystem this machine has, so the DAV path here is the 8500's both directions; the `.DAVAudio` driver rebuild (§3.5) is the one 1997 software delta on the audio half of it ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).

### 4.3 Internal storage: the seven device spaces

The new enclosure's storage law [1] §"Internal Storage Devices" p. 10: "The new tower enclosure has spaces for up to seven internal storage devices. Four of those spaces are accessible from the front; of those four, three can accommodate 5.25-inch devices and one can accommodate a 3.5-inch device. The other spaces are not accessible from the front; they can accommodate three 3.5-inch devices or one 5.25-inch device and one 3.5-inch device."

- **The four accessible bays.** The machine ships with three occupied — floppy, CD-ROM, Zip [1] §"Accessible Devices" p. 11 — leaving one front bay free. Each bay has a removable front panel, with media openings for removable-media devices, reached by pulling a side tab with the side panel off [1] p. 11. Replacement devices must be SCSI and fit the height limits: 1 inch in the 3.5-inch bay, 1.625 inches in the 5.25-inch bays [1] p. 11.
- **The three inaccessible spaces.** One is the startup hard disk's bay, replaceable with any 3.5-inch SCSI drive up to 1 inch high [1] §"Inaccessible Devices" p. 11. The other two are the bottom drive carrier — one 5.25-inch or two 3.5-inch SCSI drives, the note's example being RAID stacks — which "must be in place whenever the computer is operating" to contain electromagnetic interference [1] pp. 11–12. The supply offers two power connectors for the added drives, and "The user must install power cables and a PCI expansion card to provide the interface to the added drives" [1] p. 12: drives beyond the machine's two SCSI buses need a PCI host adapter, and their bandwidth lands on a slot's share of §4.1's budget.
- **The 9600-only tradeoff.** The note's warning that added bottom drives "occupy the space that would be used by PCI cards in the lowest one or two of the six PCI expansion slots" [1] p. 12 is written for the 9600's six-slot backplane; no equivalent tradeoff is stated for this machine's three slots (§6.11).

### 4.4 Memory, VRAM, cache and processor expansion

- **Memory**: eight slots, any population from one DIMM up, same-size pairs in corresponding slots for the interleave, inside the 4-to-64 MB bank and 128 MB two-bank-DIMM ceilings, EDO or FPM devices operated in fast page mode, 5 V only, 1.1 or 1.255-inch heights (§2.3) — ceiling 1 GB ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.3).
- **VRAM**: four slots at 1 MB per SIMM, 2 MB to 4 MB (inherited — [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §1.3).
- **Cache**: the module socket grows the L2 within the four size codes, 512 KB / 1 MB / 4 MB ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.8).
- **Processor**: the card is the stated upgrade path ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2, §6.5), and the generation's documented ceiling is the 604e's 5× bus multiple [1] p. 3 — 250 MHz at this machine's 50 MHz bus, with the later 8600 speed grades and their cards documented nowhere in the evidence corpus ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; §6.1 here). A card swap moves the bus clock and everything downstream of it (§2.2).

### 4.5 What this machine does not expand by

- **No NuBus**, and no Slot Manager without an external NuBus chassis — the 8500's contract, inherited ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §4.4).
- **No multiprocessing.** The generation's MP card is the 9600/200MP's [1] §"Dual Processor Configuration" p. 3; no MP 8600 is documented, and the platform's MP surface — Hammerhead's three registers — is the 9500/9600 pair's to exercise ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.3, §6.5).
- **Ceilings**: 1 GB RAM, 4 MB VRAM, 4 MB L2, three PCI sockets, one DAV card, seven device spaces, 390 W of supply. The added-drive PCI tradeoff is the 9600's, not this machine's (§4.3).

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The boot contract is the family's — Open Firmware performs the hardware bring-up, `HWInit` is only the POST diagnostics object, and the default `boot-command` loads the Mac OS boot path out of the ROM itself ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.2). The sequence below walks that contract naming this machine's branch points; everything unmarked is the family doc's:

1. **Reset.** The 604e fetches the hardware-reset vector at `$FFF00100`, inside the ROM decode, and enters the NanoKernel's reset entry ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7 step 1).
2. **NanoKernel.** The kernel builds the BAT and page-table mappings from the 1997 configuration record — the same templates and `LA_*` constants as 1995, with every I/O mapping now Guarded as well as cache-inhibited ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §3.5, §6.5). The 1997 kernels hand the 68k emulator an IPL halfword with bit 15 set — a kernel–emulator contract change, not a hardware one ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; [4]). On a machine carrying the `$960FC647` image, the performance-monitor SPR probe is the one guarded by the PVR special case of §3.6.
3. **Open Firmware.** The byte-identical image runs the 8500's decode — Hammerhead `$39`, machine-class bit 31, BoxID bit 11 set and bit 13 clear — and selects `"AAPL,8500"` ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.6, §6.5; *observed*). It builds the 8500's tree in full, with the CPU node carrying `bus-frequency` and `clock-frequency` for this machine's 50 MHz card and 200 MHz core (the rates of [1] Table 1-2 p. 4), sizes the eight DIMM slots through Hammerhead, manages NVRAM — reformatting a blank store before the OS reads it — and sets up `aliases`, `options` and `chosen` ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7 step 3).
4. **POST.** The diagnostics object runs; its `.L2DataCacheTest` reads the presence pin — set, the module being factory-installed — decodes size code 1 (256 KB), and writes the enable word ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.4; [pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4). Results log into NVRAM through Grand Central's bank-select and data apertures ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7 step 4). The probes are instruction-identical to the 1995 POST's, so the bring-up Hammerhead sees is the 8500's ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).
5. **`boot /AAPL,ROM`.** The 68k emulator starts the Toolbox half. The identification routine synthesizes the same software CPU ID the 8500's registers produce, and lands on the 8500's product record — product kind `$3F`, gestalt 69 ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5; *observed*) — installing the TNT decoder record. The 68k half's CPU-identification dispatch reads the 604e's PVR — version 9 — and takes the not-a-601 path the whole 604 family shares ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2; [hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §4.3).
6. **Mac OS start-up.** The machine ships Mac OS 7.5.5 with the new enabler [1] p. 16 — the enabler also supports the earlier counterparts, the 7500 included [1] p. 18. Low-memory setup, driver loading, then the native video driver's Control-register mode-set of the built-in display, pixel clock through the Cuda's I²C path ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.3, §5) — now through the 1997-rebuilt Control and Sixty6 drivers ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5).
7. **Boot-device search.** The Start Manager honours the PRAM default boot device first and walks the drive queue only after the spin-up timeout ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.7, *observed*). This machine's drive queue is three deep at the factory — the AV-capable hard disk, the 1600i, the Zip — and a system folder on a Zip cartridge is a supported startup configuration [1] §"Built-in Removable Cartridge Drive" p. 5, so the PRAM default can legitimately point at removable media the machine cannot see until the cartridge is inserted.

## 6. Open questions

1. **The later speed grades.** The 1997 note covers only the 8600/200; the machine's later cards are the family index's subject, not a documented configuration ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5). No Apple document in the corpus names a faster 8600, its card, its bus ratio or the ROM it shipped.
2. **The `$0060` PVR special case.** The `$960FC647` kernel skips its performance-monitor SPR probe for a CPU whose PVR upper halfword reads `$0060` (*observed* in the image; [4]) — a part the corpus cannot identify against 604e (`$0009`), 604ev or 750. Which shipping configuration carried it, and what the probe sequence did to that silicon, is open.
3. **The 1997 video drivers.** The Control, Platinum and Sixty6 drivers are three of the six changed Toolbox resources ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5), extracted but undisassembled in both generations [4]. Whether the 8600's monitor-mode or gamma tables differ from the 8500's — the "new display timings" hypothesis — is unverified.
4. **The L2 module's form factor.** The 1995 note's "cache SIMM" and the 1997 note's "DIMM installed in a slot" ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.4; [1] p. 4) name the same socket's module differently; whether the module, its connector or only the word changed is not stated anywhere in evidence.
5. **The DIMM-height contradiction.** The 1995 note accepts MO-161 heights to 1.255 inches on the 8500 ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §2.3); the 1997 note states that "Other Power Macintosh computers accommodate only DIMMs with the 1.1-inch height" and grants 1.255 inches to the 8600 and 9600 alone [1] pp. 16, 18. Which statement describes the 8500 correctly — and therefore how much of the taller-DIMM allowance is actually new on this machine — is unresolved.
6. **The factory memory population.** The 32 MB configuration [1] Table 1-4 p. 7 is not broken down by module count or placement; that it leaves the factory interleaved (same-size modules in corresponding slots, [tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.3) is *inferred — unverified*.
7. **The drive-to-bus assignment.** The 8500 note puts the hard disk on the internal (MESH) bus and the CD-ROM on the external bus's internal end ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §3.6); the 1997 note names no bus for the 8600's AV-capable hard disk, the 1600i or the Zip — the bay-replacement rules say only that all are SCSI devices [1] p. 11. The Zip drive's bus, SCSI ID and termination are entirely unattested.
8. **The codec silicon.** The firmware publishes AWACS on every 1997 machine ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §6.5), and no driver takes any other path; whether this board carries the AWACS part itself or the register-compatible superset is not determinable from the ROM and is not stated by the note (§3.5).
9. **A multiprocessor 8600.** The platform's MP surface ([hammerhead.md](hammerhead.md) §2.5–§2.7) is exercised by the 9500/9600 MP cards only; no document in evidence shows a dual-processor 8600, and the MP bug fixes in the 1997 ROM [1] p. 16 hint at MP testing this machine's own configurations never needed.
10. **The slot power budget.** The 8500's 50 W combined slot budget ([pm8500.md](pm8500.md) §4.1) is not restated for the 8600, and the 1997 note's 390 W supply with its six-15-watt/four-25-watt card capability figures [1] p. 12 is written for the enclosure both machines share. Whether the 8600's three sockets see the 8500's 50 W or a share of the new supply's headroom is not stated.
11. **The bottom-drive tradeoff on this machine.** The added-drives-versus-PCI-slots warning [1] p. 12 names the 9600's six slots explicitly; whether the 8600's three-slot backplane has an equivalent geometric conflict with the bottom drive carrier is not stated.
12. **The 8500/180's arithmetic.** The 1997 note names the 8500/180 as one of the 8600's earlier counterparts [1] Table 1-1 p. 2; 180 MHz is not reachable from any 40–50 MHz bus at the plain 604's ratio set ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §2.2; [3] p. 8-2), so the /180 either carries a 604e or runs an unattested bus rate — and neither answer is documented.
13. **The ROM-to-model mapping.** The two 1997 images are identified by checksum, the first covering the 7300/7600/8600/9600 and the second a late-revision 8600/9600 refresh ([tnt.md](tnt.md) §1.3; [4]) — but no Apple document ties a checksum to a serial range or a speed grade, so which 8600s shipped which image is established only by the images' own naming.
