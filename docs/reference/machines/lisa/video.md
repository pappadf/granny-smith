## 1. Overview

### 1.1 What the part is

The Lisa's display is not a peripheral with its own memory: it is a **1-bit bit-mapped page in main
RAM, scanned out by dedicated hardware on the processor board**. The Hardware Manual's summary is
one sentence long: "The video memory is used to display an image organized as a bit map in main
memory" [1] §1 p. 1-2. The video control — address counter, data shift register, timing state
machine and page register — lives on the processor board [1] §4.5; the video fetch runs on the
memory bus as a bus master in its own right, exactly like the DMA logic: "Addresses generated by the
video or the DMA logic are not subject to translation, i.e., they are physical addresses" [1] §4.1
p. 4-4. The analog half — the video amplifier, cathode drive and the horizontal and vertical
deflection circuits — lives on a separate **video board** mounted on the rear wall of the CRT
enclosure, driven by a TTL serial bit stream and sync signals from the processor board [1] Ch. 7
p. 7-1. A software-programmed **contrast** level, latched through a port of the parallel-port 6522
VIA on the I/O board, sets the black/white drive level the video board uses [1] §6.7.3 p. 6-55.

The image is black and white only — "There is no gray scale as such; grays are displayed by
intermixing black and white pixels in the same area" [1] §1 p. 1-1 — on a twelve-inch diagonal
monochrome CRT refreshed at 60 Hz [1] §1 p. 1-1. The same scan hardware feeds a rear-panel
**composite video jack** whose signal levels conform to RS-170, with a 22,400 Hz horizontal sweep
rate and a 60 Hz vertical refresh rate [1] §3.5.5 p. 3-23; the composite signal is the OR of the
serial pixel stream and the composite sync, available simultaneously with the internal CRT [1]
§8.4.4 p. 8-7.

The software contract is unusually small for a display:

- one **write-only register**, the Video Address Latch, that names which 32 KB physical page of
  main RAM is being scanned out (§2.2);
- one **interrupt**, generated at each vertical retrace, enable/disable strobes for it, and a
  status bit that mirrors the retrace (§2.3, §2.4, §3.5);
- one **contrast** byte latched through VIA2 port A (§2.6);
- and three **diagnostic status bits** — the current video output bit, the horizontal sync state,
  and the inverse-video strap (§2.4).

Everything else — the pixel grid, bit ordering, row stride, page placement — is a convention that
software observes when filling the page (§4.1).

### 1.2 Machines that carry it

| Machine | Video configuration |
|---|---|
| Lisa 1 (1983) | same processor-board video control and video board as the Lisa 2; the 1983 Hardware Manual documents one video subsystem for the line [1] Ch. 4, Ch. 7 |
| Lisa 2 (incl. 2/5, 2/10) | **720 × 364**, 1-bit, ~60 Hz, tall rectangular pixels; the subject of this page |
| Macintosh XL (Lisa 2/10 sold from January 1985) | same hardware plus the factory **screen-modification kit**, which rewires the video timing to a **608-pixel-wide square-pixel** raster (§4.7); [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1 |

The Macintosh XL's Boot ROM (the 3A ROM) "will not run the Lisa Office System" and assumes the
screen-modification timing [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1; the video register set is otherwise identical, and
the framebuffer page, base latch and VBL machinery are unchanged — only the scan geometry differs
(§4.7).

### 1.3 Division of labor

The display's hardware is split across three boards, and most of it is invisible to software:

| Function | Owner |
|---|---|
| Scan address generation, word fetch from RAM, parallel-to-serial conversion, HSYNC/VSYNC/CSYNC, VBL interrupt | processor-board **video control** [1] §4.5 |
| Framebuffer storage | a 32 KB page in ordinary main RAM, located by the Video Address Latch (§2.2) |
| Contrast D-to-A and latch | I/O board, through VIA2 port A [1] §6.7.3 |
| Video amplifier, cathode drive, spot-killer, vertical and horizontal deflection, CRT supplies | video board [1] Ch. 7 |
| Drawing, gray patterns, cursor, windows | software — the Boot ROM's mini-graphics package [3] RM248.G and the OS's screen manager [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT |

The bus glue the scan depends on — the memory interleave and the CMUX address selector — is part
of the processor board's memory architecture, described at the platform level in [Lisa](lisa.md)
§2 and §4.1.2; this page covers only what the video control itself does.

### 1.4 The raster

The raster parameters, as documented and as they fall out of the timing figures:

| Parameter | Value | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Visible resolution | **720 × 364** pixels, 1 bit each | [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19; [4] `ScreenSize` |
| Words fetched per line | **45** 16-bit words (= 720 pixels) | [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28 |
| Total lines per page scan | **379**; 364 displayed | [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19 |
| Vertical retrace | vertical sync of **6 lines**, then "vertical sync termination" of a further **9 lines** (15 total) | [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28 |
| Pixel rate | 1 bit per 50 ns (the DOTCK period) | [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19, §4.5.2 p. 4-27 |
| Word fetch interval | one word per 800 ns interleave slot (45 × 800 ns = 36 µs of active video per line) | [1] §4.1 p. 4-4 (*inferred*: the 36 µs figure is the product of the manual's two intervals) |
| Horizontal rate | 22,400 Hz (composite output spec) | [1] §3.5.5 p. 3-23 |
| Frame rate | 60 Hz | [1] §1 p. 1-1, §3.5.5 |
| Retrace status window | the Status Register retrace bit is asserted for **~90 µs** after the start of each retrace | [1] §2.8 Fig. 2-19 |

One internal consistency check pins the numbers together (*inferred* from the manual's figures, not
stated as such anywhere): 45 words × 16 pixels × 50 ns = 36 µs of active video per line, plus
blanking, fits the 44.6 µs implied by the 22,400 Hz horizontal rate; and 379 lines at that line
rate is 16.66 ms — the 60 Hz frame. The Macintosh XL screen-modification kit replaces the
horizontal geometry with 608 visible square pixels (§4.7).

### 1.5 Clocking and the memory interleave

Video timing hangs off the same crystal tree as the CPU. A 20.375 MHz oscillator generates
**DOTCK**, "used to provide timing to the video control, which shifts bits serially to the video
board" [1] §4.3.1 p. 4-21; DOTCK divided by four provides the ~5 MHz **CPUCK** that clocks the
68000 [1] §4.2.1 p. 4-13 (5.09375 MHz, ~196 ns per cycle; [Lisa](lisa.md) §2). The pixel rate is
one DOTCK tick (nominally 50 ns).

The defining constraint of the Lisa's memory system is that **the video fetch owns half of every
memory cycle**. The basic 800 ns 68000 bus cycle is divided into two 400 ns halves: "A 400 ns
video cycle is followed by a 400 ns processor cycle" [1] §4.2.1 p. 4-13 — "Every memory cycle takes
400 ns. A video cycle occurs every 800 ns; a CPU cycle can occur between any two video cycles" [1]
§4.1 p. 4-4. The **CMUX** signal selects whose address drives the memory array: "When deasserted,
it gates the video address. When asserted, it gates the physical address presented by either the
MMU or the DMA control" [1] §4.2.1 p. 4-13. This is why every 68000 instruction executes in a
multiple of 800 ns, with wait states inserted as needed [1] §1 p. 1-3, and why the Lisa's 68000
runs at half the memory bandwidth a 5 MHz 68000 would otherwise command — the scan never stops,
not even during vertical retrace (§3.1).

## 2. Register file

### 2.1 Summary

All addresses are physical I/O-space addresses, reached by software at the logical `$FCxxxx`
alias [2] "I/O Space Addresses"; [Lisa](lisa.md) §3.3. The full processor-board control block is
tabulated in [Lisa](lisa.md) §6; the video-relevant entries are:

| Physical | Logical | Access | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| `$00E800` | `$00FCE800` | write-only | **Video Address Latch** — A15–A20 of the framebuffer page (§2.2) |
| `$00E018` | `$00FCE018` | strobe, reset | **VTIR disable** — clear/inhibit the vertical-retrace interrupt and status bit (§2.3) |
| `$00E01A` | `$00FCE01A` | strobe, set | **VTIR enable** (§2.3) |
| `$00F800` | `$00FCF800` | read-only, 16-bit | **Status Register** — video bits 2, 4, 5, 6 (§2.4; full map in [Lisa](lisa.md) §7.4) |
| `$00F000` | `$00FCF000` | read-only | **Memory Error Address latch** — bit 0 flags a video access (§2.5) |
| VIA2 port A + port B bit 7 | `$00FCD9xx` | write | **contrast byte and latch strobe** (§2.6; VIA2 wiring in [Lisa](lisa.md) §10.2) |

The strobes are **address-decoded latches, not data registers**: an access to the reset address
clears the latch, an access to the set address sets it, and the data written is ignored [1]
§2.5.5 Fig. 2-17. Each 68000 bus cycle performs its own address decode, so a *longword* access to
a strobe address is two bus cycles and fires **two** strobes — the base address and the base + 2.
Both the OS and MacWorks XL deliberately rely on this: a single `MOVE.L D0,$00FCE018` performs
"reset VTIR, then enable VTIR" in one instruction (§4.6, §4.7) — the ROM source spells the sequence
out as two `TST (addr)` operations [3] RM248.S `VIDTST`, and the OS's level-1 initialization is the
longword form [4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT (`MOVE.L D0,VertReset` annotated "enable vertical retrace").

### 2.2 Video Address Latch (`$00E800`, write-only)

The latch — the Hardware Manual's **video address latch** / video page register, a 74LS374 at B-1
on sheet 4 of processor-board schematic 050-4009 [1] §4.5.4 p. 4-29 — "is used to point to the
physical address of the 32 Kbyte video page in main memory" [1] §2.5.5 p. 2-24. It "contains the
six high-order bits of the address to be presented to memory, which are not provided by the Video
Address Counter (A15–A20). This provides a physical address to the memory, that is, it is not
translated by the MMU" [1] §4.5.4 p. 4-29. The low 15 address bits come from the video address
counter (§3.2), so the byte's low 6 bits select one of 64 possible 32 KB-aligned pages — the
framebuffer may live anywhere in the Lisa's 21-bit physical space, including, as the Boot ROM's
error path proves, in memory that fails its test (§4.2).

The byte layout, as fixed by every released writer's arithmetic (base = value `>> 15`, i.e. bits
0–5 are the page number):

| Bit | Meaning | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **A15–A20** — the 32 KB page number of the framebuffer | [1] §4.5.4; [3] RM248.K `SETVLTCH`; [4] `SetScreenKeybd` |
| 6 | undocumented; no released software writes it | — |
| 7 | lights a front-panel LED, per the Boot ROM's "set LED on and default video latch setting" comment (`DEFVID2 = $AF`) | [3] RM248.E equates, RM248.K CHG004 (*inferred* — see §6.2) |

The latch is write-only; reads are not documented and no released software reads it. The Boot
ROM's equate names the logical address `$00FCE800` (`VIDLTCH`) [3] RM248.E, and the OS's
`VideoLatch` equate is identical [4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT.

### 2.3 Vertical-retrace interrupt strobes (`$00E018` / `$00E01A`)

The vertical-retrace interrupt ("VTIR" in the Boot ROM's naming; the Hardware Manual calls the
signal **VSIR/**) is enabled and disabled by the standard control-latch strobe pair [1] §2.5.5
Fig. 2-17:

| Address | Latch | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `$00E018` | VTIR disable | inhibits the interrupt, and **clears the retrace indication in the Status Register** (§2.4) |
| `$00E01A` | VTIR enable | arms the interrupt for the next retrace edge |

The Boot ROM equates them `VTIRDIS`/`VTIRENB` at `$00FCE018`/`$00FCE01A` [3] RM248.E. That the
disable strobe clears the Status Register bit is not stated in the manual — it is pinned by the
Boot ROM's video self-test, which strobes disable then enable and *expects* the status bit to have
gone inactive even though the ~90 µs retrace window cannot have elapsed (§4.3) [3] RM248.S
`VIDTST`; [Lisa](lisa.md) §7.4.

### 2.4 Status Register, video bits (`$00F800`, read-only)

The Status Register is a read-only 16-bit register at `$00F800`, read by the bus-error handler to
classify faults [1] §2.8 p. 2-30. Software reads it a byte at a time at `$00F801` — the low byte,
bits 0–7 of the word [3] RM248.E `STATREG = $00FCF801`; [4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT
(`BTST #2,StatusRegister+1`). Its four video bits:

| Bit | Manual name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Vertical Retrace | "The video circuit has begun a vertical retrace and an interrupt has been generated. The video circuit continues to set this bit for 90 microseconds after the start of the retrace. Enable Vertical Retrace Interrupt can be reset to inhibit this bit" [1] §2.8 Fig. 2-19 |
| 4 | Video Bit | "Available to the CPU for diagnostic purposes, this bit corresponds to the output of the video circuit" [1] §2.8 Fig. 2-19 — a live image of the serial pixel stream |
| 5 | Horizontal Sync | "Reflects the state of the horizontal sync signal. It is available to the CPU for diagnostic purposes" [1] §2.8 Fig. 2-19 |
| 6 | Video Mode | "Reserved for future use" [1] §2.8 Fig. 2-19 — but see §5: the Boot ROM treats it as the **inverse-video strap** |

Bit 2's polarity deserves its own warning. The manual's wording says the bit is "set" during
retrace, but every released reader treats **0 as in-retrace**: the Boot ROM's `VIDTST` waits for
the bit to go low, then high [3] RM248.S, and the OS branches on zero as "vertical retrace
interrupt" [4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT. The bit is therefore **active-low**: 0 during the ~90 µs
retrace window and while a retrace is pending acknowledgement, 1 during active scan. [Lisa](lisa.md)
§7.4 documents the same conclusion; the manual's "set" refers to the underlying condition, not the
logic level (*inferred* from the software's uniform reading of it).

The remaining bits (soft/hard memory error, bus timeout) are covered in [Lisa](lisa.md) §7.4; they
are not video.

### 2.5 Memory Error Address latch, video accesses (`$00F000`, read-only)

Because the video fetch is an ordinary memory-bus master, it can be the access that hits a memory
error. The Memory Error Address latch freezes the high-order address of the failing word, and its
low bit records who was talking: `0` = CPU access, `1` = video access — in which case "D10–D15
contain A15–A20 of failing address, D1–D9 invalid" [1] §2.7 p. 2-29, Fig. 2-19's companion bit
table. That the video-failure record carries exactly the page-latch bits is a direct consequence
of the scan address being page-bits-from-the-latch plus the sequential counter (§3.2).

### 2.6 Contrast latch (VIA2 port A)

The black/white drive level is software-controlled through the parallel-port 6522. "The data are
input via the parallel port interface 6522A port, which is latched in the CMOS 74C174 at D-3. The
data is presented to the summing network, which presents the result of the D-to-A conversion as a
DC voltage" [1] §6.7.3 p. 6-55. The **CONT** output "can vary between +2 V, fully black, and +7 V,
fully white" [1] §6.7.3 p. 6-55; on the video board it sets the level the video amplifier's
emitter-follower drives into the cathode circuit — "The higher the voltage on R8, the larger the
contrast between black and white in the final pixel on the CRT" [1] §7.3.1 p. 7-4. Two properties
matter to software:

- **reset blanks the screen**: the contrast node is biased at ~3.5 V and "The RESET signal pulls
  this point to ground, which blanks the screen" [1] §6.7.3 p. 6-55;
- the latch is written through the same VIA port the parallel-port hard disk uses, so disk I/O
  and contrast changes must not collide ([Lisa](lisa.md) §14; §4.5 below).

The February-1982-and-later I/O boards latch the byte with a **WCNT** strobe on port B bit 7; the
September-1981 boards present it directly and take the inverted value [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT
`StepContrast` (`TST.B IOBoard` selects between the two paths).

### 2.7 Reset state

No document states the power-on values of the Video Address Latch or the VTIR enable latch. The
Boot ROM never assumes either: it re-points the latch during memory sizing before it draws
anything (§4.2), and it sets, clears and re-sets the VTIR strobes explicitly in its self-test
(§4.3). The one reset-state fact that is forced by the contrast circuit is that reset holds the
contrast node at ground, i.e. the screen is blank until software raises it (§2.6).

## 3. Behaviour

### 3.1 The interleaved video memory cycle

The video fetch is a memory-bus master that takes one 400 ns slot of every 800 ns bus cycle,
forever: "CPU and video memory cycles are interleaved on the memory bus. Every memory cycle takes
400 ns. A video cycle occurs every 800 ns; a CPU cycle can occur between any two video cycles" [1]
§4.1 p. 4-4. "The video control generates physical addresses that are presented to the memory
addressing multiplexer on the processor board for direct access to the memory location. Data that
are read out of memory in this way is presented to the video control via the memory data bus" [1]
§4.1.2 p. 4-4a. The addresses are physical — the MMU is not in the video path [1] §4.1 p. 4-4 — so
no MMU context, limit register or space tag applies to the scan; relocating the screen is purely a
matter of rewriting the Video Address Latch (§2.2).

Two consequences of the interleave shape the whole machine:

- **DRAM refresh rides the video scan.** "The VA8–11 are representative of the timing of all video
  address signals. These are also used in the generation of the refresh address in the memory since
  video accesses are always to sequential locations. Refresh takes place during the video cycle" [1]
  §4.2.3 p. 4-13b. The video cycle therefore cannot be throttled or suspended without losing
  memory refresh.
- **CPU I/O can overlap the video fetch.** "This cycle can be simultaneous with an access to
  memory by the video control, since no logic or data path is common to both" [1] §4.1.3 p. 4-9b.

Fetches continue through the retrace periods — "The video does access memory during the video
retrace periods" [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28 — but the data read during horizontal retrace is ignored and
the video address counter is not incremented for it [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19, and no data is read out
during vertical retrace [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19. The visible effect is a machine that always spends
half its memory bandwidth on the display, retrace or not.

### 3.2 Video address counter

The **video address counter** (VA1–VA14 — two 74LS393s in series at B-4 on sheet 5 of schematic
050-4009) "points to the location in the 32 Kbyte video page of the next data byte to be fed to
the display. It is incremented once for every 16 active pixels that are fed into the video board,
and it is reset at the end of each vertical retrace" [1] §4.5.1 p. 4-27a. The counter is clocked
on the leading edge of each t7 state and "is reset synchronously with CMUX when the video state
machine determines that the vertical retrace has been completed" [1] §4.5.1 p. 4-27a.

Fourteen bits count the 16 K words of the 32 KB page, so the counter wraps *within* a frame: the
state machine scans 379 lines of 45 words — 17,055 words — per frame against a page of 16,384
words. The displayed image occupies the first 364 × 45 = 16,380 words; the last 4 words of the page
and the words re-fetched during the retrace lines wrap past the top of the page and are not
displayed (*inferred* from the manual's counter width and line counts; the manual does not spell
the wrap out). This wrap is why the "last pixel of the page" rule of §4.1 exists: the final word
of the page is the last thing shifted out before the beam is blanked.

### 3.3 The video data shift register

A pair of 74LS166 shift registers at B-3 on sheet 5 converts each 16-bit word the fetch returns
into the serial stream: "The clock that increments the video address counter is also used to
parallel load the shift register with data presented on MD0–MD15" [1] §4.5.2 p. 4-27a. The stream
is shifted out on DOTCK — "The shift register presents a pixel to the video board every 50 ns" [1]
§4.5.2 p. 4-27a — through a JK flip-flop "inserted in the data path to ensure a uniform hold
length for each data bit. If this were not present, the final bit in each word could be curtailed,
since the next word is parallel-loaded into the shift register" [1] §4.5.2 p. 4-27a. An
**INVID/** signal "provides an inverted polarity of the video signal. It can also be used as a
serial input to the shift register for test purposes" [1] §4.5.2 p. 4-27a — the hardware hook
behind the inverse-video option the Boot ROM tests for (§4.4).

Bit ordering within the word is MSB-first — the leftmost pixel of each 16-pixel group is bit 15.
The manual does not state it directly; it is pinned by every released painter: the Boot ROM's
`PAINT_BOX`/`PAINT_V` write whole words and set single bits by `BSET` with bit numbers counting
from the top of the word [3] RM248.G, and the OS agrees [4]. [Lisa](lisa.md) §8 records the same
convention at byte granularity (leftmost pixel = bit 7 of the byte).

### 3.4 The video state machine

The scan's master timing is a PROM state machine, not a CRTC-style programmable register set:
"the LS393 counter at D-4, the 6309-1 programmable ROM (PROM) at D-4, and the LS374 latch at D-3"
on sheet 5 [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28. "The counter is clocked with the video signal... Both the counter
outputs and the VA9 and VA15 signals are used to address the PROM. The data output from the PROM
is latched in the LS374 with the opposite edge of the same signal that clocks the counter" [1]
§4.5.3 p. 4-28. Its function: "It counts up to the number of words in one line, performs a
horizontal retrace, resets itself and then resumes. At the bottom of the screen it performs a
vertical retrace" [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28.

The fixed geometry it implements: "The state machine performs 45 normal word fetches and shifts in
each line before performing a horizontal retrace and resetting itself. When it detects the 364th
line [by monitoring the video-address lines], it generates a vertical sync of 6 lines duration,
followed by vertical sync termination of an additional 9 lines duration" [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28.
"Other outputs are used to clear the video address counter at the end of vertical retrace,
generate the VSIR/ to interrupt the CPU during vertical retrace for cursor positioning and reset
the shift register load flop and the state machine counter itself" [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28. The
frame-level result: "The video address is reset once per page. This occurs after 379 lines of 720
pixels each. Only 364 lines are displayed on the screen" [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19.

To the video board the state machine exports **HSYNC/** (end of line), **VSYNC/** (bottom of
screen), **CSYNC/** (their composite, also the rear-panel composite-video sync) and **VID** (the
serial pixel stream) [1] §4.6.3 p. 4-34. Nothing in the register set programs the geometry: the
720 × 364 raster is wired into the PROM, and the only software-controlled scan parameter in the
entire subsystem is the page base (§2.2).

### 3.5 The vertical-retrace interrupt

At the start of each vertical retrace the state machine generates **VSIR/** [1] §4.5.3 p. 4-28,
which arrives at the 68000 as an **IPL 1** interrupt — level 1 is shared by the hard-disk
interface/parallel port, the floppy-disk controller and "Video circuit controller" [1] §2.6 p. 2-27
— and dispatches through the level-1 autovector at logical `$000064` [1] §2.6 Fig. 2-18; [4]
LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT installs `Level1` there. It is edge-fired at the retrace boundary and gated by
the VTIR enable latch (§2.3): with the latch clear, no interrupt is generated.

Two timing facts bound its observable behavior:

- the **Status Register bit** mirrors it for ~90 µs from the start of the retrace (§2.4), and is
  cleared early by the VTIR-disable strobe (§2.3);
- the interrupt, once generated, must **survive indefinitely** until software acknowledges it. The
  manual does not state the latch depth; the requirement is pinned by released software's
  dependence on it — the OS's VBL handler is routinely entered long after the 90 µs window has
  passed (the kernel runs interrupt-masked for whole retrace windows), and it still expects to
  find the VBL pending: a retrace source that de-asserted itself at the end of a fixed window
  would leave the OS's retrace-paced work starved whenever the kernel was masked across it
  ([Lisa](lisa.md) §8, which records the boot-stalling symptom of modelling it as a fixed-width
  pulse; *observed* in the Office System boot). Software acknowledges by reading the Status
  Register and strobing VTIR-disable in its level-1 handler (§4.6).

### 3.6 The video board and the composite output

The video board is pure analog: it "processes the bit stream of serial display data it receives
from the processor board and translates the data into a display by means of the raster scan on the
screen" [1] §7.1 p. 7-2, with power-supply, video-amplifier, and vertical and horizontal deflection
blocks [1] §7.1 p. 7-2. The amplifier "take[s] the serial TTL data provided by the processor board
and convert[s] the digital information into black, logical 0, or white, logical 1, pixels on the
screen by controlling the cathode emission in the CRT" [1] §7.3 p. 7-3, under control of the
contrast DC level (§2.6); the cathode sits at about +60 VDC for black and 10–30 V lower for white
[1] §7.3.2 p. 7-5. There are "no gray levels in the Lisa video circuitry. Gray tones can be
generated by pixel interleaving under software control" [1] §7.3.1 p. 7-4. A decoupling network
"ensures that the beam current to the cathode is removed when the Lisa is turned off... a
'spot-killer'" prevents phosphor burn [1] §7.3.2 p. 7-5. Vertical deflection is a TDA1170-class
circuit synchronized by VSYNC [1] §7.4 p. 7-5ff.

The same stream also leaves the cabinet: the composite video output on jack J10 ORs the VID and
CVOUT signals [1] §8.4.4 p. 8-7 and conforms to RS-170 at a 22,400 Hz horizontal sweep rate and
60 Hz vertical refresh [1] §3.5.5 p. 3-23, so an external CRT can mirror the internal one.

## 4. Programming model

### 4.1 Framebuffer layout

The framebuffer is a single **32 KB page** of ordinary main RAM, scanned sequentially from the
page base: one bit per pixel, MSB-first, 16 pixels per fetched word, 45 words (90 bytes) per line,
364 lines displayed — 720 × 364 = 32,760 bytes displayed of the 32,768-byte page [1] §4.2.4,
§4.5; [4] `ScreenSize` returns 720 and 364. Concretely:

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Pixel depth | 1 bit; 1 = white, 0 = black in the active area [1] §7.3 |
| Bit order | MSB first — leftmost pixel is bit 15 of its word (bit 7 of its byte) [3] RM248.G; [Lisa](lisa.md) §8 |
| Row stride | 90 bytes (45 words) per line, tightly packed, no per-line padding |
| Page size | 32,768 bytes; base is 32 KB-aligned, from the latch (§2.2) |
| Displayed bytes | 32,760 (364 × 90) |
| Location | anywhere in physical RAM the latch names — near the top of RAM in practice (§4.2, §4.5) |

One hardware-forced rule applies to the **last byte of the page**: "The last pixel of each page
must be a 1 (one) in order that the retrace is black. This is done by having a 'one' in the least
significant bit of the 32,767th byte displayed" [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19. [Lisa](lisa.md) §8 records the
same rule as "the last displayed byte's least-significant bit must be 1 so retrace shows black."
The final bits shifted out feed the CRT while the beam is being blanked into retrace; software
must leave them at the black level. The Boot ROM maintains this on behalf of inverse-video units
(§4.4). The interaction between this rule and the active-area polarity (1 = white) is not
resolvable from the primary text — see §6.4.

Drawing is entirely software's business: the Boot ROM carries a mini-graphics package that
clears the page, fills rectangles word-at-a-time with 16-bit patterns, and gray-fills the desktop
with alternating `$AAAA`/`$5555` rows [3] RM248.G — the pixel-interleaving the video board's
"no gray levels" note licenses [1] §7.3.1.

### 4.2 Boot ROM: choosing the screen page

The Boot ROM (revision H, RM248) owns the screen from the first instant of power-on, and treats
the page base as *derived from the memory test* rather than fixed:

1. **Sizing.** The memory-sizing routine walks physical RAM, then, having remapped the MMU,
   "reset[s] video page to last page of memory" — it computes the latch value from the top of
   physical memory and writes it to the latch [3] RM248.K (`LSR.L #6,D6` / `SUBQ #1,D6` /
   `MOVE.B D6,VIDLTCH`).
2. **Screen-page test.** `SCRNTST` tests "the last 32K of memory" with the RAM test pattern,
   and "if this is invalid, do[es] a backwards scan through memory until a valid area is found",
   saving the new base and re-pointing the latch on each step [3] RM248.K.
3. **Translation.** `SETVLTCH` converts the logical screen base to the physical page number the
   latch needs — logical base, subtracted from the logical memory size to get the offset from the
   top, subtracted in turn from the physical maximum, then shifted right by 15 — and writes the
   byte [3] RM248.K:

   ```
   SETVLTCH    MOVE.L  SCRNBASE,D2     ; logical screen base
               MOVE.L  TOTLMEM,D1      ; logical amount of memory
               SUB.L   D2,D1           ; screen base offset from top
               MOVE.L  MAXMEM,D0       ; max physical address
               SUB.L   D1,D0           ; physical screen base address
               LSR.L   #8,D0           ; convert to page value
               LSR.L   #7,D0           ;  (>> 15: bits 0-5 = A15-A20)
               MOVE.B  D0,VIDLTCH      ; set latch ($00FCE800)
   ```

4. **Defaults.** When no memory at all is found, the ROM falls back to `DEFVID = $2F` —
   annotated "default setting for video latch (end of 512K board in slot 1)" — page `$2F` =
   physical `$178000`, the last 32 KB of a 512 KB board in memory slot 1; the blink variant
   `DEFVID2 = $AF` is the same page with bit 7 set, "LED on" [3] RM248.E, RM248.K. (RAM boards
   sit high in the Lisa's physical map — a 512 KB board's last page is indeed `$2F`; [Lisa](lisa.md)
   §3.1.)
5. **Error display.** If the *low* 2 KB of memory fails, the ROM points the latch at the failing
   page — `MOVE.B D0,VIDLTCH` with the failing page value — beeps twice, and enters a read/write
   loop on the failing location "with random display on screen": the classic error-screen
   garbage is the video scan pointed at memory the test is actively scribbling [3] RM248.K
   (`MEMTST1`).

The ROM keeps the chosen logical base in low-memory cell `$110` (`SCRNBASE`, "ptr to base address
for video page") [3] RM248.E, and its graphics package adds that base to every pixel offset it
draws [3] RM248.G. While the self-test runs (user-interface builds), the ROM displays its test
icons on this page — the CPU-board icon is highlighted while `VIDTST` runs [3] RM248.K, RM248.S.

### 4.3 Boot ROM: the video self-test (VIDTST)

`VIDTST` [3] RM248.S verifies the retrace machinery without using interrupts — by polling the
Status Register under strobe control, with a ~20 ms timeout (`$0DF4` loop passes at ~5.6 ms each):

```
VIDCHK      MOVEA.L #VTIRDIS,A3     ; $00FCE018
            MOVEA.L #VTIRENB,A4     ; $00FCE01A
            MOVEA.L #STATREG,A5     ; $00FCF801 (byte ops)
            MOVE    #$0DF4,D0       ; timeout, about 20 ms
            MOVEQ   #VRBIT,D2       ; status bit 2
            TST     (A3)            ; reset, then
            TST     (A4)            ;  enable VTIR
@1          BTST    D2,(A5)         ; wait for LOW (retrace)
            BEQ.S   @2
            DBF     D0,@1
            BRA.S   VIDERR          ; timeout = error
@2          TST     (A3)            ; reset VTIR
            TST     (A4)            ; then re-enable
            BTST    D2,(A5)         ; should be HIGH by now
            BEQ.S   VIDERR          ; still low = error
            TST     (A3)            ; disable VTIR on exit
```

The load-bearing step is `@2`: after the disable/enable strobe pair the ROM *requires* bit 2 to
read high immediately, even though the ~90 µs window cannot have elapsed. That pins the hardware
contract of §2.3 — the disable strobe clears the retrace status latch; it is not a per-read
toggle and not a pure raster-position wire [3] RM248.S; [Lisa](lisa.md) §7.4. On failure the ROM
sets the video error indicator and proceeds to the error-message routine [3] RM248.S.

### 4.4 Boot ROM: inverse video (INVTST)

Some Lisas carry an inverse-video option, and the ROM adapts the page to it: "Now check state of
INVID bit to see if inverse video is installed. If yes, rewrite last 4 words of screen page to
avoid retrace line... Inverse video requires zeroing of last bit in video page (32K)" [3] RM248.K
(`INVTST`, gated by the `INVERTCK` build flag). The strap is read from **Status Register bit 6**
(`INVIDBIT = 6`) [3] RM248.E — the same bit the Hardware Manual labels "Video Mode — Reserved for
future use" (§2.4). On an inverse-video unit the polarity of the display is inverted (§3.3's
INVID/ path), so the retrace-black level the last word must carry is the *opposite* bit value,
and the ROM rewrites the last four words of the page to zeros accordingly [3] RM248.K.

### 4.5 OS: screens, contrast and the frame counter

The Office System's hardware library (LIBHW) drives the display through a small trap surface
[4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT:

- **Geometry is fixed**: `ScreenSize` returns 720 and 364; the screen "requires 32,760 bytes of
  main memory" [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT.
- **Two screens.** The OS maintains *two* 32 KB framebuffer pages — a primary screen "intended
  for normal user interaction, especially graphics" (the cursor lives here) and an *alternate*
  screen "intended for debugging output" — and repoints the hardware at the visible one:
  `SetScreenKeybd` writes the Video Address Latch with the visible screen's physical page. The
  user toggles between them with Option-Enter [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT. Only one is scanned at a
  time; the hardware itself has no notion of a second page (the latch names exactly one).

  ```
  SetScreenKeybd  MOVE.L  ScrnPhys,D1     ; primary physical address
                  MOVE.B  D0,ScrnKeybd    ; 0=primary, else=alternate
                  BEQ.S   @1
                  MOVE.L  AltScrnPhys,D1  ; alternate physical address
  @1              ADD.L   MemoryBase,D1   ; minimum physical memory address
                  LSR.L   #8,D1           ; divide by 2**15
                  LSR.L   #7,D1           ; screen page
                  MOVE.B  D1,VideoLatch   ; $00FCE800
  ```

- **Contrast** is an integer 0–255 whose "low order two bits are ignored" (writers mask to a
  multiple of four), with **0 as maximum contrast and 255 as minimum**, initial value `$80`,
  and a separate **dim** level, initial `$B0` [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT. `SetContrast`/`RampContrast`
  change it gradually: an alarm fires every 50 ms and steps the level by 4 toward the goal, over
  about a second [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT. The write path brackets the transfer with the parallel
  port's disk-enable line — "DEN/ <- 1 to disable disk" around the port-A write — and skips
  entirely "if parallel port busy" (`DiskSemaphore`), because VIA2 port A is shared with the
  ProFile/Widget hard disk ([Lisa](lisa.md) §10.2, §14); February-1982-and-later boards add the
  **WCNT** strobe on port B bit 7 to latch the byte, and September-1981 boards take the inverted
  value [4] libhw-MACHINE.TEXT `StepContrast`.
- **The frame counter**: "The screen is redisplayed about 60 times per second. A frame counter
  is incremented between screen updates, at the vertical retrace interrupt" [4]
  libhw-MACHINE.TEXT — the 32-bit `ScrnFrames`, readable through the `FrameCounter` trap [4]
  LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT.

### 4.6 OS: the level-1 handler and the VBL

The OS's level-1 interrupt handler identifies the VBL by the Status Register and services it
[4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT:

```
Level1      MOVEM.L A0/D0,-(SP)
            BTST    #2,StatusRegister+1 ; vertical retrace interrupt?
            BNE.S   @0                  ; branch if not vertical retrace
            JSR     VertRetrace         ; vertical retrace interrupt
@0          ...                          ; VIA2 timer1, disk, Twiggy, alarms...
```

`VertRetrace` increments `ScrnFrames`, then — if the cursor is tracking and the mouse has moved —
erases and redraws the cursor at the new position on the spot, inside the retrace; then it
acknowledges [4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT:

```
VertRetrace ADD.L   #1,ScrnFrames       ; vertical retrace counter
            ...                         ; hide/redraw cursor at mouse position
@3          MOVE.L  D0,VertReset        ; reset vertical retrace interrupt
            BTST    #2,StatusRegister+1 ; actually reset ?
            BEQ.S   @3                  ; loop until the bit reads high
```

The acknowledgement is the longword form of the strobe pair (§2.1): one `MOVE.L` to `$00FCE018`
fires disable-then-enable, and the poll that follows confirms via bit 2 that the retrace
indication is gone [4] LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT. Initialization arms the interrupt the same way: "Level
1 Interrupt Initialization... MOVE.L D0,VertReset ; enable vertical retrace" [4]
LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT.

Because the frame counter, the millisecond/microsecond clocks and the cursor all depend on level
1, the OS also exports a `Poll` routine for code that runs with interrupts disabled: "The
millisecond and microsecond timers and vertical retrace counter stop running if level 1
interrupts are off for more than about 10 milliseconds... Software which runs for extended
periods of time with interrupts off (e.g. LisaBug) should call Procedure Poll every few
milliseconds" — `Poll` performs the same Status-Register test and `VertRetrace` call by hand [4]
LIBHW-DRIVERS.TEXT.

### 4.7 Macintosh XL and MacWorks XL

The Macintosh XL is the same motherboard with a different Boot ROM and the factory
**screen-modification kit**, which "rewires the video timing to a 608-pixel-wide square-pixel
raster (the stock Lisa 2 is 720 × 364 with tall rectangular pixels)" [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1. What
changes and what does not:

| Property | Lisa 2 (stock) | Macintosh XL (screen-mod kit) | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible raster | 720 × 364 | **608 wide**, square pixels | [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1 |
| Row stride | 90 bytes | **76 bytes** | [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1 |
| Nominal Screen-Kit raster | — | 608 × 432 | [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1 |
| Framebuffer the Finder actually paints | 720 × 364 in 32 KB | **608 × 431** — 76 × 431 = 32,756 bytes; the 432nd scanline falls past the top of the 32 KB page | [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1 (*observed*: a MacWorks Finder framebuffer auto-correlates to a 76-byte stride and fills all 431 rows; rendering it at 90 bytes shears the desktop pattern diagonally) |
| Framebuffer page / base latch / VBL | unchanged | unchanged — same single 32 KB page at the `$00E800` latch; MacWorks programs the base at the top 32 KB-aligned slot (`$F8000` on a 1 MB machine, which under MacWorks keeps RAM based low) | [Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1, §3.1, §8 |
| Boot behavior | ROM self-test icons on the 720-wide screen | the 3A ROM displays the Boot ROM and floppy-ROM version numbers "in the upper right hand corner of the Macintosh XL screen during the self test" [2] "Reading the Boot ROM version number" |

MacWorks XL loads a Macintosh ROM image from disk and remaps the hardware-dependent kernel and
low-memory globals to Lisa addresses ([Lisa](lisa.md) §17); its VBL re-arm is the same longword
strobe the OS uses — a `MOVE.L` to `$00FCE018` performing disable-then-enable in one instruction
(*observed* in a running MacWorks XL boot; the OS source [4] carries the identical idiom, §4.6).
The exact dot-clock change the kit makes is not part of the programming model: nothing
guest-visible depends on it beyond the pixel aspect and the 608-wide raster ([Lisa](lisa.md) §8.1;
the modified clock value itself is §6.6).

## 5. Quirks & errata

- **Half the memory bandwidth belongs to the display, always.** The video fetch takes one 400 ns
  slot of every 800 ns bus cycle, retrace included, and DRAM refresh is generated from the video
  addresses (§3.1) — there is no way to pause the scan without losing memory.
- **The framebuffer address is physical, not logical.** The scan bypasses the MMU entirely; the
  latch's six bits are physical A15–A20 (§2.2). Software that computes the latch value from a
  logical screen address must translate first, as both the ROM and OS do (§4.2, §4.5).
- **The retrace status bit is active-low, despite the manual's wording.** Every released reader
  treats 0 as in-retrace (§2.4); an implementer following the manual's "set this bit" phrasing
  literally inverts the polarity and breaks both the ROM self-test and the OS.
- **The VTIR-disable strobe clears the status bit** — not at the end of the 90 µs window, not on
  read. `VIDTST` depends on it (§4.3); the OS's acknowledge poll depends on it (§4.6).
- **A longword write to a strobe address fires two strobes.** `MOVE.L D0,$00FCE018` is
  disable-then-enable; software (OS init, OS acknowledge, MacWorks) uses exactly this idiom to
  re-arm the VBL in a single instruction (§2.1, §4.6, §4.7). Modelling a strobe write as one
  event per instruction mis-executes it.
- **The VBL is edge-fired and held until acknowledged.** It must survive an arbitrarily long
  interrupt-masked window; a fixed-width pulse drops the interrupt whenever the kernel is masked
  across the retrace, and the OS's retrace-paced work stalls (§3.5, §4.6).
- **The last byte of the page must be at the retrace-black level.** Bit 1 of the final displayed
  byte on a stock unit, zeroed on inverse-video units (§4.1, §4.4) — a software obligation the
  ROM fulfils and the OS inherits.
- **Status bit 6 is not reserved.** The manual says "reserved for future use"; the Boot ROM reads
  it as the inverse-video strap and rewrites the page's last words accordingly (§4.4).
- **Contrast shares VIA2 port A with the hard disk.** Every contrast write is bracketed by the
  disk-enable line and skipped while the parallel port is busy (§4.5); two I/O-board revisions
  latch it differently (Feb-82 WCNT strobe vs. Sep-81 inverted direct write).
- **Reset blanks the screen** by pulling the contrast node to ground (§2.6) — the display stays
  dark until software writes a contrast level, regardless of what the scan is fetching.
- **Pointing the latch at broken memory is a feature.** The ROM deliberately aims the scan at a
  failing page to display test garbage, and at a nonexistent page (`$2F`) when no RAM is found
  (§4.2).
- **No gray levels**: grays are software dithering only — the ROM's desktop gray is alternating
  `$AAAA`/`$5555` rows (§4.1).
- **The composite jack mirrors the internal CRT** and can be active at the same time (§1.1,
  §3.6).

## 6. Open questions

1. **The exact dot clock, and the manual's own arithmetic.** The manual gives both "a 20.375 MHz
   signal called DOTCK" and "a period of 50 ns" for DOTCK and CK [1] §4.2.1 p. 4-13, §4.3.1
   p. 4-21 — 20.375 MHz is a 49.08 ns period, and the pixel rate is quoted as "1 bit/50 ns,
   20 MHz" [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-14a. The 22,400 Hz composite horizontal rate [1] §3.5.5 is likewise
   only nominally consistent with 379 lines at 60 Hz (22,740 Hz). Which figures are exact, and
   what the crystal's true frequency is, is not pinned by the text.
2. **What latch bit 7 actually drives.** The Boot ROM comment calls `DEFVID2 = $AF` "LED on"
   [3] RM248.K CHG004, implying bit 7 of the Video Address Latch value lights a front-panel LED —
   but no manual describes any LED function on the latch, and no other released software sets the
   bit (*inferred — unverified*).
3. **The power-on values** of the Video Address Latch and the VTIR enable latch are undocumented
   (§2.7); nothing depends on them because every released boot rewrites both.
4. **The retrace-polarity puzzle.** The active-area convention is 1 = white, 0 = black [1] §7.3
   p. 7-3, yet the retrace-black requirement is a 1 in the last displayed byte [1] §4.2.4 p. 4-19,
   and the inverse-video units take 0 there [3] RM248.K. Whether the discrepancy is an inversion
   in the blanking path (the INVID/ serial input of §3.3?), a manual wording error, or a property
   of the display's blanking level is not resolvable from the primary text.
5. **The vertical-retrace interrupt's electrical contract.** The manual names VSIR/ and its
   generation [1] §4.5.3 but not its latch depth or clear input; "held until software
   acknowledges" (§3.5) is established by released software's dependence on it and the observed
   Office-System boot behavior ([Lisa](lisa.md) §8), not by a hardware description.
6. **The Macintosh XL screen-modification kit's exact timing changes** — the modified dot clock,
   line count of the 608-wide raster, and whether the 3A ROM's VBL/retrace expectations differ
   from the H ROM's. Only the guest-visible geometry (608 wide, 76-byte rows, 608 × 431 painted)
   is established ([Lisa](lisa.md) §1.1, §8).
7. **Reads of the Video Address Latch.** It is write-only in every released writer; whether the
   address `$00E800`–`$00EFFF` decodes on read (and what it returns) is undocumented. (An
   implementation returning open-bus values on read is consistent with all observed software.)
8. **What the "Video Bit" and "Horizontal Sync" diagnostics were for.** Both bits exist in the
   Status Register [1] §2.8 and no released software reads either; their intended service use is
   unknown.
9. **Whether the state machine PROM differs across board revisions** — the 1983 manual describes
   one geometry (45 words/line, 379 lines); Lisa 1 units and later I/O-board revisions are not
   separately documented.
