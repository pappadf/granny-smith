# Macintosh Quadra 900

The Macintosh Quadra 900 is the tower member of the MCU family: the same MC68040-generation
main logic architecture as the [Quadra 700](q700.md), in a floor-standing case that adds four
internal mass-storage bays, five NuBus slots, four SIMM banks, a dual SCSI bus, the IIfx-style
IOP organization for the low-speed ports, and a keyswitch — *the most powerful member of the new,
high-performance Macintosh Quadra family* at its October 1991 introduction [1] §"Introduction"
p. 2. The board project is **Eclipse** (*observed* in the title block of the surviving production
schematic [4]); the [Quadra 950](q950.md) is this same tower board at 33.333 MHz with a revised
video path, and its page carries that machine's deltas.

Everything shared by the three boards — the three-bus architecture, the MCU/Orwell memory
controller and its register treatment, JDB/Relayer, YANCC, the address map, the interrupt and
clock architecture — is documented once in the family doc, [mcu.md](mcu.md), and this page cites
it by section rather than restating it. The value of this page is everything unique to the
Quadra 900: its identity values, its clocks, the sixteen-socket memory array, the tower's
per-subsystem wiring, its five-slot expansion backplane, and its boot branch points.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

Apple's own positioning of the machine is a single sentence plus a feature list: the Quadra 700
is *"functionally similar to the Macintosh Quadra 900 but has a different case design and a
reduced feature set"* [1] §"Introduction" p. 2 — the reduction running the other way, of course —
with the differences enumerated as the memory banks, the SCSI controllers, the expansion slots,
the low-speed I/O organization and the case [1] Table 1-4 p. 29. It is one
of the two machines that introduced the MC68040 to the Macintosh line — *"the first Macintosh
model to use the MC68040 microprocessor"* [1] §"MC68040 microprocessor" p. 7 — and the two share
one boot ROM image and one identify mechanism (§1.2; mcu.md §1.2).

The machine's physical definition is the case: a new floor-standing enclosure — *"a new, larger
case that stands on the floor"* — providing *"space for more peripheral devices than was provided
in the standard Macintosh II case"* [1] §"Introduction" p. 2, with four half-high mass-storage
locations, an integrated CD-ROM option, a 300 W supply and a locking keyswitch [1]
§"Summary of hardware features" pp. 2–3, §"Floor-standing case" pp. 18–19. The case is why the
board carries the tower feature set: five NuBus slots with higher power and oversized-card room
(§4.1), the four-bank memory array (§2.3), the dual SCSI bus (§3.3), and the IOP/Caboose
organization inherited from the Macintosh IIfx (§3.4, §3.5).

### 1.2 Identity values

The platform has no programmable machine-identity register: identity is the boot ROM the machine
carries, the model-sense straps the ROM reads, and the constants the ROM derives from them. The
family-level mechanism — one ROM image for the Quadra 700 and the Quadra 900, the identify code
reading VIA1 port A before selecting machine paths — is mcu.md §1.2 and §2.10; this machine's
values:

| Identity value | Value | Witness |
|---|---|---|
| Gestalt machine type | 20 | [1] §"Gestalt and SysEnvirons values" p. 44 |
| SysEnvirons machine type | 18 | [1] p. 44 |
| Gestalt processor field | `env68040 = 5` | [1] p. 44 |
| `gestaltFPUType` | `gestalt68040FPU = 3` | [1] p. 44 |
| `gestaltMMUType` | `gestalt68040MMU = 4` | [1] p. 44 |
| `gestaltProcessorType` | `gestalt68040 = 5` | [1] p. 44 |
| Boot ROM | the 1 MB `$420DBFF3` image shared with the Quadra 700 (October 1991) | mcu.md §1.2; [3] |
| VIA1 port-A model sense | the identify code compares bits 6, 4, 2 and 1 under the mask `$56` against `$50`; the recovered port-A pattern is `$D0` with bit 0 — a diagnostic-mode strap — OR'ed in | *observed* in the identify code of the `$420DBFF3` image [3]; the complete electrical encoding is mcu.md §7 item 9 |
| Video part identity | DAFB of the 700/900 class; the version field of the test/version register reads 0, 1 or 2 | [dafb.md](dafb.md) §2.2 |
| A/UX remap | controlled by VIA1's two new bits, default off | [1] §"Macintosh Quadra 900 Direct Slot interrupt handling" p. 41; mcu.md §5.1 |

The port-A comparison is the whole hardware basis of the 700-vs-900 fork: the same ROM image boots
both boards, and the only thing that distinguishes them to software before Gestalt answers is the
strap pattern on VIA1 port A. The bits not in the mask (`$56` covers bits 1, 2, 4, 6; bits 7, 5
and 3 are ignored by the comparison) and the diagnostic strap's active level are not established
beyond the recovered values (§6.2; mcu.md §7 item 9).

### 1.4 The tower enclosure and its supply

The case is part of the machine's definition, and Apple enumerates its purpose: *"a
floor-standing case for more space and better cooling; space for as many as four internal
peripheral devices, including full-height disk drives and removable media; provision for an
internal CD-ROM player, integrated with system sound features; a larger power supply to support
internal devices and higher-power NuBus cards; a locking keyswitch for security and operation as
a server; sound input for microphone (included) and line signals"* [1] §"Floor-standing case"
pp. 18–19. Two of the four bays are at the front for removable media (§4.4).

The supply is rated in Apple's Table 1-3 [1] §"Power supply" p. 25:

| Output | Continuous | Peak |
|---|---|---|
| +5 V | up to 33 A | 33 A |
| +5V TRKL (trickle) | 125 mA, live whenever the AC cord is plugged in | — |
| +12 V | up to 9 A | 20 A (for at most 12 s, sized so every internal device can spin up at once) |
| −12 V | 1 A | 1 A |
| Total | 292 W | 424 W |

The supply adapts automatically to the applied AC voltage, includes a 120 mm fan cooling the
whole system, and carries a switched convenience receptacle for the monitor [1]
§"Power supply" p. 24. The trickle rail powers the parameter RAM and power-on logic and provides
standby power for the NuBus cards [1] p. 24 — the rail behind the keyswitch/soft-power behavior
of §3.5 and the STDBYPWR pin of §4.1.

### 2.2 Clocks on this machine

The family clock architecture is mcu.md §5.5; this machine's values:

| Domain | Value |
|---|---|
| System bus | 25 MHz — the MC68040's own clock rate [1] §"Design architecture" p. 7 |
| MC68040 internal | the card-design book's Quadra 900 column records *"50 MHz CPU clock"* against the 25 MHz bus clock [2] Table 1-1 pp. 14–16 — a doubled internal clock, the same 2X-clock arrangement the Quadra 950 note describes for the 33.333/66.666 MHz pair (mcu.md §1.1); *inferred from [2]*, since the developer note itself states only 25 MHz (§6.3) |
| I/O bus | 15.6672 MHz, asynchronous to the system bus [1] §"I/O bus adapter ICs: JDB and Relayer" p. 10 |
| NuBus | 10 MHz, plus the NuBus '90 20 MHz clock for double-rate card-to-card transfers [1] §"Design architecture" p. 7, §"NuBus '90 features" p. 27 |
| SONIC | 20 MHz-class part — the crystal-driven transmit clock and the I/O-bus-derived DMA clock are the part's (mcu.md §5.5, [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.4); the 950's note calls out its own 25 MHz version explicitly, implying a grade below it (§3.7) |
| SCSI | 53C96 at the system-bus rate class; TurboSCSI wait-state timing programmed per CPU speed ([ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §1.5, [dafb.md](dafb.md) §3.6) |
| VIA timers | derived from Relayer's VIA clock output (mcu.md §2.2, §5.5) |
| RTC | 32.768 kHz, inside Caboose (mcu.md §4.2; §3.5) |
| Video scan | DP8531 synthesizer programmed per mode ([dafb.md](dafb.md) §2.5) |

The one machine-unique clock fact is the 2X CPU clock: Apple's own developer note says only that
the system bus *"runs at the processor's clock rate, 25 MHz"* [1] p. 7, while the card-design
book's comparison table splits the machine into a 25 MHz bus clock and a 50 MHz CPU clock [2]
Table 1-1 pp. 14–16. The PDS connector carries **AUX.CPUCLK**, *a buffered version of main
processor's bus clock* [1] Table 2-3 p. 34 — buffered, not doubled — so the doubled clock, if
present, stays on the CPU's own pins (§6.3).

### 2.3 The sixteen-socket memory array

The tower's RAM rule, in Apple's words: *The MCU controls four banks of dynamic RAM. Each bank
accepts standard 80-ns SIMMs containing 1 MB, 4 MB, or 16 MB, giving total memory sizes from
4 MB to 64 MB* — with the note that *each bank of main RAM occupies 64 MB of physical address
space* [1] §"RAM control" p. 11. The geometry:

| Bank | Sockets | SIMMs accepted | Decode window |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 × 30-pin | four equal, 1/4/16 MB, 80 ns or faster | $00000000 (fixed at zero; mcu.md §2.5) |
| B | 4 × 30-pin | four equal | 64 MB window at $04000000 out of reset |
| C | 4 × 30-pin | four equal | 64 MB window at $08000000 out of reset |
| D | 4 × 30-pin | four equal | 64 MB window at $0C000000 out of reset |

Unlike the Quadra 700, no bank is soldered — the comparison table's memory row reads *"4 banks
of DRAM (all on SIMMs)"* against the 700's *"2 banks of DRAM (1 bank soldered; 1 bank SIMMs)"*
[1] Table 1-4 p. 29. The four SIMMs within a bank must match; banks may differ from one another,
and the ROM's sizing code discovers each bank's size and programs contiguous logical starts into
the MCU — the stitching behaviour whose register treatment and observed boot programming are
mcu.md §2.5 and §2.7, quoted there from the same `$420DBFF3` image this machine runs. Any total
from 4 MB (four 1 MB SIMMs in bank A) to 64 MB (16 MB × four everywhere) that can be formed from
four equal SIMMs per populated bank is a legal population; the launch note documents the 1/4/16 MB
SIMM grades only [1] p. 11, and later, larger SIMM arrangements are a documentation-era question
the family already carries (mcu.md §7 item 12).

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 The system-bus core as fitted

The Q900's system bus carries **five** controller ICs — the MCU, YANCC, DAFB and the two SCSI
controllers [1] §"Design architecture" p. 7 — the family's four plus the towers' second 53C96
(mcu.md §2.1). Nothing about the shared four is machine-specific: the MCU/Orwell register
window, its serial configuration register, its latch strobes and the boot-time programming
sequence are the family's, in full, at mcu.md §2.3–§2.7; YANCC's bridge behaviour and error
interrupt are mcu.md §2.8; DAFB is [dafb.md](dafb.md). The second 53C96 is this machine's
contribution and is covered with the SCSI buses below.

### 3.2 The I/O island on this machine

The family's I/O island allocation is mcu.md §3.2; the tower decode replaces the 700's direct SCC
and SWIM blocks with the two IOP host apertures and fills the 700's reserved second SCSI block:

| Base | Q900 function | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| $50008000 | Ethernet station-address PROM | mcu.md §3.2, [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.3 |
| $5000A000 | SONIC registers | [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §2.1 |
| $5000C000 | **SCC IOP host aperture** (replaces the 700's direct SCC block) | [iop.md](../iifx/iop.md) "Host address map" |
| $5000E000 | MCU/Orwell controls | mcu.md §2.4 |
| $5000F000 | SCSI 0 — internal bus, 53C96 | [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §2.1 |
| $5000F400 | **SCSI 1 — external bus, 53C96** (reserved for Apple on the 700) | [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §2.1 |
| $50014000 | Enhanced ASC | [asc.md](../../hardware/asc.md) §3.3 |
| $5001E000 | **SWIM/ADB IOP host aperture** (replaces the 700's direct SWIM block) | [iop.md](../iifx/iop.md) "Host address map" |
| $50028000 | YANCC controls | mcu.md §2.8 |

The second SCSI lane's internal arrangement is recovered rather than documented: the external
53C96's registers answer on the 16-byte register stride of the family's other 53C96 window, on a
byte lane two bytes into each halfword (first register at $5000F402), with its pseudo-DMA payload
port at $5000F502 (*inferred — unverified*: recovered from the decode table the boot ROM carries
for this board; no Apple document prints the block's byte-lane arrangement — the family carries
the same gap at mcu.md §7 item 10).

### 3.3 SCSI: two buses, one ID namespace

The tower splits the SCSI interface in two, in Apple's words: *the SCSI bus is divided into
internal and external buses, each controlled by its own IC. Both ICs are NCR 53C96 devices* —
and the split is electrical, *the external bus is electrically isolated from the internal bus so
that changes in external cabling and termination have no effect on the performance of the
internal SCSI devices* [1] §"SCSI controller ICs" p. 11. The internal side is tuned: *the
cabling and termination of the internal SCSI bus are optimized so that the 53C96 that controls
the internal bus can support data transfers at a faster rate—up to 5 MB/sec*, while the external
controller *may run more slowly* because external cabling and termination are less predictable
[1] p. 13.

Software sees one bus: *even though there are two SCSI buses, there is only one set of SCSI ID
numbers … for compatibility with existing applications, the total number of SCSI devices is
still only seven* [1] §"Support for SCSI controllers" p. 49 — the ROM's SCSI Manager support is
a module *addressed by a vector that is set up at startup time*, so the same ROM selects one-
or two-controller configurations by machine identity [1] p. 49 (the vectored design is the
family's, mcu.md §1.2). The physical connectors are the II-family ones: two internal 50-pin and
one external DB-25, pinouts in Apple's Table 1-1 [1] §"SCSI connectors" pp. 21–22, with the
termination rule that external devices at the end of the chain are terminated and internal
devices are not [1] p. 21.

Both controllers sit behind DAFB's TurboSCSI timing assist, one control register per channel —
channel 0 for the internal controller, channel 1 for the external ([dafb.md](dafb.md) §3.6;
the register bits and the pseudo-DMA path are that page's, at §2.2 and
[ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §3.8). The two controllers' interrupt outputs
combine before VIA2 — a level-2 request that either controller can raise [1]
§"Macintosh Quadra 900 Direct Slot interrupt handling" p. 40 — on the VIA2 handshake line the
ROM's VIA2 initialization configures for SCSI (*inferred — unverified*; the wire-OR of the two
INT outputs is recovered from the board's interrupt wiring, not stated in the note).

### 3.4 Serial, floppy and ADB behind the IOPs

The towers move the low-speed ports off the 68040's hands: *like the Macintosh IIfx, the
Macintosh Quadra 900 has intelligent I/O processors (IOPs) on the ports for the floppy disk,
Apple Desktop Bus (ADB), and serial I/O to relieve the main processor of routine I/O tasks*
[1] §"Introduction" p. 2. The parts are the IIfx's — *IOP, the I/O processors first used in the
Macintosh IIfx* [1] §"Custom ICs" p. 8 — Apple PIC devices whose internal architecture, host
aperture, shared-RAM layout, message/mailbox protocols and code-load protocol are documented at
[iop.md](../iifx/iop.md) ("What an IOP is" through "Code load protocol") and are not restated
here. The comparison table records the split as the 700's rows *"SCC"*, *"SWIM"* and *"ADB: same
as Macintosh IIci"* against this machine's *"SCC/IOP"*, *"SWIM/IOP"* and *"ADB: same as
Macintosh IIfx"* [1] Table 1-4 p. 29.

The two IOPs and their host-side wiring:

| IOP | Host aperture | Serves | Host interrupt path |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCC IOP | $5000C000 | the SCC behind it, and through it the two serial ports (RS-232/RS-422/AppleTalk [2] Table 1-1 p. 16) | CPU level 4 — the SCC's level in both interrupt mappings [1] Table 2-7 p. 41; that the IOP's host request occupies it is recovered wiring (*inferred — unverified*) |
| SWIM/ADB IOP | $5001E000 | the SWIM behind it (one internal SuperDrive, 20-pin connector [1] §"Floppy disk connector" p. 23) and the ADB transceiver — the tower's single ADB port [2] Table 1-1 p. 16 | VIA2 handshake input at level 2 (*inferred — unverified*: recovered from the ROM's VIA2 initialization, which configures the line for the SWIM IOP's request) |

Both IOPs are brought up by the boot ROM before their peripherals exist to software: the ROM
uploads each IOP's program through the shared-RAM host interface and releases its 65C02 from
reset, then conducts the alive handshake and message traffic of the IOP protocols
(*inferred — unverified*: the load-and-release sequence is the IOP architecture's documented
obligation — the Apple PIC has no onboard ROM mapping for its program — but the Q900 boot trace
of the uploads has not been captured; §6.6). ADB service rides the SWIM/ADB IOP's slot-3 message
protocol, not VIA1's shift register as on the 700 ([iop.md](../iifx/iop.md) "SWIM IOP — slot 3
ADBMsg protocol"; [adb.md](../../hardware/adb.md) "Model-Specific Differences").

### 3.5 Caboose: keyswitch, power, RTC/PRAM and sound control

The tower's system manager is **Caboose**, *a custom processor that manages the keyswitch,
system power, the real-time clock, and parameter RAM* [1] §"Custom ICs" p. 9 — with the sound
duty added in the ROM chapter: the ROM software *sends the Caboose IC a message containing a
string to be passed to the DFAC*, the Start Manager setting the DFAC's defaults and the Sound
Manager reaching the DFAC through ROM routines [1] §"Support for Caboose real-time clock IC"
p. 50. The RTC/PRAM function that sits on VIA1 on the 700 lives inside Caboose here (mcu.md
§2.10, §4.2); the clock itself is the classic 32.768 kHz part ([rtc.md](../../hardware/rtc.md),
"Hardware Interface" for the protocol family).

The keyswitch is Caboose's user surface, and its three positions are fully documented [1]
§"Keyswitch" p. 26:

| Position | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| OFF | the computer cannot be turned on; moving the switch to OFF from ON or SECURE **turns the power off immediately** — the system software gets no chance to close files |
| ON | power controlled like any Macintosh II-family machine: on from the keyboard, off by the Shut Down menu item |
| SECURE | ADB devices and the floppy disk are disabled; applying power while in SECURE **starts the computer automatically** — the server/remote-access position |

The key can be removed in any position, and the reset and NMI programmer's buttons **remain
active in SECURE** [1] §"Programmer's buttons" p. 27. The soft-power behavior rides the supply's
trickle rail (§1.4): PRAM, power-on logic and NuBus standby stay alive whenever the machine is
plugged in [1] §"Power supply" p. 24.

Caboose's host interface and silicon are the family's open ground (mcu.md §7 item 11): the surviving
production schematic carries Egret-protocol signal names on the Caboose nets [4], and the host
conversation — command bytes pumped through VIA1's shift register with port-B handshake bits, and
a reset release the CPU waits for during power-up — is recovered from the same ROM image's VIA1
initialization and power-on path (*inferred — unverified*; §6.1). Which microcontroller and
firmware Apple shipped is not established.

### 3.6 Built-in video and sound

The video subsystem is the family's DAFB design (mcu.md §3.4; [dafb.md](dafb.md) §2–§4) fitted
with more VRAM than the 700 ships: *the video frame buffer comprises four banks of video RAM
(VRAM); the basic configuration has VRAM installed in two of the four banks* [1] §"Video
frame-buffer controller IC: DAFB" pp. 13–14 — 1 MB, against the 700's 512 KB single bank
([dafb.md](dafb.md) §1.2). That configuration *supports 8 bits per pixel on all Apple monitors
(12-inch to 21-inch, monochrome and RGB) and on NTSC and PAL monitors, using Apple convolution*,
plus VGA, and *24 bits per pixel on the 12-inch RGB monitor*; filling the two expansion banks
takes the frame buffer to 2 MB and *24 bits per pixel for the 13-inch monitor* [1] p. 14. The
full depth-per-timing/VRAM matrix is [dafb-modes.md](dafb-modes.md) ("Quadra 700 and 900"); the
RAMDAC is the AC842 and the pixel clock the DP8531 ([dafb.md](dafb.md) §2.4, §2.5). The
performance claim belongs to the direct connection: because *the frame buffer … is connected
directly to the system bus, it speeds up all applications, even those that don't use QuickDraw*,
with performance *approaching that of the Macintosh Display Card 8•24GC* [1] p. 17.

The sound chain is a new design shared with the machine's IIfx/LC-era cousins: *three Apple
custom ICs plus a digital-to-analog converter* — DFAC for input with its antialiasing filter and
ADC, Enhanced ASC as the ASC-compatible generator, Sporty replacing the two Sony sound ICs with
digital attenuators, and an external DAC for higher-quality output than the earlier PWM system
[1] §"Sound ICs" pp. 16–17. The feature set is the tower's multimedia charter: monophonic
recording from the microphone input, the line inputs or the internal CD-ROM; playback of 8-bit
sound files and of CD-ROM audio; and mixing of CD-ROM audio with sound-file playback [1] p. 16.
A separate 2 W amplifier drives the larger speaker [1] p. 17.

The analog plumbing is documented in the case sections: the Mic In connector carries **+8 V on
one pin** and accepts only the supplied Apple microphone (a longer-cabled relative of the IIsi/LC
microphone, so it reaches the floor) [1] §"Microphone and Mic In connector" p. 27; the two Line
In phono connectors are mixed to a single monophonic signal with the Mic In feed before the sound
input circuit — stereo information is lost, the second connector exists only so consumer stereo
gear needs no external mixer [1] §"Audio Line In connectors" p. 28; the Sound Out connector is
electrically the Macintosh II's [1] §"Sound Out connector and speaker" p. 28. The CD-ROM's audio
reaches the board on its own cable, beside the SCSI cable, and gives the three integration
functions — playback through the internal sound system, mixing with computer-generated sound,
and digital recording from the CD [1] §"Internal CD-ROM integration" p. 24.

### 3.7 Ethernet

The Ethernet controller is the SONIC DP83932, *made by National Semiconductor*, named among the
board's third-party ICs [1] §"Custom ICs" p. 9 — an I/O-bus device and the platform's one I/O-bus
bus master (mcu.md §5.3), with its register window at $5000A000 and its station-address PROM at
$50008000 (mcu.md §3.2; [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.3, §2.1). The machine's clocking
is the 700's (§2.2): the 15.6672 MHz I/O bus times the DMA engine, and the part is the 20
MHz-grade class that the Quadra 950's note implicitly contrasts with its own 25 MHz version
(mcu.md §6.3; [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.4). The media interface is AAUI only: *the
Apple AUI (AAUI) connector, which accepts any FriendlyNet adapter* — thick AUI, thin CheaperNet,
or 10Base-T [1] §"Ethernet connector" p. 28. The ROM carries a dedicated SONIC driver [1]
§"Support for Sonic Ethernet controller" p. 48, and the buffers it sets up live on uncacheable
pages like every alternate-master communication area on this platform (mcu.md §5.4). The
controller's interrupt is one of the /SLOTIRQ constituents, polled at VIA2 PA0 (mcu.md §5.2;
[sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.3).

### 3.8 Interrupt wiring as fitted

The level map, the VIA2 slot bank mechanism and the A/UX remap are the family's (mcu.md §5.1,
§5.2); this machine's port-A bank, from Apple's Table 2-6 [1]
§"Macintosh Quadra 900 Direct Slot interrupt handling" p. 40:

| VIA2 PA bit | Request |
|---|---|
| PA0 | Ethernet IRQ |
| PA1 | NuBus slot $A IRQ |
| PA2 | NuBus slot $B IRQ |
| PA3 | NuBus slot $C IRQ |
| PA4 | NuBus slot $D IRQ |
| PA5 | NuBus slot $E IRQ (shared with the 68040 Direct Slot) |
| PA6 | built-in video IRQ (pseudo-slot $9) |

The three low slot bits are this machine's against the 700's (mcu.md §5.2): five physical
connectors means five slot lines, all latched per the Slot Manager's polling requirement
([1] p. 40; mcu.md §5.2). Beyond the bank, the level-2 composition is the note's sentence — VIA2
*can also generate an interrupt in response to SCSI requests, sound chip requests, or VIA2 timer
requests* [1] p. 40 — with the SCSI contribution coming from both controllers (§3.3) and the
IOP host request joining at VIA2 (§3.4). VIA1 carries level 1 plus the two new family bits —
software interrupt and A/UX interrupt enable — through the interrupt control PAL [1] p. 41
(mcu.md §5.1). VIA1's remaining tower-specific inputs are recovered rather than documented: a
60 Hz-class tick reference on one handshake line, the keyswitch's SECURE sense on another, and
keyswitch/soft-power/CPU-speed sense bits on VIA2 port B (*inferred — unverified* from the ROM's
VIA initialization; §6.4). The NMI programmer's button raises level 7 directly and stays active
in SECURE [1] §"Programmer's buttons" p. 27; level 7 is shared with the YANCC write-buffer error
(mcu.md §5.1).

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The five NuBus slots

The backplane is the machine's headline: *five NuBus slots and one PDS (processor-direct slot)*,
with the PDS *located in line with NuBus slot $E and us[ing] the same opening in the back*, so a
PDS card costs the machine its fifth NuBus slot [1] §"Expansion slots" p. 24. The slots differ
from other modular Macintoshes in three ways — *they provide higher power; they can accommodate
oversized NuBus cards; they support NuBus '90 features* [1] §"NuBus slots" p. 25:

- **Higher power.** The supply *is designed to provide additional current on the +5-V outputs for
  the NuBus slots*; the machine *has enough power to support a total of two 25-watt cards and
  three 15-watt cards* [1] §"Higher power" p. 25. The comparison table states the per-slot figure
  as 19 W, or two 25 W and three 15 W, totaling at most 95 W [1] Table 1-4 p. 29. The
  card-design book puts the same budget against the family norm — 13.3 W per slot everywhere
  else — and adds the failure mode: *if the amount of power used by NuBus expansion cards exceeds
  the total power budget, the Macintosh computer cannot be booted. During startup, the power
  supply attempts to turn itself on but cannot, and it continues the attempt over and over*
  [2] ch. 5 p. 115.
- **Oversized cards.** Each slot takes a standard card or an oversized one — *the same length as
  a standard NuBus card, but … 2 inches taller*; each slot ships with a card guide the user
  removes to fit the taller card, and *you cannot install an oversized NuBus card in any other
  Macintosh II-family computer* [1] §"Oversized NuBus cards" p. 25. The card-design book carries
  the physical design guides: the oversized-card clearance foldout and the NuBus card clearance
  foldout for this machine [2] Foldouts 3 and 5.
- **NuBus '90 features.** The connectors carry the 1990 revision's signals [1] §"NuBus '90
  features" pp. 26–27, Table 2-1 p. 27: **/TM2**, **/CLK2X** and **/CLK2XEN** for block transfers
  at double the standard rate — *the Macintosh Quadra 900 computer allows double-rate block
  transfers between NuBus cards but does not support double-rate transfers to or from the main
  memory*; the serial-bus pins **SB0/SB1**, *bused and terminated, but the main circuit board does
  not drive them*; and the cache-coherency pins **/CM0–/CM2**, **/CBUSY**, *assigned on the
  connector but … not supported* (mcu.md §2.1, §4.4). One machine-unique pin: **STDBYPWR** (B25),
  *low current at +5 V available … when main power is off and the AC cord is plugged in* [1]
  Table 2-1 p. 27 — the trickle rail of §1.4 reaching the slots. The card-design book defines the
  pin's purpose and its exclusivity: STDBYPWR *enables a card to turn on main power by asserting
  /PFW*, and the signal *is defined only on the Macintosh Quadra 900 and not on the Macintosh
  Quadra 700* [2] Table 5-5 p. 113.

The standing warning for the '90 pins is Apple's: *the eight lines that were connected to the
-5.2-V supply in the original NuBus specification are now used for new features. Many older NuBus
cards connect those eight lines together; the presence of such a card … will disable the new
features that use those lines* [1] pp. 26–27. The slots' physical ordering — which connector is
which of $A through $E — is drawn in the card-design book's slot-ordering figure for this
machine [2] Figure 6-4 p. 128 (§6.9); the address windows each slot answers in, the declaration-
ROM contract and the interrupt path are the family's (mcu.md §3.1, §5.2;
[declaration-rom.md](../../hardware/nubus/declaration-rom.md)).

### 4.2 The processor-direct slot

The 68040 PDS is the family's (mcu.md §3.5): wired directly to the processor's pins through the
system bus, in line with NuBus slot $E, with the pseudoslot address contract — upper RAM or slot
$E space, /PDS.SLOT.E.EN, /TA or /TEA for every slot-$E access because *there is no timeout timer
for slot $E* — and the load limits, all cited there and not restated. The machine-specific
surface:

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Connector | 140-pin KEL: board side 8817-140-170SH, card side 8807-140-170LH | [1] §"PDS card specifications" p. 30 |
| Electrical compatibility | pinouts identical to the Quadra 700's — *cards designed for one 68040-based computer will work in the other one as well* | [2] ch. 16 p. 364 |
| Card size | same dimensions as a NuBus card, with a back-panel connector optional; **an oversized PDS card is allowed on this machine** (unlike the 700) | [1] §"Processor-direct slot" p. 30; [2] Part I ch. 1 p. 5 |
| Load limits | address, data and AUX.CPUCLK lines ≤ 40 pF; all other lines ≤ 20 pF (per-signal load/drive table in the note) | [1] pp. 34–36 |
| Timing | card timing depends on the 68040's clock speed; developers *should clearly indicate on the card the maximum clock speed* | [1] §"Timing considerations" p. 39 |
| Power budget | *identical to the power budget for the NuBus card that it replaces* — the 95 W pool's per-slot share of §4.1 | [2] ch. 16 p. 381 |
| Card types | cache memory, a video frame buffer, a DMA-based I/O controller, expansion memory, or an additional MC68040 — with Apple's caveat that *the ROM software … may not support those hypothetical PDS cards* | [1] §"Processor-direct slot" p. 30 |

Cards designed for 68020/68030 machines will not work — the slot is the MC68040's own bus
(mcu.md §3.5).

### 4.3 Memory and VRAM expansion

DRAM expansion is the socket rule of §2.3: four banks of four equal 30-pin SIMMs, 1/4/16 MB
grades, 80 ns or faster, 4 to 64 MB total [1] §"RAM control" p. 11. VRAM expansion is a separate
population act on the video side: filling the two empty VRAM banks lifts the frame buffer from
the shipped 1 MB to its 2 MB maximum and buys *24 bits per pixel for the 13-inch monitor* [1]
§"Video frame-buffer controller IC: DAFB" p. 14 — the per-mode depth ceilings that follow are
tabulated in [dafb-modes.md](dafb-modes.md) ("Quadra 700 and 900"). The ROM SIMM socket of the
family design (mcu.md §2.3) rounds out the expansion surfaces.

### 4.4 Internal storage bays

The four half-high bays take any mix the case allows: *there are four half-high mounting
locations. Two of the four are in the front of the case, where they are accessible for removable
media. Any of the four locations can accommodate either 3.5-inch or 5.25-inch SCSI hard disk
drives*, with 5.25-inch full-height devices also accepted — covering the 20/40/80/160 MB Apple
drive line and third-party products [1] §"Internal mass-storage devices" pp. 20–21. *The upper
front location will normally be filled with a 1.44 MB SuperDrive*; the lower front location takes
tape, magneto-optical or similar removable media — *it can also be used for a CD-ROM drive* [1]
p. 21, whose audio integration is §3.6's. Internal drives cable to the two 50-pin connectors of
the internal SCSI bus (§3.3); the SuperDrive uses the SWIM's 20-pin connector [1]
§"Floppy disk connector" p. 23.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The family boot contract is mcu.md §2.9 (the access-triggered ROM overlay), §2.7 (the observed
Orwell programming sequence) and §5.6 (reset classes); this machine's branch points, in order:

1. **Trickle first.** With the AC cord connected and main power off, +5V TRKL keeps the PRAM, the
   power-on logic and the NuBus standby rail alive [1] §"Power supply" p. 24 — the rail that lets
   the keyboard start the machine and an STDBYPWR card assert /PFW.
2. **The keyswitch gates it.** In ON, power comes on from the keyboard and off from the Shut Down
   menu item; in SECURE the machine self-starts when power is applied, with ADB and the floppy
   disabled; in OFF it cannot start [1] §"Keyswitch" p. 26 (§3.5).
3. **Caboose releases the CPU.** The system manager's firmware runs before the 68040 does, and
   the CPU's reset is held until it is released — the power-on sequencing implied by Caboose's
   system-power role and by the reset distribution through JDB (mcu.md §2.2, §2.10)
   (*inferred — unverified*; §6.1).
4. **Overlay execution and the identity fork.** The `$420DBFF3` image runs at $00000000 from the
   overlay; the first normal-ROM-space access drops the overlay (mcu.md §2.9). The identify code
   reads the VIA1 port-A model sense, finds the Quadra 900's `$D0`-class pattern, and selects the
   tower paths — IOPs, Caboose, dual SCSI — over the 700's direct ones (*observed* in the ROM's
   identify code [3]; §1.2).
5. **Memory sizing and Orwell programming.** The sizing code discovers the four banks' sizes and
   programs contiguous logical starts into the MCU, exactly as traced at mcu.md §2.7 from this
   same image.
6. **IOP bring-up.** The ROM loads each IOP's program through its host aperture, releases the
   65C02, and runs the alive handshake before the serial and floppy/ADB stacks exist to the system
   ([iop.md](../iifx/iop.md) "Alive handshake", "Code load protocol"; the uploads' Q900 trace is
   §6.6's open question).
7. **Video bring-up.** DAFB is programmed to the monitor-sensed mode — the observed sequence is
   [dafb.md](dafb.md) §4.2's, and the pseudo-slot $9 declaration resources make the frame buffer
   a Slot Manager device (mcu.md §3.4).
8. **The vectored SCSI module.** The SCSI Manager module is selected through its startup-time
   vector, and the two buses present one seven-ID device namespace [1]
   §"Support for SCSI controllers" p. 49.
9. **Caboose handoff.** The RTC/PRAM contents come from Caboose, and the Start Manager sends the
   DFAC's defaults through it [1] §"Support for Caboose real-time clock IC" p. 50.
10. **Interrupt mode.** The A/UX remap stays off unless the VIA1 enable bit is set — the standard
    Macintosh level mapping of mcu.md §5.1 carries the machine to the desktop.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Caboose's exact silicon and firmware.** Apple defines the function [1] p. 9 and the schematic
   carries Egret-protocol nets [4]; which 68HC05-class part and firmware Apple shipped, and whether
   the 900's part matches the 950's, is the family's question (mcu.md §7 item 11) — and this page adds
   no new evidence to it.
2. **Caboose's host-interface bit assignment.** The recovered picture — command bytes through
   VIA1's shift register with port-B handshake bits, SECURE sense on a VIA1 handshake line —
   comes from the ROM's VIA1 initialization, not from any document or logic capture; the exact
   bits and the byte protocol's framing are *inferred — unverified* (§3.5).
3. **The doubled CPU clock.** The card-design book's table gives a 50 MHz CPU clock against the
   25 MHz bus clock [2] Table 1-1 pp. 14–16; the developer note says only 25 MHz [1] p. 7, and
   the PDS's AUX.CPUCLK is *a buffered version of [the] bus clock* [1] Table 2-3 p. 34. Whether
   the part receives a physical 2X clock like the 950's, and what drives the doubled domain, is
   not established (§2.2).
4. **VIA2 port B's tower bits.** The keyswitch shadow, soft-power and CPU-speed sense bits
   recovered from the ROM's VIA2 initialization (§3.8) have no Apple documentation; the
   per-bit encoding, their idle levels and whether the speed bit reads anything but 25 MHz on
   this board are unverified.
5. **The diagnostic strap.** Port A bit 0 is the diagnostic-mode strap of the identify code [3];
   its active level, what diagnostic mode changes on this machine, and the meaning of the
   unmasked bits 3, 5 and 7 are open (mcu.md §7 item 9).
6. **The IOP firmware uploads.** That the ROM uploads each IOP's program and releases its 65C02
   is architectural ([iop.md](../iifx/iop.md) "Code load protocol"); no instruction-level Q900
   capture of the uploads — which image revision goes to each IOP, and at what point of the boot —
   exists in evidence (§3.4).
7. **The SWIM/ADB IOP's interrupt line.** Its VIA2 handshake routing is recovered from the ROM's
   VIA2 PCR initialization; the note documents only that VIA2 generates level 2 for SCSI, sound
   and timer requests plus the slot bank [1] p. 40 (§3.4).
8. **The second SCSI lane's byte lanes.** The external 53C96's register stride and pseudo-DMA
   offsets ($5000F402/$5000F502) are recovered from the ROM's decode table; the family carries
   the verification gap (mcu.md §7 item 10) and this page's recovered values inherit it (§3.2).
9. **The physical slot order.** Which back-panel connector answers slot $A versus $E is drawn in
   the card-design book's Figure 6-4 [2] p. 128; the drawing's order has not been transcribed
   here, and no Apple text states it (§4.1).
10. **STDBYPWR's current budget.** *Low current* is the note's whole specification [1] Table 2-1
    p. 27; the limit, and how many cards may hold /PFW asserted simultaneously, is unstated.
11. **The factory CD-ROM's SCSI ID.** The CD option is documented as an internal-SCSI device
    [1] §"Internal CD-ROM integration" p. 24 with a single shared ID namespace [1] p. 49; the ID
    Apple shipped it at is not stated in the note.
12. **Undecoded-island and open-window reads on this board.** What a read of a reserved I/O
    island offset returns here — bus error, floating bus, or a stable value — is the family's
    open question (mcu.md §3.6 and §7 item 7), and no Q900-specific evidence resolves it.
13. **The 60 Hz-class VIA tick.** The periodic reference recovered on VIA1's handshake line
    (§3.8) is read from the ROM's timer configuration; its exact frequency derivation from
    Relayer's VIA clock (mcu.md §2.2) and its tolerance are unverified.
