# Macintosh Quadra 700

The Macintosh Quadra 700 is the desktop member of Apple's first 68040 generation — the 'Spike'
board in a vertical case the size of a Macintosh IIci: an MC68040 at 25 MHz, 4 MB of soldered RAM
plus one SIMM bank, 512 KB of dedicated VRAM under the DAFB frame-buffer controller, one 53C96
serving a single SCSI bus, SONIC Ethernet behind an AAUI port, two NuBus slots and one
processor-direct slot. Apple's own framing of the machine is the reduced member of a pair: it
*\"provides many of the same functions as the Macintosh Quadra 900 computer but has a different
case design and a reduced feature set\"* [1] §\"Introduction\" pp. 1–2. Everything the three
Quadra boards share — the buses, the custom-IC set, the memory map, the MCU/Orwell register
treatment, the interrupt and clock architecture — is documented once in the family page
([mcu.md](mcu.md)) and cited here by section, never restated. This page carries what is unique
to the 700; its tower siblings are covered by [Quadra 900](q900.md) and [Quadra 950](q950.md).

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 What the machine is

The Quadra 700 shipped in October 1991 as one of the two launch Quadras [1] §\"Summary of major
features\" pp. 1–2. Its logic board is the **Spike** project — the name appears on the
main-logic-board schematic [6] — and the machine is the desktop half of the generation: the
same custom-IC set as the [Quadra 900](q900.md) tower, in a case *\"the same size as that of
the Macintosh IIci, but … designed to stand vertically\"* [1] §\"Introduction\" pp. 1–2, with
built-in video that *\"provides 24-bit color and performance approaching that of the Macintosh
Display Card 8•24GC\"* [1] §\"Introduction\" pp. 1–2. Apple's own feature list is §1.3's spec
table's spine — the 25 MHz MC68040, the ROM SIMM socket, the two RAM banks, the separate video
RAM, the improved SCSI, AAUI Ethernet, the SuperDrive, the two NuBus '90 slots, the PDS, and
sound *\"like those of the Macintosh IIsi\"* [1] §\"Summary of major features\" pp. 1–2.
Everything board-level — the three buses, the MCU/Orwell, JDB and Relayer, YANCC, DAFB, the
I/O island, the interrupt architecture — is the family's ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2–§5); this page
adds the 700's own identity, clocks, memory population, chip set as fitted, wiring deltas,
expansion and boot specifics.

Two consequences of the shared ROM are worth stating because they shape everything else on this
page. First, the 700 and the 900 are *one software platform with two south-bridge wirings*: the
same image must drive the 700's direct SCC, SWIM and RTC and the towers' IOP-based organization,
and it tells them apart by the VIA1 port A straps ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.10). Second, the ROM
software is *\"derived from the universal ROM used in the Macintosh IIci, Macintosh IIfx,
Macintosh IIsi, and Macintosh LC computers\"* — its first half is an overpatch of the IIci ROM,
its second half the new Quadra code [1] §\"ROM memory map\" p. 48, which is why the 700's direct,
IIci-style low-speed I/O is the wiring the older half of the ROM already knew.

### 1.4 The vertical case, connectors and power

The case is the IIci's footprint turned upright: *\"a new case the same size as that of the
Macintosh IIci but designed to stand vertically\"*, with room for one internal SuperDrive and one
3.5-inch hard disk, and *\"the user can remove the feet … [and] install the extra feet provided in
the box to use the case horizontally\"* [1] §\"Vertical case\" p. 18. The back panel carries the
programmer's buttons, the sound connectors, the SCSI and Ethernet connectors and the two serial
ports; the front carries the two recessed pushbuttons.

- **Programmer's buttons.** Two recessed buttons — the reset button and the NMI button — *\"set
  into the front of the case to prevent their being pressed accidentally\"* [1]
  §\"Programmer's buttons\" p. 20. The NMI button is the level-7 programmer's switch
  ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.1, §5.6).
- **Microphone input.** A rear Mic In connector, with the same microphone as the Macintosh IIsi
  and Macintosh LC included in the box. The connector *\"includes +8 volts on one of its pins\"* —
  Apple's warning is explicit: plugging a standard microphone, headphone or speaker into it
  *\"could cause damage to the device\"* [1] §\"Microphone and Mic In connector\" p. 20. The
  supplied audio-cable adapter mixes two line-level signals to the single monophonic input, so
  stereo information is lost [1] §\"Microphone and Mic In connector\" p. 20.
- **Sound output.** An internal speaker, plus a Sound Out connector *\"similar to the one on the
  Macintosh II [with] the same electrical characteristics\"* [1] §\"Sound Out connector and
  speaker\" p. 21.
- **SCSI.** One internal 50-pin connector and one external DB-25 connector, *\"identical to those
  used on Macintosh II-family computers\"*; the external chain's last device and internal devices
  must be terminated [1] §\"SCSI connectors\" pp. 22–23 and Table 1-2. The pinout itself is the
  II-family one and is not restated here.
- **Ethernet.** The AAUI connector, accepting AUI (thick) cable directly or a FriendlyNet adapter
  for CheaperNet (thin) or 10BaseT (twisted pair) media [1] §\"Ethernet connector\" p. 25.
- **Power.** The supply is the IIci's, auto-ranging on the applied mains voltage, with the
  destination country's power cord in the localization package [1] §\"Power supply\" p. 19. The
  machine has no keyswitch and no system-management processor: power switching is the II-family
  discrete scheme, not the tower's Caboose chain (§3.11).

Two remarks. First, the 700 is the machine the family's timing tables are *named* for: the
25 MHz system bus and the 15.6672 MHz I/O bus quoted throughout [mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.1 are this
board's figures, which the 900 shares and the 950 speeds up. Second, the 53C96's TurboSCSI
programming is clock-derived, and the boot ROM writes this machine's SCSI timing configuration
into the DAFB aperture before anything else it does there ([dafb.md](dafb.md) §4.2, §3.6).

### 2.3 RAM: the two-bank geometry and its population rules

The family RAM contract — 64 MB physical decode windows per bank, ROM-sized banks stitched into
contiguous logical memory by the MCU's bank registers — is [mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.3's, with the
observed bring-up in §2.7. The 700's part of it, in the developer note's own numbers:

- **Bank A is fixed.** *\"The first bank contains 4 MB soldered to the board\"* [1] §\"RAM
  control\" p. 11 — and because Orwell's bank A is not programmable and always decodes physical
  zero ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.5), the 700's first bank can never move or be absent. This is the
  machine-level reason the family doc can say the soldered bank is \"a fixed first bank\".
- **Bank B is four 30-pin SIMMs.** *\"The second bank can accept four standard 30-pin SIMMs\"*;
  the devices *\"must have fast page mode, and access time must be 80 ns or less\"*; *\"acceptable
  SIMM sizes are 1 MB and 4 MB, and all four SIMMs must contain the same amount of RAM\"* [1]
  §\"RAM control\" p. 11. The SIMM signal set is the II-family 30-pin one — the same sockets the
  SE/30 and the rest of the II family use, documented as Table 5-6 of the hardware guide [4]
  p. 212; the note's own comparison is *\"Like the expansion RAM for the Macintosh IIci\"* [1]
  §\"RAM control\" p. 11.
- **The reachable totals.** Added RAM is 4 MB or 16 MB, *\"giving the computer possible memory
  sizes of 4 MB, 8 MB, and 20 MB\"* [1] §\"RAM control\" p. 11 — the summary list puts it as
  *\"4 MB in bank 1 and 0, 4, or 16 MB in bank 2\"* [1] §\"Summary of major features\" pp. 1–2.
  Bank B physically sits at the 64 MB boundary until the ROM programs its logical start
  ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.3).
- **What the boot actually writes.** On the traced 8 MB configuration (4 MB soldered plus four
  1 MB SIMMs) the ROM's memory-sizing bring-up runs the full Orwell sequence of [mcu.md](mcu.md)
  §2.7 — the early first-longword reads from the overlay and again from the normal aperture, the
  thirty-four-bit halving chain, the latch strobes — and finishes with a reprogramming burst that
  serializes the discovered contiguous geometry ($008B0810, $00030810, $00030810, $00030810,
  $00C58408, … , written one longword per configuration bit at $5000E000) followed by its strobe
  writes (*observed* in the boot-time Orwell access trace, [5]).

SIMM sizes beyond the documented pair are a real-world question, not a documented one: later
practice and larger 30-pin parts can produce 36 or 68 MB configurations, but nothing in the
launch note establishes them, and the ROM's sizing path against such parts is unverified (§6).

### 2.4 VRAM as fitted

The family video contract is [mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.4's; the part, its registers and its mode tables
are [dafb.md](dafb.md) and [dafb-modes.md](dafb-modes.md). The 700's fit:

- **Four banks, one populated.** *\"The video frame buffer comprises four banks of video RAM
  (VRAM); the basic configuration has 512 KB of VRAM installed in the first of the four banks\"*
  [1] §\"Video frame-buffer controller IC: DAFB\" p. 15.
- **The expansion parts.** *\"Each of the other three banks has two slots for VRAM SIMMs. The
  VRAM SIMMs hold 256 KB each and are the same as the VRAM SIMMs used in the Macintosh LC and the
  Macintosh Display Card 4•8\"* [1] p. 15 — so the population ladder is 512 KB (as shipped), 1 MB
  (one pair added), 2 MB (all three pairs). The comparison table records the asymmetry against
  the tower: the 700 has *\"1 bank of VRAM soldered; 3 banks for expansion VRAM\"* where the 900
  has two banks soldered and two for expansion [1] Table 1-4 p. 26.
- **What each step buys.** One pair: *\"24 bits per pixel on the 12-inch monitor and 8 bits per
  pixel on NTSC and PAL monitors, using Apple convolution to reduce flicker\"*. The remaining
  pairs: *\"24 bits per pixel for the 13-inch monitor and for NTSC and PAL monitors without
  convolution\"* [1] p. 15. Apple's own depth matrix is Table 1-1 [1] p. 16, transcribed with
  the resolution rows filled in at [dafb-modes.md](dafb-modes.md); note the 24-bit nuance there —
  on this generation a 24-bit pixel occupies a full 32-bit VRAM longword, so \"24-bit\" rows are
  statements about VRAM consumption as much as colour.
- **The chip set behind the frame buffer.** The AC842 palette/DAC and the DP8531 pixel-clock
  synthesizer, as this board carries them, are [dafb.md](dafb.md) §2.4 and §2.5's subject; the
  950's AC842a 16-bit mode is not this machine's ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §6.3).

### 2.5 What this machine does not carry

Worth stating explicitly, because the towers carry every one of these and the shared ROM knows
about them all:

- **No IOPs.** The serial, floppy and ADB ports have no intelligent I/O processors behind them —
  the rows of the comparison table read \"SCC\", \"SWIM\", \"ADB: same as Macintosh IIci\" where
  the 900's read \"SCC/IOP\", \"SWIM/IOP\", \"ADB: same as Macintosh IIfx\" [1] Table 1-4 p. 26.
- **No Caboose.** No keyswitch, no system-management processor, no trickle-power contract:
  the 900's *\"+5V TRKL … available whenever the computer is plugged in\"* [2] §\"Power supply\"
  p. 24 and its STDBYPWR slot pin [2] §\"NuBus '90 features\" pp. 26–27 are tower hardware; the
  700's own NuBus '90 signal table carries no standby-power pin at all [1] Table 2-1 pp. 23–24.
- **No slots $A–$C, no high-power or oversized-card support.** Two physical connectors
  (VIA2 PA1–PA3 unconnected, [1] Table 2-6 p. 37); 15 W per slot, 30 W total, standard-size
  cards only [1] Table 1-4 p. 26.
- **No parity RAM subsystem.** The DRAM interface carries parity/test signal wiring on the
  schematic [6], and Orwell has parity fields in its recovered configuration register
  ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.5, §7 item 1), but no Apple document for this machine describes a parity
  option and the boot ROM never touches the parity latches ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.6).

## 3. Per-subsystem wiring

### 3.1 The processor and the system bus as fitted

The MC68040 is the family's processor ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §4.1): a redesigned integer unit, a
built-in FPU (not the MC68881's), built-in instruction and data MMUs (not the MC68851's), and
4 KB each of instruction and data cache in the new CopyBack mode [1] §\"MC68040
microprocessor\" pp. 5–7. The one machine-level wiring fact that belongs on this page is the
snooping rule, because it is a contract every PDS card on this specific slot must meet: *\"The
MC68040 has another new feature called cache snooping. That feature is not used in the Macintosh
Quadra 700 computer and is not supported by the software. Devices that transfer data on the
system bus, such as PDS bus masters, must drive the snoop control pins to indicate no snooping\"*
[1] §\"MC68040 microprocessor\" p. 7. The consequences for alternate masters — the ROM's
uncacheable communication pages, the one-image MMU map — are the family's ([mcu.md](mcu.md)
§5.4).

Four controller ICs sit on the 25 MHz system bus with the processor: the MCU, YANCC, DAFB and the
SCSI controller [1] §\"Design architecture\" p. 4 — on this board, four means four, where the
towers' second 53C96 makes five ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.1). The PDS connector is wired to these
same pins unbuffered ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.5, §4.2 below).

### 3.2 Memory: Orwell as this board drives it

The MCU/Orwell register file at $5000E000 — the serial configuration register, the latch
strobes, the power-up bank split — is documented once in the family doc ([mcu.md](mcu.md)
§2.3–§2.7) and is not restated here. What is this machine's own:

- **The fixed bank.** Orwell's bank A is not programmable and always decodes physical zero
  ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.5), which on this board is the 4 MB of soldered RAM: the 700 is the family
  member whose first bank is a board constant, so the ROM's sizing only ever has to discover
  bank B ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.3).
- **The boot trace is this machine's.** Every observed Orwell sequence in the family doc — the
  early first-longword reads executed from the overlay at $0000315E and re-executed from the
  normal aperture at $4080315E, the halving-chain burst at +$000–+$084, the strobes at
  +$0A0–+$0BC, the reprogramming during sizing — was recorded on a Quadra 700 configuration
  (8 MB, 4 MB soldered + 4 MB of SIMMs) [5], so the family's §2.7 numbers *are* this machine's
  numbers; the 900/950 inherit them by architecture, not by trace.
- **The ROM SIMM socket is Orwell-side.** The 1 MB boot ROM is two 4-Mbit devices, each 256K ×
  16-bit at 150 ns, and *\"a ROM SIMM socket is available for future expansion; however, when a
  ROM SIMM is installed in this socket, the on-board ROM will be automatically disabled\"* [1]
  §\"ROM control\" p. 11 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.3). The II-family ROM SIMM socket background —
  pinout and signal assignments — is the hardware guide's [4] §\"ROM SIMMs\" pp. 235–237.

### 3.3 Video: DAFB, AC842 and DP8531 as fitted

Built-in video is the pseudo-slot-$9 device: the DAFB control registers and the frame-buffer
VRAM are mapped into the locations assigned to NuBus slot $9 in earlier Macintosh models
[1] §\"Video frame-buffer controller IC: DAFB\" pp. 15–16, with the apertures — the 2 MB VRAM
window at $F9000000 and the register window at $F9800000 — in [dafb.md](dafb.md) §2.1. This
board's video path is the baseline one: DAFB plus the AC842 palette/DAC and the DP8531
pixel-clock synthesizer ([dafb.md](dafb.md) §2.4–§2.5), 512 KB of VRAM in the first of four banks
(§2.4), no 16-bit \"Thousands\" mode — that is the 950's AC842a path ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §6.3).

The machine-level video facts a re-implementation needs from *this* page are the fit and the
placement, both above; the register contracts, the monitor-sense protocol and the boot-time
initialization sequence — TurboSCSI timing write first, RAMDAC probe, DP8531 program with the
commit register last, the bring-up raster, the monitor-sense sweep, the monitor's own mode —
are [dafb.md](dafb.md) §3.5–§3.6 and §4.2's, and were recorded on this machine's boot
([dafb.md](dafb.md) §4.2, [5]). The frame buffer's position on the system bus is what makes the
machine's drawing speed: *\"Because the frame buffer in the Macintosh Quadra 700 computer is
connected directly to the system bus, it works with the MC68040 to provide high drawing speed
for all applications, even those that don't use QuickDraw\"* [1] §\"Video frame-buffer
controller IC: DAFB\" p. 16.

### 3.4 SCSI: the single 53C96 and its one bus

The SCSI protocol controller is one NCR 53C96 [1] §\"SCSI controller IC\" p. 11 — the part
itself, its register file, its pseudo-DMA aperture and the DAFB TurboSCSI timing assist that
interposes between it and the system bus are [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md)
(§2.1 for the address decode, §3.8 for the TurboSCSI aperture, §4.2 for this generation's
firmware). What is this machine's own:

- **One bus, two connectors.** The internal 50-pin connector and the external DB-25 connector are
  served by the single controller [1] Table 1-4 p. 26, and both connectors sit on one electrical
  SCSI bus — one arbitration and ID namespace, one termination chain (*inferred — unverified*:
  the recovered board wiring shows a shared bus; Apple's text says only that one controller
  serves \"both internal and external SCSI devices\" [1] Table 1-4 p. 26, in contrast to the
  900's electrically separate pair, [mcu.md](mcu.md) §6.2).
- **Termination is the II-family rule.** External devices must be terminated if last on the
  chain, and *\"SCSI devices designed for internal use should also be terminated\"* [1]
  §\"SCSI connectors\" pp. 22–23.
- **One internal bay.** Room for one internal 3.5-inch hard disk [1] Table 1-4 p. 26 — the
  900's four-device internal bay complement is tower hardware.
- **Host address space.** The controller's register block is the $5000F000 I/O island entry, with
  its pseudo-DMA data aperture following the register block ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.2;
  [ncr-53c96.md](../../hardware/scsi/ncr-53c96.md) §3.8); the 700's $5000F400 block — SCSI 1 on
  the towers — is reserved for Apple on this board ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.2).
- **Interrupt.** The SCSI request is one of VIA2's level-2 sources [1] §\"Macintosh Quadra 700
  Direct Slot interrupt handling\" pp. 36–37; its VIA2 pin is recovered as CB2 (*inferred —
  unverified*, §3.9).
- **The ROM's SCSI support is deliberately machine-agnostic** — the SCSI Manager module is
  *\"a separate module addressed by a vector that is set up at startup time\"* because the same
  ROM code serves future models that do not use the 53C96 [1] §\"Support for SCSI controller\"
  p. 45 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §1.2).

### 3.5 Ethernet: the SONIC and the AAUI port

Ethernet is the DP83932 SONIC on the I/O bus — the one I/O-bus bus master of the family
([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.3) — with its register window at $5000A000 and its station-address PROM at
$50008000 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.2; [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.3, §2.1). The chip,
its DMA engine and its driver contracts are [sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md)'s; this
machine's contributions are the media attachment and the clock: the back-panel AAUI connector
accepts AUI (thick) cable directly or a FriendlyNet adapter for CheaperNet or 10BaseT [1]
§\"Ethernet connector\" p. 25, and the part is the 20 MHz class fed by the 15.6672 MHz I/O bus
([sonic.md](../../hardware/sonic.md) §1.4) — the 950's 25 MHz SONIC is the faster-I/O-bus
machine's part, not this one's ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §6.3). The interrupt is VIA2 port A bit PA0
([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.2), and the ROM carries a dedicated driver for the part [1] §\"Support for
Sonic Ethernet controller\" p. 44.

### 3.6 Serial: the direct SCC

The serial ports are the two channels of a Z8530-class SCC wired directly to the I/O bus at
$5000C000 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.2, §4.2) — no IOP intervenes, which is the whole delta against
the towers [1] Table 1-4 p. 26. The SCC answers on the upper byte of its word accesses with the
classic Macintosh register-select conventions, inherited unchanged from the II family
([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.2; [4] §\"SCC\" p. 169). Its interrupt is CPU level 4 in both the Macintosh
and A/UX mappings ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.1). The channel-to-port assignment — channel A to the
modem port, channel B to the printer/LocalTalk port — follows the II-family convention this
board inherits (*inferred — unverified*; the note does not name the channels). The motherboard
provides no DMA engine for the SCC; the ROM services it from interrupt context (*inferred —
unverified*: the direct II-family wiring carries none, and the note describes none).

### 3.7 Floppy: the direct SWIM

*A \"single SWIM chip controls the internal SuperDrive\"* [1] §\"Floppy disk connector\" p. 24 —
again direct, not behind an IOP [1] Table 1-4 p. 26. The SWIM itself — IWM compatibility, GCR and
MFM modes, the 400/800 KB and 1.44 MB media classes — is [swim.md](../../hardware/swim.md)'s; the
register block is the $5001E000 I/O island entry with the II-family spacing and upper-byte
conventions ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.2). The drive cable is this machine's own 20-pin connector;
Apple's pinout, worth reproducing because it is the whole electrical interface between the SWIM
and the drive [1] §\"Floppy disk connector\" p. 24 and Table 1-3:

| 1 | GND | ground | 11 | +5V | drive power |
| 2 | PH0 | phase 0 state control | 12 | SEL | head select |
| 3 | GND | ground | 13 | +12V | drive power |

### 3.8 VIA1: ADB, RTC/PRAM and the model sense

VIA1 at $50000000 is the level-1 interrupt source and the carrier of this machine's low-speed
housekeeping — the duties the towers hand to Caboose and the IOPs ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §4.2,
§2.10). Its port B carries the II-family assignment, which this board inherits with the IIci
ADB and direct RTC of the comparison table [1] Table 1-4 p. 26 and which matches the 700
schematic's wiring [6]:

| VIA1 port B bit | Function | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | RTC serial data (rTCData) | the II-family assignment [4] Table 4-14 p. 171; RTC bits Table 3-11 p. 144 |
| 1 | RTC serial clock (rTCCLK) | [4] Table 4-14 p. 171 |
| 2 | RTC enable (rTCEnb, active low) | [4] Table 4-14 p. 171 |
| 3 | ADB interrupt (vFDBInt, active low) | [4] Table 4-14 p. 171 |
| 4–5 | ADB state lines ST0/ST1 | [4] Table 4-14 p. 171 |

The port-B assignment itself is the II-family convention ([4] Table 4-14 p. 171, the SE/30 and
II-family table); that the 700 follows it is the note's \"same as Macintosh IIci\" row plus the
schematic's wiring [1] Table 1-4 p. 26, [6] (*inferred — unverified* at the bit level: no Apple
document republishes the 700's own port B table). The RTC behind it is the classic
seconds-since-1904 part with its one-second interrupt into VIA1 [4] §\"The one-second
interrupt\" p. 145; the schematic carries an Apple RTC/PRAM device of the 3430042 class on these
lines [6]. The ADB transceiver is discrete, driven from the VIA1 state lines — the IIci scheme,
not the IIfx/IOP scheme ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §4.2).

Port A is where this machine's identity lives. The boot ROM's identify code reads VIA1 port A
and selects the machine path from the strap pattern ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.10); on the 700 the
pattern is `$C0`, with bit 0 following the programmer's-button diagnostic state (*observed* in
the identify code of the `$420DBFF3` image, [5]). The upper port A bits also carry board I/O —
the recovered wiring has bit 5 as the floppy side/head select — but that assignment is not
established by any Apple document (§6, item 1).

### 3.9 VIA2 as fitted: the slot bank and the sound path

VIA2 at $50002000 concentrates level 2 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.2). This machine's port A bank —
Apple's own table, with the two physical slots and the empty tower positions [1] Table 2-6
p. 37:

| VIA2 port A bit | Source on the 700 |
|---|---|
| PA0 | Ethernet IRQ |
| PA1 | not connected |
| PA2 | not connected |
| PA3 | not connected |
| PA4 | NuBus slot $D IRQ |
| PA5 | NuBus slot $E IRQ, shared with the 68040 Direct Slot |
| PA6 | built-in video IRQ (pseudo-slot $9) |
| PA7 | reads high; no source identified (*inferred — unverified*) |

The slot, video and Ethernet lines are OR-gated into /SLOTIRQ on VIA2's CA1, and the handler
polls PA0–PA6 to find the source — the family contract of [mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.2, unchanged here.
The rest of this machine's VIA2 wiring: the Enhanced ASC's interrupt arrives on CB1
([asc.md](../../hardware/asc.md) §6.1); the SCSI request is a further handshake pin, recovered
as CB2 (*inferred — unverified*); and the VIA2 PB7 output chains to VIA1 CA1 as the 60.15 Hz
periodic interrupt — the II-family /VBLK chain [4] §\"Functions of VIA1\" pp. 153–154 — which
this board keeps as its vertical-blanking-era heartbeat. Three more port B bits are recovered as
the DFAC's serial control (latch, data, clock on PB0/PB3/PB4) — see §3.10 and §6.

### 3.10 Sound: DFAC, Enhanced ASC and Sporty

The sound subsystem is the IIsi/LC-generation design Apple names in its feature list [1]
§\"Summary of major features\" pp. 1–2: *\"Three Apple custom ICs plus a digital-to-analog
converter provide the sound interface\"* [1] §\"Sound ICs: DFAC, Enhanced ASC, and Sporty\"
pp. 17–18 — DFAC for input (antialiasing filter and ADC), the Enhanced ASC as the ASC-compatible
generator, and Sporty replacing the two Sony sound ICs, with the external DAC giving *\"better
sound quality with less noise and distortion\"*; Sporty *\"contains digital attenuators and power
amplifiers\"* as the Sony parts did [1] pp. 17–18. The capabilities are modest and stated:
*\"the ability to record monophonic sound from the microphone input\"* and *\"the ability to play
8-bit sound files\"* [1] pp. 17–18.

The wiring: the Enhanced ASC's register block is the $50014000 I/O island entry ([mcu.md](mcu.md)
§3.2, [asc.md](../../hardware/asc.md) §3.3), its interrupt reaches VIA2 CB1 and dispatches at
level 2 — level 5 under the A/UX remap ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.1; [asc.md](../../hardware/asc.md)
§6.1). The DFAC is controlled serially from VIA2 port B, with latch, data and clock recovered
on PB0, PB3 and PB4 (*inferred — unverified*: the assignment is recovered from the board's
serial-control wiring and the DFAC's LC-era conventions; no Apple document publishes this
board's port B table). The audio section of the 700 schematic carries the title **Batman
Sound** on its sheet [6] — a project label for the subsystem, not a chip name. The analog ends
are §1.4's: the +8 V-biased Mic In connector, the mono-mixing line adapter, the II-class Sound
Out jack and the internal speaker [1] pp. 20–21.

### 3.11 Reset and power on this machine

The reset classes — power-on, the CPU reset that re-arms the overlay, peripheral resets that are
not motherboard resets — are the family's ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.6), as is the overlay contract
itself ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.9). What is this machine's own is everything the towers route
through Caboose: the 700 has no system manager, so power sequencing is the II-family discrete
scheme. The supply is the IIci's, auto-ranging, with the localization power cord [1] §\"Power
supply\" p. 19; the II-family power-control circuit — keyboard power-on, software shutdown from
the Shut Down menu, the soft-power chain that keeps PRAM alive — is the hardware guide's design
[4] §\"Power-control circuit in the Macintosh II-family computers\" p. 269 and §\"Power up,
system startup, and power down\" pp. 241–245, which this board follows as the IIci's supply and
connector imply [1] §\"Power supply\" p. 19, [4] Table 6-5 p. 250 (*inferred — unverified*: the
note says only that the supply is the IIci's; the control scheme follows from the family this
board's power logic belongs to, and from the schematic's discrete power-control wiring [6]).
The DC outputs of the shared IIcx/IIci-class supply are the guide's [4] p. 261. The
programmer's reset and NMI buttons are the front-panel resets of §1.4, wired to the CPU reset
and level-7 paths respectively ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.1, §5.6).

## 4. Expansion

### 4.1 The two NuBus slots

The 700 has two NuBus slots and one PDS [1] §\"Expansion slots\" p. 22. The physical connectors
serve slots **$D and $E** — the arrangement drawing shows the two connectors plus the PDS in
line with slot $E, against the IIci's three-slot layout, and *\"notice the alignment of the PDS
slot with NuBus slot $E\"* [1] §\"Expansion slots\" p. 22 (Figure 2-1). The slots are the earlier
modular Macintoshes' with the NuBus '90 additions [1] §\"NuBus slots\" p. 23:

- **Double-rate block transfers, card-to-card only.** The new signals /TM2, /CLK2X and /CLK2XEN
  *\"support block transfers at double the standard rate\"*, and *\"the Macintosh Quadra 700
  computer allows double-rate block transfers between NuBus cards but does not support
  double-rate transfers to or from the main memory\"* [1] §\"NuBus slots\" p. 23.
- **Serial-bus pins present but undriven.** NuBus '90's SB0 and SB1 occupy the formerly reserved
  pins A2 and C2; *\"the serial-bus signals are bused and terminated, but the main circuit board
  does not drive them\"* [1] §\"NuBus slots\" p. 23.
- **Cache-coherency pins present but unsupported.** /CM0, /CM1, /CM2 and /CBUSY have connector
  pins assigned, *\"but the Macintosh Quadra 700 system doesn't support them\"* [1] §\"NuBus
  slots\" p. 23 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §4.4). Apple's signal table for the connector adds the footnote
  that all of these — SB0, SB1, /TM2, /CM0–/CM2, /CBUSY — are *\"not driven or monitored by
  circuits in the Macintosh Quadra 700 computer\"* [1] Table 2-1 pp. 23–24.
- **The -5.2 V warning.** *\"The eight lines that were connected to the -5.2-volt supply in the
  original NuBus specification are now used for new features. Many older NuBus cards connect
  those eight lines together; the presence of such a card in a Macintosh Quadra 700 computer
  will disable the new features that use those lines\"* [1] §\"NuBus slots\" p. 23 — one legacy
  card anywhere on the bus costs every card the NuBus '90 features.
- **Power and card size.** 15 W per slot, or 30 W total, standard-size NuBus cards only [1]
  Table 1-4 p. 26 — the tightest budget in the family.
- **No standby-power pin.** The 700's NuBus '90 signal table carries no STDBYPWR; the 900's
  connectors add it (pin B25, live whenever the machine is plugged in) [1] Table 2-1 pp. 23–24,
  [2] §\"NuBus '90 features\" pp. 26–27 — the tower trickle rail is not this machine's hardware.

The full connector pin assignment beyond the NuBus '90 additions is the standard one, published
in the NuBus card electrical design chapter of the card-design book [3] ch. 5, which Apple's
note points to by name [1] §\"NuBus slots\" p. 23. Slot interrupts arrive on VIA2 PA4 and PA5,
slot $E's line shared with the PDS ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.2); the slot probe contract — an empty
slot must fault cleanly — is the family's ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.6).

### 4.2 The processor-direct slot

The PDS is the family's 68040 Direct Slot, and its system-level contract — the unbuffered 68040
pins, the pseudoslot address decode in upper RAM and slot $E, the /TA-or-/TEA obligation, the
absence of a slot-$E timeout — is documented once in the family doc ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.5) and
in the card-design book's Quadra chapter [3] ch. 16, and is not restated here. This machine's
own specifications:

- **Mechanics.** A PDS card has the same dimensions as a NuBus card and may include a back-panel
  connector; the maximum board length is the Apple-preferred 322.30 mm that matches the standard
  panel opening [1] §\"PDS card specifications\" p. 25 (Figure 2-2). The connector is a 140-pin
  KEL part — part number 8817-140-170SH on the main circuit board, 8807-140-170LH on the card
  [1] §\"PDS card specifications\" p. 25.
- **Electrical limits.** The signals are the MC68040's own with no buffers: address, data and
  AUX.CPUCLK lines must present *\"capacitive loads of not more than 40 pF\"*, all other lines
  not more than 20 pF [1] §\"PDS card specifications\" p. 25; the per-signal load/drive table is
  Table 2-5 [1] p. 31. The AUX.CPUCLK line is series-terminated, and *\"to reduce reflections on
  this line, all loads on the card should be lumped\"* [1] Table 2-2 note p. 27.
- **The non-microprocessor signals** — the PDS pins that are not the processor's own — are
  Apple's Table 2-3 [1] p. 30: /ANALOGRESET (open-collector system reset, *\"used only for
  testing\"*), AUX.CPUCLK (buffered bus clock), /BG.40SLOT and /BR.40SLOT (the PDS bus
  grant/request pair), /MEMRESET (JDB's fast reset for the MCU), /MI.SLOT (memory inhibit into
  the MCU), /NMRQ6 (the slot-$E NuBus interrupt line, shared) and /PDS.SLOT.E.EN (the pseudoslot
  claim into YANCC). Two microprocessor signals are restricted — /IPL0–/IPL2 are *\"from PAL;
  not to be used as wire-OR lines\"*, and /TIP.CPU goes nowhere else in the machine [1] Table 2-4
  p. 30 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.1).
- **Timing.** *\"The signal timing for the PDS connector depends on the clock speed of the 68040
  microprocessor\"* — on this board, 25 MHz — and a card's outputs *\"must be equal to or better
  than the worst-case delay output timing of the 68040\"*; developers are told to mark the
  card's maximum clock speed on the card [1] §\"Timing considerations\" p. 34. Cards designed
  for 68020/68030 machines *\"will not work\"* [1] §\"PDS card design considerations\" p. 29.
- **Intended uses.** Apple's list is cache memory, a video frame buffer, a DMA-based I/O
  controller, expansion memory, *\"or even an additional MC68040 microprocessor\"* — with the
  caveat that *\"the ROM software in the Macintosh Quadra 700 computer may not support those
  hypothetical PDS cards\"* [1] §\"Processor-direct slot\" p. 25. The one PDS slot is shared
  hardware with the family: *\"cards will also work in Macintosh Quadra 900\"* [1] Table 1-4
  p. 26.

### 4.3 The ROM SIMM socket

The 700 carries a 1 MB ROM SIMM socket beside the soldered boot ROM; installing a SIMM there
*\"automatically disable[s] the on-board ROM\"* [1] §\"ROM control\" p. 11. Apple's framing is
*\"a ROM SIMM socket … for future expansion\"* [1] §\"Summary of major features\" pp. 1–2 — the
socket is the service-and-update path for the image, not a user option at launch; the II-family
ROM SIMM conventions (socket pinout, signal assignments) are the hardware guide's [4] §\"ROM
SIMMs\" pp. 235–237. What a populated socket must contain — image format, checksum obligations,
whether any Apple ROM SIMM ever shipped for this socket — is not documented (§6).

### 4.4 Internal storage and termination

Inside the case: one SuperDrive bay and one 3.5-inch hard-disk bay [1] §\"Vertical case\" p. 18,
both devices hanging off the single internal 50-pin SCSI connector ([§3.4](#34-scsi-the-single-53c96-and-its-one-bus)). Termination follows the II-family rule Apple restates for this machine:
external devices must be terminated if they are the last device on the external chain, and
*\"SCSI devices designed for internal use should also be terminated\"* [1] §\"SCSI connectors\"
pp. 22–23. The floppy's 20-pin interface is §3.7's table. Beyond the drive bays, the 700's
storage expansion story is entirely the SCSI chain's: there is no second controller, no
internal 5.25-inch provision and no room beyond the single hard-disk bay [1] Table 1-4 p. 26.

## 5. Boot sequence summary

The family boot contract — the access-triggered ROM-at-zero overlay, the identify code's model
selection, the Orwell bring-up — is [mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.9–§2.10 and §2.7's. This machine's
branch points, in order, with the observed witnesses from the `$420DBFF3` trace on the 8 MB
configuration [5]:

1. **Power-on.** The II-family power-up sequence applies supply power through the discrete
   soft-power circuitry (§3.11); the overlay is armed — ROM decodes at $00000000, RAM disabled
   ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.9).
2. **Reset vector fetch.** The 68040 takes its initial SSP and PC from the overlay, and the
   start-up code runs at low addresses — the trace's first Orwell reads execute at $0000315E
   [5] ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.7 step 1).
3. **Overlay drop.** The first access to the normal ROM aperture disarms the overlay — observed
   as the drop at PC $40802E3E, with the same early code then re-executing from $4080315E [5]
   ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.7 step 1, §2.9).
4. **Machine identify.** The identify code reads VIA1 port A and selects the Quadra 700 path on
   the `$C0` pattern — the direct low-speed I/O, single-SCSI and two-slot wiring of this page,
   against the towers' IOP organization ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.10; §3.8).
5. **Memory bring-up.** The Orwell programming runs: the early first-longword reads, the
   thirty-four-bit halving chain at +$000–+$084, the latch strobes at +$0A0–+$0BC, then the
   sizing reprogramming that ends with the discovered banks stitched contiguously — on the
   traced machine, the $008B0810-class chain and its strobes [5] ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.7; §2.3
   above).
6. **Video bring-up.** The DAFB initialization: the TurboSCSI timing write first, the RAMDAC
   presence probe, the DP8531 program with the commit register last, the core and CRTC
   programmed for the bring-up raster, then the monitor-sense sweep and the monitor's own mode —
   two complete mode sets in one boot, observed on this machine ([dafb.md](dafb.md) §4.2, [5]).
7. **SCSI Manager.** The SCSI support module is selected by the startup vector — the
   machine-agnostic indirection that lets one ROM image serve this 53C96 design and later
   designs ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §1.2; [1] §\"Support for SCSI controller\" p. 45).
8. **Slot Manager.** The pseudo-slot enumeration polls slot $9 (built-in video, whose
   declaration resources live in the boot image), slots $D and $E, and the PDS pseudoslot
   ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.4, §3.5); empty slots must fault cleanly ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §3.6).
9. **System software handoff.** The ROM's 68040-era machinery comes up: the MMU tables built
   for the one-ROM-image, one-I/O-image map ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.4), the CopyBack cache
   discipline with its flush points [1] §\"Cache management by the ROM\" p. 41, the patches for
   pre-68040 NuBus video cards that relied on byte smearing [1] §\"ROM support for the video
   hardware\" p. 43, and the MOVE16-based QuickDraw and BlockMove optimizations [1]
   §\"Enhancements to QuickDraw\" p. 47.
10. **Warm-start behaviour.** A restart re-arms the overlay and re-runs the same path, with the
    ROM's reset-signature machinery preserving a RAM disk across the restart if one is running
    [1] §\"Support for RAM disk\" p. 46 ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §5.6).

## 6. Open questions

1. **VIA1 port A beyond the model sense.** Bit 0's diagnostic-switch role and the `$C0` identify
   pattern are observed in the ROM [5]; the recovered wiring puts the floppy side/head select on
   bit 5, but no Apple document publishes this board's port A table, and which of bits 1–4 and
   6–7 are straps, pull-ups or real I/O is unverified against silicon.
2. **VIA2 port B's sound-control bits.** The DFAC serial control on PB0/PB3/PB4 (latch, data,
   clock) is recovered wiring; the bit roles are not published in any Apple source for this
   board, and the II-family port B convention this board's other bits follow gives no DFAC
   precedent.
3. **VIA2 CB2 as the SCSI request pin.** The SCSI request is documented as a VIA2 level-2 source
   [1] pp. 36–37; the specific pin is recovered as CB2 and unverified.
4. **VIA2 PA7.** The bit reads high with no identified source; whether a real signal, a strap
   or an unused input is not established.
5. **SIMM sizes beyond the documented pair.** Whether bank B accepts 8 or 16 MB 30-pin SIMMs
   (36/68 MB totals) — later practice suggests it — is established nowhere: the launch note
   documents 1 and 4 MB parts only [1] §\"RAM control\" p. 11, and the ROM's sizing behaviour
   against such parts is unobserved.
6. **The power supply rating.** The note says only *\"the same as that in the Macintosh IIci\"*
   [1] §\"Power supply\" p. 19; the 700's own wattage, input range and rail budget appear in no
   Apple document — the IIci figures [4] p. 261 are the nearest statement, and the note's own
   15 W/slot NuBus budget is the only 700-specific power number in evidence.
7. **The single-bus claim for SCSI.** That the internal 50-pin and external DB-25 connectors
   share one electrical bus with one termination chain is recovered wiring; Apple's text says
   only that one controller serves both ([§3.4](#34-scsi-the-single-53c96-and-its-one-bus)) —
   a statement of controller count, not of bus topology.
8. **The ROM SIMM socket's contract.** What image a populated ROM SIMM must carry, its checksum
   obligations, whether the disable is purely the presence-detect Apple describes, and whether
   any Apple ROM SIMM shipped for this machine — all undocumented ([§4.3](#43-the-rom-simm-socket)).
9. **The Enhanced ASC's clock.** The 22.5792 MHz-class sample domain is inferred from the
   LC/IIsi generation this sound subsystem belongs to; the note gives no clock figure and the
   board's sound-sheet clock source is not traced end to end.
10. **STDBYPWR on the physical connector.** The 700's NuBus '90 signal table [1] Table 2-1
    pp. 23–24 carries no standby-power pin, but the complete physical pin list for this
    machine's connectors is not republished; whether the pin position the 900 uses is simply
    absent or unconnected on the 700 connector is unverified.
11. **The RTC part's exact identity.** The schematic carries a 3430042-class Apple RTC/PRAM
    device on the VIA1 serial lines [6]; the exact part marking and its PRAM size are not
    documented for this board.
12. **Orwell's clock-speed bit on this board.** The recovered configuration register has a
    system-clock-speed select ([mcu.md](mcu.md) §2.5), and the traced 700 boot serializes
    timing fields but not that bit [5] — whether the 25 MHz selection is a strap, a power-up
    default or simply unused on this board is unresolved.
